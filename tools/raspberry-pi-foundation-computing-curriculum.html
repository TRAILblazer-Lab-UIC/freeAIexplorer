Raspberry Pi Foundation Computing Curriculum

Free computing units for ages 5 to 16 with lesson plans, slides and assessments.

FREEComputingUnpluggedUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

The Raspberry Pi Foundation offers free classroom computing resources for ages 5 to 16. The resources match the curricula of England, India and Kenya. A Foundation blog post describes units that bundle an overview, lesson plans, slides, activity sheets and assessments. The units mix unplugged work with work on a device.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn computing in steps that suit their age from 5 to 16.
  • Students work on paper for some lessons and on computers for others.
  • Students can later move to related programmes such as Experience AI.

For teachers

  • Each unit comes with lesson plans, slides, activity sheets and assessments.
  • Country-specific versions exist for England, India and Kenya.
  • The teach page links to teacher training and to Ada Computer Science.

For schools

  • The classroom resources are free.
  • The materials need the internet at setup. Some lessons need computers and some do not.
  • The research did not confirm the privacy details or the account rules.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI inside the core curriculum. The Foundation links to a separate programme called Experience AI. A teacher can look there for AI lessons.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which accounts students need or where student data goes. Some lessons run on paper and some use a browser. A teacher should read each lesson before class. Check any online tool for sign-in rules and data collection before students use it.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Work through a unit overview for your age group and pick a first lesson.
  2. Complete an unplugged activity sheet with a partner.
  3. Use the lesson slides to explain an algorithm in your own words.
  4. Take an end of unit assessment and talk about the answers.
  5. Compare the England version of a unit with the India version and note the differences.

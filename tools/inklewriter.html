inklewriter

A free browser tool for writing and publishing branching interactive stories.

FREEComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

inklewriter is a free browser tool from inkle for writing and publishing branching interactive stories. It tracks which paths are finished and which still need writing. Each story gets its own web page to share. inkle promotes it to teachers and students who want to mix computer skills with creative writing. Students need no programming.

Benefits

For students

  • Students write choose-your-own-adventure stories.
  • Students practice creative writing and logical thinking.
  • Students can publish a story as a web page.

For teachers

  • The tool runs in a browser and is easy to learn.
  • It shows which story paths are unfinished.
  • Teacher blog posts describe choose-your-own-adventure writing with it.

For schools

  • The tool is free per the official site.
  • It works on Chromebooks and other devices with a browser.
  • The education page offers no lesson plans and tells teachers to email inkle for materials.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

inklewriter has no AI. It builds logic and writing skills. Students plan branches and make sure every path reaches an ending. The tool runs in the browser and stories are stored in the vendor's cloud.

Privacy and safety

Students can work locally but must register with an email address and password to save and return to their stories. The stories are stored by the vendor, and the internet is needed at all times. Inkle says the email is used only for forgotten password notices. No privacy policy was found, and the privacy page returned a 404. For students without email, inkle suggests a unique school based name entered with @inklewriter as the address. A teacher should check the privacy terms and review stories before they are shared.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a story with a choice in the first scene.
  2. Add three endings and make sure each one fits the story.
  3. Check the tool for unfinished paths and complete them.
  4. Swap stories and read each other's branches.
  5. Publish a finished story and write a short note for readers.

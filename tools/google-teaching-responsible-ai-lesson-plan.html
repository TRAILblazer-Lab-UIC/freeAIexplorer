Google teaching responsible use of AI lesson plan

A discussion lesson that helps students plan responsible use of AI models.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This Google lesson plan helps students become informed, responsible users of AI models such as Gemini. Students explore scenarios on how AI can support learning and where it poses risks. They then create a personal plan for using AI responsibly. This is the Australian edition. Search results say the full lesson runs about 90 to 125 minutes.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn what AI and generative AI are.
  • Students practice checking outputs for accuracy and bias.
  • Students write their own plan for protecting privacy and academic integrity.

For teachers

  • The download includes a PDF, student handouts, and slides.
  • The lesson is built around discussion.
  • The Gemini exercises are optional.

For schools

  • The download is free.
  • The core lesson runs on paper and discussion.
  • The vendor does not state a license in the text that was read.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The core lesson has no running AI program. It teaches students to use AI models with care. The optional exercises use Gemini, which runs in Google's cloud, and need Gemini access. Students check what the AI says for accuracy and bias, then make their own use plan.

Model accuracy
Accuracy is the share of answers an AI model gets right.
Prompt
A prompt is the request or question you give an AI tool.

Privacy and safety

The core lesson runs on paper and no account is needed. The internet is used to download the materials. The optional exercises use Gemini, which has its own accounts and data handling. The research did not review Gemini terms or read a privacy policy for this item. Prompts in those exercises go to Google's cloud. A teacher should check school policy on Gemini before using the optional exercises.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Read an AI answer and list each claim to check against a trusted source.
  2. Spot a biased statement in an AI answer and explain why it is biased.
  3. Discuss what private information should never go into an AI model.
  4. Compare an AI answer with a library source on the same topic.
  5. Build a checklist for judging AI outputs and share it with the class.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a personal plan for using AI responsibly in schoolwork.
  2. Discuss a scenario where AI helps with writing and one where it crosses a line.
  3. Write a short paragraph that explains academic integrity in your own words.
  4. Revise a piece of AI text so the final writing is your own.
  5. Write a letter to a younger student with advice about using AI.

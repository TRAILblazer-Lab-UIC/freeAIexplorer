Ardour

A free open source audio workstation for recording, editing and mixing audio and MIDI.

Computing

Overview

Ardour is a free and open source digital audio workstation. It records, edits, mixes and masters audio and MIDI. It runs on Linux, macOS and Windows and uses the GNU GPL v2 license. The site has a manual, a getting started tutorial and video tutorials. It does not target education specifically.

Benefits

For students

  • Students record, edit and mix multitrack audio.
  • Students can work with MIDI as well as audio.
  • Students use a tool that professionals also use.

For teachers

  • The site has a manual and video tutorials.
  • The source code is free to use.
  • Teachers can plan a recording project around a single tool.

For schools

  • The source is free, but the price of ready to run builds was not stated.
  • Paid builds appear to be optional.
  • IT needs to install it, and students need an audio interface and microphone to record. Release checks contact the vendor.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Ardour has no AI. Students build skills in sequencing, sound and layering as they arrange tracks. They hear how each change shapes a mix.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the audio projects stay on the local machine. Ready to run builds contact ardour.org whenever the computer is online to check for new releases. That check stores a hashed IP address, the operating system name, and a country code. The policy says personal data is not used for marketing and is hosted on AWS in the US. A teacher should read the site privacy policy and check who holds recordings of student voices.

How this can be used for Music
  1. Record a short melody on one track.
  2. Add a second track and layer a harmony.
  3. Edit a recording to fix a mistake.
  4. Mix three tracks and adjust the volume of each.
  5. Write a MIDI part and change its instrument.

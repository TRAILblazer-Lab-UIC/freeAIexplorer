KStars

Free astronomy software that simulates the night sky from any place and time.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

KStars is free, open source astronomy software from KDE. It simulates the night sky for any location, date, and time with adjustable speeds. It covers up to 100 million stars, deep sky objects, planets, and comets. Tools include an Astrocalculator, an observation planner, and What's up Tonight.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see the sky from any place and any date.
  • Students can speed up time to watch the sky move.
  • Students plan what to look for on a clear night.

For teachers

  • The handbook states the main goal is an interactive educational tool.
  • The tool is easy for students and moderate for teachers.
  • No documented school lessons were found. Teachers write their own.

For schools

  • The software is free and the code is open.
  • It runs offline after setup.
  • The apps page lists nightly builds for Windows and macOS, and IT may need to help.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

KStars has no AI. It calculates star and planet positions by fixed rules. Students build data and pattern spotting skills as they track how objects move.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the program runs offline after setup. The app page does not say what is sent over the network. It mentions optional downloadable catalogs and tools for retrieving data from online databases. A teacher should check which online features students use. A teacher should also plan any real night observing with parents and school rules.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Set the sky to your town tonight and list three objects to find.
  2. Speed up time to watch the sun and stars move.
  3. Compare the sky on two dates six months apart.
  4. Use What's up Tonight to plan an observing night.
  5. Look up a planet and record how its position changes over a week.

Celestia Navigation activity (Royal Museums Greenwich)

A Royal Observatory Greenwich teacher guide for flying through Celestia.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

This Key Stage 3 teacher guide from the Royal Observatory Greenwich covers the Celestia spaceflight simulator. The 8-page PDF gives a basic tutorial for flying to the Sun and planets, with velocity and time controls. A sample activity is included. The topics are ICT, Solar System, exoplanets, orbits, and Milky Way.

Benefits

For students

  • Students fly to the Sun and planets on screen.
  • Students use real astronomical data.
  • Students try an activity about shadows from the moons of Jupiter.

For teachers

  • The PDF gives a keyboard tutorial that teachers can follow.
  • A sample activity is ready to use.
  • The notes recommend the tour method. The flight controls are hard.

For schools

  • Celestia and the guide are free.
  • Celestia runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux.
  • IT will likely need to approve the install.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This activity has no AI. It builds the skill of pattern spotting, as students notice how moons and shadows move. Students also practice using data.

Privacy and safety

Celestia runs as a local desktop program after download, and the PDF mentions no account. The internet is needed to download it. The PDF links to the Celestia downloads at shatters.net. The research read no privacy policy, and the materials list no student data collection. A teacher should check that the download comes from the official page.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Identify the shadow of a moon on Jupiter, as in Catching Shadows on Jupiter.
  2. Fly from Earth to the Sun and describe the trip.
  3. Change the time rate and watch how a planet moves.
  4. Compare the orbits of two planets.
  5. Explain why a shadow appears on a planet in your own words.

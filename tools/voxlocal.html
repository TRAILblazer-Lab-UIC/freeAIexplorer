VoxLocal

A free Chrome extension that reads text aloud using an AI voice inside the browser.

FREEAIStays on the device

Overview

VoxLocal is a Chrome extension for text to speech. It runs the open source Kokoro-82M model inside the browser using WebGPU. It offers several English voices. The listing says all data stays local. Students in grades 6 to 12 use it to hear web text read aloud.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can listen to web text in a natural sounding voice.
  • Students can choose among several voices.
  • Students can use the tool offline after the model loads.

For teachers

  • The extension is simple to install and use.
  • It supports students who read better by listening.
  • Teachers can ask students to listen to a draft.

For schools

  • The extension is free.
  • The listing says nothing is sent to outside servers.
  • IT may need to approve it on managed devices, and it needs a WebGPU capable computer.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is the Kokoro-82M text to speech model. It runs in the browser on the device's graphics hardware. Students select text and hear it read in a chosen voice. Lower end GPUs may cause stutter.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed. The internet is used at setup. The developer's privacy policy says no data is collected, no analytics or telemetry or cookies are used, and text never leaves the device. These are developer claims and have not been audited. The extension is new with few users, and its permissions were not listed. A teacher should check that it works on school devices and read the policy.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Select a passage and listen to it read aloud while you follow along.
  2. Try each voice and pick the one that is easiest to follow.
  3. Listen to your own draft and mark any sentence that sounds unclear.
  4. Use the extension to review notes before a quiz.
  5. Describe what helps you most about listening and tell the teacher.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Listen to a short story online and write a summary.
  2. Read a poem aloud with the extension and note how the voice shapes it.
  3. Proofread an essay by listening to it.
  4. Compare reading a text with hearing it.
  5. Pick a passage and write how a human reader would say it differently.

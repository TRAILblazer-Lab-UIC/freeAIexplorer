Chibitronics Circuit Stickers

Peel and stick circuit stickers for building lit paper circuits and crafts.

ComputingUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

Chibitronics makes peel and stick circuit stickers. These are flexible circuit boards with LEDs and sensors. Learners build circuits on paper with copper tape and stickers. The company sells LED kits, a sketchbook of lessons, and educator products.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build working circuits on paper.
  • Students light LEDs and add sensors in craft projects.
  • Students mix art and electronics in one project.

For teachers

  • The sketchbook in the Level 1 and Level 2 kits holds lessons.
  • The education page offers project templates.
  • The materials are small and easy to store.

For schools

  • Kits are sold by the company, and the pages read show no prices.
  • An Educator Kit was sized for 30 students, and batteries were not included per reseller snippets.
  • No internet or accounts are needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This kit has no AI. It builds an understanding of circuits and sensors. Students learn how electricity flows through a path to light an LED.

LED
An LED is a small light that uses little power.
Physical computing
Physical computing is building projects where code connects to the real world.
Printed circuit board (PCB)
A printed circuit board is a flat board with copper paths that connect electronic parts.
Sensor
A sensor is a part that measures something in the world and turns it into a signal.

Privacy and safety

The basic LED kits have no software and no accounts, and they need no internet. The research did not review other Chibitronics products. A teacher should keep coin cell batteries away from young children. The vendor materials read do not describe other safety steps.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build a simple circuit that lights one LED on paper with copper tape.
  2. Add a second LED and compare how the circuit changes.
  3. Fix a circuit that does not light and name the likely break.
  4. Use a sensor sticker in a paper project and describe what it does.
  5. Test two battery sources and describe which works best.
How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Draw a picture with a light-up star or eye using copper tape paths.
  2. Design a greeting card with a glowing LED.
  3. Make a paper sculpture that hides the circuit.
  4. Plan a pop-up scene with lit windows.
  5. Share a project and explain the art choices and the circuit.

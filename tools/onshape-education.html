Onshape Education

A free cloud-based professional CAD tool with class and assignment tools for schools.

FREEAIComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

Onshape for Education is a cloud-based version of professional CAD software for schools. It runs in a web browser or in iOS and Android apps. Students design parts and work together in real time. Teachers manage classes and assignments inside the tool.

Benefits

For students

  • Students design in a real professional tool.
  • Students can edit together and see the version history.
  • Students can follow an Intro to CAD learning path made for K-12 or college learners new to CAD.

For teachers

  • Built-in class and assignment workflows manage student work.
  • Usage analytics show how much students engage.
  • Curriculum and teacher courses are listed at learn.onshape.com.

For schools

  • The education page says users sign up for free. The page read did not give eligibility or plan details.
  • Nothing is installed. A modern browser and an internet connection are enough.
  • IT may need to review the privacy terms.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Onshape has an optional AI Advisor that answers questions from the Onshape documentation. It runs in the vendor's cloud. Students or teachers can ask it how a tool works and then apply the answer to their own design. The CAD software itself has no AI.

Privacy and safety

Every student needs an account, and the work lives in the PTC cloud. The education page says personal details fall under Onshape's privacy policy. The policy pages the research tried returned 403 or 404. The research read no policy text. The research could not confirm COPPA or FERPA status or the age rules for student accounts. A teacher should ask IT to read the privacy terms and confirm the account rules for the grade level.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Follow the Intro to CAD path and sketch a simple part such as a bracket.
  2. Add dimensions to a sketch and extrude it into a solid shape.
  3. Make a CAD drawing of a part and check each measurement.
  4. Work with a partner in the same document and review each other's edit history.
  5. Redesign a school object, such as a pencil holder, and explain each change.
How this can be used for Career and Technical Education
  1. Complete the CAD for Robotics Competitions path and design a part for a robot.
  2. Model a product that solves a real classroom problem and present the design.
  3. Compare two versions of a design using the version history.
  4. Create a drawing that a manufacturer could read and explain each view.
  5. Research a design career and describe how CAD fits into that work.

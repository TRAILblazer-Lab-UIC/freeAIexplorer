Amplify Classroom (formerly Desmos Classroom)

A free teacher platform for K-12 activities in math, science, and literacy.

FREEComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

Desmos Classroom is now branded Amplify Classroom, and teacher.desmos.com redirects to classroom.amplify.com. The page calls it a free instructional platform with K-12 lessons in literacy, math, and science. It includes customizable activities, Polypad virtual manipulatives, Starter Screens for building activities, collections by grade and subject, and a teacher dashboard.

Benefits

For students

  • Students use virtual manipulatives to explore math ideas.
  • Students can join with a PIN.
  • Students find lessons for many subjects.

For teachers

  • Teachers can customize activities.
  • Starter Screens help teachers build their own activities.
  • A dashboard shows student work.

For schools

  • The page calls the platform free.
  • It runs in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
  • Amplify's privacy policy describes FERPA school-official handling and no sale of student data.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are optional, and students can join an activity with a PIN. Amplify handles student data as a FERPA school official and says it does not sell it. For students under 13 it relies on the school to consent. For accounts, the Common Sense evaluation lists first and last name, email, class assignment data and classroom content. That evaluation covers Desmos under earlier ownership and rates it 72 percent Pass. Data goes to the vendor, and the research did not check AI features in Amplify Classroom. A teacher should read the Amplify privacy policy and choose PIN joining where possible.

How this can be used for Math
  1. Explore a Polypad manipulative to model a fraction.

  3. Compare answers with a partner on the dashboard.
  4. Build a pattern with tiles and describe the rule.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Complete a science activity from the grade collections.
  2. Graph a set of measurements from an experiment.
  3. Compare a prediction with the data.
  4. Write a claim with evidence.
  5. Discuss how a model helps explain an idea.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Complete a literacy activity from the grade collections.
  2. Respond to a text in a short written answer.
  3. Compare a classmate's response with your own.
  4. Revise an answer after feedback.
  5. Share a favorite response with the class.

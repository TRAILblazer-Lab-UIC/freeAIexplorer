Elecfreaks TPBot for micro:bit

A micro:bit smart car with sensors and lights, used in Year 3 to 6 lessons.

ComputingRoboticsStays on the device

Overview

TPBot is a smart car robot that carries a BBC micro:bit as its controller. The car has wheels, RGB headlights, an ultrasonic sensor and line-following sensors. It can also connect to LEGO bricks. Students program it with MakeCode blocks after adding the TPBot extension. The CSER lending library at the University of Adelaide uses it in lessons for Year 3 to Year 6.

Benefits

For students

  • Students pick sensors and lights and build an algorithm that moves the car.
  • Students can attach LEGO bricks to the car.
  • Students test their code on a real robot.

For teachers

  • The CSER lesson called Moving TPBot gives a ready plan.
  • A companion CSER lesson explores the TPBot without a micro:bit.
  • The lesson assumes students already know basic MakeCode.

For schools

  • The price was not confirmed in the materials.
  • The micro:bit and batteries are separate.
  • The car runs about 1.5 hours on 4 AAA batteries.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The TPBot has no AI. It follows the algorithm students build in MakeCode. Students build skills in sequencing and sensors.

Privacy and safety

The CSER lesson mentions no accounts, and the robot runs code on the micro:bit with no cloud service. The MakeCode web editor needs the internet to load. The ELECFREAKS store policy covers store visitors only and lists Google Analytics, Google Ads, Meta, Klaviyo and Shopify as third parties. It says the site is generally intended for adults and that the company does not knowingly collect children's personal information. A teacher should check that students already know how to download code to a micro:bit and review the MakeCode privacy terms.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Choose two or more TPBot features and build an algorithm that moves the car.
  2. Program the headlights to change color as the car turns.
  3. Use the ultrasonic sensor to stop the car before a wall.
  4. Follow a taped line with the line-following sensors.
  5. Present the algorithm to the class and explain each block.

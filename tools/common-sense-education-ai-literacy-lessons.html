Common Sense Education AI Literacy Lessons

Free short lessons that teach grades 6-12 what AI is and how it affects society.

FREEAILocal with optional cloud or accountsGood for AI beginners

Overview

Common Sense Education offers a free collection of AI literacy lessons for grades 6 to 12. The grades 6 to 8 set has 11 lessons of 15 to 20 minutes. The grades 9 to 12 set has longer 45 minute lessons. Students watch videos, discuss dilemmas, and fill in handouts about topics such as AI bias, training data, chatbots, and deepfakes.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how AI is trained and why training data shapes results.
  • Students practice taking a position on real dilemmas such as deepfakes and consent.
  • Students build the habit of asking who is helped or harmed by an AI tool.

For teachers

  • Each lesson comes with slides and a student handout. Prep is light.
  • The short 15 to 20 minute format fits into a homeroom or a library visit.
  • Teachers need no coding background to lead the discussions.

For schools

  • The lessons are free.
  • Students need no devices. A projector or printed handouts are enough.
  • IT has little to do because the lessons run on paper and in a browser.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lessons teach about AI and do not run an AI tool. Nothing in the lessons runs on a device or in the cloud. Students learn how AI is trained, how bias enters, and how chatbots and deepfakes work. They use that knowledge to judge AI systems they meet outside school.

Key terms on this page

Short definitions of words used above and below. Select a term for the full glossary entry.

AI bias
AI bias is a pattern of unfair or one-sided results from an AI system.
AI literacy
AI literacy is the knowledge and skill to understand, use, and judge AI.
Chatbot
A chatbot is a program that holds a written or spoken conversation.
Cloud service
A cloud service is software that runs on a company's servers and reaches you over the internet.
Deepfake
A deepfake is a fake video, image, or audio clip made with AI.
Face recognition
Face recognition is AI that finds faces in images or matches a face to a person.
Training data
Training data is the set of examples an AI model learns from.

Privacy and safety

The lessons are discussion based with slides and handouts, and the internet is needed to load the site. The research did not confirm whether a free sign in is required. One related lesson page asks teachers to sign in to unlock materials. Students do not need accounts for discussion lessons. The research did not fetch the privacy policy. The materials list no student data collection. A teacher should check the school's rules for teacher accounts and for streaming the videos.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Use the lesson on deepfakes and consent to start a class talk about sharing images online.
  2. Write a short guide that tells younger students how to spot a chatbot answer that may be wrong.
  3. Build a display of the lesson vocabulary words with a student drawing for each one.
  4. Compare two sources on the same AI topic and list which one explains training data better.
  5. Host a lunchtime panel where students share one thing they learned about AI bias.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Hold a class debate on a dilemma from the lessons and defend a position with reasons.
  2. Write a one page letter to the principal about a rule the school should have for AI tools.
  3. Map who benefits and who is harmed by facial recognition in a public place.
  4. Role play a town meeting about whether a city should use AI to watch its streets.
  5. Research a news story about deepfakes and explain how consent was involved.

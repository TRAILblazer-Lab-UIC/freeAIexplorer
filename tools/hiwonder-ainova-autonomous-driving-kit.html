Hiwonder AiNova autonomous driving kit

A mini self-driving car kit that reads traffic lights and road signs.

$$$AIRoboticsStays on the device

Overview

The Hiwonder AiNova is a mini autonomous driving car kit for AI education. A WonderCam vision module lets the car recognize traffic lights and road signs and act on them. The CoreX controller has a button, buzzer, sound sensor, and infrared receiver. Students program it with Scratch-based blocks in the WonderCode software and upload the program over USB.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build a car and teach it to react to signs and lights.
  • Students program with Scratch-based blocks.
  • Students see how a self-driving car senses its road.

For teachers

  • The Hiwonder wiki has a full AiNova tutorial set.
  • A 31-item packing list helps teachers check the parts.
  • The kit also plays MP3 audio, which adds fun to projects.

For schools

  • RobotShop lists the kit at $319.99 and another retailer at $425.99.
  • The AI runs on the device and the internet is needed only at setup.
  • The wiki names no account need. IT should plan on days for assembly and WonderCode installation.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI recognizes traffic lights and traffic signs. It runs on the WonderCam module and the car's controller. Students write blocks that tell the car what to do after each sign. The wiki does not name the computer operating systems. A COM port example hints at Windows.

Privacy and safety

The Hiwonder wiki says the program is uploaded over USB and then runs on the robot. It names no account or cloud service. The research could not read the vendor product page and did not review a privacy policy. The materials do not state what student data the software keeps. The internet is used at setup. A teacher should check the WonderCode installer with IT before students use it.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Write a Scratch-based program that makes the car stop at a red light.
  2. Teach the car three road signs and record how often it reads each one.
  3. Change the code so the car slows down at a yield sign.
  4. Test the car with a sign held at an angle and describe the result.
  5. Draw a flowchart that shows how the car decides what to do.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Assemble the car and test every wheel and sensor.
  2. Build a small road with signs and a traffic light.
  3. Measure the car's reaction distance at different speeds.
  4. Use the buzzer to add a warning sound to the car.
  5. Write an assembly guide with photos of each stage.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Test the camera in bright and dim light and record how recognition changes.
  2. Compare how the car senses a sign with how a human driver sees one.
  3. Measure the car's stopping distance at three speeds.
  4. Use the sound sensor to make the car react to a clap and record the range.
  5. Explain how a self-driving car senses its surroundings in a short poster.

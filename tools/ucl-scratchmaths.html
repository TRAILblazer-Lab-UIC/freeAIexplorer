UCL ScratchMaths

A two year Scratch programming curriculum linked to the maths curriculum.

Computing

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

ScratchMaths is a two year Scratch programming curriculum for England Year 5 and Year 6 pupils aged 9 to 11. It has six modules and more than 40 hours of activities. Year 5 and 6 roughly match US grades 4 and 5. Students program in Scratch 3.0 to explore maths ideas.

Benefits

For students

  • Students use code to explore maths ideas such as tiling patterns and coordinates.
  • Students build with starter projects.
  • Students work through six modules over two years.

For teachers

  • Teacher materials, classroom presentations, and posters are free downloads.
  • Year 5 modules link to the maths curriculum.
  • Starter projects save planning time.

For schools

  • The materials are free to download per a search snippet of the project page.
  • The curriculum is designed for Scratch 3.0.
  • The standards listed are from the England national curriculum and are not US standards.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The materials describe no AI. Students build sequencing and logic skills. They also use patterns and coordinates in their code.

Privacy and safety

All UCL pages blocked the research. No data statement was read. Students program in Scratch 3.0 and the research did not review Scratch's own data practices. The materials do not state which accounts students need or whether the internet is needed. Check whether students need Scratch accounts and where saved projects are stored.

How this can be used for Math
  1. Build tiling patterns in Scratch and describe the shapes that fit.
  2. Explore a mathematical relationship by changing a number in a script.
  3. Use coordinates to move a sprite to a target point.
  4. Draw angles and polygons and measure them.
  5. Predict what a script will draw and then check it.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Open a starter project and describe what each block does.
  2. Use a loop to repeat a drawing step.
  3. Add a variable to control a sprite.
  4. Debug a script from a partner.
  5. Remix a starter project with a new feature.

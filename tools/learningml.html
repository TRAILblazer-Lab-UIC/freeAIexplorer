LearningML

A free tool where learners train a text or image classifier and use it in Scratch.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

LearningML is a platform where learners collect labeled texts or images and train a classification model. They then export the model to Scratch to build an app. The site offers an online editor, a Scratch version, and a desktop download. A cited study involved 135 students aged 10 to 16.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build their own training data and see how a model learns from it.
  • Students use the trained model in a Scratch project.
  • The interface comes in Spanish and English.

For teachers

  • The online editor opens in a browser in minutes.
  • A review paper reports significant gains in AI understanding, larger for learners with less prior AI knowledge.
  • Teachers can follow the same cycle of collecting data, training, and testing.

For schools

  • The tool is free and the code is open source.
  • It needs no install for the online editor, with a desktop download as an option.
  • The vendor materials do not state data handling. IT may want to review it.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a supervised classifier that students train on their own labeled texts or images. The vendor materials do not say where the training runs. Students collect examples, train the model, and test it. Then they use it in a Scratch project to extend their own program.

Privacy and safety

The research notes say accounts are optional, and the materials do not state which accounts students need. The homepage links terms of use and a contact form and describes no privacy policy. The review lists TensorFlow.js as the backend and does not say where training runs or where data is stored. A desktop version exists. A teacher should check the site's terms and whether training data leaves the device. A teacher should also review any images students upload. Photos of people are possible.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Train a classifier to sort texts into two moods and test it with new sentences.
  2. Add a mislabeled example and observe how the model changes.
  3. Export a trained model to Scratch and build a small game that uses it.
  4. Compare a model trained on ten examples with one trained on thirty.
  5. Write a short note on what the model got wrong and why.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Collect photos of leaves and train a model to sort them into types.
  2. Record how accuracy changes as the data set grows.
  3. Test the model on a leaf that was left out of training and explain the result.
  4. Design a fair test with a balanced set of examples.
  5. Present the data and results as a short class report.

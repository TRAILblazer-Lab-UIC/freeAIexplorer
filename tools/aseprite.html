Aseprite

A pixel art and animation editor with layers, frames and sprite sheet export.

$ComputingStays on the device

Overview

Aseprite is a pixel art and animation editor. It supports layers, frames and cels, tagged animations, onion skin, palette control and tiled mode. It exports PNG sequences, GIFs and sprite sheets. It is sold as a paid binary, and the source can be compiled for personal use.

Benefits

For students

  • Students draw pixel art and animate it frame by frame.
  • Students export GIFs and sprite sheets.
  • Students learn animation with onion skin.

For teachers

  • Teachers can request an educational license for classroom redistribution.
  • A school project page describes a class that explored animation with pixel art.
  • Free browser options such as Piskel and LibreSprite exist.

For schools

  • The 1.x series costs 19.99 USD at minimum.
  • The source is free to compile for personal use.
  • IT needs to install it, and it is not available on Chromebooks.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Aseprite has no AI. Students build skills in sequencing and patterns as they draw frames in order. They see how small changes in each frame create motion.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the internet is used at setup. Work is saved on the device. The materials do not say what data the software collects. The FAQ links to a separate privacy page that the research did not read. A teacher should read that privacy page and check the license terms before installing it on many devices.

How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Draw a small character in pixel art with a limited palette.
  2. Make a walk cycle in four frames.
  3. Use onion skin to keep a drawing steady between frames.
  4. Create a seamless pattern with tiled mode.
  5. Export your art as a GIF and share it with the class.
How this can be used for Theater and Media Arts
  1. Animate a short loop for a school event.
  2. Plan an animation with a storyboard before drawing.
  3. Make a sprite sheet for a character.
  4. Tag animations as idle, walk and jump.
  5. Compare frame rate choices and describe the effect.

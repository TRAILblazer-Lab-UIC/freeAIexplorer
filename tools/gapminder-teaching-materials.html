Gapminder teaching materials

Free slides, posters, videos, and data tools from the Gapminder Foundation.

FREEComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

The Gapminder Foundation is an independent Swedish foundation. Its teaching page lists interactive tools such as Dollar Street and Bubble Charts. It also lists downloadable slides, a World Health Chart poster, and a video collection. Teachers use the materials to help students explore world data.

Benefits

For students

  • Students explore world data with animated bubble charts.
  • Students see how health and income have changed over time.
  • Students build comfort with graphs and statistics.

For teachers

  • Slides and a poster can be downloaded and used right away.
  • The page says the tools and materials are free and always will be.
  • Documented uses cover global history, statistics, data science, and geography.

For schools

  • The materials cost nothing.
  • Gapminder Tools Offline runs without internet on Windows, Mac, and Linux.
  • Student accounts are not needed and the page lists no data collection.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This item has no AI. It builds data skills. Students read charts and spot trends over time. The tools run in the browser or on the computer.

Privacy and safety

The page mentions no accounts and no student data collection. The online tools run at gapminder.org and need the internet each time. Only the offline tools and downloaded slides work without it. The research did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should check the privacy policy of any online tool before students use it. A teacher should also note that Dollar Street photos illustrate conditions and are not exact statistics.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Use the slides to compare how life changed in two regions over fifty years.
  2. Study global history through a quantitative lens, as an NYC iSchool course does.
  3. Pair Gapminder charts with Worldmapper maps in a geography lesson.
  4. Browse Dollar Street photos and write what the pictures show about daily life.
  5. Hang the World Health Chart poster and ask students to find one surprising fact.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Read a bubble chart and name what size, color, and position each mean.
  2. Look at statistics with the data tools, as a Year 8 class has done.
  3. Teach Dollar Street in a data science class and sort photos by income.
  4. Find two indicators that move together and describe the pattern.
  5. Explain why one data point cannot describe a whole country.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Animate a bubble chart of life expectancy and describe the trend.
  2. Compare child health data between two regions across decades.
  3. Use the video collection to start a discussion about global health.
  4. Predict what a chart will show, then run the tool and check the guess.
  5. Write a short summary of a health trend using one chart as evidence.

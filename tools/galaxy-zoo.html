Galaxy Zoo

A citizen science project where volunteers classify galaxies by shape.

Computing

Overview

Galaxy Zoo is a citizen science project where volunteers classify galaxies by shape from telescope images. It is hosted on the Zooniverse platform and has run for over 15 years. It now includes early images from the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. Classifications are combined with those of other volunteers to support astronomers.

Benefits

For students

  • Students help real astronomers.
  • Students practice looking closely at images.
  • Students can chat with the research team on the Talk forum.

For teachers

  • Zooniverse states the project is used in classrooms worldwide.
  • It is listed in Zooniverse Curated K-12 Projects.
  • Lessons include Astronomy 101 with Galaxy Zoo and Using Galaxy Zoo in the Classroom.

For schools

  • It is free.
  • It runs in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
  • An account is optional.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI for the volunteer work. The project builds pattern spotting and data skills. Students sort galaxies by shape and contribute to a shared data set.

Privacy and safety

The site is always online and runs in the vendor cloud. An account is optional. The data collected is classifications, and account details if a user registers. The Zooniverse privacy page did not load in the research. Its content was not read. Common Sense Education lists the privacy as not yet rated. The research did not confirm the account rules for students under 13. A teacher should read the Zooniverse privacy terms and decide whether students register or classify without an account.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Use the Astronomy 101 lesson to classify a first set of galaxies.
  2. Compare spiral and smooth galaxies in a table.
  3. Ask a question on the Talk forum with teacher review.
  4. Count the shapes in a set of images and make a chart.
  5. Write a short note on what the class learned.

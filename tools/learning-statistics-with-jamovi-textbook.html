Learning Statistics with jamovi (textbook)

A free open-access statistics textbook that teaches with jamovi software.

FREEUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

This is an open-access introductory statistics textbook by Danielle Navarro and David Foxcroft, published by Open Book Publishers in 2025. It adapts Navarro's Learning Statistics with R and teaches with the jamovi software. Chapters cover research design, jamovi basics, descriptive statistics, graphs, probability, hypothesis testing, t-tests, correlation, regression, ANOVA, and factor analysis. The stated audience is undergraduate psychology, health, and social science students.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can read a full statistics text for free.
  • Students follow jamovi walkthroughs to practice on real data.
  • Students can see how statistics is used in research.

For teachers

  • The book comes as a PDF and as an HTML edition.
  • Data files come from a jamovi module or the book website.
  • Suitability for grades 10-12 is not confirmed. Teachers should pick chapters with care.

For schools

  • The PDF and HTML editions are free. Print editions are sold.
  • jamovi must be installed. IT will likely need to help.
  • The book was first released in 2018, and the publisher edition followed in January 2025.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This book has no AI. It teaches the statistics that machine learning later builds on. Students build data skills as they summarize and graph data.

Privacy and safety

The book is free to read and needs no account. The research did not evaluate the jamovi software itself, and the materials describe no data sent out. The jamovi Cloud version runs on vendor servers and the desktop app works on the computer. A teacher should use sample data files and avoid putting student information into jamovi.

How this can be used for Math
  1. Open a data file in jamovi and describe the variables.
  2. Make a graph of one variable and describe its shape.
  3. Compute the mean and spread of a class survey.
  4. Test whether two groups differ using a t-test from the book.
  5. Draw a regression line for two variables and explain what it shows.

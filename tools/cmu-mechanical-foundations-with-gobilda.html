CMU Mechanical Foundations with goBILDA

A three-unit mechanical design course using goBILDA parts and radio control.

RoboticsSends student data to a company

Overview

This is a three-unit mechanical design course that follows the same objectives as the REV DUO version. It uses goBILDA structure, motion and drivetrain parts. It also uses the goBILDA Element-6 radio control system. The course is hosted on the CS-STEM Network. A search result ties both versions to FIRST Tech Challenge preparation.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build with real robot parts.
  • Students work through three units of mechanical design.
  • Students prepare for FIRST Tech Challenge.

For teachers

  • The course follows a clear three-unit sequence.
  • Teachers may use CS2N to follow progress.
  • Teachers need strong robotics experience.

For schools

  • A batch note says free with a CS2N account. The pages read do not state a price.
  • Schools must buy goBILDA parts and the Element-6 system.
  • IT and teacher setup are likely needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This course has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is understanding how machines move. Gears, motors and drivetrains are the parts of many robots.

Privacy and safety

The course is hosted online on CS2N, which is a cloud service. Course pages say access and tracking go through CS2N, and the research did not confirm whether accounts are required. The CS2N policy lists name, email, username, group membership, and course activity. It says it does not sell or rent personal information. It allows use by children under 13 with consent from a parent, school, district, or teacher. It does not name COPPA or say where data is stored. A teacher should read the CS2N privacy policy and get approval from the school before creating accounts. Students should wear safety glasses when building.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build a strong frame and test it under a small load.
  2. Compare two structures and say which is more stable.
  3. Pick a motor and gear set for a given speed.
  4. Assemble a drivetrain and test it on the floor.
  5. Upload evidence of your project to the course.

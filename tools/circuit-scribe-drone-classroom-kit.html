Circuit Scribe Drone Classroom Kit

A classroom bundle where students draw drone circuits with conductive ink.

Robotics

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This is a classroom bundle of the Circuit Scribe Drone Builder Kit. Listings describe 10 drones, 10 conductive ink pens, 50 g of conductive paint, motors, spare propellers, 10 hubs with a camera and 20 batteries. Students draw the drone's circuits onto cardboard arms with silver ink. They attach the hub and motors and fly the drone from a phone app. The focus is building and flying and not programming.

Benefits

For students

  • Students draw real circuits and see them power a drone.
  • Students fly the drone and see live video from the camera.
  • Students learn how electricity flows through a path.

For teachers

  • The kit includes pre-made PCB arms as a backup.
  • The bundle supplies enough parts for a class.
  • Lesson materials for the classroom kit were not found.

For schools

  • The price was not confirmed.
  • A reseller showed the kit as unavailable. A school should check stock.
  • A review says the cardboard arms can break in crashes and the clip-on motors can come off.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This kit has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is understanding how sensors and motors connect. Students see how a circuit sends power to each motor.

Privacy and safety

The drone makes its own wifi network, and the phone connects to it. Video streams from the drone camera to the phone. The materials do not state which accounts the app needs or what data it stores. The research reviewed no privacy policy and did not verify whether the app collects data. A teacher should check the app rules and the camera before use. Flying drones need adult supervision and open space away from faces.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Draw a circuit on a cardboard arm and attach the motor.
  2. Build the drone body and attach the hub.
  3. Test each motor and fix any loose clip.
  4. Fly the drone through a simple course.
  5. Repair a damaged arm and describe the fix.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Trace how current moves along a conductive ink path.
  2. Test a broken line of ink and see the motor stop.
  3. Predict which circuit will power a motor.
  4. Compare ink lines of different thickness.
  5. Explain in your own words why a complete loop is needed.

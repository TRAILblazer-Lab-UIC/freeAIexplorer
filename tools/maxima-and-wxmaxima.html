Maxima and wxMaxima

A free computer algebra system for symbolic math and plotting.

Computing

Overview

Maxima is a free computer algebra system for symbolic and numerical math. It handles differentiation, integration, differential equations, matrices, and 2D and 3D plotting. It has been under the GPL since 1998. The wxMaxima and XMaxima interfaces give a notebook style view, and it installs on Windows, Linux, and macOS.

Benefits

For students

  • Students check algebra and calculus work with a tool.
  • Students plot functions in two and three dimensions.
  • Students see exact answers as well as decimals.

For teachers

  • The wxMaxima notebook is easier than the plain terminal.
  • Teachers can demonstrate calculus steps live.
  • The tool is advanced. Plan time to learn it.

For schools

  • The software is free.
  • It runs on an ordinary computer with no account.
  • It is not for Chromebooks without Linux support. IT may need to approve the install.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Maxima has no AI. The vendor describes no AI features. It follows exact rules of algebra. Students build skills in logic and pattern spotting as they check symbolic results.

Privacy and safety

Maxima runs on the device and needs no account. The internet is used for download and setup. The research read no privacy policy. The site also offers an online try out, which the research did not examine. A teacher should check where students save notebooks on shared computers and avoid the online try out until it is reviewed.

How this can be used for Math
  1. Factor and expand expressions and compare the results with hand work.
  2. Differentiate a function and plot both the function and its derivative.
  3. Solve a system of equations with a matrix.
  4. Integrate a function and explain what the answer means.
  5. Plot a 3D surface and describe its shape.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Plot the path of a projectile from its equation.
  2. Solve a simple differential equation for cooling.
  3. Compare two models by plotting them on one graph.
  4. Use symbols to solve a physics formula for one variable.
  5. Explain how the plot matches an experiment.

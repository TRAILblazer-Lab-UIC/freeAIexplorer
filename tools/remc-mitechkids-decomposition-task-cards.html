REMC MiTechKids decomposition task cards

A second grade task card that teaches decomposition with goal, materials and steps.

FREEComputingUnpluggedStays on the device

Overview

This is a second grade task card on decomposition, the first step of computational thinking. Students break a problem such as making a pizza into a goal, materials and steps. They write the steps and share them with the class. The page also lists extension ideas for ELA, math, science and social studies.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn to split a big task into small parts.
  • Students write their own steps and hear how classmates did it.
  • Students see decomposition in other subjects.

For teachers

  • Objectives and vocabulary sit on one page.
  • Assessment is by observation with a simple rubric.
  • The card matches MITECS Computational Thinker 5a and 5c.

For schools

  • The resource is free.
  • The task needs only paper and a short setup.
  • It collects no student data.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This task card has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is breaking a problem into parts. Computer programs and AI tools are also built from small steps.

Privacy and safety

The activity runs on paper with an optional web page for the poster and videos. No accounts are needed and no student data is collected. The internet is used only at setup. A teacher should preview the videos before showing them to the class.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Break the problem of making a pizza into a goal, materials and steps.
  2. Write the steps in order and read them to the class.
  3. Swap lists with another group and look for a missing step.
  4. Pick a morning routine and decompose it.
  5. Explain in a sentence why smaller steps make a task easier.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Decompose a riddle into clues and write each clue as a step.
  2. Break a short story into beginning, middle and end.
  3. List the goal, materials and steps for writing a paragraph.
  4. Retell a story by writing its main events in order.
  5. Write your own riddle and show how it breaks into parts.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Decompose a word problem into what you know and what you need to find.
  2. Break a number into tens and ones.
  3. List the steps to solve an addition problem.
  4. Write the goal and steps for sorting shapes.
  5. Explain a math problem to a partner by listing its parts.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Decompose a plant growth experiment into a goal, materials and steps.
  2. List the steps for observing the weather each day.
  3. Break the water cycle into smaller stages.
  4. Write the steps to sort objects by what they are made of.
  5. Compare your steps with a partner and fix any gaps.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Decompose the job of planning a class event into a goal, materials and steps.
  2. List the steps a community helper takes to do a job.
  3. Break a map task into small parts.
  4. Write the steps to make a class rule.
  5. Share your steps and discuss which part is most important.

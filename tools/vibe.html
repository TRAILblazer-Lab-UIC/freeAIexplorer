Vibe

A free desktop app that transcribes audio and video on your own device.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

Vibe is an open source desktop app that transcribes audio and video on the user's own device. It runs Whisper, Nemotron 3.5, and Parakeet TDT v3 models. It can label speakers and process files in batches. It suits students in high school and teachers who need captions or interview transcripts.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can turn a recorded interview into text.
  • Students can transcribe a microphone recording or system audio.
  • Students can export captions to SRT, VTT, TXT, HTML, PDF, JSON, or DOCX.

For teachers

  • Teachers can make captions for class videos.
  • Speaker labels help with group recordings.
  • A command line and HTTP API are available for advanced use.

For schools

  • The app is free and open source.
  • Transcription runs on the school computer.
  • IT will likely need to install it, and Chromebooks are not supported.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a speech recognition model that runs on the device. It turns spoken words into text and can tell speakers apart. Optional summaries use the Claude API, and Ollama is offered as a local option. Students use the transcript to review, quote, and edit their own recordings.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed. The internet is used at setup for the model download and for any audio download from video sites. The project page says transcription runs fully offline and no data leaves the device. No formal privacy policy was found. The optional summary feature sends text to the Claude API, which takes student data off the device. A teacher should leave the summary setting off or use the local Ollama option.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Transcribe a classroom video and check the captions for errors.
  2. Add captions to a short student presentation.
  3. Compare the transcript with the audio and fix mistakes.
  4. Use the transcript as a study aid for a lesson.
  5. Export a transcript in two formats and decide which is easier to read.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Record an interview with a classmate and transcribe it.
  2. Edit the transcript into a clear written profile.
  3. Mark the speakers in a group discussion transcript.
  4. Compare spoken and written language in the same piece.
  5. Turn a podcast style recording into a short article.

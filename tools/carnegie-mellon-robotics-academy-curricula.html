Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy curricula

Robotics courses for many platforms, with some AI courses and virtual robots.

AIRobotics

Overview

The Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy publishes robotics curricula hosted on the CS-STEM Network, called CS2N. Categories are Virtual Robotics, Mobile Robotics, Robotics Technician, and Legacy. Platforms include LEGO SPIKE and EV3, VEX IQ, V5 and EXP, Arduino ShieldBot, NVIDIA Jetson Nano, REV DUO, goBILDA, CoDrone EDU, and Crazyflie. AI content includes machine vision and a LEGO CS and AI course for grades 6-8.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn robotics from intro to advanced levels.
  • Students can use virtual robots with no hardware.
  • Students try AI topics such as machine vision and collision avoidance.

For teachers

  • CS2N offers class management and progress tracking.
  • Courses cover many robot platforms.
  • Paid onsite and online teacher training is offered.

For schools

  • Search results say physical-robot curricula are free on CS2N.
  • Training is paid.
  • The internet is used throughout, and CS2N accounts track progress. IT should review the data practices closely.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Some courses teach AI, including machine vision, collision avoidance, path following, AprilTag navigation, SLAM, and AI training on JetBot. The AI runs on the robot. The CS2N site itself runs in the cloud. Students extend their work by training and testing robot behavior.

Privacy and safety

Curricula are accessed and progress is tracked through CS2N accounts, which are optional for educators and students. The internet is needed throughout, and student data goes to the vendor. The research did not read the CS2N privacy policy, and the materials do not state what data is collected. Robot code runs on the robot or in each platform's programming software. A teacher should read the CS2N privacy policy and ask whether accounts are needed for the classes.

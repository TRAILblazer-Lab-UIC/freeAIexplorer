National Geographic Training Machines Together

A grade 5 to 8 lesson on how machine learning supports citizen science with iNaturalist.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

Training Machines Together is a lesson for grades 5 to 8 from the National Geographic Society. It shows how machine learning can support citizen science, with iNaturalist as the example. Students describe machine learning in their own words and discuss how iNaturalist names species. They then design a citizen science project and make an infographic.

Benefits

For students

  • Students link machine learning to real science that volunteers do.
  • Students work in small groups to research another example.
  • Students make an infographic that explains their own project idea.

For teachers

  • The lesson is built on discussion and an infographic. Prep is light.
  • The page lists a clear sequence of group tasks.
  • No special software is needed.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • It works in a web browser on a Chromebook.
  • Students need internet-enabled devices for the research step.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This lesson teaches about machine learning and does not run an AI tool of its own. Students learn how a system like iNaturalist can suggest a species from a photo. Students then extend the idea by designing a project that uses machine learning to sort citizen science data.

Privacy and safety

The materials list no accounts or tools, and they describe no student data collection. Students do not use iNaturalist in the described steps. The internet is needed for the research step and sends searches to the sites students visit. A teacher should preview any site students use and set rules for group research.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Explain in your own words how iNaturalist helps name a plant or animal.
  2. Research another citizen science project that uses machine learning and present it to the group.
  3. List the data a citizen science project would need to collect.
  4. Design a project that tracks local birds and describe how a model could help.
  5. Make an infographic that shows how volunteers and machine learning work together.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Describe how a model can learn to name a species from photos.
  2. Discuss what could go wrong when a model sees an unusual photo.
  3. List what training data an image model would need for local trees.
  4. Draw the steps from a photo to a species suggestion.
  5. Compare two group projects and say which one needs more data.

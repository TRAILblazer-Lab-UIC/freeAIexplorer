ACMI Creative Writing Using Twine unit

A Year 9 and 10 unit where students write interactive fiction in Twine.

Computing

Overview

This English unit is for Year 9 and 10 students. They play free interactive fiction and then build their own in Twine. The unit runs 4 to 5 weeks in 50 minute lessons. Students study second-person narration, plan and build a story, and give structured feedback.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how second-person narration shapes a reader's experience.
  • Students plan a branching story and build it in Twine.
  • Students give and receive feedback and then revise their work.

For teachers

  • The unit is a full plan for 4 to 5 weeks.
  • The lesson plan is licensed CC BY 2.0.
  • Teachers choose the interactive fiction texts to play.

For schools

  • The unit and Twine are free.
  • Twine runs in a browser or as a download on Windows, Mac, Linux and Chromebooks.
  • Student stories stay on the device, and no account is needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The unit has no AI. Twine builds the skill of branching logic as students link passages by choices. Students plan how each choice leads to a new part of the story.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the page mentions no accounts. Twine runs in a browser or as a download and the dossier lists the data destination as the device. The research did not read Twine's privacy terms. The internet is used at setup to get Twine and the texts. A teacher should check the Twine terms with IT. A teacher should also decide how students will back up their files.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Play an interactive fiction text and note how second-person narration addresses the reader.
  2. Plan a story map with a clear start and three different endings.
  3. Write a scene in second person with a choice at the end.
  4. Swap stories with a partner and give structured feedback.
  5. Revise a passage after feedback and explain what you changed.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a Twine story with links between passages.
  2. Draw a flow chart of the choices in your story.
  3. Track a variable such as courage and change a scene based on it.
  4. Test every path in a classmate's story and report any dead ends.
  5. Explain how the story branches like a program with if then logic.

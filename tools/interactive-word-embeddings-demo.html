Interactive Word Embeddings demo

A web demo for exploring how word vectors capture meaning in 3D.

FREEAIUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This is a web demo for exploring word embeddings built from Word2Vec and GloVe vectors with 300 dimensions. Users add words to a board, run vector analogy arithmetic, and define their own semantic axes. It also has an Odd One Out game and a Neighbor Navigator. A 2023 review paper says the University of Maryland and Carnegie Mellon University developed it for high school and college students.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see words as points in 3D space.
  • Students can test analogies such as king minus man plus woman.
  • Students play games that make word relationships clear.

For teachers

  • A Tutorial section and Experiments section guide the first use.
  • The demo runs in a browser.
  • The paper reports that 3D plots and analogies were intuitive, and that user-defined dimensions needed more explanation.

For schools

  • The demo is free.
  • No installation or account is described.
  • No formal classroom deployment is reported.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is word embeddings, which turn words into lists of numbers. The vendor does not say where the demo runs. Students add words, run analogy arithmetic, and watch which words sit close together. They use what they see to explain how a language model might link words.

Privacy and safety

No account or login appears on the demo page, and the internet is always needed. The page states nothing about data collection, a privacy policy, or whether embeddings are computed in the browser or on a server. The research could not confirm where typed words go. A teacher should open the site first and ask students not to type personal words.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Add ten words to the board and describe which cluster together.
  2. Run an analogy and judge whether the answer makes sense.
  3. Play Odd One Out and explain why the word does not fit.
  4. Use Neighbor Navigator to move from one word to another and record the path.
  5. Explain in a paragraph what a 300 dimension vector means.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Compare words with close meanings and note how near they sit.
  2. Test an analogy about gender or jobs and discuss any bias you see.
  3. Group words by tone and look for a pattern.
  4. Define an axis such as formal to casual and place ten words on it.
  5. Write a short note on how a computer could misread a word with two meanings.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Add and subtract two word vectors and describe the result.
  2. Place words on a 3D graph and read their coordinates.
  3. Compare distances between three pairs of words.
  4. Explain why a direction in space can stand for a meaning.
  5. Predict a nearest neighbor and check the guess.

Lesley University Our Neighborhood through Scratch

A third grade lesson plan where students map their neighborhood in Scratch.

Overview

Our Neighborhood through Scratch is a third grade social studies lesson plan. A search summary says one part has students overlay a Scratch maze on a Google Map of their neighborhood. Another part has them build a neighborhood map in Scratch with each landmark as a sprite. The PDF could not be fetched. These details come from a search summary.

Benefits

For students

  • Students show their own neighborhood in a project.
  • Students make a maze that others can play.
  • Students practice coding in blocks.

For teachers

For schools

  • The lesson and Scratch are free.

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI. Students code with Scratch blocks that follow exact steps. They build sequencing and logic skills as they plan a path through a maze.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. The plan uses a Google Map as a backdrop according to a search snippet. Scratch hosting and Google Maps terms apply if the project is used online, and neither was reviewed. The research could not read the PDF. A teacher should check the Scratch account rules and the map source before class. A teacher should also avoid maps that show student homes.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Map the school neighborhood with a landmark sprite for each place, as the lesson plan describes.
  2. Label each landmark and say why it matters.
  3. Add a path that shows how to walk from school to a park.
  4. Compare two maps made by classmates.
  5. Present the map and tell a neighbor story.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a maze with walls that a sprite cannot cross.
  2. Program a sprite to move with the arrow keys.
  3. Add a goal that ends the game.
  4. Add a second level with a harder path.
  5. Test a partner's maze and suggest one change.

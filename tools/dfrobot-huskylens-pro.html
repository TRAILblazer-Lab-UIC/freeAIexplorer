DFRobot HuskyLens PRO

An AI vision camera with nine built-in functions and online programming through Mind+.

AIRoboticsStays on the deviceGood for AI beginners

Overview

HuskyLens PRO is an AI vision camera from DFRobot with a Kendryte K210 chip and a 5.0 MP camera. It offers nine built-in functions, which add object classification, QR code, and barcode recognition to the original six. The page says it is designed for grades 6 to 12. Students teach it objects and can program a controller with Mind+.

Benefits

For students

  • Students choose from nine vision functions for a robot or sensing project.
  • Students teach the camera objects and watch it learn.
  • Students can program a controller online with Mind+.

For teachers

  • The vendor page names grades 6 to 12 as the target.
  • A Gravity cable is included for UART and I2C connections.
  • Teachers can start with the on-screen results and add a controller later.

For schools

  • The DFRobot page shows no price. A school must check with the vendor.
  • The AI runs on the device and needs no internet or account.
  • The research found no cloud service. Setup takes about an hour and needs a teacher install.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a set of nine vision functions that run on the camera itself. They include face, object, color, tag, QR code, and barcode recognition. Students teach the camera with examples and then use the results in their own programs. The vendor sources describe no cloud AI.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the page lists wired interfaces only with no cloud service. The research found no student data collected, but no privacy policy was found and only vendor sources were read. Mind+ is described as online programming, and its internet and account needs are unconfirmed. A teacher should check that Mind+ fits school rules for web tools.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Teach the camera two similar objects and test how well it tells them apart.
  2. Use object classification to sort pictures into groups and record the accuracy.
  3. Scan a QR code with the camera and make a controller show a matching message.
  4. Program a controller in Mind+ to react to a recognized face.
  5. Compare tag recognition and color recognition as ways to identify objects.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Add the camera to a robot and use line tracking to follow a course.
  2. Build a barcode checkpoint that makes a robot stop at a certain code.
  3. Design a mount that gives the camera a clear view of the floor.
  4. Wire the Gravity cable to a controller and test both UART and I2C.
  5. Run a test of the 5.0 MP camera at several distances and chart the results.

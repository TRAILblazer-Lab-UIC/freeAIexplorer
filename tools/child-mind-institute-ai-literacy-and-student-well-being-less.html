Child Mind Institute AI Literacy and Student Well-Being lesson

A 30-minute lesson on how chatbots work and when students should turn to people.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

This is a 30-minute lesson for grades 6-12 from the Child Mind Institute. It explains how AI chatbots make answers, where they fall short, and why students should seek people for mental health help. It uses three short videos, a pre and post poll, and a discussion game called Identifying AI. Teachers lead the whole class.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how a chatbot makes a response.
  • Students learn the limits of AI for mental health support.
  • Students leave with a handout of mental health resources.

For teachers

  • The lesson fits in one 30-minute period.
  • Polls show what students think before and after.
  • Teachers need only a screen, audio, and printed worksheets.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • Students do not need accounts or devices.
  • The lesson runs from a browser and printed pages.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no AI tool that students use. It teaches how chatbots work, what they cannot do, and what healthy AI use looks like. The videos are hosted in the vendor's cloud. Students build the skill of judging when a chatbot is the wrong place to turn for help.

Privacy and safety

The lesson is teacher led with printed worksheets, and the videos stream over the internet. No student accounts are needed. The page does not describe student data collection. Teachers complete the polls and a feedback form. Video 2 touches on suicide. A teacher should preview it and prepare support. A teacher should also share the school's counseling contacts before the lesson.

Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Take the pre-poll and compare it with the post-poll results.
  2. List people at school and at home who can help with a hard feeling.
  3. Discuss why a chatbot is the wrong place for a mental health crisis.
  4. Create a poster with trusted contacts and a short rule for healthy AI use.
  5. Role play asking a trusted adult for help.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Play the Identifying AI game and note the clues that gave it away.
  2. Describe in your own words how a chatbot makes an answer.
  3. List three things a chatbot does well and three things it does poorly.
  4. Compare a chatbot answer with a source from the library.
  5. Write a guideline for healthy AI use in your school.

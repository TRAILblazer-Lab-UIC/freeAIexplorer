Kitronik Inventor's Kit and Discovery Kit for micro:bit

Two breadboard component kits that teach micro:bit electronics with no soldering.

ComputingStays on the device

Overview

These are two component kits for the BBC micro:bit. The Discovery Kit has a breadboard, LEDs, resistors, a buzzer, and jumper wires with a booklet of five MakeCode experiments. The Inventor's Kit adds a motor, a fan, a potentiometer, a transistor, and sensors with a tutorial book. Neither kit includes a micro:bit and neither needs soldering.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build real circuits on a breadboard.
  • Students link code to lights, sound, and motion.
  • Students can start with the Discovery Kit and move up to the Inventor's Kit.

For teachers

  • Each kit comes with a booklet of experiments.
  • Teachers can use MakeCode or Python.
  • Reviews call the kit good value with more emphasis on electronics than coding.

For schools

  • The Discovery Kit costs 10.70 GBP ex VAT for one and 8.60 GBP at 50 or more.
  • The Inventor's Kit price on the page was unclear. Confirm it with the vendor.
  • A micro:bit V1 or V2, a USB cable, and a screwdriver are needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The kits have no AI. Students build circuits and code that follow exact steps. They build sensor, logic, and sequencing skills as they link inputs to outputs.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed for the kit. Programs load onto the micro:bit over USB. MakeCode is a web editor whose data handling was not reviewed, and the Kitronik privacy page returned a 404. A teacher should check the MakeCode terms. A teacher should also check the battery and wiring steps before class and supervise the motor and the fan.

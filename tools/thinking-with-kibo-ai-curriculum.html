Thinking with KIBO (AI curriculum)

A free five-lesson unit that uses the KIBO robot to explore AI ideas.

FREEAIRoboticsUnpluggedStays on the deviceGood for AI beginners

Overview

Thinking with KIBO is a 2023 five-lesson unit for ages 6 to 9 from KinderLab Robotics. KIBO is a screen-free robot kit that serves here as an object to think with about AI. KIBO is not an advanced AI system. The lessons use its sensors and behavior to raise questions about automation and AI. Each lesson runs about one hour.

Benefits

For students

  • Students explore AI ideas through a robot they can touch.
  • Students see how sensing leads to action.
  • Students talk about what KIBO can and cannot do.

For teachers

  • The unit is a free PDF with five ready lessons.
  • Lesson titles make the plan easy to follow.
  • Teachers who know KIBO can start quickly.

For schools

  • The PDF is free, but KIBO kits are bought separately and no price was given.
  • The unit needs no internet and no accounts.
  • Student data is not collected.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

KIBO has no advanced AI. The unit uses its sensors and behavior to discuss automation and AI. The lessons are Lesson 1 AI and Automation, Lesson 2 Sensing and Organizing, Lesson 3 A KIBO Rover, Lesson 4 What is KIBO's Personality, and Lesson 5 KIBO Can Help Us. Students build skills in sensing, sequencing, and asking what a machine can do.

Privacy and safety

KIBO has no AI model and no screen. The unit teaches concepts offline with a KIBO kit and no accounts. The dossier records no student data collected. The research did not fetch the unit PDF. Check that each KIBO kit is ready before class. Younger children need supervision with small robot parts.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Discuss how KIBO follows a program and compare it with a person following steps.
  2. Show KIBO a sensor and explain what it tells the robot.
  3. Sort a set of KIBO behaviors into automated and not automated.
  4. Create a short program that reacts to a sensor.
  5. Name one task in school that a robot might help with and one it should not.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build a KIBO rover from the lesson and test how it moves.
  2. Test a KIBO sensor at different distances and record what happens.
  3. Redesign the rover to help with a classroom task.
  4. Draw how the sensor, the blocks, and the wheels work together.
  5. Present the rover and explain how it helps.

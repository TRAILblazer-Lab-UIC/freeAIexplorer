FizziQ

Free apps that turn phones and tablets into science measurement tools.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

FizziQ is a set of free apps that turn smartphones, tablets, and computers into science measurement tools. It offers over 50 instruments, such as a sound meter, luxmeter, and gyroscope, using the device sensors. Versions exist for iOS and Android, for the web, and for ages 7 to 12 in FizziQ Junior. Students record data in an experiment notebook and can export PDF, CSV, or Python.

Benefits

For students

  • Students measure the world with devices they know.
  • Students record results in an experiment notebook.
  • Students export data for deeper study.

For teachers

  • The site lists over 120 activities.
  • Video analysis and chronophotography support kinematics.
  • It runs in seven languages.

For schools

  • It is free for teachers and students.
  • No account is needed.
  • FizziQ Connect is an optional Bluetooth box with a separate cost that was not checked.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

FizziQ has no AI. It reads the sensors in the device and runs on the device itself. It builds data and sensing skills. Students collect measurements and look for patterns.

Privacy and safety

The official site says there is no sign-up, no personal data collection, and no ads. That is the vendor's own statement. FizziQ Junior has its own privacy policy that the research did not read. Another privacy policy page could not be reached. The apps run on the device, and the web version needs the internet to load. A teacher should read the Junior privacy policy before using that app. A teacher should also check that students keep exported files in a safe place.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Measure sound levels in three places at school.
  2. Measure light in a room at different times.
  3. Use video analysis to study a moving object.
  4. Record a result in the notebook and write a conclusion.
  5. Compare two measurements and explain the difference.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Export data as CSV and make a graph.
  2. Find an average of repeated measurements.
  3. Compare two data sets using a table.
  4. Use chronophotography to find speed from distance and time.
  5. Write a short Python script to read the exported data.

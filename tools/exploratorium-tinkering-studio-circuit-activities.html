Exploratorium Tinkering Studio circuit activities

Free activity guides for paper, sewn, and board circuits from the Exploratorium.

FREEUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

The Exploratorium Tinkering Studio publishes activity guides for circuit boards, paper circuits, and sewn circuits. Students build simple circuits with batteries, LEDs, copper tape, conductive thread, and other basic parts. The studio describes itself as primarily a research and development lab that shares its projects online. The specific guide pages were not read directly for this entry.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build circuits with their hands.
  • Students explore in an open-ended way.
  • Students mix craft and electronics.

For teachers

  • A charter school used a paper circuits guide with kindergarten to grade 3 and with middle school.
  • Other schools have posted classroom adaptations.
  • The guides need no devices.

For schools

  • The guides are free.
  • Supplies are low-cost and were not priced here.
  • No IT help is needed. The teacher reads the guides online.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

These guides have no AI. Students build circuits and see lights turn on. They build skills in cause and effect and sensing.

Privacy and safety

The activities are hands-on and the guides describe no student data collection. No account is needed. The teacher reads the guides online and uses the internet to view them. The research did not read the specific guide pages. A teacher should keep the coin cell batteries stored safely and watch young children closely.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Light an LED with a battery and copper tape.
  2. Predict which way the LED must face to light.
  3. Test which materials let the current flow.
  4. Draw the path the electricity takes.
  5. Explain why the light turns off when the circuit breaks.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build a paper circuit with one LED.
  2. Add a second LED and test the circuit.
  3. Fix a circuit that does not light.
  4. Add a switch made from paper and tape.
  5. Compare two designs and say which is easier to build.
How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Draw a picture and place an LED in it.
  2. Hide the copper tape in the artwork.
  3. Sew a small circuit with conductive thread.
  4. Choose LED colors to match the mood of the picture.
  5. Show the lit artwork to the class and explain it.

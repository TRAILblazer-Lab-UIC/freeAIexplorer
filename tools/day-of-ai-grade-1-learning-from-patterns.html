Day of AI: Grade 1 Learning from Patterns

An unplugged Grade 1 unit on how machines find patterns and make predictions.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This is an unplugged unit for ages 6 to 7. It teaches that technology is programmed to find patterns in examples and uses them to make predictions. It has two lessons, How Do Machines Learn Patterns and How Do Machines Make Predictions. They take about two class periods of 40 to 45 minutes. Materials are a slide deck, Educator Guide, resource folder, and a downloadable resource pack.

Benefits

For students

  • Students find patterns in simple examples.
  • Students make predictions from those patterns.
  • Students learn that machines are taught by people.

For teachers

  • A slide deck and Educator Guide guide each lesson.
  • The unit is two short lessons.
  • Device use is not listed.

For schools

  • The unit is free with a login.
  • A Day of AI login or registration is needed to view all resources.
  • The unit page states no license or standards.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The unit has no software and no AI runs on any device. It teaches that machines find patterns in examples and then predict what comes next. Students build skills in noticing patterns and making simple predictions.

Privacy and safety

A teacher account is needed to view all resources. The lessons are unplugged, and no student accounts are mentioned. The research did not read the Day of AI privacy policy. The data it collects from teachers is unknown. The research found no student data collected in the unit. A teacher should register with a work email and check the privacy terms on the site.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Look at a row of picture cards and say what comes next.
  2. Tell a partner the pattern you found in a set of examples.
  3. Act out a machine that learns a pattern from a few examples.
  4. Make a prediction and check whether it was right.
  5. Draw a machine that helps people by finding a pattern.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Continue a color pattern with blocks.
  2. Describe a number pattern in words.
  3. Make your own pattern and ask a partner to extend it.
  4. Count how many items repeat before the pattern starts again.
  5. Predict the tenth item in a simple pattern and check it.

Out Loud

A desktop app that reads text, web pages, PDFs, and ebooks aloud offline.

FREEAIStays on the device

Overview

Out Loud is a desktop text-to-speech app. It reads text, web pages, PDFs, and ebooks aloud with neural voices that run on the device. The site lists 50 or more voices in about eight languages. Students listen while they follow the text.

Benefits

For students

  • Students hear PDFs, ebooks, and web pages read aloud.
  • Students who find reading hard can follow along by ear.
  • Students can pick from many voices and several languages.

For teachers

  • The app is simple to use.
  • Teachers can load class readings and hand students the listening version.
  • A Chrome extension is also offered.

For schools

  • The app is free. The site says free for personal, non-commercial use, and school use terms are not stated.
  • Voices run on the device after a one-time download of 80 to 320 MB. The offline claim is unverified.
  • IT will likely install it. Chromebooks may use the Chrome extension, which was not tested.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a neural text-to-speech model that turns written words into spoken audio. It runs on the device. Students choose a voice, play a text, and use the audio to understand the reading and then talk or write about it.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup to download a voice model. The site says nothing typed or read leaves the computer, and the SourceForge page describes the app as cloud free, account free, and telemetry free. The research did not verify those claims. A teacher should ask IT to check network activity and check the license terms for school use.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Listen to a short class reading while following the words on the page.
  2. Try three voices and pick the one that is easiest to understand.
  3. Listen to a story and retell it aloud in your own words.
  4. Pause the audio after each paragraph and say the main idea.
  5. Open a web page about a class topic and listen to it as an alternative to reading.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Listen to a chapter of an ebook and write a short response.
  2. Read a poem silently, then listen to it and describe what you noticed.
  3. Listen to your own essay and mark the sentences that sound awkward.
  4. Compare two voices reading the same passage and discuss how tone changes meaning.
  5. Listen to a news article and list the key facts you heard.

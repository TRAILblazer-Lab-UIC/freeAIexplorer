TCI AI Literacy lesson and resource collection

A TCI page of AI literacy lessons, a course, and videos for checking AI content.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

This TCI resource page gathers an AI, Digital Literacy and Research Skills course and a lesson called AI Literacy. The lesson is about checking AI generated content and sources. The page also has video sessions from a TCI summit, a careers handout, and blog posts. It suits social studies, science, and English teachers in grades 6 to 12.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how AI works and how to judge its output.
  • Students practice checking AI content against sources.
  • Students see how AI and digital citizenship connect.

For teachers

  • The page collects a course, a lesson, and videos in one place.
  • The lesson fits social studies and science classes.
  • TCI states that its programs work without AI features.

For schools

  • The page lists no prices and links to shop and license pages.
  • The vendor does not say which resources are free.
  • TCI states that AI features are opt in at the district or teacher level and labeled.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The materials teach students to evaluate AI generated content and sources. TCI AI features are optional and run on TCI's cloud. The course launches on TCI's own platform. Students use the lessons to judge AI output with care.

Privacy and safety

TCI AI features run on TCI's cloud and the page needs the internet. TCI says teacher and student data is not used to train its AI models. TCI also says it holds 1EdTech TrustEd Apps Data Privacy certification and an ISTE Seal. These are vendor statements. The newsletter form needs Performance and Analytics cookies. The page does not say which items need a login or a paid license, and the research did not open the course page. Check TCI's privacy terms and whether AI features are switched on for your students.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Check an AI generated summary of a historical event against a textbook.
  2. Find the source behind a claim in an AI answer.
  3. Discuss how AI content can spread false history.
  4. Rate three sources for trust and explain your ranking.
  5. Write a short guide for classmates on checking AI content.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Check an AI answer about a science topic against a trusted source.
  2. List signs that a science claim may be made up.
  3. Compare two AI answers to the same science question.
  4. Trace one fact to its original study or data.
  5. Explain how an AI tool could be useful in a lab report and where it could mislead.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Compare an AI paragraph with a human paragraph and list the differences.
  2. Evaluate the sources in an AI generated essay.
  3. Practice asking an AI tool for sources and verify each one.
  4. Write a reflection on trusting AI text.
  5. Make a checklist for reading AI generated content.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Trace an AI claim back to its source.
  2. Make a poster on steps for checking AI content.
  3. Compare search results with AI answers on one topic.
  4. Discuss why labels on AI content matter.
  5. Share a verification tip with another class.

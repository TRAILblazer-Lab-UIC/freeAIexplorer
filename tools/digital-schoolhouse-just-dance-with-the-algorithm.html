Digital Schoolhouse Just Dance with the Algorithm

A two part workshop that teaches algorithms through dance and then Scratch.

FREEComputingUnpluggedUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Just Dance with the Algorithm is a two part workshop for ages 7 to 14. Part 1 needs no technology and teaches algorithms, flowcharts, repetition, and selection through dance. Part 2 moves the dance routines into Scratch. Digital Schoolhouse developed it with Ubisoft. The official page could not be fetched. Details come from search results.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn algorithms by moving their bodies.
  • Students use flowcharts, repetition, and selection in a dance.
  • Students turn their routines into Scratch projects in Part 2.

For teachers

  • The page hosts recordings of live follow-along sessions.
  • The workshop is based on an earlier workshop called Get with the Algo-rhythm.
  • Part 1 needs space to dance and materials for flowcharts.

For schools

  • The sweep notes describe the workshop as free, but this was not confirmed on the page.
  • Part 1 needs no devices.
  • Part 2 needs devices that run Scratch.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This workshop has no AI. It builds algorithm skills such as sequencing, repetition, and selection. Students plan a dance as a set of steps and then code it.

Privacy and safety

Part 1 runs on paper and in a dance space. Part 2 uses Scratch in a browser, and its data practices were not reviewed. The materials do not say which accounts students need or whether the internet is needed. The official page could not be fetched. A teacher should check the Scratch account choices and make sure the dance space is safe.

Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Write a dance routine as a flowchart with clear steps.
  2. Add a repeat to a routine to make it shorter.
  3. Use a selection step, such as a move that depends on a signal.
  4. Build the routine in Scratch and compare it with the dance.
  5. Debug a classmate's routine and fix one step.
How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Perform a dance routine that a partner wrote as an algorithm.
  2. Create a warm-up with a repeated move.
  3. Switch routines with another group and follow their steps.
  4. Count the repeats in a routine and adjust the tempo.
  5. Reflect on how a clear plan helps a group move together.

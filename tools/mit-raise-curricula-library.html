MIT RAISE Curricula Library

A free library of research-based K-12 AI curricula from MIT RAISE.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

The MIT RAISE Curricula Library lists free, research-based AI curricula for K-12 students. The first page includes AI and Ethics, which covers how AI systems work and examines algorithmic bias. Other units include How to Train Your Robot. The listing has 8 pages, and the research read only the first.

Benefits

For students

  • Students find AI units written for many grade levels from K to 12.
  • Students explore algorithms, datasets, and prediction through hands-on work.
  • Students examine algorithmic bias in the AI and Ethics unit.

For teachers

  • One library gives teachers many curricula to compare.
  • The AI and Ethics unit comes in English, German, Korean, and Portuguese.
  • Each unit has its own setup steps. Teachers can pick the one that fits.

For schools

  • The curricula are described as free.
  • Grades, tools, and licenses vary by unit. Schools should check each one.
  • Setup depends on the unit, and the library does not give a single IT plan.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The library teaches about AI, including AI literacy, algorithmic bias, machine learning, and robotics. The vendor does not say where AI runs. It depends on the unit. Students use the units to build and question AI systems.

Privacy and safety

The library does not state what student data any unit collects. The materials do not say which tools or accounts students need. Several units use browser tools, and the research did not review them. A teacher should read each unit's setup page and check any tool it uses. Privacy terms can differ from one unit to the next.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Work through the AI and Ethics unit and list how an AI system learns from data.
  2. Train a simple model from a small dataset and test its predictions.
  3. Compare how an algorithm treats two groups and describe any bias.
  4. Follow the How to Train Your Robot unit and describe how a robot learns a task.
  5. Present one unit to the class and say what surprised you.

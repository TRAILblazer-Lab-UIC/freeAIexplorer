Code.org Hour of AI unplugged and Our AI Code of Ethics

A lesson where student groups research AI ethics and write a class code of ethics.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Our AI Code of Ethics is Lesson 5 in the Hour of Code unplugged unit for grades 6 to 8 and 9 to 12. Students discuss what AI and ethics mean. In groups of three or four they choose an AI ethics research area and study it with articles and videos. They answer reflection questions and draft at least one guiding principle. The class combines the principles into one resource.

Benefits

For students

  • Students research a real AI ethics topic.
  • Students write guiding principles in their own words.
  • Students build a class code together.

For teachers

  • The lesson has handouts for research areas and reflection.
  • A teacher slide template helps build the class code.
  • An optional How AI Works video series adds background.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • It runs in about 55 minutes.
  • Groups need an internet connection to research.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no software and no AI runs on any device. Students discuss AI in everyday technology and the ethics around it. They build skills in research, reasoning, and writing principles. The same lesson appears as Lesson 14 or 15 in the Code.org AI and Machine Learning units.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state whether an account is needed or how student data is handled. Students research with articles and videos online. The internet is needed. Groups share handouts online, and the class publishes its code in a format the teacher picks. No student data collection is described. A teacher should preview articles and videos and decide which sites students may visit.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Choose an AI ethics area such as fairness or privacy and research it with a group.
  2. Explain who is affected by one AI issue.
  3. Write a guiding principle that could protect people.
  4. Debate which principle should come first for a school.
  5. Combine the principles of every group into one class code.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Read an article on AI ethics and summarize its main point.
  2. Answer the reflection questions in complete sentences.
  3. Write a guiding principle in clear plain words.
  4. Edit a classmate's principle for clarity.
  5. Present the class code to another class.

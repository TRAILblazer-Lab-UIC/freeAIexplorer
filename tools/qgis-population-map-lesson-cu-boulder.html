QGIS population map lesson (CU Boulder)

A free 3-hour high school lesson for making a first population map in QGIS.

Computing

Overview

Your First Map (QGIS) is a high school geography lesson by Alek Berg, posted October 1, 2026. Students create a basic population map in QGIS over about 3 hours. The lesson is a PDF linked from the page through Google Drive. A search snippet says a companion activity covers thematic map symbology.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make a real population map in one lesson.
  • Students learn the basic steps of a mapping program.
  • Students leave with a map they built themselves.

For teachers

  • The lesson is a free PDF handout.
  • The lesson fits in about 3 hours.
  • It is tagged for high school and lesson plans.

For schools

  • The lesson and QGIS are free.
  • Students work on local computers.
  • IT needs to install QGIS on each computer.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is working with data. Students link numbers to places and see patterns on a map. That practice supports later work with larger data sets.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used to download QGIS and the lesson. Student maps stay on the computer. The lesson PDF is hosted on Google Drive, and the research did not read it. A teacher should check that students can open the Google Drive link on school devices.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Build a basic population map in QGIS by following the lesson steps.
  2. Compare a region with high population and one with low population and explain why.
  3. Change the map colors and describe how it changes the message.
  4. Add a title and legend so another student can read the map.
  5. Write three questions that the map raises about where people live.

NC State color chemistry and AI virtual pH meter curriculum

A weeklong high school unit that trains a neural network to read pH test strips.

FREEAIStays on the deviceGood for AI beginners

Overview

This weeklong high school unit was published in the Journal of Chemical Education by Jiang, McClure, Chen, Liu, and Zhang. Students photograph pH test strips dipped in everyday liquids and predict the pH by eye. They crop the photos and train a neural network in the free codeless Orange software. They then compare its predictions to pH meter readings.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build a virtual pH meter from their own photos.
  • Students compare human estimates with model predictions and with real meter readings.
  • Students learn to judge a model with data.

For teachers

  • Orange has a point-and-click interface and needs no code.
  • Surveys in the report show higher motivation and knowledge.
  • NC State reports the student model was about 5.5 times more precise than visual reading.

For schools

  • Orange is free and runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux.
  • The unit needs phones or cameras, pH test strips, and everyday liquids.
  • The paper may need a journal subscription, but an NSF public access copy exists.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a neural network that students train in Orange. It runs on the local computer. Students feed it cropped photos of strips with known pH values, test it on new photos, and compare its guesses with a meter and with their own eyes.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. Orange is described as a free desktop program, but the research did not verify how it runs locally. Photos are taken on phones, and where they are stored is up to the school. The research did not open the journal paper and did not review the unit materials. A teacher should tell students to keep faces out of the photos and check where the phones save them.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Dip pH strips in everyday liquids and predict the pH by eye.
  2. Measure each liquid with a pH meter and compare it with the predictions.
  3. Photograph strips under the same light and note how color changes.
  4. Compare human errors with the model's errors in a table.
  5. Discuss how lighting and camera choice might affect results.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Crop strip photos and label each with its pH value.
  2. Train a neural network in Orange on the labeled photos.
  3. Test the model on new strips and record its errors.
  4. Change the training set and describe how the results shift.
  5. Explain in a short report why more varied photos help.

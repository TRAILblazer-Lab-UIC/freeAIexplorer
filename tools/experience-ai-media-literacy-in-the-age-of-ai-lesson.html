Experience AI Media literacy in the age of AI lesson

An unplugged lesson on checking AI generated media before sharing it.

FREEAIUnpluggedNo device neededGood for AI beginners

Overview

Media literacy in the age of AI is a lesson in the Experience AI AI safety unit. It is listed for ages 11 to 14. Students describe the media types that generative AI can produce. They explain why information needs checking before sharing. They also build expectations for fairness, accountability, and transparency on social platforms.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn words such as misinformation, disinformation, and deep fakes.
  • Students practice judging trusted sources.
  • Students write an action plan for handling AI generated media.

For teachers

  • The lesson has two unplugged activities, Trusted Sources and Action Plan.
  • A lesson plan, slides, and activity sheets come with a free account.
  • The lesson fits into a larger AI safety unit.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • The activities run on paper. Devices are not needed.
  • The materials describe no student data collection.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no running AI program. It teaches about generative AI media and the problems it can cause. Students learn to spot misinformation, deep fakes, and bias. They build skills in checking sources before they share.

Privacy and safety

The lesson runs on paper and students use no device for the unplugged activities. The materials describe no student data collection. Teachers need a free Raspberry Pi Foundation account to download the plan, slides, and sheets. The research did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should read the Foundation privacy terms before signing up. A teacher should also check that the deep fake examples suit the age of the class.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Rank a set of sources from most trusted to least trusted and explain the order.
  2. Check one claim by finding two other sources that agree with it.
  3. Sort sample media into real, edited, and AI generated and tell why.
  4. Make a checklist that a student can use before sharing a post.
  5. Design a poster that shows how to stop misinformation from spreading.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Write an action plan that a social platform could follow to protect its users.
  2. Discuss who is responsible when a deep fake spreads.
  3. List expectations for fairness, accountability, and transparency for a platform.
  4. Compare how misinformation and disinformation differ and give one example of each.
  5. Role-play a community meeting about AI content on social media.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Define each key word in your own sentence.
  2. Read a short post and mark phrases that might signal misinformation.
  3. Write a short note to a friend that explains why to check before sharing.
  4. Compare the same news story told by two sources.
  5. Write a reflection on a time you changed your mind after checking a fact.

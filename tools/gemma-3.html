Gemma 3

Google open-weight language models that run on a local computer.

AI

Overview

Gemma is a family of open-weight language models from Google DeepMind. Gemma 3 came out in March 2025. A teacher or advanced student downloads the weights and runs them through a local tool such as Ollama or LM Studio. Students chat with the model on their own machine.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can chat with a language model that runs on their own computer.
  • Advanced students learn how local AI models are loaded and used.
  • Smaller models run on laptops, which lets students test different sizes.

For teachers

  • The models are free to download.
  • Teachers choose the runtime, such as Ollama or LM Studio.
  • Teachers can show how a model works without signing in to a chat service. Downloading the weights may need a Hugging Face or Kaggle account.

For schools

  • The weights are free, but larger sizes need a strong GPU or plenty of RAM.
  • With a local runtime, chats are expected to stay on the device. The runtime's data handling was not reviewed.
  • IT may need to approve the runtime install.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a family of text generation models. They run on the local computer through a runtime. Students type a question, read the answer, and check it against other sources. The weights use the Gemma Terms of Use with a Prohibited Use Policy. This is a custom agreement and not an OSI open source license.

Privacy and safety

The weights are files and collect nothing themselves. Data handling depends on the runtime, such as Ollama or LM Studio, and the research did not review those. Downloading the weights needs the internet and sometimes a Hugging Face or Kaggle account to accept the terms, which the research did not verify. Google also offers hosted Gemma through its APIs, which would send data to Google. The model has no classroom controls. A teacher should check the Gemma terms, review the runtime's data handling, and review how students use the chat.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Install a runtime and load a small Gemma model, then write down each step.
  2. Ask the same question to two model sizes and compare the answers.
  3. Time how long a reply takes on your computer and explain what affects speed.
  4. Read the Gemma Terms of Use and list two use restrictions.
  5. Test the model with a tricky question and describe where it fails.

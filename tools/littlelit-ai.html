LittleLit AI

A paid K-12 platform with AI tutors, lessons, and report cards for homeschools.

$ LicenseAISends student data to a company

Overview

LittleLit is a commercial AI curriculum platform aimed at homeschools, microschools, and special education. It offers 16 subjects, adaptive AI tutors, AI literacy and financial literacy content, and generated report cards and gap lessons. A parent or school creates accounts for students. Students chat with a tutor and work through lessons.

Benefits

For students

  • Students get an adaptive AI tutor for each subject.
  • Content covers 16 subjects including AI literacy.
  • A separate AI for Kids portal offers a younger entry point.

For teachers

  • The platform generates a school year calendar and report cards.
  • Gap lessons target skills a student still needs.
  • Teachers can serve many grades from one place.

For schools

  • It is a paid subscription. A family plan of $239.99 per child per year was quoted in a promotion, and standard prices were not read.
  • It runs in a browser.
  • Student chat goes to cloud AI providers, which matters for a school with strict data rules.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a set of tutors and lesson generators that run on the vendor's cloud. Student chat goes to cloud AI providers. Students ask the tutor questions and work through generated lessons, and they use the feedback to improve their own work.

Privacy and safety

A parent or school creates the main account and gives consent, and the internet is always needed. The platform stores an alias, grade level, lesson progress, answers, and AI conversation history. The general policy section also lists names and identifiers, and the student data sections are inconsistent. The policy says AI providers are contractually prohibited from training on student data. It names sub processors for hosting, analytics, payment, AI, and support, and Google Analytics cookies. It states COPPA and GDPR compliance and does not mention FERPA. A teacher should read the policy and confirm consent steps before any student signs in.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Chat with a tutor about a book and write a short summary in your own words.
  2. Complete a generated gap lesson on grammar and review the answers.
  3. Ask the tutor for feedback on a paragraph and revise it.
  4. Compare a tutor explanation with a class lesson on the same skill.
  5. Keep a reading log that records one new idea from each tutor session.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Work through an adaptive set of problems and note which skills improve.
  2. Ask the tutor to explain a missed problem a second way.
  3. Create a list of the steps used to solve a multi-step problem.
  4. Check a tutor's answer by solving the same problem on paper.
  5. Track progress in a weekly chart of correct answers.
How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Use short lessons paced to one student's needs and record what helps.
  2. Ask the tutor to restate a lesson in simpler words.
  3. Practice a skill from a gap lesson with a teacher nearby.
  4. Review progress reports with a teacher and set one goal.
  5. Pick a favorite subject and set a small weekly target.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Complete the AI literacy lessons and write what AI can and cannot do.
  2. Ask the tutor a question and judge whether the answer is correct.
  3. Compare two different prompts and describe how the replies differ.
  4. List the information a tutor tool collects and discuss why that matters.
  5. Write a short guide for younger students on safe use of an AI tutor.

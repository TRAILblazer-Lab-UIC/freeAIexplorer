Large Language MadLibs

A two-day unplugged lesson that simulates next-word prediction with dice.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

Large Language MadLibs is a two-day unplugged lesson by Kristin Fasiang and Duri Long of Northwestern University. It covers probability and the ethics of large language models. Students simulate how a language model makes text by predicting words. The lesson is listed for grades 7 to 12 and runs over two 50-minute sessions.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see how context and word frequency shape a model's output.
  • Students use dice and coins to model probability.
  • Students discuss how bias can enter a language model.

For teachers

  • The pack has slide decks and student and teacher worksheets for both days.
  • A discussion guide and a learning check with a rubric are included.
  • The lesson needs no AI background.

For schools

  • The materials are free.
  • Groups need only dice and coins, or a dice-rolling website.
  • The lesson collects no student data.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no running AI program. Students act as a language model that picks the next word by chance. They see how the words around a choice and how often each word appears change the result. They build skills in probability and in spotting bias.

Privacy and safety

The lesson is worksheets and dice, and the materials describe no student data collection. A dice rolling website is an optional substitute for physical dice. The research did not confirm whether the download needs an account. A teacher should read the worksheets before class. A teacher should also plan how to guide the talk on bias.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Roll the dice to pick the next word and build a sentence one word at a time.
  2. Change the word choices on the sheet and see how the sentence changes.
  3. Explain to a partner why a model prefers common words.
  4. Find an example where the choices lead to a biased sentence.
  5. Complete the learning check and describe how a language model predicts text.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Work out the chance of each word with the dice and coins.
  2. Compare the chance of two word paths and tell which is more likely.
  3. Roll 20 times and compare the results with the chances you predicted.
  4. Make a table of word frequencies and find the most common word.
  5. Explain how adding more words to the table changes the chances.

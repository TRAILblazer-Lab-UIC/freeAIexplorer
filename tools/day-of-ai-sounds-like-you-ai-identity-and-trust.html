Day of AI: Sounds Like You, AI Identity and Trust

A one lesson Day of AI unit on voice cloning and verifying identity.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Sounds Like You is a single lesson of about 55 to 60 minutes for ages 12 to 18. The lesson is titled Who Is Really There. Students weigh identity evidence such as voice, face, and communication style. They consider how AI could copy each one. They then design and test a verification process built around a realistic voice message scenario.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how AI can copy a voice or a face.
  • Students practice judging what counts as evidence of who someone is.
  • Students design a way to check a message before trusting it.

For teachers

  • The lesson fits in one class period.
  • A Google Slides deck, an educator guide, and student resources are provided.
  • Teachers need no technical background.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • Students need a browser.
  • The materials do not say which accounts students need.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson teaches about voice cloning, deepfakes, and identity checks. No AI tool is run in the lesson. Students look at how AI could copy a voice or face and then design a way to check who is really sending a message. Only Google Slides is named as a tool.

Privacy and safety

A Day of AI login or registration is needed to view all resources, and the login is for the teacher. The materials do not state what student data is collected or where it goes. The page does not say whether students record their voice or use a voice cloning tool. The research did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should read the Day of AI privacy notice and keep real student voices out of any demonstration.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. List clues in a voice message that could show it is fake.
  2. Rank voice, face, and writing style as evidence of identity and defend the order.
  3. Design a three step check that a family could use to verify an urgent message.
  4. Test the check on a scripted scenario and revise it.
  5. Create a short guide that teaches younger students how to stay safe from voice scams.

Waveforms and Spectrograms activity

An AI4K12 activity where students capture sound and view waveforms and spectrograms.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This AI4K12 activity guide has students look at the audio signal behind speech recognition. They use a laptop microphone to capture musical tones and speech. An oscilloscope demo shows waveforms and a spectrogram demo shows frequency over time. The activity covers grades 3 to 12 and ties to AI4K12 Big Idea 1 on perception.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see what their own voice looks like as a signal.
  • Students compare musical tones and speech on a spectrogram.
  • Students learn that a computer must turn sound into data first.

For teachers

  • Two browser demos and a PDF guide are all that is needed.
  • The activity fits computer science, science, and music.
  • It spans grades 3 to 12. One plan can be adjusted for many ages.

For schools

  • The activity is free.
  • It runs in a browser with a microphone.
  • IT involvement is none.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The activity shows the signal that speech recognition systems start with. The demos draw waveforms and spectrograms in the browser. They do not run a recognition model. Students build the skill of understanding how a computer perceives sound.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed. The demos run in the browser and use the laptop microphone. Students share microphone audio with the page. The internet is needed to open the demos. The research did not verify where audio is processed on the demo sites. A teacher should test the demos first and allow microphone access only for these pages. The materials do not say whether any audio is stored.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Say a vowel into the microphone and describe the waveform.
  2. Compare the spectrogram of two different words.
  3. Explain why a computer needs a signal before it can recognize speech.
  4. Record a quiet room and a noisy room and compare the displays.
  5. Draw what you think a speech recognizer looks for in a spectrogram.
How this can be used for Music
  1. Capture a note on an instrument and read its waveform.
  2. Play a high note and a low note and compare the frequencies.
  3. Sing a pitch and watch it on the spectrogram.
  4. Compare two instruments playing the same note.
  5. Describe how the spectrogram shows a change in volume.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Relate the waveform to a sound wave and label the peaks.
  2. Predict how the display changes with a higher pitch and test it.
  3. Measure how loud sounds change the waveform height.
  4. Compare a clap and a hum on both views.
  5. Write a short explanation of what frequency means.

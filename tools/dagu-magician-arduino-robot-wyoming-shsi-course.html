Dagu Magician Arduino robot (Wyoming SHSI course)

A 27-hour summer course where students build and program Arduino robots.

ComputingRobotics

Overview

An ASEE paper describes a 27 contact hour course in the University of Wyoming Summer High School Institute for rising sophomores. Each student built a low cost Dagu Magician robot with infrared sensors and an Arduino UNO R3 board. They programmed the robot and the course ended with a maze competition.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build and program their own robot.
  • Students use infrared sensors to navigate a maze.
  • Students take part in a competition at the end.

For teachers

  • The paper gives a model for a three-week course of 12 sessions of 2.25 hours.
  • Pre and post surveys showed a substantial increase in student understanding.
  • The course uses common parts and the Arduino IDE.

For schools

  • The Dagu Magician chassis is about 20 USD per the search summary. The Arduino and sensors add cost.
  • No account or internet is needed.
  • IT should help install the Arduino IDE on student computers.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The robot has no AI. It runs the program that students write in the Arduino IDE. Students build skills in sensors, logic and sequencing.

Privacy and safety

The robots run locally on an Arduino, and the paper does not discuss data or accounts. The research found no student data collected, and no privacy policy applies to the hardware. The Arduino IDE runs on a computer and needs the internet to download. A teacher should plan for soldering and wiring safety and check that the parts match the paper.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Assemble the Dagu Magician chassis and test its wheels.
  2. Mount the infrared sensors and test their readings.
  3. Design a maze and test how the robot moves through it.
  4. Compare two sensor positions and pick the better one.
  5. Present a finished robot at a class maze competition.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Write an Arduino program that drives the robot forward.
  2. Read an infrared sensor and print its value.
  3. Program the robot to turn at a wall.
  4. Change a threshold and note the effect on the maze run.
  5. Debug a program that crashes the robot into the wall.

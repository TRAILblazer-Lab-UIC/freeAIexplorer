Datasets and Data Brokers

A paper activity showing how data brokers profile people to market products to them.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This unplugged activity shows how third-party data brokers use AI algorithms to collect, combine, and analyze personal data. Students receive fake profiles with interests and search history. They then choose a product to market to each profile. Materials are a student worksheet, product cards, and four datasets, with a facilitator guide, slides, and an answer key. It sits under Data Privacy and Ethics on the Northwestern AI Unplugged site.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see how data builds a profile of a person.
  • Students compare four datasets and see what each reveals.
  • Students think about privacy in daily life.

For teachers

  • A facilitator guide, slides, and answer key are included.
  • The activity was piloted with middle school students.
  • Materials are simple prints.

For schools

  • The activity is free.
  • It uses made-up profiles. No real student data is needed.
  • No devices or accounts are needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The activity has no software and no AI runs on any device. It teaches how data brokers use algorithms to profile people. Students build skills in reading data and thinking about privacy. They act as the marketer and see how more data makes a sharper guess.

Privacy and safety

The activity uses made-up profiles, and the research found no student data collected. No account is needed, and the site states that activities need no computer. The internet is needed to download the files. A teacher should remind students not to use real names or details in class. A teacher should also be ready for questions about students' own online data.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Read a fake profile and choose a product to market to that person.
  2. Discuss how it feels when a company knows your interests.
  3. Compare Dataset 1 with Dataset 4 and explain what extra data reveals.
  4. Debate whether companies should be allowed to sell personal data.
  5. Write a short rule for protecting privacy online.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Sort the profiles into groups using the interests in each dataset.
  2. Explain how combining datasets makes a guess more exact.
  3. Find a mistake in a profile guess and say why it happened.
  4. List what data an app might collect from a person.
  5. Design a simple rule that limits what data an app can collect.

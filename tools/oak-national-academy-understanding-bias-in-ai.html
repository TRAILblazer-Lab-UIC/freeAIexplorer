Oak National Academy Understanding bias in AI

A Year 8 lesson on how flawed training data leads to biased AI output.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This Year 8 digital literacy lesson shows how flawed or unrepresentative training data leads to biased AI output. It has slides, a lesson video with transcript, a worksheet, a starter quiz, and an exit quiz. Tasks A and C can be run as a card sort. It is lesson 4 of 6 in the unit Using AI and digital tools responsibly.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn that AI is not neutral just because it is a machine.
  • Students practice judging AI output with a worksheet.
  • Students can use an online pupil version for homework or revision.

For teachers

  • The package includes slides with checks for understanding.
  • Starter and exit quizzes have 6 questions each.
  • The video has a transcript and the card sort needs no devices.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • The code is listed as open source.
  • IT involvement is low, and the lesson uses UK terms for Key Stage 3.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson teaches about AI and does not run an AI tool. It focuses on how training data shapes the output of a model. Students learn to question AI results and then practice being a critical user of AI tools.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state whether accounts are needed or what data is collected. The online pupil version runs on the vendor's cloud. A teacher should check how students open the pupil version and review the Oak terms.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Sort cards of training examples into fair and unfair sets.
  2. Explain how a model trained on one group may fail for another.
  3. Predict how biased data would change an AI answer.
  4. Complete the worksheet and share one answer with the class.
  5. Answer the exit quiz and describe what you would check next time.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Judge an AI answer and list questions to ask about its source.
  2. Compare two answers and say which one seems more complete.
  3. Write a short guide to being a critical user of AI tools.
  4. Discuss the idea that AI is neutral because it is a machine.
  5. Find a news story about biased AI and summarize it.

Skill Struck AI Literacy

Four K-12 AI literacy courses with lessons, quizzes, and a teacher dashboard.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

Skill Struck offers AI literacy courses for four grade bands, K-2, 3-5, 6-8, and 9-12. The page says they are at no cost to districts for 2026-2027. Each band has lesson plans, textbook content, quizzes, slides, and handouts, with plugged and unplugged activities. The platform has a built-in code editor and a teacher dashboard with student rostering. The courses align to AI4K12 guidelines.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn AI at a level that fits their age.
  • Students try plugged and unplugged activities.
  • Older students use a built-in code editor.

For teachers

  • Each band has plans, slides, quizzes, and handouts.
  • The dashboard handles student rostering.
  • Courses have 22, 28, 48, and 48 lessons across the four bands.

For schools

  • The courses are free for districts for 2026-2027 per the vendor. The free terms may change.
  • Upgrades are sold after a sales follow-up.
  • A teacher can set up the account. Students log in on the vendor's platform.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The courses teach AI literacy concepts and ethics. The platform runs in the vendor's cloud. Students work through lessons and activities and use the code editor to try ideas of their own.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are required, and the internet is needed at all times. The vendor collects student progress and login credentials and stores them on its platform. The page links a privacy policy and a COPPA policy that the research did not read. Search snippets mention rostered AI chat and teacher built tutors on the platform. A teacher should read the privacy terms and ask how student data is protected. A teacher should also check district rules for student rostering.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Sort everyday tools into those that use AI and those that do not.
  2. Train a simple model in a plugged activity and describe the results.
  3. Use the code editor to change one line and observe the effect.
  4. Draw how a machine learns from examples.
  5. Explain one of the Five Big Ideas in AI to a classmate.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Discuss how AI affects jobs in your community.
  2. Debate whether an AI tool should make decisions about people.
  3. Research an example of AI bias and report on who was affected.
  4. Write community rules for the fair use of AI.
  5. Interview an adult about how AI changed their work.

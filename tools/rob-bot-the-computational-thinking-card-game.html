Rob-Bot The Computational Thinking Card Game

A printable matching card game linked to computational thinking skills.

ComputingUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

Rob-Bot is a printable card game sold on Tes by Robbot Resources. The deck has 57 cards with 8 characters or objects each. Every card shares one item with every other card. Players find the matching item between the card in play and their own card to get rid of their cards. The listing links the game to decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction and algorithm design.

Benefits

For students

  • Students practice quick visual matching and pattern spotting.
  • Students play with two to four players in a friendly race.
  • Students think about which details to look for and which to ignore.

For teachers

  • The PDF includes printing instructions and rules.
  • The listing names the computational thinking skills the game supports.
  • The Tes listing had no reviews when checked. A teacher should try the game first.

For schools

  • The game was listed at 4.95 GBP on Tes Australia. Other regions showed other prices.
  • The cards print on school equipment.
  • The game uses no devices at play time.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This game has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is pattern recognition. Students scan two cards and find the one shared item.

Privacy and safety

The game runs on paper. The internet is used only to buy and download the PDF. The materials do not state whether a Tes account is needed. A teacher should check that before buying. No student data is collected during play.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Play a round and say out loud how you searched for the matching item.
  2. Describe the steps you follow to find a match as an algorithm.
  3. Ignore the background details on a card and name the one item that matters.
  4. Teach the game to a younger student using your own written steps.
  5. Spot a pattern across several cards and describe it.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Compare two cards and find the one item they share.
  2. Count the items on a card and confirm that every card has 8.
  3. Keep score for a round and find who has the fewest cards left.
  4. Make a tally of how often each item appears in your hand.
  5. Explain why every pair of cards has exactly one match.

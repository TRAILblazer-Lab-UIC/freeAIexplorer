Twine

A free tool for writing interactive stories that publish to HTML.

FREEComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Twine is an open source tool for making interactive, nonlinear stories. Simple stories need no code. Authors can add variables, logic, images, CSS, and JavaScript. Version 2 runs in a browser or as a desktop app. Students write branching stories and publish them as web pages.

Benefits

For students

  • Students plan a story as a map of linked passages.
  • Students can start with plain writing and add code later.
  • Students publish a story that classmates can play.

For teachers

  • A reference, cookbook, and format guides are available.
  • The tool suits English, social studies, and other writing tasks.
  • Several research papers cover Twine in English classrooms.

For schools

  • The tool is free and the code is open source.
  • No account is needed. The research did not confirm where stories are stored.
  • Students may use their work any way they like, including commercially.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This tool has no AI. The story follows the links an author builds. Students practice logic as they use variables and choices to change what the reader sees.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Play a free work of interactive fiction and describe how choices shape the story.
  2. Write a short branching story with three endings.
  3. Build a game around one passage of a poem and explain your choices.
  4. Revise a classmate's story and suggest a new branch.
  5. Publish a class story collection and write a review of each one.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Rewrite a battle of the American Revolution as a second person text adventure.
  2. Build a story that shows two sides of a historical decision.
  3. Add a variable that tracks supplies in a journey story.
  4. Link primary source quotes into a branching timeline.
  5. Play a classmate's story and list the facts it teaches.

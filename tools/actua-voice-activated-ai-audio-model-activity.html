Actua Voice Activated AI audio model activity

A classroom activity where students train a keyword listening model in Teachable Machine.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This is Activity 7 in the Actua AI Activities Series. A space-station story frames the task, in which an AI named DANN hears but does not understand. Students start with a pre-trained audio classifier. Then they train their own keyword recognition model in Google Teachable Machine and judge whether it suits a defined task.

Benefits

For students

  • Students record their own sounds and see a model learn from them.
  • Students test a model and decide whether it is good enough for a job.
  • Students learn that a model can hear words without understanding them.

For teachers

  • The activity is free and takes minutes to set up.
  • The story gives a clear frame for a first lesson on audio AI.
  • The series says the activities can be done alone. Teachers can pick this one.

For schools

  • The activity costs nothing.
  • Students need only a browser and a microphone, and Chromebooks are listed.
  • No IT install is needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is an audio classifier that students first test and then train themselves. It runs in the web browser on the student's device. Students record keywords, train the model, and then test how well it works. The activity builds the skill of judging a model against a task.

Privacy and safety

This activity needs a microphone and an internet connection during use. Students record their own voices in Google Teachable Machine in the browser. The browser asks permission to use the microphone. The research did not review Teachable Machine privacy terms. Where the recordings go was not verified and no account rules were confirmed. A teacher should check the Teachable Machine terms with the school before class.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Run the pre-trained audio classifier and list the sounds it gets right and wrong.
  2. Train a keyword model with several recordings of two or three words.
  3. Test the model with a classmate's voice and record how accuracy changes.
  4. Add more training samples and compare the results with the first model.
  5. Decide whether the model is good enough for a task such as opening a door in the story, and defend the choice.

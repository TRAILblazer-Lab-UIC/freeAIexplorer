Elementari

A browser platform where students make interactive stories and games with writing, art, and blocks.

FREEAIComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

Elementari is a K-12 platform where students turn what they learn into interactive stories, games, and experiences. They use writing, art, and block code. It runs in a modern browser on Chromebooks and tablets. Teachers get ready-made lessons with a plan, reading passage, project, storyboard, rubric, and exit tickets.

Benefits

For students

  • Students combine writing, drawing, and block code in one project.
  • Students plan with a storyboard before they build.
  • Students share finished work after the teacher approves it.

For teachers

  • Lessons are tagged to Common Core, NGSS, CSTA, and ISTE.
  • Teachers approve work before it is published.
  • Students sign in with Google, Clever, or ClassLink, and work can go to Google Classroom.

For schools

  • The classroom plan is free with unlimited students and classrooms.
  • It needs only a modern browser and no install.
  • Paid upgrades exist for the full lesson bank, premium assets, and AI lesson generation. The site does not list their prices.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The core tool has no required AI. The site says a paid upgrade adds an AI Lesson Builder that generates lessons for teachers. The vendor does not say where that AI runs. Students extend their own work by writing, drawing, and coding the story, and the AI is only for teachers who pay.

Privacy and safety

Teachers sign up and students sign in with Google, Clever, or ClassLink. The site needs the internet at all times and work is stored on the vendor's servers. The vendor says it follows COPPA and FERPA and shows no ads, which is self-reported. Search results say it signed the Student Privacy Pledge, and Common Sense Education has not yet rated its privacy. The research did not read the privacy policy. What student data it collects is unknown. A teacher should read the privacy policy and the district data privacy agreement documents the site lists. The AI Lesson Builder is a paid add-on and the vendor does not say where it runs.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a short game with block code and a simple score.
  2. Add a character that moves when a key is pressed.
  3. Plan a project with a storyboard and then code each scene.
  4. Trade projects with a partner and report one bug.
  5. Add a choice that leads to two different endings.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a short story and turn each paragraph into a scene.
  2. Draw the setting and add the narration as text.
  3. Use a reading passage from a lesson to make an interactive retelling.
  4. Add dialogue that shows how a character feels.
  5. Present the story to the class and explain the writing choices.
How this can be used for World Languages
  1. Design an interactive story in Spanish or another target language.
  2. Add a vocabulary scene with labeled pictures.
  3. Write character dialogue in the target language.
  4. Swap stories and answer questions about a partner's story.
  5. Add a choice that asks the reader to pick a word in the target language.

Common Sense AI Basics for K-12 Teachers

A teacher course on generative AI basics, privacy, and ethics from Common Sense.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

This course is for K-12 teachers who want to understand the basics of generative AI. It covers AI literacy, safety and data privacy, and ethics. It includes a reflection workbook for planning AI use in teaching. The course page does not state a duration or cost. Search snippets describe it as free, self-paced, and about one hour.

Benefits

For students

  • Students gain from a teacher who understands AI privacy.
  • Students get clearer rules about safe AI use.
  • Students benefit from planned and thoughtful AI use in class.

For teachers

  • The course covers literacy, privacy, and ethics in one place.
  • A reflection workbook helps with planning.
  • Teachers can take it on their own time.

For schools

  • Search snippets and a press release headline describe it as free.
  • The course page itself states no price.
  • Nothing needs to be installed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The course teaches about generative AI and does not use an AI tool. It covers literacy, privacy, and ethics. The course is hosted in the vendor's cloud. Teachers use what they learn to plan safe and thoughtful AI use with their own students.

Privacy and safety

The course is for teachers, and it asks for no student data. The Go to the course button links to a login page, and the research did not confirm whether an account is required. The course needs an internet connection. A search snippet says Common Sense Media launched the course with OpenAI. The research read no data handling statement for the course platform. A teacher should check the Common Sense terms before logging in with a school account.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Write a short AI use guide for your library with three safety rules.
  2. Check an AI answer against two library sources.
  3. List the types of personal data that students should never share with AI.
  4. Create a poster that explains how AI can be wrong.
  5. Plan a lesson on how to cite AI use.

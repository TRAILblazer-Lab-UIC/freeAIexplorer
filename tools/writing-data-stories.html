Writing Data Stories

Bilingual middle school units where students write stories from science data.

Computing

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Writing Data Stories is an NSF funded middle school science project from 2019 to 2022. Students critique public datasets about nutrition, climate, and the environment. They then write data stories that link the data to their own lives. Materials come in English and Spanish and are shared free through Google Drive folders.

Benefits

For students

  • Students connect real data to their own lives.
  • Students write and speak about data in English or Spanish.
  • Students analyze data in CODAP, a free online tool.

For teachers

  • Units run three to four weeks and Data Story Bytes take 30 minutes or less.
  • A Getting Started document and a how to video guide setup.
  • The page notes the materials can be used in math or social studies.

For schools

  • The materials are free per the Friday Institute page.
  • Students use CODAP in a browser, and the internet is always needed.
  • The project ran from 2019 to 2022. Maintenance status is unknown.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The materials describe no AI. CODAP draws graphs from the data students choose. Students build data and pattern spotting skills as they look for trends and tell a story about them.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which accounts students need or what data is collected. CODAP runs in the browser and the internet is always needed. The materials sit in shared Google Drive folders. No privacy policy was read. A teacher should check the Google Drive sharing and the CODAP privacy terms before students start.

Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Science
  1. Analyze a public nutrition dataset and describe what it shows.
  2. Compare climate data over several years and name a trend.
  3. Choose a variable and predict how it changes another.
  4. Graph environment data from your region and explain the pattern.
  5. Write a data story that links one graph to a daily habit.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a data story that opens with a question and ends with a claim.
  2. Read a classmate's story and mark the evidence in it.
  3. Translate a data story into the second language and compare the wording.
  4. Choose strong verbs to describe a trend.
  5. Present a story to the class with a graph.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Find the mean and range of a dataset in CODAP.
  2. Make a graph and explain how the scale affects the picture.
  3. Compare two groups in a dataset and describe the difference.
  4. Look for a link between two measures with a scatter plot.
  5. Check a claim by testing it against the data.

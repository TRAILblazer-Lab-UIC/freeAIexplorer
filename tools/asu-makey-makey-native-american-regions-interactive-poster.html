ASU Makey Makey Native American regions interactive poster

A 4th grade lesson where students build a touch poster about Native American regions.

FREEComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This 4th grade lesson by Cori Long is on the ASU STEM Teachers site. Students learn about the Makey Makey and create conductive touch points. They record their voice in Scratch and connect the Makey Makey to build an interactive poster about Native American regions. The page says it can be used for any topic.

Benefits

For students

  • Students touch a poster and hear their own recorded voice.
  • Students learn about circuits through touch points.
  • Students can reuse the method for other topics.

For teachers

  • Full steps are in a linked Google Slides lesson plan.
  • The page tags ISTE style technology categories, Engineering and ELA standards.
  • The lesson can be reused for any topic.

For schools

  • The lesson plan is free.
  • A Makey Makey kit is a paid purchase and no price was checked.
  • IT may need to help with Scratch and the microphone on each computer.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no AI. Students build skills in circuits, sequencing and input. They link a touch to a recorded sound with Scratch.

Privacy and safety

No account is listed as required. Students record their own voices in Scratch. The materials do not state whether Scratch Desktop or the online editor is used. The online editor needs no account to use but needs one to save projects. The materials do not say whether the internet is needed or what data is collected. A teacher should check which Scratch version the lesson uses and where recordings are saved.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Research one Native American region and write three facts for the poster.
  2. Draw a map of the region on the poster.
  3. Record a short spoken description for each touch point.
  4. Check each fact against a trusted source with a partner.
  5. Present the finished poster to another class.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a short script for each recording.
  2. Practice reading the script aloud with a clear voice.
  3. Revise a script after a partner listens to it.
  4. Listen to a classmate's poster and ask one question.
  5. Reflect in writing on how speaking helped your learning.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Make a Scratch program that plays a sound when a key is pressed.
  2. Connect a Makey Makey touch point to a key.
  3. Record your voice and add it to a Scratch sprite.
  4. Test each touch point and fix any that fail.
  5. Explain how touching foil starts the sound.

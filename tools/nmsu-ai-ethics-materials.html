NMSU AI Ethics Materials

A page of AI ethics readings, guides, and a microlearning course for students and educators.

FREEAIUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

The NMSU Youth Workforce Innovation Center gathers AI ethics readings on one resource page. It lists three student resources and three educator resources. The page also links a self-paced microlearning course with short modules on case studies, ethical frameworks, and reflection. Most items come from other organizations such as SAP, UC Davis, Cornell, the Adapt Centre, and ORISE.

Benefits

For students

  • Students read short pieces on AI ethics and bias.
  • Students work through case studies and reflection in the microlearning course.
  • Students can try the Picture Perfect AI lesson from ORISE.

For teachers

  • The page offers educator guides from Cornell and the Adapt Centre.
  • The AI Ethics Discussion Guide supports class conversation.
  • One page collects many resources. Teachers can choose what fits.

For schools

  • The resources are free.
  • No setup is needed beyond a web browser.
  • Most links lead to outside sites, which a school may want to review.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The materials teach about AI ethics, bias, fairness, and transparency. They do not run an AI tool. Students read, discuss, and reflect, then apply the ideas to the tools they already meet.

Key terms on this page

Short definitions of words used above and below. Select a term for the full glossary entry.

AI bias
AI bias is a pattern of unfair or one-sided results from an AI system.
AI ethics
AI ethics is the study of how AI should be built and used fairly and safely.
Copyright
Copyright is the legal right of creators to control copies of their work.
Face recognition
Face recognition is AI that finds faces in images or matches a face to a person.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which accounts students need or what data they collect. Most items sit on other organizations' sites. A teacher should open each link first and check whether the microlearning course asks students to sign in.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Read a student resource on AI bias and write a short response.
  2. Hold a discussion with the Adapt Centre guide and summarize the main points.
  3. Write a letter to a company about a fairness concern with an AI tool.
  4. Compare two readings and say which argument is stronger.
  5. Reflect in writing on one case study from the microlearning course.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Debate whether AI decisions about people should be open to review.
  2. Study a case where an AI system treated groups differently.
  3. List who is helped and harmed by a facial recognition tool.
  4. Write a class rule for fair use of AI at school.
  5. Present the Picture Perfect AI lesson ideas to another class.

Cubetto teaching guide

A free Primo Toys guide for teaching coding with the Cubetto robot.

FREEComputingRoboticsStays on the device

Overview

This is a free teaching guide from Primo Toys for introducing the Cubetto wooden robot to children ages 3 to 6. It is about 15 pages long. The guide covers coding concepts in early years, the board and blocks, action causality, debugging and functions. Children program the robot with physical blocks and no screen.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn coding through play with physical blocks.
  • Students practice sequencing, debugging and functions.
  • Students work together in teams.

For teachers

  • The guide covers classroom setup and a challenge sequence.
  • It includes unplugged activity ideas.
  • It ends with pointers to free lesson plans on the Primo Toys education page.

For schools

  • The guide is free. The Cubetto playset is needed and costs about 225 to 310 USD per reviews and retailers.
  • Cost is the main criticism in reviews.
  • No account, internet or student data is involved.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This guide and the robot have no AI. Children place blocks and the robot follows them. Students build skills in sequencing, debugging and functions.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed for the robot, which runs on its own with no internet. The guide mentions no accounts or apps, and the research found no student data collected. One reviewer reports that a similar guide download asks for an email address. No privacy policy was found for the guide. A teacher should check whether the download asks for an email and plan the budget for the playset.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Try the action causality activity and name what each block does.
  2. Give unambiguous instructions to a partner who acts like the robot.
  3. Place a queue of blocks and predict the result.
  4. Find the mistake in a queue and fix it.
  5. Use the Function block to shorten a long program.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Work in a team to plan a story route and talk about each step.
  2. Describe the robot's path with new words.
  3. Retell an adventure after the robot finishes the route.
  4. Take turns giving clear spoken directions.
  5. Draw the route and label each stop in the story.

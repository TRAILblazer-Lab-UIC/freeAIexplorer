Day of AI: AI Foundations for Middle Grades

A five lesson Day of AI unit using Quick Draw and Teachable Machine.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

AI Foundations for Middle Grades is a five lesson unit for ages 11 to 13 that takes about 2 to 3 hours. Lessons cover what AI is, exploring datasets with Google Quick Draw, writing algorithms, training a model in Google Teachable Machine, and bias in AI. A slide deck is provided. A Day of AI login is needed to see all of the resources.

Benefits

For students

  • Students train and test a model in Teachable Machine.
  • Students explore a real dataset with Quick Draw.
  • Students investigate how bias enters an AI system.

For teachers

  • Five lessons come with a ready slide deck.
  • The unit needs little technical background.
  • A teacher only needs to copy the slides and prepare devices.

For schools

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The unit teaches supervised learning, datasets, algorithms, and bias. Students train an image model in Teachable Machine, which runs in the browser. They use it to see how training examples change the model's answers.

Privacy and safety

A Day of AI login is needed to view the full resources. The Day of AI privacy notice says it collects personal information when you register. It does not mention COPPA or FERPA and does not say where data is stored. Quick Draw and Teachable Machine are Google tools, and the research did not verify their data flow. Teachable Machine uses a webcam. A teacher should decide whether students use the camera or upload images, and read the privacy notices of Day of AI and the tools used.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Draw prompts in Quick Draw and look at how many different drawings the dataset holds.
  2. Write the steps of an algorithm for a simple task and test them with a partner.
  3. Train a Teachable Machine model on two classes and test it with new examples.
  4. Change the training images and describe how the accuracy changes.
  5. Look for bias in a model and suggest a fix for the training data.

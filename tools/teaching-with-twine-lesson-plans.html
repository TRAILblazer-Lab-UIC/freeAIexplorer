Teaching with Twine lesson plans

Two classroom plans for teaching students to build stories in Twine.

Computing

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This 2015 blog resource page offers two Twine lesson plans, Twine in One Day and Twine in Two Days. The first is one 50 minute session that introduces Twine and starts building. The second adds styling and scripts over two 50 minute sessions. Slides and a full lesson plan PDF are linked. The plans were written for Twine 1.4 or 2.0.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build an interactive story in one class period.
  • Students can add styling and scripts in the second plan.
  • Students practice story structure by writing branching paths.

For teachers

  • Slide PDFs for each day are ready to use.
  • A full lesson plan PDF is linked.
  • The author uses the plans in English and literature courses.

For schools

  • The plans are posted publicly and no price is stated.
  • Students use a browser version of Twine 2.0 or a downloaded Twine 1.4.
  • Newer Twine versions may have different menus. IT should check the version.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The plans describe no AI. Students link passages of story together. They practice logic and branching as they plan how each choice leads to the next.

Privacy and safety

The page says the plans need no student accounts. Students download Twine 1.4 or use the browser version of Twine 2.0 at twinery.org. The materials do not say how much internet access Twine needs. The research did not review how Twine handles data or where it stores stories. The plans date from 2015 and newer Twine versions may differ. Check where the browser version saves stories and decide how students save and share their work.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a short story with three passages and link them with choices.
  2. Build a story that ends differently depending on the reader's choice.
  3. Add a plot twist and test whether the links still work.
  4. Trade stories and write feedback on the structure.
  5. Present a story and explain how the choices change the mood.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Make a link from one passage to another and test it.
  2. Add styling to change the look of a story, as in the two day plan.
  3. Use a short script to track a variable in a story.
  4. Find a broken link and fix it.
  5. Explain how the story map shows the logic of your game.

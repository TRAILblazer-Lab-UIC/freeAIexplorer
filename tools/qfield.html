QField

A free app for collecting map data in the field on phones and tablets.

FREEComputingLocal with optional cloud or accounts

Overview

QField is a free open source app for collecting and managing geospatial data in the field. It is built on QGIS projects. Teachers prepare a project with the QFieldSync plugin and open it in QField. It works online or offline and can optionally sync with the QFieldCloud service.

Benefits

For students

  • Students collect real locations and photos outdoors.
  • Students work offline in the field.
  • Students bring their data back to a map.

For teachers

  • Teachers prepare the project in QGIS and hand it to students.
  • The app runs on Android, iOS, Windows, Linux, and macOS.
  • Teachers can copy a project to devices or sync it through QFieldCloud.

For schools

  • The app is free.
  • Field data can stay on school devices.
  • School-managed devices may need app approval, and QFieldCloud pricing was not read.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

QField has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is working with data. Students collect points, notes, and photos and then study the map. That practice shows how data gathering feeds later analysis.

Privacy and safety

An account is optional and is needed only for QFieldCloud. The internet is used at setup. The data that students collect includes locations and photos. Data stays on the device unless it is synced to QFieldCloud. QFieldCloud is a vendor service that stores synced data in Swiss datacenters or a private instance. The research did not read its privacy policy or the app privacy policy. A teacher should decide whether to copy files by hand to keep data on school devices.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Survey trees on school grounds and record each species and location.
  2. Map litter or water drainage spots and photograph each one.
  3. Compare two areas of the school yard and record sunlight and soil notes.
  4. Return to the classroom and view the points on a map.
  5. Write a short report on what the map shows about local habitats.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Map places on a neighborhood walk and add a photo to each point.
  2. Mark features such as benches and bus stops for an accessibility study.
  3. Collect points of a historic route and add a note to each stop.
  4. Compare class maps and discuss how each group chose its points.
  5. Build a story map from the field data.

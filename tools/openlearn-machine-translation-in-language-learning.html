OpenLearn Machine translation in language learning

Free Open University guidance on using machine translation in language classes.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

This is free material from the Open University on using online machine translation in language teaching. The audience is adult learners and teachers. The linked article suggests activities such as comparing student and machine translations. Students translate a text themselves and then check it against a tool.

Benefits

For students

  • Students compare their own translation with a machine version.
  • Students use translation tools to explore grammar.
  • Students practice rewriting a text to match a learner's level.

For teachers

  • The article lists ready activities for language classes.
  • It discusses academic integrity in plain terms.
  • A companion free course offers a statement of participation.

For schools

  • The material is free and needs only a web browser.
  • No IT work is needed.
  • The named tools are DeepL and Google Translate, which the school must approve separately.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is machine translation. It runs in a company's cloud when students paste text into a tool such as DeepL or Google Translate. Students translate first and then compare the machine result with their own. They use the differences to improve their own work.

Privacy and safety

An account is optional and is needed only for the companion course. Text that students paste goes to a third party translation company such as DeepL or Google. The research did not open the Open University privacy policy and did not read the companion course. The materials do not say what student data is collected. A teacher should check district approval for the translation tools and remind students not to paste personal details.

How this can be used for World Languages
  1. Translate a short paragraph yourself, then compare it with a machine translation and mark each difference.
  2. Use a translation tool to check one grammar point and explain what you learned.
  3. Take a complex text and ask a tool for a version at your level, then judge it.
  4. Find a sentence the machine translated badly and write a better one.
  5. Write a class guide on when machine translation helps and when it harms learning.

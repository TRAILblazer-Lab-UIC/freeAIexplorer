Zooniverse Classroom

A citizen science site where classes classify real research data.

AI

Overview

Zooniverse is a citizen science platform where volunteers classify images, audio, and other data for research teams. The Classroom site lets teachers set up classes and assign projects. Students make real classifications that go to project research teams. The Classroom page itself loaded with only a title when checked.

Benefits

For students

  • Students contribute to real research.
  • Students work with images, audio, and other data.
  • Students can choose projects in many science fields.

For teachers

  • Teachers can create a class and assign projects.
  • Classifications are stored in a Zooniverse database for research teams.
  • No age limit applies and an account is not needed to classify.

For schools

  • The platform is free.
  • It runs in a browser.
  • Users under 16 need parent or guardian approval for accounts. Teachers should plan around that step.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI that students use. Students classify data by hand and their work goes to research teams. Some projects may use the data to train models. Students build the skill of labeling data, which is the basis of much machine learning.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are optional for classifying and need only an email and a username. Classifications go to Zooniverse and the project research teams. Users under 16 are pointed to a Youth Policy, and a blog post says they need parent or guardian approval to create an account. The research did not read the privacy policy, and it is unconfirmed whether teacher created classroom accounts change that step. A teacher should read the Youth Policy and the privacy terms before students join. A teacher should also preview each project for suitable content.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Classify a set of animal images and report how many you finished.
  2. Compare your classifications with a partner's on the same images.
  3. Read the project page and explain the research question.
  4. Track the class total each week on a chart.
  5. Discuss how many volunteers a project needs to trust its data.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Join a humanities project and transcribe a historical record.
  2. Explain why researchers need many people to label data.
  3. Study the history behind a document you classified.
  4. Discuss how citizen science changes who takes part in research.
  5. Write a short note on one thing a classification taught you.

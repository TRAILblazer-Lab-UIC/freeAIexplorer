Project moveSMART (micro:bit activity monitor)

A fifth grade unit where students build a micro:bit activity monitor.

AIComputingGood for AI beginners

Overview

Project moveSMART is a research project that joins computer science and physical education in grades 4 and 5. The fifth grade unit has three lessons. Students build and improve a physical activity monitor with the micro:bit accelerometer. A 2022 IEEE MASS paper by Jeong, Payton, Julien, and Castelli describes it.

Benefits

For students

  • Students program a calibrated step counter.
  • Students graph movement data and look for patterns.
  • Students use machine learning to sort activity level.

For teachers

  • Blocks in MakeCode keep coding approachable.
  • The unit links to Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills.
  • Teacher materials beyond the paper were not found.

For schools

  • The paper is free.
  • Each group needs a micro:bit board. Price was not checked.
  • MakeCode runs in a browser, and a teacher should plan about an hour of setup.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a machine learning classifier that sorts activity level from accelerometer data. It runs on the device. Students collect movement data, build the classifier, and then check how well it works. They also graph the data to find patterns.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The micro:bit board collects the movement data. The research did not confirm where students store or export that data, or whether the machine learning runs on the board or on a computer. The materials do not state what student data is collected. A teacher should decide how to handle student activity data before sharing any graphs.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Wear a micro:bit while walking and compare its step count with a hand count.
  2. Move at slow, medium, and fast speeds and record the step count each time.
  3. Set a daily step goal and track progress with the monitor.
  4. Test whether the activity monitor can tell jogging from walking.
  5. Discuss what the data can and cannot say about health.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Program a micro:bit step counter in MakeCode.
  2. Calibrate the counter by changing its threshold.
  3. Train a machine learning monitor and test it on new movements.
  4. Fix a bug that counts too many steps.
  5. Explain how the micro:bit decides that a step happened.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Graph step counts for a week of class activity.
  2. Find the average steps for each activity.
  3. Compare two graphs and describe the pattern.
  4. Spot an outlier in the data and suggest a cause.
  5. Predict the next day's count from earlier counts.

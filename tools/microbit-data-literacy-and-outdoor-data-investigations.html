micro:bit data literacy and outdoor data investigations

Playground survey lessons where pupils collect outdoor data with micro:bit boards.

AIComputing

Overview

This is a 2025 news post that points to the micro:bit playground survey activities. Pupils compare surface temperatures, count plants and animals, measure playground area, and track physical activity. The activities were created with BBC Education and suit primary schools. A link to an Exploring machine learning activity is also listed.

Benefits

For students

  • Students collect real data outdoors.
  • Students use the micro:bit sensors and counters.
  • Students graph and discuss their results.

For teachers

  • Five linked activities come with worksheet templates.
  • The lessons are tied to the curricula of England, Northern Ireland, Scotland, and Wales per the post.
  • Teachers can use the activities across science, math, and PE.

For schools

  • The lessons are free.
  • A micro:bit board costs money, and a V2 is best for data logging.
  • Data stays on the board.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is optional. One activity tracks physical activity with a model that classifies movement, and another links to Exploring machine learning. The model runs on the micro:bit. Students wear the board and then study what the model reports about their movement.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. Logged sensor readings and counts stay on the micro:bit and are copied by USB or exported by the teacher. The pages read do not discuss privacy. The research did not check account use for the micro:bit app on tablets. Optional machine learning activities run on the board. A teacher should check that students share their activity data only with the class.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Measure the temperature of grass, asphalt, and sand and compare them.
  2. Count the plants and animals in one square of the playground.
  3. Predict which surface will be hottest and check the readings.
  4. Compare readings from two times of day.
  5. Write a short report on what the biodiversity counts show.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Measure the playground area with the micro:bit and a rule.
  2. Graph the temperature data from three surfaces.
  3. Find the highest and lowest reading in a data set.
  4. Round measurements to the nearest whole number and compare.
  5. Compare estimates of area with measured results.
How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Wear a micro:bit during break and read the activity graph.
  2. Compare the graphs of two classmates.
  3. Describe which part of the break was most active.
  4. Set a goal for next week's activity and test it.
  5. Discuss how accurate the activity model was.

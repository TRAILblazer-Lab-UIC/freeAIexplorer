Scoilnet AI Pyramids fact-check lesson

A 40 minute lesson that fact-checks an AI image of the Egyptian pyramids.

FREEAIStays on the deviceGood for AI beginners

Overview

This is Lesson 4 in a Scoilnet series. Students look at a supplied AI-generated image of the Egyptian pyramids and ask what seems questionable. They then check the details against several credible sources. The sweep notes list 40 minutes for ages 12 to 15. The PDF returned an error. This entry uses search snippets.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn to question an image before they trust it.
  • Students practice checking details against credible sources.
  • Students build critical analysis of AI generated history.

For teachers

  • The image is supplied in the lesson PDF.
  • The lesson is short at about 40 minutes.
  • Students can research on Chromebooks or iPads, or work from a printout.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • It needs only the PDF and a device or a printed page.
  • No IT work is needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

No AI tool runs in this lesson. The lesson gives students an AI generated image to study. Students learn to spot errors and to verify claims. They build the skill of checking before they share.

The lesson supplies the AI image. No AI tool or account is needed. Students may browse the web to check sources. The research could not fetch the lesson PDF. Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. List three details in the pyramids image that look questionable.
  2. Look up how the pyramids were really built and mark what the image gets wrong.
  3. Compare the image with a photo from a museum or a trusted site.
  4. Write a short caption that tells the true facts about the pyramids.
  5. Explain how people might share a false image by mistake.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Choose three credible sources and say why each is trustworthy.
  2. Search for the image online and note where it appears.
  3. Make a checklist of questions to ask about any image.
  4. Compare two sources that disagree and decide which to trust.
  5. Teach a classmate one way to verify an image.

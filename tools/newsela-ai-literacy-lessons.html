Newsela AI literacy lessons

AI literacy lessons inside Newsela Social Studies on bias, data privacy, and checking AI answers.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

Newsela has a collection of AI literacy lessons in its Social Studies area, described in a June 2026 blog post. The units cover Foundations of AI, Algorithmic Thinking and Data Analysis, and Data Privacy and Security. One lesson asks students to check an AI answer about a current event against trusted sources. The post says students do not need to log in to an AI tool.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn what AI is in the Foundations of AI unit.
  • Students check an AI answer against trusted sources.
  • Students end the Algorithmic Bias lesson with an awareness campaign.

For teachers

  • Many activities are low tech, such as sorting examples and holding debates.
  • The data privacy unit includes an op-ed task.
  • Teachers do not need student logins to an AI tool.

For schools

  • A free account can be created.
  • Pricing is not listed, and the page offers Contact Sales.
  • The research did not confirm whether the AI collection is in the free tier.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The collection teaches about AI and does not require students to use an AI tool. It covers AI literacy, algorithmic bias, data privacy, and checking AI output. The lessons run in Newsela's cloud. Students apply what they learn by questioning AI answers on their own.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are needed, and the internet is required. Teachers can use a free account, and students sign in per assignment. Student information goes to Newsela, and the research did not read its privacy policy. The lesson pages sit behind the app, and only the blog post was read. The post advises students not to enter personal details into AI tools. A teacher should review the vendor's privacy terms and district agreements before students sign in.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Ask an AI tool about a current event and check each claim against trusted sources.
  2. Sort examples of AI uses into helpful and risky groups.
  3. Hold a class debate on who should decide what data apps collect.
  4. Plan an awareness campaign about algorithmic bias.
  5. Write an op-ed about data privacy for students.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Read an article from the collection and note the main claim and evidence.
  2. Compare an AI summary of a text with your own summary.
  3. Write a short guide on how to check a source.
  4. Rewrite a vague AI answer so it names its sources.
  5. Practice writing an op-ed with a clear position.

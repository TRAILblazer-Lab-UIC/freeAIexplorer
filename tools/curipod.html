Curipod

A web platform for teacher-led writing lessons with AI feedback on student responses.

FREEAISends student data to a company

Overview

Curipod is a web platform for K-12 teachers who run whole-class writing and reading lessons. The teacher starts a session and students join. The homepage highlights real-time AI feedback on student writing and lessons aligned to more than 55 curricula. The site says students do not use AI independently.

Benefits

For students

  • Students get quick feedback on their written responses.
  • Students join a session from any device with a browser.
  • Students see revision reports that show how their writing changed.

For teachers

  • Teachers lead the session and see all responses in one place.
  • Reports show student responses and revisions.
  • A free plan covers weekly writing sessions for an individual teacher.

For schools

  • Schools can sign in with ClassLink, Clever, or Google.
  • Schools can request a data privacy agreement.
  • School and District plans are custom quotes. Devices need only a modern browser.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI gives feedback on student writing and can help generate lessons and read text aloud. It runs in a company's cloud. The vendor says AI calls go to OpenAI, Google, and ElevenLabs without personal data. Students write, read the feedback, and revise their own work.

Privacy and safety

A teacher account is needed. Students can join without accounts per the pricing page. The vendor collects student responses and optionally student names, and keeps IP addresses for up to 3 weeks. Responses go to the vendor, and AI calls go to OpenAI, Google and ElevenLabs without personal data per the vendor. The policy does not name its sub-processors. A teacher should read the privacy policy, avoid real names where possible, and ask the school for a data privacy agreement. How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a short response to a class prompt and revise it after reading the AI feedback.
  2. Compare a first draft with a revised draft and describe three changes.
  3. Join a reading lesson and answer questions about a short passage.
  4. Give a classmate feedback on one response and then compare it with the AI feedback.
  5. Write a paragraph about whether the AI feedback was helpful and why.

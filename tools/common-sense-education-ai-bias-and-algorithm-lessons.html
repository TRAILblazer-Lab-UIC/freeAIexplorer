Common Sense Education AI bias and algorithm lessons

A 20-minute Common Sense lesson on how training data creates AI bias.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

Understanding AI Bias is a 20-minute lesson for grades 6 to 12. Students define AI bias, learn how biased or incomplete training data can produce biased AI, and consider ways to reduce it. The teacher plan uses a fruit detector example. A companion lesson named How AI Bias Impacts Our Lives follows it. The lesson page asks teachers to sign in or join to open the free lesson.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn what AI bias means.
  • Students see how training data and testing data differ.
  • Students think of ways to reduce bias.

For teachers

  • The lesson has slides ready to use.
  • The fruit detector example is easy to follow.
  • A companion lesson continues the topic.

For schools

  • The lesson is free with an account.
  • It runs in a browser.
  • The teacher version was last updated in October 2023.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no AI tool that students run. It uses a fruit detector example to show how training data shapes an AI system. The lesson content is hosted in the vendor's cloud. Students build the skill of spotting biased or incomplete data.

Privacy and safety

The lesson page asks teachers to sign in or join to open the free lesson, and the internet is needed. The page does not say what students submit, and the materials do not say what student data is collected. Slides are viewed online or downloaded after sign in. A teacher should check the Common Sense privacy terms before signing up. A teacher should also use the slides without student accounts.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Define AI bias in your own words and give an example.
  2. Find a news story about AI bias and summarize it.
  3. Compare two sources on the same AI problem.
  4. List ways to check whether a data set is fair.
  5. Write a short tip sheet on spotting bias in AI tools.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a fruit detector example on paper with training and testing data.
  2. Change the training data and predict how the results change.
  3. Explain the difference between training data and testing data.
  4. Find what is missing from a data set and add it.
  5. Suggest one way to test an AI for bias.

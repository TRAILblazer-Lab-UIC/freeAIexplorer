Civic Data Project (TERC and Boston University)

Civic datasets for middle school social studies, formatted for the CODAP tool.

FREEComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

The Civic Data Project is a partnership of TERC and Boston University that works with middle school social studies teachers to co-design data-rich activities. It curates civic datasets for use in CODAP, a free browser-based data analysis tool. Four datasets cover New England county health, youth voting in the 2022 midterms, Rhode Island EPA environmental data, and United Nations and Gallup poll data. Teachers join a mailing list to request access.

Benefits

For students

  • Students explore real civic data in social studies.
  • Students practice reading graphs and finding patterns.
  • Students use a free browser tool.

For teachers

  • Five middle school teachers co-designed the activities.
  • The page links to routines such as What's Going On in This Graph, Slow Reveal Graphs, and Gapminder.
  • Datasets are formatted and ready for CODAP.

For schools

  • Everything is free.
  • CODAP runs in a browser, and Chromebooks are listed.
  • Datasets arrive by email. Classroom use needs a short wait.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The project has no AI. It builds data literacy, as students graph data and draw conclusions. It also builds pattern spotting.

Privacy and safety

Students use CODAP in the browser, and the internet is needed throughout. The project page states no student data collection, and no account is needed. The research did not review a privacy policy or CODAP's data handling. Teachers give an email address to the project mailing list to receive datasets. A teacher should check the datasets for topics that suit the age of students.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Compare youth voting across states using the 2022 midterm data, as co-designed by the teachers.
  2. Look at county health data and describe one pattern.
  3. Explore poll data and form a claim.
  4. Use a graph routine to discuss a graph without a title.
  5. Write a short report with one chart.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Make a graph from a dataset in CODAP.
  2. Compare the average of two groups.
  3. Find an outlier and explain it.
  4. Describe a trend from a scatter plot.
  5. Write one question that the data cannot answer.

Arm EducateAI Coalition and Arm Introduction to AI course

A teacher training coalition and a free online Introduction to AI course from Arm.

FREEAISends student data to a company

Overview

This entry bundles two offerings from Arm. The EducateAI Coalition plans teacher training, credentials, and grade-level AI pathways from kindergarten through higher education. The Introduction to AI course is a free-to-access beginner course on edX and Coursera. It covers machine learning basics, neural networks, ethics, and model deployment with Python and PyTorch.

Benefits

For students

  • Students may gain from teachers who learn how machine learning works.
  • Older students can follow the course topics of neural networks and ethics.
  • Students see a clear path from basic ideas to real Python work.

For teachers

  • The course is aimed at educators and early-career developers.
  • The course takes about one week according to a Coursera listing.
  • Teachers practice with Python and PyTorch.

For schools

  • Arm describes the course as free to access.
  • Nothing is installed because the course runs in the browser.
  • Certificates on the host platforms may cost extra, and that was not checked.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The course teaches how AI works, including machine learning basics, neural networks, ethics, and deploying models. The course platform runs in the vendor's cloud. Teachers use Python and PyTorch exercises to build their own sense of how a model is made. They can then bring simpler versions of those ideas to students.

Privacy and safety

An account is needed and the course needs an internet connection at all times. The course is hosted on edX or Coursera. The research did not review the privacy policies of those platforms. The materials do not say what data is collected. The course is aimed at educators and older learners. A teacher should check the platform terms before signing up.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Discuss the history of AI in a timeline that students build as a class.
  2. Draw a small neural network on paper and label the inputs and outputs.
  3. Run a short Python notebook that trains a tiny model and report the result.
  4. Debate an AI ethics case from the course and write a position statement.
  5. Compare the energy cost of training a model with other everyday activities.

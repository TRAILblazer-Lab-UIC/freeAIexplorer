MIT Dancing with AI

A week-long middle school workshop on building movement projects with Scratch and pose tracking.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

MIT Dancing with AI is a week-long workshop for middle school students. Students build interactive movement projects in Scratch. They use one extension for hand, body, and face tracking and another that adds Teachable Machine blocks. The group also talks about bias, representation, and how people and AI work together.

Benefits

For students

  • Students move their own bodies to control a project on screen.
  • Students train a custom image or pose model and see how it learns from examples.
  • Students discuss bias and representation in machine learning with real projects in front of them.

For teachers

  • The materials come as a ready curriculum with an overview page to follow.
  • The block interface in Scratch is familiar to many middle school teachers.
  • The workshop gives a clear way to link movement, media, and computing.

For schools

  • The materials are free and use a CC BY-NC 4.0 license.
  • The tools run in a web browser on Windows, Mac, and Chromebook computers.
  • IT involvement is low, but the workshop needs a webcam on each student computer.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is pose, hand, and face tracking plus image classification from Teachable Machine blocks. The materials run in the browser, but the vendor does not say where the model processing happens. Students train their own models, use them to control Scratch projects, and then discuss where the models fail for some people.

Privacy and safety

Students use webcam video during the projects. The materials do not state where that data goes, and they do not say whether accounts are needed. An internet connection is needed the whole time. A teacher should check how Teachable Machine handles images and get permission for webcam use before the first class.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Theater and Media Arts
  1. Build a Scratch project that reacts to a dancer's pose and shows matching visuals.
  2. Choreograph a short routine with a partner and train a model to recognize each move.
  3. Design a stage effect that follows a performer's hands on screen.
  4. Record a short demo of the project and explain the design choices to the class.
  5. Discuss how a tracking tool might miss some bodies and how to improve the design.
How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Train a pose model to recognize three exercise positions such as squat, lunge, and stretch.
  2. Build a game that counts how many times a student holds a pose.
  3. Compare how well the model recognizes a pose from different students.
  4. Create a warm-up routine that the computer checks by watching the movements.
  5. Discuss why a pose model might work better for some body types than others.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Add the body tracking extension to Scratch and print the position of a hand on screen.
  2. Collect pose samples for a Teachable Machine model and test how many samples are enough.
  3. Write Scratch blocks that react when the model sees a chosen pose.
  4. Test a model with a new person and record when it makes mistakes.
  5. Explain in a short talk how the training examples shaped the model.

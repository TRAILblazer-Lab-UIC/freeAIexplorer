Scoilnet AI history lesson series

Twelve lesson plans where students fact-check AI output about history.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

Scoilnet hosts a learning path of twelve lesson plans on AI in history, contributed by Patrick Hickey. Students examine history topics through AI output and fact-check it. Search results show one lesson using Ideogram.ai to analyze an AI image of wartime London. Others check AI claims about the pyramids and Irish neutrality. The lessons map to Junior Cycle History outcomes.

Benefits

For students

  • Students practice checking AI claims against real sources.
  • Students analyze AI generated images of historical scenes.
  • Students learn that AI can present errors with confidence.

For teachers

  • The series gives twelve ready lesson plans.
  • Lessons map to Irish Junior Cycle History outcomes.
  • Lesson plans download as PDFs.

For schools

  • The lessons are free.
  • Lessons that use online AI tools need student or teacher access to them.
  • The official page returned an error. Details come from search snippets.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lessons use AI image generators and AI written claims as material. These tools run in companies' clouds. Students study the output and check it against sources. They build the skill of judging whether AI content is accurate.

Key terms on this page

Short definitions of words used above and below. Select a term for the full glossary entry.

Image generation
Image generation is AI that creates new pictures from a text description or a sketch.

Privacy and safety

Lessons that use online AI tools send what students type to a company cloud. At least one lesson uses the Ideogram.ai image tool. The research did not check its account terms or data handling. The materials do not state which accounts students need. A teacher should decide whether students or only the teacher use the tool. A teacher should also check each tool's age rules.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Study an AI generated image of London in World War II and list what looks wrong.
  2. Check an AI claim about Irish neutrality against two reliable sources.
  3. Compare an AI description of an event with a textbook account.
  4. Write a short report that corrects an AI error in a historical claim.
  5. Rank sources by how much you trust them and explain your choices.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Make a checklist for judging a historical image.
  2. Trace a claim back to its first source.
  3. Compare three sources on the same historical event and note the differences.
  4. Write tips for classmates on how to fact-check AI output.
  5. Create a display of an AI error and the true facts that correct it.

Dash robot (Wonder Workshop)

A wheeled robot that children program with the Blockly app on a tablet.

$ LicenseComputingRoboticsSends student data to a company

Overview

Dash is a wheeled robot programmed with the Blockly app and other Wonder apps on tablets and phones. The apps are listed for iOS, Android and Chromebook. The Blockly app is for ages 7 and up. The Make Wonder platform adds classroom lessons and teacher and student logins.

Benefits

For students

  • Students program a robot with drag-and-drop blocks.
  • Students see their code move a real robot.
  • Students work through classroom lessons in Make Wonder.

For teachers

  • St. Louis Public Schools rolled out Dash with Make Wonder across 39 sites for grades K-5.
  • Teachers create a classroom and student profiles.
  • Make Wonder lessons guide the class.

For schools

  • Price runs about 150 to 200 USD per robot. The MSRP rose to 189.99 USD on October 9, 2025.
  • Make Wonder subscriptions cost extra.
  • Wonder Workshop assets were acquired by MORAVIA Consulting and MORAVIA Education launched in February 2026.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Dash has no AI. The robot runs the blocks that students build in the app. Students build skills in sequencing, loops and events.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are optional. The app collects profile name, programs, time in app, robot diagnostics and device and log data, and the data goes to the vendor. Teachers may enter student names to create a classroom. The policy states COPPA compliance and says data is not sold and is hosted on AWS. It also says Google Analytics runs on its websites and apps, and data may transfer in a company sale. Wonder Workshop assets moved to MORAVIA in 2026, and the research did not confirm whether the policy changed. A teacher should decide what names to enter and ask about the MORAVIA transition.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a Blockly program that makes Dash drive in a square.
  2. Add sounds and lights to a short program.
  3. Use a loop to shorten a long program.
  4. Complete a Make Wonder lesson and describe the new idea.
  5. Debug a program that a partner wrote and explain the fix.

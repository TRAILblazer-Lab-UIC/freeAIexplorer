WhisperTyper

A free Mac app that types what you say into any program using Whisper.

FREEAIStays on the device

Overview

WhisperTyper is a free macOS app. It transcribes the user's voice with OpenAI's Whisper and types the text at the cursor in any app. The listing mentions Metal GPU acceleration on Apple Silicon. It is closed source and free. The listing says it works offline, but that claim was not verified.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can speak their words into any app.
  • Students who find typing hard can draft with their voice.
  • Students see their words appear as they talk.

For teachers

  • The app works in any program that accepts typing.
  • It is simple to try on one Mac.
  • Teachers can show dictation as an accessibility aid.

For schools

  • The app is free with no accounts or subscriptions.
  • The listing says it works offline.
  • The app is unsigned and has no automatic updates. It is not suited to managed Macs without review.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is the Whisper speech recognition model. It runs on the Mac and can use Metal on Apple Silicon. Students speak and the text appears at the cursor. They then edit the text in their own words.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed. The AlternativeTo listing says audio is processed locally and not sent to the cloud. The source code is closed. This claim cannot be audited. No privacy policy was found, and the developer is a single individual with no company or website. A teacher should have IT review the unsigned app and watch its network behavior. A teacher should also keep it off managed school Macs until that review is done.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Dictate a short paragraph into a document and fix the errors.
  2. Compare how long it takes to speak and to type a sentence.
  3. Dictate answers to a worksheet and read them back.
  4. Test the app with a quiet voice and a loud voice.
  5. Tell a partner when dictation helps you most.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Dictate the first draft of a short story.
  2. Speak a thesis statement and refine it in writing.
  3. Dictate a journal entry and add punctuation.
  4. Compare your spoken sentences with your written ones.
  5. Read a dictated page aloud and mark places that need editing.

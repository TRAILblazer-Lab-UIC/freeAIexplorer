Gapminder Dollar Street

A photo database of family homes around the world, sorted by monthly income.

FREEComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

Dollar Street is a photo database from Gapminder that shows family homes and belongings arranged by monthly income. The site lists 468 families in 67 countries with 46,070 photos and 8,586 videos. Teachers of older students can use it in class. Students browse and compare photos across income levels to study everyday life.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see real homes, beds, stoves, and toys from many countries.
  • Students compare daily life at different income levels and form their own questions.
  • Students practice reading evidence from photos and from data.

For teachers

  • The site runs in a web browser and needs no setup.
  • Documented lessons exist, including a Federal Reserve Education middle school lesson that pairs the photos with GDP figures.
  • Teachers can browse by country, income, and topic to find a fitting example fast.

For schools

  • The site is free for anyone to use.
  • It works on Chromebooks and other devices with a browser.
  • IT has nothing to install. The content carries a Creative Commons BY 4.0 logo.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Dollar Street has no AI. It is a data set that students explore through a website. It builds data skills. Students compare groups and look for patterns across income levels.

Privacy and safety

The site runs in the vendor's cloud and needs the internet at all times. No account is described as required. The navigation shows My albums, which may be an optional feature that involves an account. The pages read did not cover privacy. The research does not know what student data the site collects. A teacher should check the site's privacy policy before students use it on school devices. A teacher should also preview the photos. They show real families.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Compare the homes of two families at different income levels and list what looks the same and what looks different.
  2. Choose one everyday item such as a bed or a stove and sort its photos from the poorest to the richest family.
  3. Use the Measuring the Past idea and treat Dollar Street as a first data project about people today.
  4. Write three questions about a family photo that the picture alone cannot answer.
  5. Map the countries in one income range and discuss what the map shows.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Count photos in each income group for one topic and make a bar graph.
  2. Compare the monthly income of two families and describe the gap as a ratio.
  3. Read the income scale from poor to rich and place five photos on a number line.
  4. Pair the photos with GDP figures as in the Federal Reserve lesson and discuss what the numbers show.
  5. Explain why a few photos cannot stand for a whole country.

Draw Things

A free app that makes images from text on an iPhone, iPad, or Mac.

FREEAILocal with optional cloud or accountsGood for AI beginners

Overview

Draw Things is a native iPhone, iPad, and Mac app that makes images from text prompts and from other images. The site says it generates images locally and offline. The free edition includes local generation, model downloads, an infinite canvas, and on-device LoRA training. Optional Cloud Compute sends work to Draw Things servers for signed-in users.

Benefits

For students

  • Students turn written ideas into pictures within the app.
  • Students can edit images and train a style on the device.
  • Students learn how prompts shape an image.

For teachers

  • The app is free to install from the App Store.
  • The app has an infinite canvas for larger projects.
  • Teachers should plan time to download models.

For schools

  • The free edition costs nothing. Draw Things+ is $8.99 per month for higher cloud limits.
  • Generation runs on the device with no account.
  • Large models need recent hardware and storage.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a diffusion model that makes images from text and images. It runs on the device and can also train a LoRA style on the device. Students write a prompt, look at the result, and revise it. They use the tool to extend their own art ideas.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed for local use, and sign-in is optional with Sign in with Apple. The internet is used at setup to download models. Prompts and images stay on the device unless Cloud Compute is turned on. The policy does not say whether prompts or images go to the server in Cloud Compute, and it does not mention minors or COPPA. App Store snapshots show conflicting age ratings of 4+ and 17+. A teacher should confirm the current age rating, set content rules, and review student prompts.

How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Write a prompt for a landscape and revise it three times to improve the picture.
  2. Use an image to start a new picture and describe what the app changed.
  3. Draw a rough sketch by hand and ask the app to build on it.
  4. Compare the same prompt on two different models and note the style differences.
  5. Create a series of images with one theme and write an artist statement.

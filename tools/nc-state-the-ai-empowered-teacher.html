NC State The AI-Empowered Teacher

A free self-paced online course on AI literacy for K-12 educators.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

The AI-Empowered Teacher is a free, self-paced online course from the Friday Institute at NC State. It is for K-12 educators. It has nine modules on values, how generative AI works and fails, bias, ethics, designing AI-supported learning, personalization, and assessment. It is worth 15 professional development hours.

Benefits

For students

  • Students gain from teachers who understand how AI works and fails.
  • Teachers can bring lessons on bias and ethics into the classroom.
  • Students benefit when teachers plan AI use with clear values.

For teachers

  • The course counts for 15 professional development hours.
  • A certificate comes with completed activities, self-assessments, and discussion posts.
  • Educators can join with a cohort or on their own.

For schools

  • The course is free.
  • CEU credit depends on state and local agencies.
  • Access lasts 365 days from registration, and registration is open until July 1, 2027.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The course teaches about AI and does not run an AI tool for students. It covers how generative AI works and fails, bias, and ethical decisions. It runs on the vendor's cloud in a web browser. Teachers use what they learn to design AI-supported learning for their own classes.

Privacy and safety

An account is required, and the internet is needed throughout. Registration goes through the NC State REPORTER system and needs a Brickyard account. The course runs on a hosted platform, and the research did not review its privacy terms. Students do not use the course. A teacher should read the registration and platform terms before signing up.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Explain to students how a generative AI model produces text and where it can fail.
  2. Show a class example of a wrong AI answer and trace what might have caused it.
  3. Run a class talk on what AI can and cannot do.
  4. Ask students to write a guide on checking AI output.
  5. Compare two AI tools on one task and discuss what to trust.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Lead a discussion on whose values an AI tool reflects.
  2. Study an example of bias in an AI system and list who is affected.
  3. Debate an ethical choice about AI in schools.
  4. Ask students to write class rules for AI use based on shared values.
  5. Interview a community member about how AI affects their work.

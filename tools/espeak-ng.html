eSpeak NG

A small free speech synthesizer that reads text aloud in over 100 languages.

AIComputing

Overview

eSpeak NG is an open source text-to-speech tool. It supports more than 100 languages and accents and takes only a few megabytes. Its voices sound robotic. Students and teachers use it to turn text into speech, and many other tools use it as their voice engine.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can hear any text read aloud.
  • Students can listen to the same sentence in many languages.
  • Students can learn how a computer builds a voice from rules.

For teachers

  • The tool is free and runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, and Android.
  • It works from the command line or from other programs.
  • Teachers can use it with students who need text read aloud.

For schools

  • The software costs nothing and needs minimal hardware.
  • It is a local synthesizer and no account is needed.
  • IT or a technical teacher must install it. It is an engine and has no classroom interface.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

It has no AI. It uses rule based formant synthesis to make speech. A skill it builds is pattern spotting. Students hear how spelling rules turn into sounds. Students can compare how the same rule gives different results in different languages.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the internet is used at setup. The research found no privacy policy and no documented network component. No data collection is documented. The page does not say whether it works offline. Text is turned into speech by a local synthesizer. Other apps and screen readers bundle the engine, and they may handle data differently. A teacher should check which app bundles the engine and read that app's privacy terms.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Listen to a short reading passage read aloud and follow along with the text.
  2. Change the speed of the voice and choose the pace that helps most.
  3. Proofread a sentence by listening to it.
  4. Compare the robot voice with a human voice and explain which is easier to follow.
  5. Hear a spelling word and write it down.
How this can be used for World Languages
  1. Hear the same greeting in five languages.
  2. Type a phrase in the target language and listen to the pronunciation.
  3. Compare two accents of one language.
  4. Write a short dialogue and listen to both parts.
  5. Find a word the voice says oddly and discuss why.

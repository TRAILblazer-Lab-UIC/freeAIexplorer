Apple VoiceOver and Live Captions

Built-in Apple tools that read the screen aloud and caption speech on the device.

FREEAILocal with optional cloud or accounts

Overview

VoiceOver is the screen reader built into Apple devices. It reads or brailles what is on screen. Live Captions shows a real time transcript of speech around the user and in calls. Both tools help students with vision or hearing needs. Students turn them on in Settings and use them in daily work.

Benefits

For students

  • Students who are blind or have low vision can hear or feel what is on screen.
  • Students who are deaf or hard of hearing can read what people say as they say it.
  • Students can use the same tools on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

For teachers

  • The features are part of the operating system and need no install.
  • Teachers can turn them on in Settings under Accessibility in a few minutes.
  • VoiceOver works with gestures, a trackpad, a keyboard, or a braille display.

For schools

  • The features are included with Apple devices at no extra cost.
  • No accounts are needed, which lowers the setup load.
  • Mobile device management can manage the devices at the school level.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Live Captions uses speech recognition to turn spoken words into text. Apple states that this work happens on the device. VoiceOver Recognition and Live Recognition use machine learning to describe images and nearby objects. Students rely on these descriptions to reach content that they could not read or hear otherwise.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed. Apple states that Live Captions transcriptions are generated on the device. The VoiceOver description makes no such claim. Apple says Apple Intelligence powers Image Explorer and Live Recognition but does not say where that processing runs. The research did not read the Apple privacy policy and the materials do not say what student data is collected. A teacher should check the Apple privacy terms for the VoiceOver image features and confirm which iPhone models and languages support Live Captions.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Practice moving through a school web page with VoiceOver gestures and describe what each swipe reads.
  2. Turn on Live Captions during a class talk and mark any words the captions got wrong.
  3. Use VoiceOver Recognition to hear a description of a photo and compare it with what sighted peers see.
  4. Write a short how-to guide for a classmate who wants to try VoiceOver.
  5. Set up a Bluetooth braille display with a teacher and read one paragraph in braille.

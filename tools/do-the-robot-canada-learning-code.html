Do The Robot (Canada Learning Code)

A 15 minute unplugged lesson where one student programs another as a robot.

FREEComputingUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

Do The Robot is a 15 minute unplugged lesson on simple instructions, sequences, and algorithms. Learners work in pairs, with one acting as the programmer and one as the robot. The programmer gives step-by-step instructions with words only, for tasks such as tying a shoe or opening a door. Pairs then switch roles and reflect on which role was harder.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn that exact words make a good algorithm.
  • Students try both roles and compare them.
  • Students see how a robot follows only what it is told.

For teachers

  • The lesson takes only 15 minutes.
  • The page lists the level as For Everyone.
  • Optional extension videos and a society discussion are included.

For schools

  • The batch notes describe the lesson as free. The page does not state a price.
  • The lesson needs a printed plan and activity ideas drawn from a hat.
  • No devices are needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This lesson has no AI. It builds sequencing and algorithm skills. Students write exact instructions and watch a human robot follow them.

The lesson runs on paper and speech, and it describes no data collection. The plan download is a linked Google Doc. The page does not say whether an account is needed, and no privacy policy was read. The internet is used to download the plan. A teacher should check how the Google Doc opens on school devices. A teacher should also set safe rules for tasks, such as opening a door, before pairs begin.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Pull a task from the hat and give a partner word-only instructions.
  2. Follow a partner's instructions exactly as spoken.
  3. Switch roles and compare which job was harder.
  4. Rewrite a set of instructions to remove any unclear step.
  5. Watch an extension video and discuss how robots follow instructions.
How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Give a partner instructions to move across the gym.
  2. Follow spoken steps to complete a movement pattern.
  3. Create a movement routine and write it as an algorithm.
  4. Test a routine and fix any step that fails.
  5. Discuss how teamwork depends on clear directions.

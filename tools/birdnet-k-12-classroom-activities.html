BirdNET K-12 classroom activities

Kid-friendly projects that combine AI, birds, and sound, from listening games to a Raspberry Pi birdhouse.

Overview

The BirdNET K-12 page offers projects that mix AI, birds, and acoustics. They range from simple listening exercises to a low-cost recording station. The DIY Smart Finch birdhouse uses a Raspberry Pi and a small microphone to identify visitors in real time. Ready-to-use activities for grades 1 to 12 sit on the Schlaumeise platform, with SmartFinch as the English counterpart.

Benefits

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is the BirdNET model, which classifies bird sounds. In the birdhouse project it runs on the Raspberry Pi. Other activities may use other tools. The vendor does not give every detail. Students collect sound, read the AI suggestions, and use them to answer research questions.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. They do not say where student data goes. The live citizen science activity contributes recordings to the global BirdNET database. A Raspberry Pi station can run identification locally but the research did not confirm that. A teacher should check what the citizen science upload shares and keep a microphone away from student voices.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Listen to bird calls and sort them by pattern.
  2. Compare calls recorded near a road and near a park to study noise pollution.
  3. Identify the local species heard each week.
  4. Add observations to a citizen science database.
  5. Write a question about bird behavior and plan a test.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build the Smart Finch birdhouse with a Raspberry Pi and microphone.
  2. Design a weather cover for the microphone.
  3. Test where in the yard the station picks up the clearest sound.
  4. Record the number of detections over a week.
  5. Suggest one change that would make the station work better.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Explain in simple words how the model decides a species.
  2. Look at the list of detections and spot a likely error.
  3. Compare the AI result with an expert's field guide.
  4. Test the model with a sound it has not heard before.
  5. Discuss how noise changes the model's confidence.

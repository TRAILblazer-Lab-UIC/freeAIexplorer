MIT App Inventor

A block-based web tool for building Android and iOS apps, with AI extensions.

AIComputingGood for AI beginners

Overview

MIT App Inventor is a block-based web environment for building Android and iOS apps. Students test on a connected phone with a companion app or in an emulator. Projects are stored on MIT servers. The site lists AI tutorials and extensions, including image classification, a trainable image classifier, face filters, and ChatGPT, DALL-E, and Gemini projects.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build apps they can run on a real phone.
  • Students try AI features such as image classification.
  • Students work with blocks that snap together.

For teachers

  • A school-oriented site is available at code.appinventor.mit.edu.
  • AI tutorials and extensions are listed.
  • The materials list lessons for AI with MIT App Inventor.

For schools

  • The tool is free and open source.
  • Students need an Android device, an iOS device, or the emulator.
  • IT approval may be needed for emulator installs or Google sign in.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is optional. Extensions offer image classification, face detection, text generation, and image generation. Some run on the phone and some call outside services. Students add an AI block to their own app and use it to respond to a photo or a prompt.

Privacy and safety

Students and teachers need a Google account on the main site, and the internet is always needed. Apps and project data are stored on MIT servers that run on Google App Engine. The site collects activity data, IP address, device details, approximate location, cookies, project data, and the account email. Information may be shared with service providers and research collaborators. Gallery posts are public. The terms do not mention COPPA. They state that code.appinventor.mit.edu, intended for schools, does not collect personally identifiable information, and the research did not fetch that site. The AI tutorials likely send inputs to outside services such as OpenAI and Google, which the research did not confirm. A teacher should read the terms and check which AI extensions send data to a cloud service.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a first app that shows a message when a button is pressed.
  2. Add an image classification block and show what the app sees.
  3. Make an app that speaks the name of an object in a photo.
  4. Test the app on a phone and fix one bug.
  5. Compare two AI extensions and describe what each one does.

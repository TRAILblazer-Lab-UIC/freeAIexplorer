Google AI Edge Gallery

An open source app for trying AI chat, image, and audio models on a phone.

FREEAIStays on the deviceGood for AI beginners

Overview

Google AI Edge Gallery is an open source app for trying machine learning and generative AI models on a phone or computer. It has AI Chat with speech input and read-aloud, Ask Image for camera and photo questions, and Audio Scribe for transcription and translation. Models download from Hugging Face and then run on the device. It suits middle and high school students.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can talk to an AI model by voice or text.
  • Students can point a camera at an object and ask a question about it.
  • Students can transcribe and translate audio.

For teachers

  • The app is easy to install and has a simple menu.
  • Prompt Lab and model benchmarking give teachers a way to compare models.
  • Teachers can show how AI runs without a cloud connection.

For schools

  • The app is free and the code is open source.
  • Model files are about 1 to 3 GB each, according to a third party review.
  • School device management may need to approve the install, and Chromebooks are not supported.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a set of on-device models for chat, image questions, speech, and translation. They run on the phone or computer after a one time download. Students ask questions, review the answers, and check them against other sources. Context windows are short, according to a review.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used to download models from Hugging Face. The README says all model inference happens on the device. A third party review reports no sign in and no telemetry by default, and the research did not confirm that. The research did not review the app store listings or their data declarations. It also did not review content safety for minors. The camera and microphone are used for some features. A teacher should read the store data declarations and check that students point the camera only at class materials.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Download a model and write down its size and how long it took.
  2. Ask the same question in AI Chat and compare answers across models.
  3. Use Ask Image on three objects and note any wrong answers.
  4. Turn on airplane mode and show that the model still works.
  5. Use model benchmarking to compare two models and explain the numbers.
How this can be used for World Languages
  1. Record a short sentence in one language and read the transcript from Audio Scribe.
  2. Translate a classroom phrase and check the result with a teacher.
  3. Use speech input in AI Chat and test how well it hears accents.
  4. Listen to read-aloud and compare it with your own pronunciation.
  5. Write a list of translation errors and explain how to fix them.

ISAT Forward Future self driving car curriculum unit

A middle school STEM unit on self-driving cars with a micro:bit BitCar.

AIRoboticsUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This is a middle school STEM unit on self-driving cars by Jeff Bush at CU Boulder, published July 2024. It covers data collection, training classifiers, neural networks, and the ethics of AI decision-making. Students program a miniature BitCar for line following, obstacle avoidance, and switching between autonomous and human control. The unit runs 12 to 15 classes, typically 3 to 4 weeks.

Benefits

For students

  • Students work in specialist groups and then in mixed expert groups.
  • Students program a real BitCar.
  • Students discuss ethical dilemmas about AI decisions.

For teachers

  • The unit includes launch videos and ethical dilemma discussions.
  • It follows a jigsaw structure with expert groups.
  • A search snippet links the unit to the AI4K12 standards.

For schools

  • The unit price is not stated.
  • Each group needs a BitCar and micro:bit hardware.
  • Over 100 students used the unit at a July 2024 iSAT summer camp. The announcement does not link a download, and the iSAT hub may host it.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI topics are data collection, classifier training, neural networks, and AI ethics. Students train classifiers and program the BitCar, which runs on the device and in the browser. The unit also uses the CoBi AI partner for collaboration. Students use their own data to shape how the car behaves.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. The BitCar runs on the device and in the browser, and the research did not confirm whether the internet is needed. The unit also uses the CoBi AI partner, and its data handling was not read. A teacher should check whether CoBi needs accounts and review what student data it keeps.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Collect sensor data from the BitCar and sort it into two classes.
  2. Train a classifier on the data and test it on new readings.
  3. Compare a simple rule with a trained classifier for the same task.
  4. Discuss who is responsible when a self-driving car makes a poor choice.
  5. Code a switch between autonomous and human control.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Program the BitCar to follow a taped line.
  2. Add obstacle avoidance and test it with a box.
  3. Tune the BitCar's speed to reduce errors on turns.
  4. Design a test course and record each group's results.
  5. Share findings in an expert group and combine them into one design.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Record sensor readings and graph how they change along a track.
  2. Compare how the car senses its path with how a person sees it.
  3. Predict where the car will fail and test the prediction.
  4. Discuss how scientists use data to check a model.
  5. Present the data from a test in a short group report.

Data bias in AI (Digital Technologies Hub)

A lesson on how biased data skews AI, using sketches and a browser neural network.

FREEAIUnpluggedUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This lesson shows how bias appears in AI systems, where it comes from, and how to reduce it. Students first sketch people in five occupations and discuss bias. They then use the browser-based MyComputerBrain neural network tool. Part 1 trains on black shapes on white backgrounds and fails on inverted shapes. Part 2 uses balanced data and succeeds. It suits Years 5 to 8.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see bias appear in their own sketches.
  • Students train a small neural network in the browser.
  • Students retrain with balanced data and see the difference.

For teachers

  • Training takes under 30 seconds in part 1 and 2 to 3 minutes in part 2.
  • The lesson maps to Australian Curriculum codes in Digital Technologies and English.
  • A related Hub lesson, Anti-bullying AI, uses the same tool.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • The training runs in the browser on the student device.
  • The internet is needed only for setup.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a small neural network that trains in the browser on the student device. Students train it on biased data and watch it fail on new shapes. They then retrain with balanced data and compare. The lesson also covers confidence, weights, and random starting values.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state whether an account is needed. The lesson page says the tool runs locally in the browser and shows no sign of a login. The research did not read the MyComputerBrain site or its data policy. The internet is needed to load the tool. A teacher should check the MyComputerBrain terms with IT. A teacher should also handle the occupation sketches with care and discuss stereotypes kindly.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Train the network on black shapes on white backgrounds and test an inverted shape.
  2. Retrain with balanced data and record how the result changes.
  3. Compare confidence scores before and after retraining.
  4. Run the training twice and note what changes with random starting values.
  5. Explain in a short note where the bias came from.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a short paragraph on what the sketches showed about bias.
  2. Compare how two authors describe people in the same job.
  3. Discuss how word choice can add bias to a description.
  4. Rewrite a biased description to be fair.
  5. Present findings in a clear short talk.
How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Sketch people in five occupations and compare them with a partner.
  2. Count how many sketches share the same features.
  3. Redraw one sketch to show a wider range of people.
  4. Make a poster that shows variety in one occupation.
  5. Discuss how pictures can build or break stereotypes.

Monster Mapping

A paper lesson where groups cluster monster cards by hand in the style of k-means.

FREEAIUnpluggedNo device neededGood for AI beginners

Overview

Monster Mapping is a paper lesson from the AI Unplugged collection that introduces clustering. Student groups receive a bank of monster cards and group the monsters by their features. They use string and rulers to find cluster centers in the style of k-means clustering. A challenge task adds extra monsters. Students must then adjust their clusters. The reflection covers real uses of clustering.

Benefits

For students

  • Students find groups in data by hand.
  • Students measure distances to find cluster centers.
  • Students see why diverse data matters for tools such as facial recognition.

For teachers

  • The pack has a facilitator guide, slides, a worksheet, and a textbook page.
  • The guide offers a change for younger students.
  • The guide gives extra support for students who struggle with measuring distances.

For schools

  • The materials are free.
  • Classroom cost is color printing, paper, and string.
  • No devices or accounts are needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson has no running AI program. It teaches unsupervised learning, where a machine finds groups without labels. Students find cluster centers with string and rulers, as k-means does. They then think about training data diversity and about mistakes that clustering can make.

Privacy and safety

The lesson runs on paper, no account is needed, and the materials describe no student data collection. The setup needs internet only for the download. The reflection touches on facial recognition and fairness. A teacher should plan time for that talk.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Group the monster cards by features and describe each group.
  2. Use string and rulers to find the center of each cluster.
  3. Add the extra monsters and adjust your clusters.
  4. Explain to a partner why different features give different groups.
  5. Discuss why a clustering tool needs diverse data to treat people fairly.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Measure the distance from each monster to a cluster center with a ruler.
  2. Move a center to make the total distance smaller.
  3. Make a table of distances for three monsters and two centers.
  4. Compare two sets of clusters and tell which fits the data better.
  5. Find the middle point of a group of monsters on a grid.

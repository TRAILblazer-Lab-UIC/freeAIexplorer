Cornell K-12 Bird Bingo and Merlin activities

A free bird bingo activity with an optional Merlin app to find local birds.

FREEAIUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Bird Bingo is a free Cornell Lab activity for K-12. Students make a 3 by 3 bingo card of birds likely to be seen near school. The teacher can build the list with the Merlin Bird ID app, which has an Explore feature. Students then watch for birds outdoors or at a window, mark them off, and talk about what they saw.

Benefits

For students

  • Students practice careful observation of local birds.
  • Students learn which birds live near their own school.
  • Students share what they saw in a group discussion.

For teachers

  • The activity needs only a bingo card.
  • It works in a yard or at a classroom window.
  • Teachers can build a card by hand or with the Merlin app.

For schools

  • The activity is free. Fuller Cornell curricula can be purchased.
  • Paper cards need no devices.
  • IT has no work to do unless the school wants the Merlin app on a device.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Bird Bingo itself needs only the card and has no AI. The optional Merlin app uses machine learning to help identify birds from photos and sound. A teacher can use Merlin to build the list. Students then check their own sightings against the list and think about how a tool can name a bird.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state what data Merlin collects or where it goes. Merlin needs email verification on first download. Teachers with students under 13 can use a classroom or teacher email. The research did not review Merlin data practices, and the facts come from search snippets. A teacher should check the Merlin privacy terms before students use the app. The paper card works alone with no app or account.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Make a bingo card of local birds and mark each one seen during a ten minute watch.
  2. Record where each bird was seen and describe its habitat.
  3. Compare two bingo cards from different days and note which birds changed.
  4. Sketch one bird in detail and list three features that help identify it.
  5. Discuss why some birds on the card were never seen and suggest reasons.

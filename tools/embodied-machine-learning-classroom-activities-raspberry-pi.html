Embodied machine learning classroom activities (Raspberry Pi Foundation write-up)

Hands-on machine learning activities with drawings and micro:bit movement.

FREEAIUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This Raspberry Pi Foundation blog post turns embodied machine learning research into classroom activities. Activities include ML application prompt cards, a Machine Learning Machine where students draw suns and moons to train a model, and micro:bit ML-Machine gesture activities. Students train on about 20 accelerometer examples to detect moving or still. The post describes work with primary and secondary learners.

Benefits

For students

  • Students train a model with their own bodies and drawings.
  • Students see how training examples shape a model.
  • Students discuss ML ethics with prompt cards.

For teachers

  • Activities range from paper cards to micro:bit gestures.
  • The post links to micro:bit CreateAI and the open source ML-Machine tool.
  • The post is short and the vendor does not give full lesson plans.

For schools

  • The activities are free and the ML-Machine code is open source with an MIT license.
  • Micro:bit boards are needed for the gesture work.
  • The blog says training data stays on the device.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a small model that students train on movement or drawing data. It runs on the device in the browser or on the micro:bit. Students record examples, train the model, and test it with new movements. They use the results to explain how a model learns from data.

Privacy and safety

The blog says the activities collect no data and need no account. It gives no other privacy details and does not say whether the internet is needed. The small models run on the device in the browser or on the micro:bit. The post links to outside tools such as micro:bit CreateAI, and the research did not review how those tools save data. A teacher should open each linked tool, check what it saves and where, and avoid putting student names in project files.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Draw suns and moons to train the Machine Learning Machine and test it on new drawings.
  2. Record about 20 moving and still examples with a micro:bit and train a model.
  3. Add more examples and describe how the accuracy changes.
  4. Use an ML prompt card to discuss who could be harmed by a model.
  5. Build a dance detector from the micro:bit and explain how it works.

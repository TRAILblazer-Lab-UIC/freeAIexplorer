Erase Your Face

An online drawing demo that tests how to avoid face detection.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Erase Your Face is an online demo about facial recognition. It was created by teens and young adults from YR Media and the Stanford d.school K12 Lab. It is a drawing tool that lets users test what it takes to go undetected by a camera. The AI4K12 site lists it as a demo for all ages. The pages read do not explain how it works technically.

Benefits

For students

  • Students test what a face detector can and cannot see.
  • Students talk about privacy and surveillance.
  • Students learn from a project made by young people.

For teachers

  • The demo is quick to try.
  • It starts talks about facial recognition and ethics.
  • The pages offer little teaching help.

For schools

  • The demo is free.
  • It runs in a browser with no install.
  • The host site certificate was expired during research. IT should check it.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a face detector. The vendor does not say where it runs. Students draw or place marks on a face and see whether the detector still finds it. They use the results to ask how facial recognition works and how people might respond.

Privacy and safety

The internet is always needed. The materials do not state whether accounts are needed, whether a webcam is used, or where images and drawings are processed. The host site certificate had expired during the research. The research did not inspect the tool itself. The research also did not confirm that the demo still works. A teacher should open the demo first, check whether it asks for the camera, and confirm that the site loads safely. Students can use drawings or approved images for tests.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Draw marks on a face and record whether the detector still finds it.
  2. Compare two different drawings and note which one hid the face better.
  3. List the features a face detector might look for.
  4. Test the demo with a simple cartoon face and a photo of an object.
  5. Explain why a detector can miss a face in poor light.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Discuss when people might want to avoid being detected by a camera.
  2. List places where facial recognition is used in daily life.
  3. Debate whether people should be able to opt out of face scans.
  4. Write a short note on who benefits and who is at risk.
  5. Research a news story about facial recognition and summarize it.

Day of AI: Why Does This Chatbot Feel Like a Person?

A short Day of AI lesson on why chatbots sound human and how to keep boundaries.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This one lesson Day of AI unit runs 40 to 45 minutes for ages 11 to 18. Students read a scripted conversation between a student named Sam and a chatbot named Echo. They find language that makes Echo seem caring or human and think about how it affects trust. They then rewrite Echo's response to encourage healthy boundaries.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn the language cues that make a chatbot feel like a person.
  • Students think about how trust can grow too fast.
  • Students practice writing a reply that sets a healthy boundary.

For teachers

  • The lesson fits in one short class period.
  • A Google Slides deck, educator guide, and student resources are included.
  • An optional extension uses a summary of an MIT Media Lab study.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • Students can work on paper or in a browser.
  • The lesson uses a scripted chat. No live chatbot is needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson teaches about chatbots and trust. No live AI tool is run. Students study a scripted chat and look for words that make the chatbot seem human. They then rewrite the reply to show how a chatbot can stay helpful and clear about being a machine.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Mark the words in the Sam and Echo chat that make Echo seem caring.
  2. Rewrite one reply from Echo to set a healthy boundary.
  3. Compare the original and the rewrite and explain the changes.
  4. Write a short dialogue where a chatbot says clearly that it is a machine.
  5. Write a paragraph about how word choice builds trust.
How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. List signs that a person is relying too much on a chatbot for support.
  2. Write three healthy boundaries for chatting with an AI.
  3. Name trusted people a student can talk to about feelings.
  4. Role play a talk with a friend who spends too long with a chatbot.
  5. Make a poster on balancing screen time and time with friends.

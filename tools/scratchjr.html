ScratchJr

A free tablet coding app for ages 5 to 7 to make stories with blocks.

FREEComputingLocal with optional cloud or accounts

Overview

ScratchJr is an introductory programming language for children ages 5 to 7. Children snap together graphical blocks to make interactive stories and games. The blocks are icons. Children still learning to read can use it. The app supports pages, customizable characters, and voice recording for narration.

Benefits

For students

  • Young children build stories without needing to read.
  • Students record their own voices for narration.
  • Students create characters and pages of their own.

For teachers

  • Teachers can pair stories with read-alouds or oral instructions.
  • The ScratchJr Connect database holds shared lessons.
  • The learning curve is easy.

For schools

  • The app is free and the code uses a BSD-3-Clause license.
  • It runs on iPad, Android, and Amazon devices.
  • School device management may need to approve the app.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

ScratchJr has no AI. Children snap together icon blocks in a set order. The app runs the blocks in that order on the tablet. They build sequencing skills as they decide what happens first, next, and last.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The discovery notes say projects stay on the device and sharing is by exported files. The research could not read the official privacy page to confirm this. The App Store privacy label lists coarse location and product interaction used for analytics. The developer reports that label, and it says the data is not linked to the user. A teacher should read that label and check that the app is current for the school tablets.

Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Make a character move across the screen using a short chain of blocks, following the first lesson in ScratchJr Connect.
  2. Add a second character and make them take turns.
  3. Use a repeat block to make a character hop three times.
  4. Change a background and add a new page.
  5. Show the project to a friend and tell what each block does.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Retell a read-aloud story with characters on a ScratchJr page.
  2. Record a voice narration for each page.
  3. Build a beginning, middle, and end using three pages.
  4. Act out a story and then program the same actions.
  5. Share a story with the class and answer one question.

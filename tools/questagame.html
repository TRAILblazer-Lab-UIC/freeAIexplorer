QuestaGame

A nature mapping game where players log wildlife sightings from their phones.

AI

Overview

QuestaGame is a nature mapping game. Players record wildlife sightings with their phones and earn points. Other users and experts verify the sightings by identifying them. The site says players are moving to a newer app called Spirits of the Realms. Schools can join the BioQuest competition each April and October.

Benefits

For students

  • Students explore nature outdoors and log what they see.
  • Students earn points and expertise levels.
  • Students get their sightings checked by other players and experts.

For teachers

  • Class teams can join the Global Schools BioQuest Competition.
  • A 2019 forum post describes class teams of 25 members. The details may be outdated.
  • Teachers can use it for field work in science.

For schools

  • The vendor does not state a price for the game or the competition.
  • Students need a phone that can submit sightings.
  • A teacher can install the app. Schools join through the Spirits of the Realms app or the P-12 page on guardiansofearth.io.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI. The game has no AI. Verification comes from other users and experts. It builds the skill of recording data carefully, which is the basis for how many AI tools learn from labeled examples.

Privacy and safety

An account is required. The game collects a user name, password, email, an optional sighting location, an optional profile photo and text, and score levels. All of this goes to the vendor. The user name, score, expertise levels, and profile content are visible to other players. Public locations are blurred to a point 5 to 10 km from the true sighting. The policy does not mention children, age limits, or COPPA. The site says players are moving to a newer app. The product may be superseded. A teacher should check the privacy policy and keep locations and profile details off for students.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Record five sightings in the school yard and add notes on each one.
  2. Compare your identifications with the checks from other players and discuss any differences.
  3. Map the sightings of the class and describe where wildlife is most common.
  4. Compete in the BioQuest competition as a team and report the results.
  5. Return to the same spot each week and track how the species change.

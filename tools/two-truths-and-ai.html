Two Truths and AI

A free Common Sense game where students spot the AI made movie poster.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

Two Truths and AI is a free web based digital literacy game from Common Sense Education for grades K to 12. Students see three movie posters. Two are real and one was made or changed by generative AI. They pick the fake and can use hints that remove one option. Each round has 10 sets and ends with a score and an accuracy percentage.

Benefits

For students

  • Students practice looking closely at images.
  • Students get hints at easy, medium, and hard levels.
  • Students see their score and accuracy after each round.

For teachers

  • Teachers can project the game for whole class play.
  • Timed and untimed modes suit different groups.
  • Poster content is matched to grade bands.

For schools

  • The game is free for educators.
  • It runs in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
  • IT involvement is none.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The game has no AI of its own. It shows pictures where generative AI changed or made the poster. It builds the skill of spotting AI generated images. Students learn to look for clues and to ask where an image came from.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the game needs the internet. The Common Sense page says it collects no names, emails, account IDs, student answers, hints, or poster content. It does collect start and end times, grade band, difficulty, round results, and score. It uses an unnamed analytics tool with truncated or anonymized IP logs and keeps data up to 60 days, then only aggregates. The page includes a COPPA disclosure and does not mention FERPA. Play the game first because the posters may not suit every age.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Play a round and list the clues you used to find the fake poster.
  2. Compare your accuracy score with a partner's and discuss the differences.
  3. Find a real poster online and decide how you would check it.
  4. Make a checklist for spotting AI altered images.
  5. Share one tip with the class on checking where an image came from.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Describe how the words and images on a poster try to persuade a viewer.
  2. Write a short note about what made one fake poster look real.
  3. Discuss why visual literacy matters as much as reading.
  4. Compare a real poster with a fake one and name the design choices.
  5. Write a caption that warns readers an image was made with AI.

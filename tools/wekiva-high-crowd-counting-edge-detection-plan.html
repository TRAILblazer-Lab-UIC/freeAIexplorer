Wekiva High crowd counting and edge detection plan

A four day AP Computer Science Principles plan on computer vision and edge detection.

FREEAIUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This four day plan is for AP Computer Science Principles in grades 9 to 12. A Wekiva High School teacher wrote it in the UCF 2018 Research Experience for Teachers. Day 1 introduces computer vision. On days 2 and 3 students count a crowd photo by hand and compare it with UCF crowd counting software. Day 4 teaches edge detection with a paper convolution example.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how a computer finds edges in an image.
  • Students compare their own count with a software count.
  • Students meet vocabulary such as machine learning, neural networks, and boosting.

For teachers

  • A lesson plan and a poster are provided.
  • Slides and a worksheet are described in the plan.
  • The plan uses a Nearpod session.

For schools

  • The plan is free to download.
  • Nearpod plan costs were not checked.
  • The poster says all students at Wekiva High have laptops and some lack home internet.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The plan teaches computer vision, crowd counting, and edge detection by convolution. Students work much of it by hand on paper. The vendor does not say where the crowd counting software runs. Students learn how a machine reads an image and then compare that with their own method.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which accounts students need or whether the internet is needed. The plan uses Nearpod.com for student answers, and Nearpod is a vendor cloud service. Student answers entered there go to that company. The research did not review any privacy policy and could not find where the UCF crowd counting software runs. A teacher should check Nearpod's privacy terms and the crowd counting software before use. A teacher should also use photos without identifiable people.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Count the people in a crowd photo with the grid method and record your total.
  2. Compare your count with the software count and explain the gap.
  3. Work an edge detection example on paper with a convolution grid.
  4. Describe in your own words what computer vision means.
  5. Discuss where counting people by machine could help or harm.

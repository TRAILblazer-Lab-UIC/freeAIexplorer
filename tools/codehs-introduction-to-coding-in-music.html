CodeHS Introduction to Coding in Music

A CodeHS mini-course where students write JavaScript that plays beats and songs.

ComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

This CodeHS mini-course has two modules. Module 1 introduces a JavaScript block environment where students write an 8-count beat of their own. Module 2 adds chords, notes, and visualizations. Students finish with a full-length song and a music visualization. It is listed for grades 6 to 12 at about 15 contact hours.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make music with code.
  • Students need no prior musical training.
  • Students end with a finished song and visualization.

For teachers

  • The course can run alone or beside another CodeHS course.
  • It suits music teachers and computer science teachers.
  • Teachers can follow the course outline.

For schools

  • The notes say it is likely part of a CodeHS school license, which was not confirmed.
  • Students need accounts, and data goes to the vendor.
  • It runs in a browser on Chromebooks, Windows, or Mac.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This course has no AI. It builds skills in sequencing and pattern spotting. Students arrange sounds in order and repeat patterns with code.

Privacy and safety

Student accounts are needed, and the internet is needed at all times. CodeHS holds name and email as the school chooses, coursework, and saved code. The policy says it acts as a school official under FERPA, relies on school consent for users under 13, and does not sell or rent personal information. The research saw the course page only in search snippets. A teacher should confirm what student information the school will share.

How this can be used for Music
  1. Write code for an 8-count beat of your own.
  2. Change one note and describe how the song changes.
  3. Build a chord progression with code.
  4. Compare two beats and explain the rhythm.
  5. Perform a finished song for the class.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Use a loop to repeat a drum pattern.
  2. Create a function for a chorus and call it twice.
  3. Change a variable to speed up a beat.
  4. Debug a song that plays the wrong note.
  5. Build a visualization that reacts to the music.

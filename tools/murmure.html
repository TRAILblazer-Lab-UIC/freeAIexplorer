Murmure

A free speech-to-text app that runs entirely on the user's computer.

AI

Overview

Murmure is a free, open source speech-to-text desktop app. It runs entirely on the user's machine with NVIDIA's Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 model. It supports 25 European languages. Students speak and the app types the text into any text field.

Benefits

For students

  • Students who struggle to type can speak their ideas instead.
  • Students can dictate into any text field on the computer.
  • Students can work in one of 25 European languages.

For teachers

  • The app is simple to use with a spoken dictation shortcut.
  • It works with the writing tools students already have.
  • There is no account to manage.

For schools

  • The app is free, with optional donations.
  • It needs about 2 GB of RAM and 1 GB of disk, and it runs on the CPU.
  • IT must install it on Windows, Linux, or Mac computers.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a speech recognition model that turns spoken words into text. It runs on the local computer and does not need the internet for transcription. Students speak, read the text, and edit it to fit what they meant to say.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the project says it has no telemetry. The website says audio is deleted after transcription and that the last five history entries stay in memory until the app quits. The research found no formal privacy policy. The internet is used at setup. The app has an optional connection to a user's own LLM server, which would receive text. A teacher should leave that connection off.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Dictate a short journal entry and correct the text with a helper.
  2. Practice speaking a sentence clearly and check that the app writes it right.
  3. Dictate answers to a class worksheet into a text document.
  4. Compare the time it takes to type a paragraph and to speak it.
  5. Set up a personal dictation routine for homework with a teacher.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Dictate a first draft of a story and then revise it by typing.
  2. Read a poem aloud and check how the app wrote it.
  3. Dictate a short summary of a book and edit the errors.
  4. Talk through an essay outline and turn the text into paragraphs.
  5. Mark words the app misheard and discuss what made them hard.
How this can be used for World Languages
  1. Speak a short sentence in a European language and read the text the app writes.
  2. Compare spoken pronunciation with the app's text and note the gaps.
  3. Dictate a description of a picture in the target language.
  4. Record a short dialogue with a partner and check the text for errors.
  5. List words the app often misses and practice saying them more clearly.

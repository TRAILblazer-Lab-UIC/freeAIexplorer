Code.org K-5 AI and Data lessons

A five-lesson Code.org elementary unit on how AI learns from training data.

FREEAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

This elementary unit is titled How AI Makes Decisions. An ITU AI for Good page describes it as five lessons for ages 8 to 11. Students learn how AI systems learn from training data. They use Code.org AI tools and join discussions on AI, bias, and daily life. The unit page itself showed only navigation. Lesson detail is limited.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn that AI learns from examples.
  • Students use hands-on Code.org AI tools.
  • Students talk about bias in simple terms.

For teachers

  • The unit has five lessons.
  • A free teacher account is optional.
  • No reviews from other teachers were found.

For schools

  • The unit is free.
  • It runs in a browser on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and tablets.
  • Little IT help is needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a simple classifier that students train with data. It runs in the browser and the vendor's cloud. Students pick training data, see the results, and then talk about how the data shapes decisions. This builds an early sense of how AI makes choices.

Privacy and safety

Optional accounts store progress. Without accounts only an IP address is collected. The data goes to the vendor, and the internet is needed throughout. The policy says some text in elementary tools is screened to block personal data such as emails and phone numbers. It says the nonprofit is not subject to COPPA and treats school accounts as FERPA records. The research did not confirm which tools each lesson uses. A teacher should decide whether students need accounts. A teacher should also read the Code.org privacy terms and the district rules for young children.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Sort pictures into groups and explain the rule used.
  2. Train a simple classifier with examples and test it.
  3. Change the training data and see how the answer changes.
  4. Talk about a time when a computer made a wrong guess.
  5. Draw a picture that shows how AI learns from examples.

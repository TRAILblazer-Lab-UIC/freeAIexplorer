Sweet Learning Computer

A cups and sweets machine that learns to play Hexapawn by removing losing moves.

AIUnplugged

Overview

The cs4fn page presents a learning machine built from cups and sweets that plays Hexapawn. The machine learns from its mistakes. A sweet is removed when a move loses. The build follows the matchbox computers of Donald Michie and Martin Gardner.

Benefits

For students

  • Students watch a machine get better after losing.
  • Students see learning as removing bad choices.
  • Students count positions and moves in a small game.

For teachers

  • The idea is physical and easy to demonstrate.
  • Related classroom versions exist at Manchester, Queensland, MRSEC Wisconsin and Discovere.
  • The official page was not read. Teachers should check the steps first.

For schools

  • The cost is not stated.
  • Cups and sweets or counters are the main supplies.
  • No devices or accounts are needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The machine is a paper version of reinforcement learning and machine learning. It runs by hand with no computer. Students play games against it and remove the losing moves. They then see the machine learn through their own actions.

Privacy and safety

The activity uses cups and sweets on paper. No accounts or devices are involved. The research did not read the official page directly. These details come from search snippets. Check food rules before using real sweets. Counters are a safe option.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Play Hexapawn against the cup machine and record who wins.
  2. Remove a sweet after each losing move and note how play changes.
  3. Explain how the machine learns without a computer.
  4. Predict how many games it needs to play well.
  5. Compare the machine to a person who learns from mistakes.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Count the possible board positions in a small Hexapawn game.
  2. Count the possible moves in one position.
  3. Keep a tally of wins and losses over many games.
  4. Graph the machine's wins as games go on.
  5. Explain why the number of cups grows with the size of the board.

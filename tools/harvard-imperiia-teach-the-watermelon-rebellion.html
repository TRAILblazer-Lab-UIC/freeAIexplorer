Harvard Imperiia Teach the Watermelon Rebellion

A history lesson that uses AI-generated images to study the 1796 Kherson rebellion.

FREEAIUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This lesson covers the 1796 watermelon rebellion in Kherson. It uses AI-generated images as objects of study. Students read visual material critically and tell primary source images from AI-generated ones. The lesson is hosted on Harvard Scalar.

Benefits

For students

  • Students practice looking closely at images.
  • Students learn how an AI image can get history wrong.
  • Students trace likely training data for an AI image.

For teachers

  • Three prompt options let teachers pick the task that fits the class.
  • The galleries are pre-generated. A teacher can lead much of the lesson without a generator.
  • Preparation is one podcast episode and a Who's Who page.

For schools

  • The page is free to read.
  • The cost of any image generator was not stated.
  • No install is needed, and students use a browser.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is an image generator that students use for Prompt A, plus pre-generated galleries of AI images. The generator runs in a vendor's cloud. Students make an image of a historical event, note what the generator refused to do, and find three likely training data images. They use the results to question how accurate the image is.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which accounts or tools students need, and the internet is always needed. The lesson page names no image generator. A search result says the project images were made with DALL-E 3. Prompt A needs an image generator whose terms depend on the tool chosen. Prompts B and C can use the provided galleries only. A teacher should choose the image generator, check its terms for student use, and consider using only the galleries. A teacher should also review any generated image before sharing it.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Compare a primary source image of the rebellion with an AI-generated image.
  2. Write a list of details that the AI image got wrong.
  3. Write research questions about the accuracy of an AI image.
  4. Compare two variation galleries and describe the differences.
  5. Explain why a historian should check where an image came from.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Find three images that may have shaped an AI generated picture.
  2. Write a source note for a primary image and one for an AI image.
  3. Create a checklist for telling real photographs from AI images.
  4. Note what the generator refused to draw and explain a possible reason.
  5. Present a short talk on why image sources matter.
How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Critique an AI-generated image for color, composition, and mood.
  2. Draw your own version of the event and compare it with the AI image.
  3. Describe what the AI got wrong about clothing and setting.
  4. Explain how artists in the past recorded events.
  5. Write an artist statement that says how AI changed the image.

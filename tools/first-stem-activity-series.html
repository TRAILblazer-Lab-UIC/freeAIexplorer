FIRST STEM Activity Series

A free PreK to 12 set of hands-on STEM activities from FIRST.

FREERoboticsUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

FIRST offers a free STEM Activity Series for PreK to 12. The activities use materials found at home or in classrooms. Search results say each activity targets one skill and uses the engineering design process. The same page offers camp guides and Kahoot quizzes.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build and test ideas with everyday materials.
  • Students practice the engineering design process.
  • Students take robotics and computational thinking quizzes in Kahoot.

For teachers

  • Activities need little setup.
  • Camp guides use FIRST LEGO League or FIRST Tech Challenge materials.
  • FIRST claims alignment with CSTA, NGSS, ISTE, and ITEEA in general.

For schools

  • The series is free.
  • Materials are everyday items.
  • No IT help is needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This series has no AI. Students build and test with simple materials. They practice problem solving and testing. These skills support later work with data.

Privacy and safety

The activities use everyday materials and the research found no student data collection. The page does not state whether an account is needed for the series or for Kahoot. Kahoot is a third party service and the research did not review its data practices. The internet is used to open the activities. A teacher should check the Kahoot quizzes against school rules and privacy terms before use. A teacher should also check the safety needs of each activity.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Pick an activity and name the problem it asks students to solve.
  2. Sketch a design before building.
  3. Build the design from everyday materials.
  4. Test the design and record what happened.
  5. Improve the design and test it again.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Predict what will happen in an activity before starting.
  2. Record observations in a simple table.
  3. Compare results from two designs.
  4. Explain one result using a science idea.
  5. Share what you learned with another group.

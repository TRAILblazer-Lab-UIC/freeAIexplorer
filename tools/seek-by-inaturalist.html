Seek by iNaturalist

A free app that identifies plants, animals, and fungi from the phone camera.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Seek is a free mobile app that identifies plants, animals, and fungi from the phone camera. Search results state that the identification model sits on the device and covers roughly 86,000 taxa. Users earn badges and join challenges, which gives it a game-like feel. The App Store listing states Seek does not require registration.

Benefits

For students

  • Students point a camera at a plant or animal and get a name.
  • Students earn badges and join challenges.
  • Students can use it without signing up.

For teachers

  • A UBC teaching resource describes Seek as gamified and suited to younger students.
  • The app has a low learning curve.
  • Optional sign-in is for users 13 and older, or with parental permission.

For schools

  • The app is free.
  • It runs on iOS and Android phones and tablets.
  • The code is MIT licensed. A teacher may need to install the app on school devices.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is an image classification model that matches a camera view to a species. Per search results, it runs on the device. Students point the camera, read the suggestion, and check it against a field guide.

Privacy and safety

No account is required, and sign-in is optional for users over 13 or with parental permission. The official privacy policy could not be read. The App Store label lists precise location and photos or videos as data linked to the user, and the developer reports this label. Signing in shares some user information with iNaturalist. Common Sense Media carries a privacy warning. A teacher should verify the privacy claims before school use and keep sign-in off.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Identify five plants on school grounds and record the names.
  2. Earn a badge by finding a set of species and share what you found.
  3. Compare the app name for a plant with a field guide entry.
  4. Make a class list of the species seen in the school yard.
  5. Photograph the same tree in two seasons and describe the changes.

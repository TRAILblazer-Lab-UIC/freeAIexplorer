AAAI K-2 co-designed unplugged AI activities

A conference paper on screen-free K-2 AI activities that teachers co-designed around literacy.

FREEAIUnpluggedNo device neededGood for AI beginners

Overview

This is a conference paper from the AAAI 2026 EAAI K-12 resources track. It describes a professional development program for K-2 teachers. Teachers co-designed screen-free activities aligned to English Language Arts. The activities use storytelling, sorting, and movement to teach topics such as machine learning and fairness.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn AI ideas through stories, sorting, and play.
  • Students move and talk with no screens.
  • Students link AI topics to their reading and writing time.

For teachers

  • The activities fit existing K-2 literacy routines.
  • The paper shows how other teachers shaped the activities.
  • The work follows the AI4K12 Five Big Ideas.

For schools

  • The paper is free on the AAAI open access server.
  • The activities are described as screen-free.
  • Activity materials were not located. Teachers may need to make their own.

Privacy and safety

The activities are described as screen-free and run on paper in the classroom. No account is needed. The paper itself was not readable. The research did not review the study's student data practices. The individual activities and materials are also unconfirmed. A teacher should read the materials before use and review any picture cards for fairness.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Read a picture book and sort the characters by features the class chooses.
  2. Act out a story in which a helper learns from examples and share how it changed.
  3. Create a class story about a robot that makes a mistake and learns.
  4. Retell a story using picture cards sorted by what happens first and last.
  5. Discuss whether a sorting rule in a story is fair to every character.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Sort a pile of classroom objects into groups and explain the rule.
  2. Play a game where one child guesses the group of a hidden object.
  3. Change the sorting rule and watch how the groups change.
  4. Move to a spot on the floor that matches a rule and talk about who is left out.
  5. Draw a picture of a machine that learns from examples.

Overview

This is a Vancouver Public Schools article on Sphero robots moving into physical education. A search snippet says students use district iPads and an app to code instructions for the Sphero and for classmates to follow. The dance unit idea comes from a separate EdTech Magazine article by Sam Patterson. In that article students program a Sphero as a dance partner using the Tickle app.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The dance detail belongs to the EdTech Magazine author. This lesson has no AI. Students use block coding to order moves. They build sequencing skills.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state whether students need accounts. They do not say what data the app collects. The research found no data handling information. The research did not verify that the robot connects to a tablet app over Bluetooth. A teacher should check the app's privacy details with the school before use. A teacher should also keep the gym floor clear for the rolling robot.

How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Follow a classmate's coded instructions as a movement pattern.
  2. Create a short dance with a Sphero as a partner.
  3. Match your steps to the robot's turns and stops.
  4. Teach the dance to another group.
  5. Perform the dance for the class and describe how the robot helped.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Use blocks to make the Sphero roll in a line.
  2. Add a turn and a pause to the program.
  3. Write instructions for a classmate to follow.
  4. Test the code and fix any step that goes wrong.
  5. Combine three moves into a dance sequence.

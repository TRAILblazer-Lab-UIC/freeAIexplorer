Circuit Jam

An Android puzzle game where students modify circuits to match signals.

FREEComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Circuit Jam is a mobile puzzle game about electronic circuits. Students modify circuits to produce target signal shapes. The puzzle groups cover Current, Voltage, Resistor, Ohm's Law, and Capacitor. Solving puzzles unlocks components for a sandbox mode where students build and test their own circuits.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn circuit ideas through puzzles.
  • Students unlock components as they solve puzzles.
  • Students build their own circuits in sandbox mode.

For teachers

  • Puzzles unlock in sequence.
  • Common Sense Education lists grades 4-12.
  • Common Sense Media recommends it for ages 10 and up.

For schools

  • The first puzzle set is free, and the rest unlock with a one-time in-app purchase, per Common Sense Media.
  • It runs on Android phones and tablets.
  • A teacher may need to install it on each device.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Circuit Jam has no AI. It builds the skills of logic and cause and effect, as students change a circuit and see the signal change. Students also practice problem solving.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, per the review. The materials do not say what data the app collects or whether it needs the internet. Common Sense Media sources conflict. One page refers to the publisher's privacy policy and shows no rating. A search snippet says Common Sense rates privacy as Fail since the app has no privacy policy. The research did not settle this. A teacher should check the app permissions and the privacy terms on Google Play.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Solve the Current puzzles and describe what current is, as in the first group.
  2. Change a resistor and predict the signal.
  3. Use Ohm's Law to explain a puzzle answer.
  4. Compare the effect of a capacitor with a resistor.
  5. Write a short summary of voltage in your own words.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build a circuit in sandbox mode to match a target.
  2. Test a design by changing one component.
  3. Record the change and the effect.
  4. Design a circuit for a simple task.
  5. Explain a design to a partner.

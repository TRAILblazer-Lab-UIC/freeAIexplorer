Playful NLP activities for middle school (embeddings card game)

Three playful activities on word embeddings, language models, and chatbots for ages 11 to 14.

FREEAIUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This AAAI 2026 paper describes three language activities for ages 11 to 14. They connect to the AI4K12 Natural Interaction big idea. One is an unplugged card game on embeddings and similarity. Another is a team sentence-building challenge that shows how language models work. The third is a web game where students design chatbots.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how computers compare word meanings with cards.
  • Students build sentences together and see how a language model guesses.
  • Students design chatbots in a web game.

For teachers

  • Two activities are unplugged and need printed materials.
  • The authors added scaffolding after early classroom runs.
  • The activities were refined across teacher professional development workshops.

For schools

  • The authors say the activities are openly available.
  • The card games need no devices.
  • The chatbot game needs a browser. The hosting and data flow are unknown.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Play the embeddings card game and explain why two cards count as similar.
  2. Sort word cards by meaning and compare your groups with a partner.
  3. Take part in the sentence generation challenge and describe how each guess was made.
  4. Design a chatbot in the web game and test it with a classmate.
  5. Explain in a short talk how a language model chooses the next word.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Build a sentence one word at a time with a team and read the result aloud.
  2. Pick words that are close in meaning and write a sentence for each.
  3. Write chatbot replies for three different questions.
  4. Compare a chatbot reply with a human reply to the same question.
  5. Revise a chatbot script to sound more friendly and describe the changes.

mBlock Blockly app

A free iPad app with game-style block levels that lead to mBot robots.

ComputingRobotics

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This is a free iOS and iPadOS app that teaches robot programming through game-style block coding levels. After the levels, children customize mBot robots. The App Store listing shows version 0.8.9 dated 21 March 2022. It may be stale. It lists a Work without Internet connection feature, and new course content downloads when online.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn blocks through game-style levels.
  • Students customize an mBot after the levels.
  • Students can work offline.

For teachers

  • The app installs from the App Store.
  • The teacher learning curve is easy.
  • The levels guide students step by step.

For schools

  • The app is free.
  • An mBot robot is sold separately.
  • The app is not available on Chromebooks.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The app has no AI. The levels teach block coding, and the robot follows the code. Students build skills in sequencing and logic.

Privacy and safety

The App Store privacy label says the developer does not collect any data from this app. That is the developer's own report. The research did not confirm whether an account is needed. The internet is used to download new course content, and the app works offline after that. The listing shows a last update in 2022. A teacher should read the App Store privacy label and check whether the app still works on current iPads.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Play the first block levels and describe what each block does.
  2. Use a repeat block to solve a level with fewer blocks.
  3. Fix a level that fails and explain the change.
  4. Compare two solutions to the same level.
  5. Teach a partner how to finish a level.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Customize an mBot after the levels and describe the changes.
  2. Test an mBot on a taped path.
  3. Sketch an add-on for the mBot.
  4. Compare two mBot designs.
  5. Present the robot to the class.

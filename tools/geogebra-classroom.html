GeoGebra Classroom

A teacher dashboard for assigning GeoGebra math activities and watching progress live.

Computing

Overview

GeoGebra Classroom lets teachers assign interactive math activities and watch student progress live. Teachers can ask the whole class a question and view all responses at once. Students join with a code and work in the browser on the GeoGebra tools. It suits middle and high school math.

Benefits

For students

  • Students work on interactive tasks in the browser.
  • Students join with a code, which is quick.
  • Students answer questions that the whole class can discuss.

For teachers

  • Teachers see which tasks students have started.
  • Teachers can hide student names when showing responses.
  • Teachers can add co-teachers and lead discussion with the whole class, groups, or individuals.

For schools

  • The platform is listed as free. The official page did not state a price.
  • It works for in-person, online, and hybrid teaching.
  • It needs only a device with a browser and an internet connection.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

GeoGebra Classroom has no AI. It builds math reasoning skills. Students explain their answers and compare them with classmates. It runs in the vendor's cloud.

Privacy and safety

The service runs in the vendor's cloud and needs the internet at all times. Teachers create accounts, and students can join a live lesson with a code. Student work is saved only when an account exists. Registration needs a username and email, and automatic data includes IP address, browser, and feature use. The policy says GeoGebra does not collect personal data from children under 13 without permission from a parent or school. It says parents handle account decisions for students under 14. The policy lists advertising and analytics third parties. A teacher should read the policy and ask the school about student accounts before use.

How this can be used for Math
  1. Join a class with a code and answer a live question on a graph.
  2. Compare class responses to one task and explain the differences.
  3. Work in a small group on a geometry activity and share one finding.
  4. Review a classmate's response with the names hidden.
  5. Finish a task and write what the teacher dashboard might show about it.

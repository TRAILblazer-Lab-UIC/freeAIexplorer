Anthropic AI Fluency for PK-12 Educators

A free online course that teaches educators a four-part framework for working with AI.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

This is a self-paced professional development course made by Anthropic and Teach For America. It teaches the 4D AI Fluency Framework, which covers Delegation, Description, Discernment, and Diligence. It is built for PK-12 educators and needs no earlier AI experience. Teachers take it on their own and bring the ideas back to their classrooms.

Benefits

For students

  • Students gain from a teacher who has a clear way to judge AI answers.
  • Students hear a shared vocabulary for asking AI good questions.
  • Students learn that AI output needs checking before anyone trusts it.

For teachers

  • The course starts at a beginner level and needs no AI background.
  • Teachers work at their own pace on their own schedule.
  • The four-part framework gives teachers a simple checklist for any AI task.

For schools

  • The course is described as free in the discovery notes and in a Teach For America announcement.
  • Nothing needs to be installed. IT has little to do.
  • A separate Train the Trainer course exists for schools that want to train their own staff.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The course teaches about AI and does not run a tool of its own. Lessons cover generative AI literacy, prompting, evaluating AI output, and responsible use. The course content is hosted in the vendor's cloud. Teachers use the framework to extend their own planning and to guide students in checking AI answers.

Privacy and safety

This is a teacher course and students do not use it directly. A learner account on Skilljar or Coursera is needed and the course needs the internet at all times. The research did not review the platform privacy policies. Course examples use Claude.ai. Student data rules apply when teachers try the tools with students. Whether Claude.ai access is needed for the exercises is unconfirmed. A teacher should check the platform privacy terms before signing up with a school email.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Use the Discernment step to check an AI answer about a local history topic against two library sources.
  2. Write a short guide for students that explains the four Ds in plain words.
  3. Compare a vague prompt with a clear prompt and discuss how the answers differ.
  4. Run a mini lesson where students mark what an AI answer got right and wrong.
  5. Build a bookmark or poster that reminds students to stay diligent when they use AI.

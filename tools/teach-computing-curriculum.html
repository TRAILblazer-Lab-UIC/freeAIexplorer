Teach Computing Curriculum

A free computing curriculum for ages 5 to 16 with lesson plans and slides.

AIComputing

Overview

The Teach Computing Curriculum was created by the Raspberry Pi Foundation for England's National Centre for Computing Education. It covers key stages 1 to 4, which are ages 5 to 16. Each unit includes lesson plans, slides, activity sheets, homework, and assessments. Teachers adapt the units to their own learners.

Benefits

For students

  • Students move through a clear path from early years to age 16.
  • Many lessons are unplugged. Students learn ideas away from the screen.
  • Units include a Year 8 lesson on machine learning.

For teachers

  • Each unit comes with lesson plans, slides, and assessments.
  • Curriculum journey maps show how units connect.
  • Teacher guides explain each key stage.

For schools

  • The curriculum is free.
  • Most lessons collect no student data.
  • Some units need devices or software. IT may need to help.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The curriculum is mostly general computing. It includes one machine learning unit in Year 8 called Thinking machines. In that unit students train a model and discuss how it works. Other units build skills such as programming, data, and logic.

Privacy and safety

Most lessons collect no student data. Teachers may need a free STEM login to download, but the research did not test the account rules. Some lessons run in the browser or on the device, and the research did not read the privacy page. Student data depends on the tools each unit uses, including the Year 8 machine learning unit. Check each outside tool and its privacy terms before students use it.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Work through a key stage unit and keep a log of what each lesson teaches.
  2. Complete an unplugged activity and explain the idea to a partner.
  3. Use the activity sheets to practice an algorithm on paper.
  4. Take the unit assessment and list the skills still to practice.
  5. Complete the Year 8 Thinking machines lesson and describe how a model learns.

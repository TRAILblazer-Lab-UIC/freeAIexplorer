MediaSmarts recommendation engine unplugged game

A card game that shows how recommendation algorithms use personal information.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This appears to be the #ForYou card game from MediaSmarts. The research team could not confirm the source with certainty. It is a card based pattern matching game with three phases named Popularity, Advertising, and Machine Learning. Each phase shows a different way that recommendation algorithms work. It is designed for ages 13 to 18, and a lesson plan exists for grades 9 to 12.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see how recommendations are built from popularity, ads, and personal data.
  • Students play a game that makes hidden systems visible.
  • Students think about how their own data shapes what they see.

For teachers

  • Printable cards, tokens, quick play rules, and a discussion guide are available.
  • A lesson plan supports grades 9 to 12.
  • A limited online version exists on Playingcards.io.

For schools

  • The printable downloads are free.
  • The page lists no price for ordered cards.
  • The paper game needs no devices.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The game has no running AI program. It models how recommendation algorithms choose what to show a person. The three phases show popularity, advertising, and machine learning. Students play each phase and then talk about how personal data feeds the system.

Privacy and safety

The paper game needs no account and no devices. The materials describe no student data collection. The optional online play on Playingcards.io is a third party platform, and the research did not review its data practices. The research read no privacy policy for this item. A teacher should check the Playingcards.io site before students use it. A teacher should also prepare for talk about personal data.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Play the Popularity round and explain why some items rise to the top.
  2. Play the Advertising round and tell how ads change what you see.
  3. Play the Machine Learning round and describe what data the system used.
  4. List the personal information that a feed might collect.
  5. Write a short guide that helps friends take control of their recommendations.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Match each game phase to a way a recommendation algorithm works.
  2. Predict the next card a recommendation system would show and test the guess.
  3. Explain to a partner how a pattern matching rule picks items.
  4. Change one rule and see how the recommendations change.
  5. Design a simple recommendation rule for a class library.

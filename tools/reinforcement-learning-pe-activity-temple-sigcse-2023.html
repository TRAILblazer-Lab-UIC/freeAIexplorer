Reinforcement learning PE activity (Temple SIGCSE 2023)

A physical education game where students act as spy agents that learn from rewards.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This is a one-page SIGCSE 2023 poster paper by Annaluru, Julien and Payton. Elementary students act as spy agents in a PE class. They move between cone-marked stations, choose moves, and earn points. Students then revise their sequences to earn the maximum reward.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn reinforcement learning by moving their bodies.
  • Students see how the order of moves changes the total points.
  • Students practice revising a plan to get a better result.

For teachers

  • The setup needs only cones and a point list.
  • The authors align the activity to the K-12 CS Framework, AI4K12 and ISTE.
  • The paper is a poster abstract. Teachers design the stations themselves.

For schools

  • The paper is free on NSF PAR.
  • No devices, accounts or internet are needed during the activity.
  • Cones are the only equipment named.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The activity has no computer AI. Students act as reinforcement learning agents. The paper says the rewards are deterministic but not always greedy. Students build an understanding of how a learner improves by trying choices and seeing rewards.

Privacy and safety

The activity is on paper and in the gym. No student data is collected and no accounts are needed. A teacher should check the space for safe movement. A teacher should also plan how to include students of all abilities in the moves.

How this can be used for Health and PE
  1. Move between cone stations as a spy agent and collect points for each move.
  2. Try two different move orders and compare the total points.
  3. Design a new set of moves with point values for a partner to test.
  4. Race a teammate to find the best path and explain the choice.
  5. Cool down by describing which moves felt most rewarding and why.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Draw a diagram of the stations and the moves between them.
  2. Explain what a reward is and how it guides the next choice.
  3. Compare a greedy choice with a choice that earns more later.
  4. Write the best sequence as a numbered list of steps.
  5. Predict how the score changes when one point value is changed.

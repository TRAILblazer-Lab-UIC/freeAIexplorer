Day of AI Grade 2: Data All Around Us

A Grade 2 unit on building datasets and asking whether they are fair.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This Grade 2 unit for ages 7 to 8 teaches how people collect and organize data and how AI learns from examples. It has three lessons, What Is a Dataset, Is It Fair, and Career Spotlight Digital Animator. They take about three periods of 40 to 45 minutes. Materials are a slide deck, educator guide, student resources, and downloadable resources.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build and sort their own datasets.
  • Students ask who is in a dataset and who is left out.
  • Students meet a digital animator who uses AI.

For teachers

  • A slide deck, with a Google Slides copy, and an educator guide support each lesson.
  • The unit has three short lessons.
  • Student resources are included.

For schools

  • The unit is free with a login.
  • A Day of AI login or registration is needed to view all resources.
  • The unit does not list required devices.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The unit has no software and no AI runs on any device. It teaches that AI learns from examples in a dataset. Students build skills in sorting data and in checking a dataset for fairness.

Privacy and safety

A teacher account is needed to view all resources. The unit page does not specify devices or student accounts. The research did not read the Day of AI privacy policy. The data it collects from teachers is unknown, and the research found no student data collected in the unit. A teacher should register with a work email and check the privacy terms on the site.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a dataset of classroom objects and sort it into groups.
  2. Look at a dataset and say who or what is missing.
  3. Add examples to make the dataset fairer.
  4. Explain to a partner how a machine could learn from your dataset.
  5. Draw how an animator might use AI in a film.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Sort a set of objects by size and count each group.
  2. Make a tally chart of a class survey.
  3. Draw a picture graph from the survey results.
  4. Compare two groups in the data using more and fewer.
  5. Ask a question that the data can answer.

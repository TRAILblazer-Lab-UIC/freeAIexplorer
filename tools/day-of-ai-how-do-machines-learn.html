Day of AI: How Do Machines Learn?

A Day of AI unit on supervised learning with Teachable Machine and bias.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

How Do Machines Learn is a Day of AI unit with versions for ages 8 to 10, 11 to 13, and 14 to 18. The younger versions pair a 60 minute supervised learning lesson using Google Teachable Machine with a Bias in AI lesson. The high school version compares supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning and acts out a neural network. The unit also uses the simulation The Most Likely Machine.

Benefits

For students

  • Students train an image model in Teachable Machine.
  • Students look at bias in AI through case studies and a Bias Detective activity.
  • High school students act out the nodes of a neural network.

For teachers

  • Three age versions let a teacher pick the right level.
  • The slide deck is a Google Slides copy.
  • An optional community problem extension is included.

For schools

  • The unit is free.
  • Students need a browser. A webcam or image files are needed for Teachable Machine.
  • IT should review the Day of AI privacy notice.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Students train a model in Google Teachable Machine. The model trains in the browser on the student's own device. Students add examples, test the model, and then look at where it fails. They use the results to explain how training data shapes what a model can do.

Privacy and safety

A teacher login is needed for the Educator Guide and Student Resources, and the research did not confirm whether students need accounts. Google reports that Teachable Machine examples stay on the device and training happens in the browser. Projects can be saved to Google Drive if a user chooses. The research did not review how Day of AI handles teacher login data, and it did not read the Google privacy policy. A teacher should check the webcam setting and the privacy notices of Day of AI and Google.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Train a Teachable Machine model on two classes and test it on new images.
  2. Add more examples to the weak class and compare the new results.
  3. Complete the Bias Detective activity and list the clues you found.
  4. Act out a small neural network with classmates as nodes.
  5. Compare supervised learning with a guess game and explain the difference in your own words.

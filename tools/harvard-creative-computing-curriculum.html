Harvard Creative Computing Curriculum

A free seven-unit Scratch curriculum built around creative expression and design.

Computing

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This is a free introductory computing curriculum built on Scratch 3.0. It has seven units, from Getting Started through Exploring, Animations, Stories, Games, Diving Deeper, and a Hackathon. It focuses on creative expression and design thinking more than technical detail. It was written by the ScratchEd team led by Karen Brennan.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make animations, stories, and games.
  • Students keep a design journal and take part in critique groups.
  • Students end the course with a showcase.

For teachers

  • Some activities are unplugged, such as Programmed to Dance and Performing Scripts.
  • Debug It challenges teach problem solving.
  • The curriculum guide and workbook are free to download and modify for student use.

For schools

  • The curriculum is free.
  • Scratch runs in the browser and works on Chromebooks.
  • The unplugged activities need printing.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This curriculum has no AI. It builds programming and design skills. Students use sequences, loops, and events to make their own projects. Scratch runs in the browser.

Privacy and safety

Unit 0 has students create a Scratch account, and the guide does not state account needs for other activities. The Scratch Foundation runs Scratch and the research did not review its data practices. The offline editor could avoid accounts, but the guide does not say so. The internet is needed for setup. A teacher should decide whether students make accounts and read the Scratch privacy terms first. Students can also work without sharing online.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Explore Scratch with a first project from Unit 1.
  2. Fix a broken script in a Debug It challenge.
  3. Build a game from Unit 4 with a score.
  4. Perform a script out loud as in the Performing Scripts activity.
  5. Present a hackathon project and explain how it works.
How this can be used for Theater and Media Arts
  1. Animate a character that speaks and moves.
  2. Tell a short story with scenes and sprites.
  3. Design a game and write the story behind it.
  4. Perform a script with classmates as actors.
  5. Share a finished project at the showcase.

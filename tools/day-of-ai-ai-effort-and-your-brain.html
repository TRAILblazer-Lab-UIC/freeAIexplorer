Day of AI: AI, Effort, and Your Brain

A single Day of AI lesson on how AI use affects effort and learning.

AIUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

AI, Effort, and Your Brain is a single Day of AI lesson of about 50 to 60 minutes for ages 11 to 18. It comes with a Google Slides deck, an Educator Guide, and Student Resources. A Day of AI login or registration is needed to see all of the resources. A Traditional Chinese version is linked.

Benefits

For students

  • Students think about how AI use affects their own effort and learning.
  • Students reflect on when AI helps and when it gets in the way.
  • Students practice talking about habits that build skill.

For teachers

  • The lesson comes with slides, a guide, and student resources.
  • It fits in one class period.
  • The slides are a Google Slides copy that a teacher can edit.

For schools

  • The vendor does not state a price on the unit page.
  • Students need a browser. The teacher registers for the resources.
  • IT should review the Day of AI privacy notice.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson teaches about AI and does not run an AI tool. Students look at how using AI can change the effort they put into learning. They use the ideas to decide how they want to use AI for their own work.

Privacy and safety

A Day of AI login or registration is needed to see all of the resources. The Day of AI privacy notice says it collects personal information when you register and does not share it beyond the Day of AI group. The notice does not mention COPPA or FERPA and does not say where data is stored. The materials do not state what student data is collected. The slide deck is in Google Slides, and the research did not read the lesson content. A teacher should read the privacy notice and decide whether students log in or the teacher shares the slides.

How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Write a short reflection on a time AI made a task easier and what you learned or missed.
  2. List three tasks where effort matters for learning and explain why.
  3. Interview a classmate about how they use AI for homework and share the results.
  4. Create a poster with tips for using AI without skipping the thinking.
  5. Compare a task done with AI and without it and note the differences.

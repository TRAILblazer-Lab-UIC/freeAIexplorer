Google Sheets

A cloud spreadsheet for collecting, sorting, and charting class data.

FREEAIComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

Google Sheets is a cloud spreadsheet and a core service in Google Workspace for Education. Students and teachers use it for classroom data analysis. CODAP can import a Google Sheet by URL or from a CSV download. Admins of education editions may turn on Gemini AI features.

Benefits

For students

  • Students enter, sort, and chart data in a browser.
  • Students can work on the same sheet at the same time.
  • Students can move data into CODAP for deeper analysis.

For teachers

  • Teachers share sheets through Drive or Classroom.
  • Sheets works on Chromebooks and in any browser.
  • Only the first tab imports into CODAP, and a CSV download is the reliable path.

For schools

  • Sheets is free with Workspace for Education.
  • The Google AI Pro for Education add-on is sold separately.
  • An admin must enroll the school and create student accounts.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is optional. Gemini in Workspace may be available, depending on the edition and admin settings. It runs in Google's cloud. The school admin decides whether students can use it. Students can then use it to explore patterns in their own data.

Privacy and safety

Every student needs a school Google account and the internet at all times. Spreadsheet content and account data are processed under the school's agreement with Google. The education terms say Google will not process customer data for advertising. They say Google is a School Official under FERPA and the school gives COPPA consent for users under 13. They also require parental consent for users under 18 of Additional Products. The research read only part of the terms and did not read Google's separate Workspace for Education privacy notice. A teacher should check the sharing settings and ask the admin about Gemini before students use it.

How this can be used for Math
  1. Collect class survey data and find the mean and median.
  2. Make a chart that compares two sets of numbers.
  3. Write a formula to total a column and check it by hand.
  4. Sort a list and describe the pattern you see.
  5. Export a CSV and open it in CODAP to look for trends.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Record daily temperatures and graph the change over a week.
  2. Collect plant growth measurements and compare groups.
  3. Share one sheet so each group adds its own lab results.
  4. Calculate an average from several trials.
  5. Import experiment data into CODAP and describe what the graph shows.

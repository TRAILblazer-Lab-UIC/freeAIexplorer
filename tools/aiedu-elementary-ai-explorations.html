aiEDU Elementary AI Explorations

Short hands-on AI activities for grades 3 to 5 with teacher guides.

FREEAIUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Elementary AI Explorations are short hands-on activities for grades 3 to 5 from aiEDU. They introduce foundational AI ideas. The page states that students and teachers need no prior AI knowledge. Search results describe them as 15 to 20 minute warmups with teacher guides.

Benefits

For students

  • Students try hands-on activities that fit a short class slot.
  • Students meet foundational AI ideas with no background needed.
  • Students build early AI literacy in a fun way.

For teachers

  • Teacher guides come with each exploration.
  • Teachers need no prior AI knowledge.
  • Browsing is open and a teacher can preview before choosing.

For schools

  • The activities are free to browse.
  • A free account is needed only to download materials.
  • The page does not state a price for materials.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The explorations teach AI literacy, and the vendor does not say where any AI runs. One sample exploration is about a blind spot in AI. Students try an activity and talk about what AI can and cannot see. The activities build the skill of spotting what AI misses.

Privacy and safety

Browsing needs no account and a free account is needed to download materials. The research did not read the aiEDU privacy terms. The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. Devices and student data use are unconfirmed because no exploration page was opened. A teacher should read each exploration to see whether students use a device or sign in. A teacher should also check the aiEDU privacy terms before registering.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Try the sample exploration about a blind spot and name what the AI missed.
  2. Sort objects into groups and talk about how a computer might sort them.
  3. Describe one thing a person sees that an AI might not.
  4. Draw a picture of an AI helper and explain what it can do.
  5. Share one new AI word with the class and explain it in plain language.

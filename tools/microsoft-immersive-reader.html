Microsoft Immersive Reader

A reading support tool built into Microsoft 365 apps and Edge.

FREEAISends student data to a company

Overview

Immersive Reader is a reading support tool built into Microsoft 365 apps such as Word, OneNote, Outlook, Teams, Forms, Edge, and Minecraft Education. It offers read aloud, line focus, a picture dictionary, parts of speech highlighting, translation, and syllable splitting. Microsoft says it supports new readers, language learners, and people with learning differences such as dyslexia. Students turn it on inside the apps they already use.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can hear text read aloud.
  • Students can split words into syllables and see a picture dictionary.
  • Students can translate text in real time.

For teachers

  • Teachers turn it on inside apps they already use.
  • It supports many learners with one tool.
  • Teachers can show students how to adjust the view.

For schools

  • The tool is free inside Microsoft 365 apps, per the education page.
  • The Azure developer service has separate billing that was not checked.
  • A Microsoft 365 school account is typically needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI includes text to speech, translation, and text analysis. It runs on Microsoft's cloud. Students listen, translate, and highlight text, and then use that support to read and write for themselves.

Privacy and safety

A Microsoft account is required in the research, though it was not confirmed for each host app. Text sent to read aloud or translate goes to Microsoft's cloud. Microsoft docs say the service processes content in a backend and does not store customer data. The education product page does not cover accounts, privacy, or offline use. The research did not confirm whether the internet is always needed. A teacher should check the school's Microsoft 365 privacy terms and who administers student accounts.

Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Listen to a passage read aloud and follow along with the highlighted words.
  2. Use syllable splitting to read a long word and say it aloud.
  3. Use line focus to read one line at a time and note what helps.
  4. Look up a word in the picture dictionary and draw it.
  5. Choose the settings that work best and share them with a teacher.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Highlight nouns, verbs, and adjectives in a paragraph.
  2. Read a passage aloud with the tool and then summarize it.
  3. Compare reading silently with reading while listening.
  4. Use the dictionary to learn three new words and use each in a sentence.
  5. Revise a sentence after hearing it read back.
How this can be used for World Languages
  1. Translate a short passage and compare it with your own translation.
  2. Listen to a text read aloud in the target language.
  3. Use the picture dictionary to learn new vocabulary.
  4. Translate a classmate's sentence and check the meaning.
  5. List words that the translation handled well or poorly.

Hexapawn matchbox robot inquiry plan (DiscoverE)

A half-day plan where students train a container robot to win at hexapawn.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This DiscoverE inquiry plan is titled AI Machine Learning (Hexapawn). Students train a container-based robot to beat them at hexapawn. The robot is 24 small containers, one per board position, with colored markers that choose its move. After a robot loss, students remove the marker for the losing move. The page lists grades 6-8 and 9-12 and a half-day duration.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build and train a learning machine with their own hands.
  • Students discuss what the game shows about AI.
  • Students evaluate and redesign the robot.

For teachers

  • The plan has a student handout on how to play.
  • The steps run from play to discussion to redesign.
  • The site says all its content is free.

For schools

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The plan has no running AI program. The container robot shows reinforcement learning, in which a machine learns from consequences. Students remove the marker for each losing move. The robot avoids it later. They then evaluate how well it learned and redesign it.

Privacy and safety

The plan runs on paper and containers, and no account is needed. The internet is used to open the plan. The materials describe no student data collection and the research did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should check the rights notice on the site. A teacher should also supervise the small parts used as markers.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build the board and the 24 containers from the handout.
  2. Test the robot over many games and record how often it wins.
  3. Redesign one part of the robot to make training faster.
  4. Compare two robot designs and tell which learned better.
  5. Write a short report on what you changed and why.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Train the robot by removing the marker for each losing move.
  2. Explain how the robot uses past losses to choose moves.
  3. Discuss what the game shows about how AI learns from consequences.
  4. Predict how many games it takes for the robot to stop losing.
  5. Describe a real task where a machine could learn this way.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Count the board positions that the 24 containers cover.
  2. Chart the robot's wins over each set of five games.
  3. Find the chance that the robot picks a certain move from a container.
  4. Work out how the chance changes after a marker is removed.
  5. Draw the possible first moves for the robot.

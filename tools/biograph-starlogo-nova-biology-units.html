BioGraph (StarLogo Nova biology units)

Five high school biology units built on StarLogo Nova models.

Computing

Overview

BioGraph is a high school biology project from the MIT Scheller Teacher Education Program and Penn GSE. It adds ideas such as randomness, self-organization, and emergence. Five resources cover a pond ecosystem, gene regulation, sugar transport, evolution, and enzymes. Each has teacher materials, student materials, and a StarLogo Nova model that students run and explore.

Benefits

For students

  • Students watch a model change as they adjust its settings.
  • Students see how simple rules can lead to large patterns.
  • Students work with blocks and type no code.

For teachers

  • Each unit comes with teacher materials and student materials.
  • The five topics fit common high school biology courses.
  • The models give teachers a way to show processes that are hard to see.

For schools

  • The materials are free.
  • The models run in a browser and need no software install.
  • The internet is needed all the time, and student data goes to the vendor cloud.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

BioGraph has no AI. It builds the skill of modeling, as students study how a system changes over time. It also builds data skills as they compare results from many runs.

Privacy and safety

The models run through StarLogo Nova on slnova.org and the internet is needed throughout. The materials do not say whether accounts are required or what student data is collected. Teacher and student materials are on Google Drive. A teacher should check the StarLogo Nova account and privacy terms before assigning work.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Run the pond ecosystem model and record how the populations change over time.
  2. Change one setting in the gene regulation model and predict the result before running it.
  3. Trace sugar through the sugar transport model and draw the path.
  4. Explore the evolution model Something's Fishy and explain which traits spread.
  5. Use the enzyme model Chew on This to test how a change affects the reaction.

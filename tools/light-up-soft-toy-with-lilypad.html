Light up soft toy with LilyPad

A lesson where students sew an LED circuit into a soft toy.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

This lesson sequence is for Years 7 and 8. Students sew a circuit with conductive thread to light LEDs on a plush toy. They plan the circuit on a drawn template before they sew. A later task suggests programming the board with the Arduino IDE.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how a circuit works by making one with their hands.
  • Students plan before they build and then test the result.
  • Students see that computing can mix with textiles and craft.

For teachers

  • The lesson is organized as hook, map, input, construction, demo, and reflection.
  • No programming is needed at first.
  • The lesson links to Australian Digital Technologies codes AC9TDI8P07 and AC9TDI8P10.

For schools

  • The lesson is free, but a LilyPad ProtoSnap kit must be purchased.
  • The kit includes a battery, LEDs, conductive thread, and alligator clips.
  • The optional programming step needs the Arduino IDE installed on a Mac or PC.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This lesson has no AI. Students build a circuit and optionally program a board. The skills are logic and cause and effect. Students learn how a switch and a power source make an output happen.

Privacy and safety

The lesson is done offline on a physical toy, and the page lists no accounts. The internet is needed to download the Arduino IDE for the programming follow-up. The research found no student data collection described. A teacher should check needle safety and make sure the conductive threads do not touch.

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Draw the circuit on the template and mark where the battery and each LED will go.
  2. Sew the circuit with conductive thread and keep the positive and negative paths apart.
  3. Test the toy and write down what to fix if an LED does not light.
  4. Evaluate the finished toy against the design criteria.
  5. Redesign the toy to add one more LED and explain the change.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Describe the circuit as a list of steps that electricity follows.
  2. Install the Arduino IDE and load a simple blink program on the board.
  3. Change the delay in the blink program and observe the result.
  4. Predict what will happen before running each new version of the code.
  5. Write a short reflection on the difference between a sewn circuit and a programmed one.

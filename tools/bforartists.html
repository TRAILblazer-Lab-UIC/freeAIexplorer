Bforartists

A free Blender fork with a reworked interface made for artists.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

Bforartists is a free fork of Blender with a reworked interface for artists. Every tool has an icon, and workspaces and panels can be customized. It covers modeling, sculpting, texturing, rigging, animation, and rendering. It opens Blender files, and most Blender add-ons work with it.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see an icon for each tool, which helps them find features.
  • Students can model, sculpt, texture, and animate in one program.
  • Students get bundled materials, models, and HDRIs to start with.

For teachers

  • Blender lessons mostly apply, although the layout differs.
  • The stylus friendly interface suits drawing tablets.
  • Teachers can arrange workspaces to show only what a lesson needs.

For schools

  • The software is free.
  • It runs on the device.
  • It needs a computer able to run Blender, ideally with a discrete GPU. IT will likely need to approve the install.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Bforartists has no AI. It builds the skill of spatial thinking, as students build forms in three dimensions. It also builds sequencing through animation keyframes.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the site says no subscription is required. The program runs on the device and the internet is used at setup. The research found no privacy policy or telemetry statement. A teacher should confirm the install source with IT and check that the lab computers have enough graphics power.

How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Model a simple object from the room and add a color.
  2. Sculpt a small creature from a sphere and describe the steps.
  3. Texture a model with one of the bundled materials.
  4. Arrange a still life scene and render it as an image.
  5. Customize a workspace and explain why the layout helps.
How this can be used for Theater and Media Arts
  1. Animate a ball bouncing across a scene.
  2. Render a short animation and watch it as a class.
  3. Build a simple set and light it from two angles.
  4. Rig a simple character and pose it in three frames.
  5. Plan a short scene on paper and recreate its camera view.

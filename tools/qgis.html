QGIS

A free open source mapping program for making and analyzing maps.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

QGIS is a free open source desktop geographic information system. Students load spatial data, make thematic maps, and run spatial analysis. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux. Resources include a user guide, a training manual, and tutorials.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make real maps from real data.
  • Students can install it free at home.
  • Students practice spatial thinking and analysis.

For teachers

  • The user guide, training manual, and tutorials support lesson planning.
  • A Portuguese 12th grade class used QGIS and GPS to map dustbins and benches around the school.
  • Teachers quoted in a TCEA article suggest it for social studies.

For schools

  • The software is free with no license cost.
  • Work stays on the local computer.
  • Managed computers may need IT approval, and the interface is dense for younger students.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

QGIS has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is working with data. Students load layers, join facts to places, and look for patterns. That practice is a base for any later data analysis.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed. The internet is used for setup and for optional online basemaps and plugins. Maps and data stay on the device. No privacy policy was reviewed. A teacher should check any data that students load and which online layers they add. A teacher should also plan extra support for younger students.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Map objects around the school with GPS, as a Portuguese 12th grade class did.
  2. Make a thematic map of population by region.
  3. Compare two maps of the same place from different years.
  4. Plot historic sites and describe how they cluster.
  5. Build a project that tells a short story about a local area.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Map the locations of water samples and color them by result.
  2. Show rainfall or temperature across regions on a thematic map.
  3. Plot where animals or plants live and look for patterns.
  4. Measure the distance between two habitats.
  5. Write a short claim about the pattern on your map and support it with the map.

Arduino Cloud for Education

A teacher managed space in Arduino Cloud for student dashboards and device projects.

ComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

Arduino Cloud for Education is a teacher managed space inside Arduino Cloud. Students connect devices, visualize data, build dashboards and control projects from the cloud. Teachers can collect project reports with photos and videos. The School Plan works with Google Classroom.

Benefits

For students

  • Students connect real devices and see live data.
  • Students build dashboards for their projects.
  • Students submit reports with photos and videos.

For teachers

  • Shared spaces let teachers track class progress.
  • Search results describe Cloud Courses with over 30 lessons and projects.
  • The School Plan integrates with Google Classroom.

For schools

  • The School Plan is paid and sold per member.
  • A 30 day free trial was mentioned in search results.
  • Student data goes to the vendor cloud, and the pricing is unconfirmed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI in this product. Students build skills in data, sensors and dashboards. They connect a device, collect readings and show them in a chart.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are required and work is stored in the vendor cloud. The account details and project data are inferred from the privacy policy and not stated for this product. Arduino says the Web Editor is for users over 14 because of COPPA, according to a search snippet. The policy offers Junior Accounts under 14 with parental consent. It lists sharing with data processors, Qualcomm Group companies, and advertising partners. It says data of people under 18 is not sold or shared. A teacher should read the Arduino privacy policy and the rules for student accounts.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Connect a board to a cloud dashboard and show a sensor value.
  2. Write code that sends a reading to the cloud each minute.
  3. Add a switch on the dashboard to control an output.
  4. Debug a dashboard that shows no data.
  5. Explain how a device sends information to the cloud.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build a device that monitors the temperature in a room.
  2. Add a second sensor and show both on one dashboard.
  3. Control a light from the dashboard.
  4. Test your device for a day and review the data.
  5. Write a report with photos about how the device works.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Collect light data over a school day and graph it.
  2. Compare data from two spots in the school.
  3. Look for a pattern in the data and write a claim.
  4. Predict what the readings will do and check later.
  5. Share the data with the class on a dashboard.

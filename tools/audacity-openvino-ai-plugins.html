Audacity OpenVINO AI plugins

Intel plugins that add local AI audio effects to the Audacity editor.

FREEAIStays on the device

Overview

These plugins from Intel add AI effects to the free Audacity audio editor. Features include music separation, noise suppression, Whisper transcription, music generation, and audio super resolution. The effects run locally through OpenVINO on a CPU, GPU, or NPU. Students can clean audio, transcribe speech, or split a song into stems.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can remove background noise from a recording.
  • Students can split a song into vocals, drums, bass, and other parts.
  • Students can turn speech into a label track of text.

For teachers

  • The tools sit inside an editor that many teachers already know.
  • Teachers can show how AI helps with real audio work.
  • Transcription gives a quick first draft of captions.

For schools

  • The software is free and open source.
  • The project states that the AI runs locally on the computer.
  • Only a Windows version is offered, and lab computers may struggle with the heavy models.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a set of models for source separation, noise suppression, speech transcription, music generation, and audio upscaling. They run on the local computer through OpenVINO. Students pick an effect in Audacity, hear the result, and use it in their own audio projects.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the internet is used at setup. The project states that the AI features run fully on the local PC. The model downloads are separate and the research did not find the exact steps. The Audacity website uses Matomo analytics cookies, according to its notice. The research found no privacy policy for the plugins. A teacher should check that the computer is powerful enough and review any music that students generate.

How this can be used for Music
  1. Separate a song into stems and listen to each part alone.
  2. Remove the vocals and perform a new vocal line.
  3. Clean a noisy recording of a school band practice.
  4. Generate a short music clip and continue it with the tool.
  5. Compare a generated clip with a recorded one and describe the differences.
How this can be used for Theater and Media Arts
  1. Clean the audio of a student interview before editing it.
  2. Transcribe a podcast episode and fix the text by hand.
  3. Add captions to a short class video using the transcript.
  4. Generate background music for a scene and decide where it fits.
  5. Record before and after clips and discuss what noise suppression changes.

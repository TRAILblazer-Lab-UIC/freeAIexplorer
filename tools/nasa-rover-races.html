NASA Rover Races

A role-play activity where students drive a blindfolded classmate as a Mars rover.

FREEComputingRoboticsUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

Rover Races is a role-play activity about driving a robot on Mars from Earth. Rover drivers write and upload commands to a rover played by a team of three blindfolded students. The rover moves around obstacles on a simulated Martian surface. NASA lists the activity for grades 5 to 8 and 9 to 12 with a PDF activity guide and a one-pager.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn why robot commands need to be clear.
  • Students take roles as driver and rover.
  • Students solve a problem in a team of three.

For teachers

  • The activity guide is a free PDF.
  • A one-pager gives a quick view.
  • The activity runs without any technology.

For schools

  • The download is free.
  • A search listing estimated 5 to 10 USD in supplies per group.
  • Supplies are blindfolds, red paper, cones, and floor space.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The activity has no AI. Students write commands that a human rover follows exactly. They build skills in sequencing and precise instructions.

Privacy and safety

The activity uses paper, and the page states no accounts. The research found no student data collection. The internet is used only to download the guide, and the research did not open the PDF. A teacher should plan for the safety of blindfolded students and clear the floor of hazards.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Write a command list that moves the rover around obstacles.
  2. Upload the commands by reading them to the rover once.
  3. Find the bug when the rover bumps an obstacle.
  4. Rewrite the commands with fewer steps.
  5. Trade command lists with another team.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Explain why a rover on Mars cannot be driven in real time.
  2. Map the surface and mark where the rover can travel.
  3. Record how many tries the mission needs.
  4. Compare the activity with how real rovers are driven.
  5. Describe what a rover might look for on Mars.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Plan a route that avoids all the obstacles.
  2. Test the route and note which commands failed.
  3. Improve the command list after each run.
  4. Divide team roles for planning, driving, and checking.
  5. Present the final plan to the class and explain the choices.

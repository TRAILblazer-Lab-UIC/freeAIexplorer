Buzz

A free desktop app that transcribes and translates audio offline using Whisper.

FREEAIStays on the deviceGood for AI beginners

Overview

Buzz is an open source desktop app that transcribes and translates audio on a personal computer. It uses OpenAI's Whisper models. It handles audio and video files, YouTube links, and live microphone input. Transcripts can be searched and exported as TXT, SRT, or VTT. Students can use it to caption recordings and interviews.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can caption a recording within minutes.
  • Students can translate speech into another language.
  • Students can search a transcript for key words.

For teachers

  • Live microphone transcription works for classroom talks.
  • Exports include caption formats for video.
  • The app can identify speakers.

For schools

  • The software is free and open source.
  • Transcription runs on the computer after the model download.
  • The Windows installer is unsigned and may need an IT exception.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is the Whisper speech recognition model. It runs on the local computer and can use a graphics card. Students load audio, read the text it produces, and fix mistakes by hand. They can then export captions for their own videos.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and transcription runs on the computer. The internet is used to download the app and the model files once. YouTube links and the AI summary plugin need the internet, and the research did not examine where their data goes. The research found no privacy policy. A teacher should decide whether students may use those online features. A teacher should also check that students have permission to record anyone who speaks, and review transcripts for errors before sharing.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Caption a short class talk and share the text with a peer.
  2. Compare a live transcript with the spoken words and mark errors.
  3. Export an SRT file and add it to a video.
  4. Test how background noise changes the accuracy.
  5. Create a transcript of a lesson for a student to read later.
How this can be used for World Languages
  1. Record a short speech in the target language and read the transcript.
  2. Translate a transcript into English and mark errors.
  3. Compare the transcript with the script the student read.
  4. Interview a speaker and caption the answers in two languages.
  5. Discuss why accents change the accuracy.

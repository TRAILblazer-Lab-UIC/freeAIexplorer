Stellarium

A free planetarium that shows a realistic 3D night sky on your computer.

Computing

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Stellarium is a free, open source planetarium. It shows the sky as seen by the naked eye, binoculars, or a telescope. It has over 600,000 stars by default, 80,000 deep-sky objects, and constellations from more than 40 cultures. Students find objects, change the date and place, and watch the sky move.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see a realistic sky for any time and place.
  • Students can explore constellations from many cultures.
  • Students can watch eclipses, planets, and comet tails.

For teachers

  • Teachers use it for sky-observation lessons, including a Grade 6 lesson from Science North.
  • Free lessons from Science North, Instructables, and the Smithsonian use Stellarium.
  • The Science North series has a version for classrooms without internet.

For schools

  • The software is free and open source.
  • It needs a 64-bit system, an OpenGL 2.1 graphics card, 1 GiB of RAM, and 1 GiB of disk space.
  • IT will need to install it on each computer. Chromebooks can use the web version.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Stellarium has no AI. It draws the sky from stored star and planet data. Students build observation skills and learn to spot patterns in how objects move.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The desktop app runs locally. The research did not confirm whether optional online features such as location lookup or catalog downloads contact servers. It found no privacy policy page for the desktop app. A teacher should ask IT to check those features and confirm that the installed copy comes from the official site.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Find the Moon and name its phase using the Grade 6 Science North lesson.
  2. Locate the North Star and explain how it helps you find direction.
  3. Speed up time and record how a constellation moves across the night.
  4. Build a 3D constellation model after studying a constellation in the program.
  5. Compare a sky from one culture with the usual star patterns and discuss the stories.

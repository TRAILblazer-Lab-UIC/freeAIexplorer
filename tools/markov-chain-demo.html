Markov Chain Demo

A web demo where students generate text with simple Markov chain language models.

FREEAIComputingUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

The Markov Chain Demo lets students generate text with simple statistical language models called Markov chains. The AI4K12 activity guide notes that large language models such as ChatGPT are also statistical language models, only more complex. It is listed under the AI4K12 Natural Interaction big idea for grades 6 to 12. It was released in November 2024.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see how a model picks the next word.
  • Students connect a simple model to the chatbots they know.
  • Students can try the demo in minutes.

For teachers

  • An activity resource guide supports the lesson.
  • The demo needs only a web page.
  • Teachers can use it to open a talk about language models.

For schools

  • The demo is free.
  • It needs a web browser and the internet.
  • The vendor does not say what data it collects. The tool details need a manual check.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a statistical language model called a Markov chain. The materials do not say where the demo runs. Students use it to generate text and see how each new word follows from the words before it. They can then compare the output with a large language model.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at all times. The page fetch returned only the title. The research did not confirm how the demo works or whether it sends text to a server. The research found no privacy policy. The materials do not state what the page stores. A teacher should check the page for data collection and keep student names out of any text they enter.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Generate text from a short sample and read the result aloud.
  2. Change the model setting and compare the new text with the old one.
  3. Predict the next word in a sentence and compare your guess with the model's.
  4. Explain in your own words how a Markov chain chooses a word.
  5. List two ways a large language model is more complex than a Markov chain.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Feed the demo a paragraph from a book and read the new text it makes.
  2. Compare generated text with a human written paragraph and note the differences.
  3. Find sentences that make sense and sentences that do not.
  4. Write a short story that starts from a generated sentence.
  5. Discuss why the model repeats certain phrases.

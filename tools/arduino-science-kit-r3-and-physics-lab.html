Arduino Science Kit R3 and Physics Lab

A physics kit with a sensor board that links by Bluetooth to a phone app.

ComputingLocal with optional cloud or accounts

Overview

The Arduino Science Kit R3 is a physics kit for middle and high school. It has a storage box, an Arduino Nano RP2040 Connect, the Science Carrier R3 with embedded sensors and parts for the experiments. Students connect the board to the free Arduino Science Journal app over Bluetooth. The Explore Physics course has 15 experiments that can be taught in any order.

Benefits

For students

  • Students run hands-on physics experiments.
  • Students use embedded sensors and see live data.
  • Students follow an amusement park physics theme.

For teachers

  • The 15 experiments can be taught in any order.
  • The course aligns with NGSS, AP Physics, IB Diploma and England Key Stages 3 to 5.
  • The Physics Lab page lists 9 experiments totaling 14 hours.

For schools

  • The product pages show no price.
  • A separate phone or tablet is needed for the app.
  • Four AA batteries are needed and are not included.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The kit has no AI. Students build skills in data and sensors. They collect readings, graph them and explain the physics behind the shape.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the kit page lists no need for the internet. Sensor readings are logged in the Science Journal app on the student device. The kit page states COPPA Safe Harbor certification. The Arduino policy covers the app and lists device type, OS version, and IP address collection. It says YouTube videos in the Physics Lab app are tagged child directed. A teacher should read the policy and decide whether student devices or school devices will run the app.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Run an experiment from the Explore Physics course and record the data.
  2. Graph speed over time for a moving object.
  3. Compare two experiments that test the same idea.
  4. Predict a result and then check it with a sensor.
  5. Write a short report with a graph and a claim.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Place the board in a moving cart and measure acceleration.
  2. Test how a design change alters a sensor reading.
  3. Build a simple ride model and measure it.
  4. Compare your results to a classmate's design.
  5. Suggest one improvement for the experiment setup.

Read Aloud browser extension

A free browser extension that reads web pages aloud with one click.

AI

Overview

Read Aloud is a browser extension for Chrome, Firefox, and Edge. It reads the content of a web page aloud with one click. Users can also highlight text and have the selection read. Standard voices run on the device. Premium voices send text to the developers' cloud.

Benefits

For students

  • Students hear a web page read aloud.
  • Students who find reading hard can follow along by ear.
  • Students can read a selected passage.

For teachers

  • One click starts the reading.
  • Teachers can model listening while reading.
  • The code is MIT licensed and Santa Clara University lists it among open source text to speech tools.

For schools

  • The extension is free. Premium voice pricing is not confirmed.
  • It works on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and Linux in supported browsers.
  • Standard voices keep text on the device. Premium voices need sign-in and send text to the cloud.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a text-to-speech system that turns written words into speech. Standard voices run in the browser on the device. Premium voices run in the developers' cloud. Students choose a voice, listen to a page, and use the audio to understand the text.

Privacy and safety

An account is optional and is needed only for premium voices. The policy says standard voices process text locally and do not save it. Premium voices send text to the developers' cloud servers on Amazon Web Services, which may briefly cache the text and speech. Premium voices collect a name, an email, and the text being read. The extension collects anonymized usage analytics. The research did not confirm whether standard voices need the internet. Some browser voices may be cloud based. A teacher should switch students to a standard voice and test the extension on school devices.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Listen to a short article while following along on screen.
  2. Highlight one paragraph and listen to it twice.
  3. Compare two voices and pick the one that is easiest to follow.
  4. Listen to directions for an assignment and say them back.
  5. Pause the audio after each paragraph and say the main idea.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Listen to a news article and write three questions about it.
  2. Read a poem on a web page, then listen to it and describe the difference.
  3. Listen to your own online draft and mark awkward sentences.
  4. Compare a silent reading and a listening of the same passage.
  5. Listen to a website about a class novel and note new facts.

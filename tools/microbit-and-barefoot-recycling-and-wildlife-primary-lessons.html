micro:bit and Barefoot recycling and wildlife primary lessons

Two primary units that use the micro:bit for litter counting and animation.

Computing

Overview

These are two free primary lesson units from the micro:bit Foundation and Barefoot Computing. Litter hunt is for ages 9 to 11 and teaches variables as students turn a micro:bit into a litter counter. Wildlife animations is for ages 7 to 9 and starts unplugged with paper animation before students program LED icons in MakeCode. Resources are in English and Welsh.

Benefits

For students

  • Students count litter outdoors and learn about recycling.
  • Students draw animations on paper before coding them.
  • Students learn variables and loops.

For teachers

  • Teacher planning guides and student resources are included.
  • Step-by-step code videos help new teachers.
  • Each unit has two lessons.

For schools

  • The lessons are free. Micro:bit boards are a paid purchase.
  • The simulator can stand in for boards.
  • The lessons map to primary computing curricula across the UK.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

These lessons have no AI. Students program simple rules on a board. They build skills in variables, loops, and sequencing.

Privacy and safety

The lessons use a micro:bit and the MakeCode editor. A search snippet from the micro:bit site says micro:bit classroom needs no accounts. The research did not open the individual lesson pages and read no privacy policy for this item. The internet is used for setup. The micro:bit stores data on the board. A teacher should check the MakeCode privacy terms. A teacher should also supervise the outdoor litter hunt and give clear safety rules about handling litter.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Program a micro:bit to add one each time a button is pressed.
  2. Use a variable to keep the litter count.
  3. Draw an animation on paper and plan its frames.
  4. Code a loop that shows LED icons in order.
  5. Share a program and explain how the counter works.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Search outdoors for litter that can be recycled and litter that cannot.
  2. Log each find with the micro:bit counter.
  3. Compare the count of two areas.
  4. Draw a wildlife animation that shows a life cycle.
  5. Discuss how recycling helps the local environment.

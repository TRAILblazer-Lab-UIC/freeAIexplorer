xeno-canto

A community library of bird and wildlife sound recordings used to train bird sound models.

AI

Overview

xeno-canto is a community library of wildlife sound recordings, mostly birds. Bob Planque and Willem-Pier Vellinga launched it in 2005. Wikipedia reports more than 1,000,000 recordings from over 12,900 species. Every recording has a Creative Commons license. Students search recordings by species, place, and type.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can hear birds from around the world.
  • Students see the recordist's name and the place of each recording.
  • Students learn where training audio for bird sound models comes from.

For teachers

  • Teachers can search by species, place, and recording type.
  • Community review checks the identifications.
  • Each recording shows its license for correct credit.

For schools

  • The library is free.
  • It runs in a browser and needs no install.
  • Some recordings are non commercial only. Teachers should read each license.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The site has no AI of its own. It is a source of training audio for sound classification. Models such as BirdNET are trained partly on these recordings. Students build the skill of understanding that AI learns from data that people collected.

Privacy and safety

An account is optional, and the discovery notes say browsing needs none while uploading does. Browsing needs the internet. The official site blocked automated reading. No privacy policy was read, and site level data practices are unconfirmed. A teacher should check the license and the credit rule for each recording. A teacher should also decide whether students need accounts.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Listen to three recordings of one species and note what stays the same.
  2. Compare the song of two similar birds and describe the differences.
  3. Find recordings from two regions and compare their calls.
  4. Match a recording to a bird in a field guide.
  5. Write a short note on how scientists use sound libraries to train models.

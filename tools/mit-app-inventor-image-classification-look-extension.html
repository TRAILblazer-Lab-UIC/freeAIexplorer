MIT App Inventor Image Classification Look extension

An App Inventor extension that identifies objects in photos with a confidence level.

FREEAIComputingUnverifiedGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

The LookExtension is an App Inventor machine learning extension for image classification. Students take photos with a mobile device and the app identifies objects with a confidence level for each result. The unit is rated beginner for grades 6 to 12 and has two 45 minute lessons. The page warns that not every device meets the hardware and software requirements.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build an app that names what the camera sees.
  • Students read confidence levels for each result.
  • Students finish the unit in two class periods.

For teachers

  • A sidebar tutorial, student guide PDF, and video are included.
  • Teacher slides and a teacher guide are included.
  • The unit is rated beginner.

For schools

  • The unit is free.
  • Students need a compatible phone or tablet with a camera.
  • Teachers should check the compatibility list and test unlisted devices.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is an image classifier that the extension runs for the app. The vendor does not say where it runs. Students take photos, see the label and confidence, and then build the result into their own app.

Privacy and safety

An App Inventor account is optional, and the research did not confirm who needs one. The extension page does not describe how photos or other data are handled. A batch note says classification runs on the phone and project storage is on MIT servers when signed in. The research did not confirm this from the pages read. The App Inventor privacy policy URL tried returned a 404. A teacher should check the terms, test the app on a school device, and tell students to photograph objects and not people.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build an app that takes a photo and shows the label it finds.
  2. Photograph five objects and record each confidence level.
  3. Find an object that confuses the model and explain why.
  4. Add a message that changes with the confidence level.
  5. Compare results on a phone and a tablet.

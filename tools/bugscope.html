Bugscope

A free program where classes view insects through a live electron microscope.

FREEComputingSends student data to a company

Overview

Bugscope is a free outreach program run by the Microscopy Suite at the Beckman Institute. Classes mail in insects, then view them under a scanning electron microscope through a live Zoom session in their web browsers. Experts answer questions by video and chat. The program began in 1999 and says it has run almost a thousand sessions with hundreds of schools.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see an insect up close through an electron microscope.
  • Students ask scientists questions live.
  • Students collect real specimens first.

For teachers

  • The session is led with expert help.
  • Teachers can request an optional test session and a recording link.
  • Bugscope can provide a sample if a class cannot collect insects.

For schools

  • The page describes the program as free.
  • It does not say who pays for mailing specimens.
  • Classes need a computer with Zoom and a projector or screens.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Bugscope has no AI. It builds the skill of careful observation, as students study tiny structures. Students also practice asking good questions.

Privacy and safety

No student account is needed. A teacher signs up through an online form, and the class joins a live Zoom session. Sessions may be recorded, and a link is sent afterward. The research found no privacy policy, and Zoom's data handling applies to the live session. The internet is needed throughout. A teacher should check school rules on recordings and student images before the session.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Collect insects from the school yard and describe where each was found, as classes do before a session.
  2. Predict what a body part looks like up close and compare it to the live image.
  3. Write three questions for the scientists before the session.
  4. Sketch an insect from the electron microscope view.
  5. Compare two insects and describe a difference in structure.

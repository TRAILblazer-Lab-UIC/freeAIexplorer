Day of AI: AI Student Senate and Student Senate AI Policy in Schools

A six lesson unit where students act as senators and draft an AI policy.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

Student Senate: AI Policy in Schools is a six lesson Day of AI unit for ages 11 to 18. Students learn about AI and the legislative process and then act as U.S. Senators. They research stakeholder views, debate, draft and revise a policy in committee, and present it. A companion unit, AI Student Senate: Classroom and Community Guides, is listed for ages 12 to 18.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how a policy moves through a legislature.
  • Students research the views of different stakeholders.
  • Students write and defend a real policy proposal.

For teachers

  • The unit has a slide deck that a teacher can copy.
  • It fits civics and social studies classes.
  • The unit runs 6 to 8 class periods of 45 to 60 minutes.

For schools

  • The unit is free.
  • Students need only paper or a word processor.
  • IT has little to do. The unit is based on discussion and writing.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The unit teaches about AI policy and ethics and does not run an AI tool. Students learn how AI affects schools and communities. They use that knowledge to write rules that guide how AI should be used.

Privacy and safety

A Day of AI login or registration is needed to view all resources, and the account is for the teacher. The research did not read a privacy policy, and student data handling is not confirmed. The unit page does not say whether the internet is needed. The unit is based on discussion and writing. A teacher should check which tools students use for drafting and keep any names out of shared documents.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Act as a senator and research how one group views AI in schools.
  2. Debate a draft AI policy in committee and vote on changes.
  3. Compare the steps of a bill in the Senate with the steps in the class simulation.
  4. Present the final policy and answer questions from classmates.
  5. Write a short summary of how stakeholders disagreed and how the group reached a decision.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a policy proposal with a clear purpose and three rules.
  2. Revise the draft after committee feedback and track the changes.
  3. Prepare a short speech that defends the policy.
  4. Write a one page position paper for a stakeholder.
  5. Edit a classmate's proposal for clarity and give two suggestions.

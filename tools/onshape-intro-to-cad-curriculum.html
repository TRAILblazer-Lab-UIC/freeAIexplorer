Onshape Intro to CAD curriculum

Educator resources and learning pathways for teaching CAD in a web browser.

FREEAIRoboticsSends student data to a company

Overview

The Onshape educator resources page links curriculum for introductory CAD classes and an introduction to Onshape for robotics teams. It also links learning pathways, teacher training courses at learn.onshape.com, and a Certified Onshape Associate certification. Onshape is cloud CAD that runs in a browser and needs an account. The page does not state a grade range.

Benefits

For students

  • Students design real 3D parts in a web browser.
  • Students can follow a CAD for Robotics Competitions pathway.
  • Students may earn the Certified Onshape Associate certification.

For teachers

  • Teacher courses such as Teaching a Class in Onshape are listed.
  • Learning pathways give a path through the material.
  • The Education plan is free for students and educators.

For schools

  • The Education plan is free for academic non-commercial use, per a search snippet.
  • It needs only a computer with a modern browser. It works on Chromebooks.
  • Student work is stored in the vendor's cloud. Free plan documents are public by default.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The page lists an AI Advisor feature as optional. It runs in the vendor's cloud. The materials say little about how it works. The main learning in the curriculum is CAD design, which does not use AI.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are needed, and the internet is needed at all times. Student designs are stored in the vendor's cloud. Education plan documents may be public. Each student document needs a privacy check. Search snippets say PTC states the Education Plan follows COPPA and FERPA. The research did not open the privacy notice to confirm that. A teacher should check the privacy settings of every student document and read the education plan privacy notice.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Complete the first Intro to CAD pathway lesson and sketch a simple part.
  2. Design a bracket for a classroom robot.
  3. Compare two designs for the same part and choose one.
  4. Use the CAD for Robotics Competitions pathway to model a robot chassis.
  5. Share a finished design with a partner and ask for feedback.
How this can be used for Career and Technical Education
  1. Practice naming and organizing design files like a professional.
  2. Document a part with a drawing and a short description.
  3. Study the Certified Onshape Associate skills and make a study plan.
  4. Time yourself on a simple design task and aim to improve.
  5. Present a part design to the class like a client meeting.

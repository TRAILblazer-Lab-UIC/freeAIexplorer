News Literacy Project AI or not lesson

A 30 to 60 minute lesson that teaches students to judge AI-generated images.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

This lesson from the News Literacy Project is for grades 6 to 12. It teaches how generative AI affects the information landscape. Students fill out a K-H-W-L chart and take a short quiz of real and AI-generated bird images. They learn tips for spotting AI images and reflect on how AI tools have changed visual evidence.

Benefits

For students

  • Students practice judging images with a bird quiz.
  • Students learn that visual clues are not reliable alone.
  • Students reflect on how AI has changed visual evidence.

For teachers

  • The lesson takes 30 to 60 minutes.
  • The plan may be copied and modified with credit to the News Literacy Project.
  • It cites standards such as CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.CCRA.R.7 and News Literacy Project S4.

For schools

  • The lesson is free.
  • It needs a projector or smartboard and one internet device per group.
  • It runs in a browser and IT involvement is low.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson teaches about generative AI and does not run an AI tool for students. Students look at AI-generated images next to real ones and learn how to question what they see. They use the tips to check images in their own lives.

Privacy and safety

The lesson plan lists no accounts, and the research found no student data collection. The slides and quiz are online, and the internet is needed. A search snippet says some listings ask for a Checkology login, and the research did not open the slides or quiz to confirm. A teacher should preview the bird quiz and slides before class and check whether a login is required.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Fill out the K and W parts of the K-H-W-L chart before the quiz.
  2. Take the bird quiz and write down the clues you used.
  3. Write a short paragraph on why a picture is not always proof.
  4. Practice the tips for spotting AI images with a partner.
  5. Reflect in writing on how AI changes the way we read images.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Compare real and AI bird images and list the differences.
  2. Create a class poster of tips for checking an image.
  3. Trace a viral image back to its source with a partner.
  4. Discuss why visual clues alone are unreliable.
  5. Write a short guide for younger students on checking images.

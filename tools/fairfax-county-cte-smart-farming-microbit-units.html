Fairfax County CTE smart farming micro:bit units

Middle school smart farming units that use micro:bit kits with sensors.

ComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

These middle school career and technical education units come from Fairfax County Public Schools. The Smart Farming 2050 unit starts with a Journey 2050 simulation of seven lessons. Students then use a micro:bit Smart Agriculture Kit with temperature, soil moisture, and water level sensors and servos. A second page covers a Climate Action Kit project programmed in MakeCode with automated watering.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build a smart insect repellent machine.
  • Students build an ecological greenhouse.
  • Students program real sensors in MakeCode.

For teachers

  • Projects are shared through Google Drive and Google Slides.
  • The Journey 2050 simulation gives a seven lesson start.
  • The units suit career and technical courses.

For schools

  • Kit cost is not stated on the pages. Forward Education lists a Climate Action kit from $225.
  • The kit is needed for the hands-on projects.
  • No independent coverage of the district units was found.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI in these units. The units build sensor and data skills. Students read temperature and soil moisture and make a machine respond.

micro:bit
The micro:bit is a small, low-cost microcontroller board made for schools.
Sensor
A sensor is a part that measures something in the world and turns it into a signal.
Servo motor
A servo motor is a motor that turns to an exact angle and holds there.
Simulator
A simulator is software that copies a real system on screen.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which accounts students need. Sensors and code run on the micro:bit. Programming happens in MakeCode, and files are shared through Google Drive and Slides. The internet is used during lessons. The Climate Action page names a Data Driven Smart Farming site on forwardedu.com that requires sign-up. No site terms were stated on the unit pages. A teacher should check the account and sharing settings with the school and read the terms of that site. A teacher should also keep student names out of shared files.

Stellarium Web

A free online star map that runs in the browser with nothing to install.

FREEComputingLocal with optional cloud or accounts

Overview

Stellarium Web is the browser version of the Stellarium planetarium. The old address now redirects to stellarium.com/sky, which presents it as an online star map. It shows the sky for the viewer's location. Students use it to find the Moon, constellations, and the North Star.

Benefits

For students

  • Students open a star map in seconds with nothing to install.
  • Students see the sky for their own location.
  • Students can look for the Moon phase and constellations on any device with a browser.

For teachers

  • A Science North Grade 6 lesson plan uses it.
  • Students without internet can answer from pictures that the lesson provides.
  • The engine source is on GitHub under an AGPL license.

For schools

  • The tool is free at the time of reading and the vendor pricing page was not read.
  • It works in a modern browser, including on Chromebooks.
  • No IT install is needed, but the internet is needed at all times.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Stellarium Web has no AI. It draws the sky from stored star and planet data. Students practice observation and learn to find patterns in how the sky changes.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are optional. The policy says a position rounded to about 25 km is used for weather and the network address is used once to pick a starting city, and neither is stored. Account holders have their email and synced content stored. Google Analytics cookies are used on the websites outside Europe, and the Android apps send an advertising identifier to Firebase. The policy says the products are not aimed at children and that the company does not knowingly collect data from a child, which is a data rule and not a minimum age. A teacher should keep students signed out of accounts and decide with the district whether the tool suits students under 13.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Find tonight's Moon phase and draw what you see.
  2. Locate the North Star and note which constellation helps you find it.
  3. Compare the sky at your location with the sky in a far away city.
  4. Step forward by an hour and describe how the constellations move.
  5. Make a short guide to three constellations that your class can see this month.

STEM Learning unplugged AI workshop activities

A STEM Learning pack of unplugged AI workshops with notes, slides and printables.

AIUnpluggedGood for AI beginners

Overview

This STEM Learning resource is described as a set of unplugged AI workshop activities. Two are a generative AI art activity and a monster recognition cards activity. In the monster activity students build a feature database and test it. Each workshop is said to have teaching notes, a classroom presentation and printable materials.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how a computer can recognize things by features.
  • Students try a generative AI art idea on paper.
  • Students test their own database and see where it fails.

For teachers

  • Each workshop is said to come with notes and slides.
  • Printable materials save preparation time.
  • The activities fit middle school classes.

For schools

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The workshops teach AI ideas on paper and do not run an AI model. Students work with generative AI, classification and feature databases. They build and test their own paper versions.

Privacy and safety

The activities are on paper. The research could not read the page. It did not confirm whether an account is needed to download the pack. A teacher should check the download page and read the full notes before class.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a feature database for the monster cards and list each feature.
  2. Test a partner's database by guessing a monster from its features.
  3. Find a monster that the database often confuses and explain why.
  4. Add a new feature and check whether accuracy improves.
  5. Explain how classification works using the monster example.
How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Try the generative AI art activity and describe the steps a machine follows.
  2. Draw a monster from a list of features written by a classmate.
  3. Compare two drawings made from the same instructions.
  4. Write a prompt that could guide an art generator and sketch the result by hand.
  5. Discuss who deserves credit for art made from instructions.

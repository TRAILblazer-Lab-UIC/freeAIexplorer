Hour of Code activity catalog

A catalog of one-hour coding activities now served through the Hour of AI site.

AIComputingGood for AI beginners

Overview

Hour of Code is Code.org's catalog of one-hour coding activities. The old address now redirects to hourofai.org. The Hour of AI site says it still hosts Hour of Code activities and calls itself an evolution of the program. Activity cards show topic, age range, type, and length.

Benefits

For students

  • Students try coding in a single class period.
  • Students pick from many topics and age ranges.
  • Some activities teach AI literacy.

For teachers

  • Teachers can browse by grade, topic, or readiness.
  • Most activities include teacher notes.
  • The mix includes hosted and unplugged activities.

For schools

  • The catalog is free.
  • Activities run in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
  • Some activities launch on partner sites such as Minecraft Education, Google, LEGO Education, Adobe, Microsoft, and Scratch.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is optional. Some activities teach AI literacy, and the AI runs in different places depending on the activity. The Mix and Move with AI activity combines dance and music with coding. Students use these activities to explore how AI works and then build their own small projects.

Privacy and safety

The catalog links to activities from many vendors, and each has its own data practices. Unplugged activities send no data. One independent roundup says most listed resources need no account, with exceptions such as Tinkercad Codeblocks, which needs a free account. The catalog moved and is now part of hourofai.org. A teacher should read the privacy terms for each activity before students sign in or use a camera.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Try a one-hour activity and write down what you learned.
  2. Compare two activities from different partners and list one difference.
  3. Complete the Mix and Move with AI activity and explain how code drives the dance.
  4. Pick an AI literacy activity and teach one idea from it to a partner.
  5. Change a finished activity to make a new version.

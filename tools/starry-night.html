Starry Night

Paid planetarium software for exploring the sky on a computer.

$$ComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Starry Night is commercial planetarium and sky-simulation software from Simulation Curriculum. The consumer line runs on Windows and Mac. A separate education line sells classroom editions for elementary, middle school, high school, and college with lesson plans. Students explore the sky, move through time, and observe celestial objects.

Benefits

For students

  • Students can see the sky from any place and time.
  • Students can look at stars, deep-sky objects, and exoplanets in 3D.
  • Students follow lessons written for their grade level in the education editions.

For teachers

  • The education editions include lesson plans and teacher resources.
  • Multi-seat licenses cover a whole class.
  • Pro editions can control telescopes that work with ASCOM.

For schools

  • The software is paid. Desktop editions list from 59.95 to 259.95 dollars. Education classroom editions are listed at about 199.95 dollars.
  • Desktop version 8 needs Windows 10 or macOS 10.12, 1 GB of RAM, 12 GB of disk space, and OpenGL 1.4 graphics.
  • Education editions are reported to run in a browser, including on Chromebooks. The vendor should confirm this.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Starry Night has no AI. It simulates the sky with stored star data. Students build observation skills and learn to read patterns in the sky over time.

Privacy and safety

The Starry Night 8 page offers optional Data Backup and Cloud Sync in cloud storage. The research did not read the privacy policy. The materials do not state whether accounts are needed, how much internet use is required or what student data is collected. A teacher should ask the vendor these questions before buying. A teacher should also check how the browser based classroom editions handle student sign in.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Open the sky for the school's location tonight and name three objects that are visible.
  2. Move the clock forward by a month and describe how the night sky changes.
  3. Follow the Moon through its phases across several weeks of simulated time.
  4. Compare the sky from two places on Earth and explain the differences.
  5. Find a planet and record where it sits against the constellations on different dates.

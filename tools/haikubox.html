Haikubox

An outdoor Wi-Fi listening device that identifies birds by sound.

$ LicenseAISends student data to a companyGood for AI beginners

Overview

Haikubox is a Wi-Fi connected outdoor device that listens for birds and identifies species by sound. One model also listens for bats. It records 12 second audio clips and shows detections in a free app and on the web. The company publishes classroom lesson plans for K through high school.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn which birds live around their school.
  • Students build tables and graphs from real detections.
  • Students join community science by sharing their results.

For teachers

  • The company provides lesson plans in grade bands from K-1 up.
  • The app and website show detections without extra software.
  • Teachers can run a long study, such as a months long project with weekly library displays that one Massachusetts school ran.

For schools

  • The Haikubox costs $269 and the bat model costs $299.
  • A Haikubox+ subscription adds notifications, real-time listening, and 30 day history. The price was not listed.
  • It needs an outdoor power outlet and 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a sound classification model that identifies bird species. The company describes a proprietary neural network, and it does not say where the model runs. Recordings are saved in a cloud database. Students read the detections and use them to build their own tables and graphs.

Privacy and safety

An account is needed and the internet is always needed. The FAQ says recordings are saved in the vendor's cloud database. The box owner gives an email address, and viewers can use a free account. The vendor privacy policy could not be read. Audio retention and sharing are unknown. The research did not check whether detections and locations are shared publicly. An outdoor microphone can capture human speech. A teacher should place the device away from places where students talk and should read the vendor privacy policy.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Build a table of five bird species that the school Haikubox detected.
  2. Graph how often one species appears across a season.
  3. Compare the birds heard in the morning with those heard in the afternoon.
  4. Identify local species and graph their annual distribution.
  5. Make a library display that updates each week with new detections.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Explain how a sound can become a species name.
  2. Check a detection against a field guide and note any mismatch.
  3. List what data the device collects and where it goes.
  4. Discuss why a model might confuse two similar bird songs.
  5. Write a short guide on how to read the app's detection list.

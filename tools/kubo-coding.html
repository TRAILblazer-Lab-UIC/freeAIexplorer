KUBO Coding

A maze-driving robot that children program with physical command tiles.

$$$ComputingRoboticsLocal with optional cloud or accounts

Overview

KUBO is a small maze-driving robot that students program by snapping together physical TagTiles. Each tile is one command. The route is the program. The robot reads RFID chips in the tiles. The official site lists ages 4 to 10 plus and says the curriculum is free on the KUBO Portal. A KUBO Play app offers a digital way to practice, with a free 30-day trial.

Benefits

For students

  • Students code by snapping tiles together.
  • Students solve puzzles that teach turns.
  • Students can practice on a device with KUBO Play.

For teachers

  • The KUBO Portal has free curriculum.
  • KUBO offers free onboarding and teacher training.
  • The hands-on version needs no screen.

For schools

  • The Coding Starter Set was listed from 235 USD and add-on sets from 73 USD. Final pricing comes from a local partner.
  • The hands-on robot collects no data.
  • KUBO Play collects a player identifier, name or nickname, class associations, gameplay events, progress and saved solutions.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

KUBO has no AI. The robot follows the tiles that students place. Students build the skill of sequencing, which means putting commands in the right order.

Privacy and safety

The robot itself needs no screen or account and collects no data. The optional KUBO Play app collects a player identifier, name or nickname, class associations, gameplay events, progress, and saved solutions. Schools create student profiles with sign in codes, and the policy advises nicknames. Named third parties include Hetzner hosting, Paddle payments, and optional ClassLink or Clever sign in. Optional teacher AI assistance uses OpenAI or Google Gemini. A teacher should review the KUBO Play privacy terms and get any school authorisation or parental consent before creating classes.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Place tiles to make KUBO drive through a maze.
  2. Add a turn tile and predict the new route.
  3. Swap two tiles and describe the change.
  4. Build a longer route and test it with a partner.
  5. Debug a route that sends KUBO the wrong way.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Use the Math add-on tiles to practice counting steps.
  2. Build a route that uses a set number of moves.
  3. Measure the distance of one step and compare routes.
  4. Solve a number puzzle by choosing the correct tiles.
  5. Draw the route on paper as a map.
How this can be used for Music
  1. Use the Music add-on tiles to play a tune with KUBO.
  2. Build a short song with a repeating part.
  3. Match a robot move to each beat.
  4. Compare two songs made with different tile orders.
  5. Perform a tile song for the class.

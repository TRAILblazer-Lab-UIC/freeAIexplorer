UltiMaker Cura

Free slicing software that prepares 3D models for printing.

Computing

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

UltiMaker Cura is free and open source 3D printing slicing software. It has an open source slicing engine and more than 400 settings. Recommended settings keep setup simple, and a custom mode gives fine control. Students use it to prepare 3D models for printing.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn the steps from model to printed object.
  • Students can start with recommended settings.
  • Students can adjust settings to see how prints change.

For teachers

  • Printer profiles for many printers are built in.
  • Plugins and a marketplace add features.
  • The fabrication workflow fits engineering and career classes.

For schools

  • The software is free and open source.
  • It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux. The research did not verify Cura telemetry.
  • IT may need to approve the install. Chromebook support is not documented.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This tool has no AI. The slicer follows fixed rules to cut a model into layers. Students build skills in logic and data as they tune settings and compare results.

Privacy and safety

Cura runs on the computer and the product page does not say whether an account is required. The research did not document or verify Cura telemetry. A school district assessment approved Cura as a locally installed application. The UltiMaker privacy policy covers accounts and says children need verifiable parental consent to register one. UltiMaker says it will review a school Data Processing Agreement. Check Cura's network use with IT and avoid creating student accounts. Supervise the printer because printers get hot and have moving parts.

How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Slice a model and describe how layer height changes print time.
  2. Change infill and compare how strong each print is.
  3. Print the same part at two speeds and note the differences.
  4. Test how support settings affect a model with an overhang.
  5. Write a short guide that explains the slicing steps to a classmate.
How this can be used for Career and Technical Education
  1. Walk through the full fabrication steps from file to finished part.
  2. Choose a printer profile and explain why it matters.
  3. Estimate print time and material for a class project.
  4. Compare recommended and custom modes and record the results.
  5. Make a small product prototype and review the print quality.

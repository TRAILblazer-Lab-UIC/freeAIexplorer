Barefoot Bytes

Twenty-four short unplugged computing activities for ages 5-11.

ComputingUnplugged

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Barefoot Bytes is a set of 24 unplugged computing activities from Barefoot Computing. There are eight activities for each of three age bands, which are 5-7, 7-9, and 9-11. A search result describes them as short flexible tasks and not full lesson plans. Students work together on paper tasks that also touch English and maths.

Benefits

For students

  • Students work in groups on short, hands-on computing tasks.
  • Students practice computing ideas through English and maths topics.
  • Students at ages 5 to 11 each have activities matched to their age band.

For teachers

  • The activities are short and flexible. A teacher can fit one into a spare slot.
  • Eight activities exist for each age band.
  • No computer is needed to teach them.

For schools

  • The Barefoot home page describes its resources as free.
  • The activities run on paper with no devices.
  • IT involvement is not needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This resource has no AI. It builds computational thinking through short unplugged tasks. Students practice working through a problem in steps and sharing ideas with a group.

Privacy and safety

The activities run on paper and the research found no student data details. The materials do not say whether an account is needed. A search result says a free teacher account is needed for the wider toolkit. The materials do not state what data the Barefoot site collects. The research did not confirm that Barefoot Bytes is still available. A teacher should check the site terms and confirm that the activities can be downloaded.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Work in a group to complete a short unplugged computing task from the age band for your class.
  2. Explain the steps of your solution to another group.
  3. Spot and fix a mistake in a classmate's set of instructions.
  4. Compare two ways to solve the same task and choose the faster one.
  5. Teach a younger pupil one of the activities.
How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Retell a short story as an ordered set of steps.
  2. Write clear instructions for a task and test them with a partner.
  3. Describe a character's actions in the right order.
  4. Discuss which words make a set of instructions easy to follow.
  5. Rewrite a confusing instruction in clearer words for a classmate.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Sort a set of number cards by following a written rule.
  2. Find a pattern in a row of shapes and write the rule.
  3. Count the steps in a plan and compare plans of different lengths.
  4. Solve a short puzzle with a partner and explain each step.
  5. Make up a number rule and challenge a classmate to find it.

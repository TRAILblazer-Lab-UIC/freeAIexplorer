Hugging Face Classroom

A free Hugging Face program where students build and share machine learning apps.

AI

Overview

Hugging Face Classroom is a program that gives teachers free infrastructure and resources to introduce machine learning. Students learn ML, build ML-powered apps, and share them with open source tools and models on the Hugging Face Hub. The Classroom-workshop page shows student work as model repositories and Spaces. An assignments leaderboard is also shown.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build and share real machine learning apps.
  • Students use the same tools that professionals use.
  • Students see their work on a public leaderboard.

For teachers

  • Teachers can request to join the Classroom organization or create one.
  • Assignments appear as repositories and Spaces that teachers can review.
  • The page lists no lesson plans. Teachers plan their own.

For schools

  • Creating a classroom is described as free.
  • Students need only a computer and a browser.
  • Student accounts and public repositories raise privacy questions that the school should review.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is model training, model sharing, and hosting of ML apps on the Hugging Face cloud. Students train or choose a model, build a Space, and share it. They use the leaderboard to compare their results with classmates.

Privacy and safety

Students need accounts, and the internet is always needed. The vendor collects email, password, username, full name, optional profile items, posted content, IP address, cookies, and device data. The policy says the services are not aimed at users under 13 and that it deletes data from under 13 users collected without verifiable parental consent. It lists eleven subprocessors, including AWS, Stripe, Google Cloud, GitHub, and Slack, and it names neither FERPA nor COPPA. Public repositories are visible to others. A teacher should get school privacy approval and make repositories private where possible.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Train a small image or text model and publish it as a model repository.
  2. Build a Space that lets a visitor try the model.
  3. Compare your model's score with the leaderboard and plan an improvement.
  4. Read another student's model card and give feedback.
  5. Write a model card that explains what the model can and cannot do.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Build an ML app that sorts photos of plants or rocks.
  2. Gather a small dataset from a class experiment and train a model on it.
  3. Test the model on new samples and record the accuracy.
  4. Write a short report on one wrong prediction and a possible cause.
  5. Share the app with a partner class for testing.

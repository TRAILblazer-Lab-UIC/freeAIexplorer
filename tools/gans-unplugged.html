GANs Unplugged

A card drawing game where student groups act as the parts of a GAN.

FREEAIUnpluggedNo device neededGood for AI beginners

Overview

GANs Unplugged introduces generative adversarial networks to secondary students. Groups act as parts of a GAN. The Real and Generator groups sketch animals on index cards, and only the Real group has secret drawing instructions. A Discriminator group guesses which cards are real and adds feedback marks. The paper by Patrick Virtue appeared in the AAAI-21 proceedings.

Benefits

For students

  • Students feel how a generator learns from feedback.
  • Students draw and guess in a lively group game.
  • Students see how fakes grow more realistic over several rounds.

For teachers

  • The activity needs only index cards and pens.
  • The paper reports that it ran at three high school AI summer camps.
  • The roles are clear and easy to assign.

For schools

  • The cost is index cards.
  • The activity needs no devices or accounts.
  • The activity collects no student data.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The activity has no running AI program. It teaches how a generative adversarial network works with two parts that compete. The Generator tries to make realistic fakes and the Discriminator tries to catch them. Students build skills in feedback and in seeing how models improve over rounds.

Privacy and safety

The activity runs on paper and the materials describe no student data collection. No account is needed. The research did not read a privacy policy. The publisher page blocked the research tool. Details come from search results. A teacher should read the full paper first. A teacher should also decide what the Real group will draw.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Play the Generator and revise your animal sketches after each round of feedback.
  2. Play the Discriminator and explain which clues revealed the fakes.
  3. Count how many fakes the Discriminator caught in each round and chart the change.
  4. Explain to a partner why the Generator and Discriminator both improve.
  5. Discuss where fake images from a GAN could cause trouble in real life.
How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Sketch an animal from secret instructions and keep the drawing simple.
  2. Compare the real sketches with the fakes and name the differences.
  3. Redraw a fake so it looks more like the real cards.
  4. Pick one feature that gave away a fake and draw it again with more care.
  5. Display the cards from the first and last rounds and describe the change.

Introduction to Generative AI for Educators (Canadian Centre for Cyber Security)

A free 90-minute online course on generative AI for K-12 educators in Canada.

AI

Overview

This is a free, self-paced online course for K-12 and post-secondary educators in Canada. Its code is EDU102S, and it comes from the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security. It covers foundational AI concepts, technical and ethical considerations, and classroom uses of generative AI. It takes about 1 hour 30 minutes and has no prerequisites.

Benefits

For students

  • Students gain from a teacher who knows the risks of generative AI.
  • Students may get clearer lessons on bias and misinformation.
  • Students share no data with the course.

For teachers

  • Objectives include defining common terms and identifying tools used in schools.
  • The course covers responsible digital citizenship and ways to reduce risk.
  • Teachers finish with a generative AI toolkit.

For schools

  • The course is free.
  • It runs in a browser.
  • A companion course, EDU101S Introduction to cyber security for educators, comes from the same Centre.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The course teaches about generative AI. It has no AI tool of its own for students to use. The course runs in the vendor's cloud. Teachers learn about bias, risks, and misinformation and use that knowledge in class.

Privacy and safety

Teachers need a Learning Hub account, and the internet is always needed. The course is for educators and not for students, and no student data is involved. The course page has no course specific privacy information and links only to general Government of Canada privacy terms. The research did not read the course modules or the Learning Hub account terms. A teacher should check the Learning Hub privacy terms before signing in.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Define generative AI in your own words and give two examples.
  2. List three risks of generative AI and one way to reduce each.
  3. Compare an AI answer with a trusted source and note any gaps.
  4. Write a class rule for good AI use.
  5. Explain how bias can enter an AI system.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Check an AI answer against two sources and report what you found.
  2. Make a poster about spotting misinformation.
  3. Discuss what makes a digital citizen responsible with AI.
  4. Rate three sources on trust and explain the scores.
  5. Write a guide for classmates on citing AI help.

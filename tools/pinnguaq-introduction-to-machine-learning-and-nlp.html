Pinnguaq Introduction to Machine Learning and NLP

A lesson on natural language processing with unplugged tasks and a Teachable Machine project.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This lesson from Pinnguaq first appeared in Root & STEM issue 8. It introduces natural language processing for grades 4 to 6. Students practice steps such as tokenizing, removing stop words, stemming, and tagging on a text. They then train an audio model on dog and cat sounds in Google Teachable Machine.

Benefits

For students

  • Students handle a real text with the same steps a computer uses.
  • Students train an audio model and see how data shapes its answers.
  • Students talk about online safety, privacy, and voice assistants.

For teachers

  • Two unplugged tasks need only a short text, pens, and paper.
  • The lesson lists the Ontario Grades 4 to 6 Coding and Emerging Technologies strand.
  • An optional Inuit text, Teachings from the Past, can serve as the practice text.

For schools

  • The lesson and Teachable Machine are free.
  • Devices need a microphone and wireless internet.
  • No IT work is needed. The lesson page has no downloads. Staff read it online.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lesson teaches natural language processing by hand and then audio classification with a trained model. The vendor does not say where the Teachable Machine model runs. Students apply each step to a text, train their own model, and use what they learn to explain how a voice assistant might work.

Privacy and safety

The lesson page does not mention accounts and does not say where data goes. The audio project uses the device microphone through Google Teachable Machine, and the internet is needed at all times. The research did not review the Teachable Machine terms. A teacher should check those terms and district rules before students record sounds.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Split a chosen text into tokens and count the words.
  2. Remove stop words from a paragraph and read what is left.
  3. Reduce words to their stems and compare them with the original words.
  4. Tag each word as a noun, verb, or other part of speech.
  5. Play Word Meaning Detective and explain how the clues show a meaning.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Train an audio model on dog and cat sounds in Teachable Machine.
  2. Test the model with a new sound and note whether it was right.
  3. Add more sound samples and record how the answers change.
  4. Draw the steps a voice assistant might use to understand a spoken question.
  5. Make a list of rules for staying safe with voice assistants.

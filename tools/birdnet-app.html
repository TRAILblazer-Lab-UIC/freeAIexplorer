BirdNET app

A free phone app that identifies birds from the sounds they make.

AIGood for AI beginners

Overview

BirdNET is a free app that identifies birds from recorded sound with a neural network. It recognizes over 11,000 species and is on Google Play and the App Store. Sharing observations is optional and helps citizen science research. Students record sounds and see species suggestions.

Benefits

For students

  • Students hear a bird and get a species name in seconds.
  • Students can add to citizen science research if they choose.
  • Students need only a phone with a microphone.

For teachers

  • The app is easy to learn.
  • Teachers choose whether students allow location and submissions.
  • It works well for outdoor class walks.

For schools

  • The app is free.
  • No account is needed.
  • A teacher can install it on shared phones, which keeps IT work small.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is a neural network that classifies bird sound. The newer BirdNET Live app runs its model on the phone, and the page says it works fully offline. The vendor materials suggest the original app may use a cloud service. Students record a sound, read the suggestions, and use them as a starting point for their own notes.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed. The app collects a random device ID, GPS location, and the audio recordings that are submitted. Name and email are optional. Submitted audio is processed on the developers' servers and all submitted recordings are stored there. The policy advises against submitting private recordings. Recordings are used for research and shared in anonymized form under a CC BY-SA 4.0 license. The policy does not mention children. A teacher should check the location and submission settings before students use the app.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Record a bird call on school grounds and compare the suggestion with a field guide.
  2. Keep a class log of species heard each week.
  3. Compare the birds heard in a quiet place and a noisy place.
  4. Record at three times of day and graph the number of species.
  5. Choose one species and write a short report on its habitat.

iStopMotion

A stop motion animation app for Mac, iPad, and iPhone with step by step guidance.

Computing

Overview

iStopMotion is an animation app for stop motion, claymation, and cartoon animation. It runs on Mac, iPad, and iPhone. The interface uses visual cues and step by step guidance for young students. Students take pictures frame by frame and play them back as a movie.

Benefits

For students

  • Students see onion skin layers of the last frame, which helps them line up each move.
  • Students can match their animation to a soundtrack with a sound waveform.
  • Students can export a GIF or a video to share.

For teachers

  • The interface is designed for young students.
  • The free Remote Camera app turns an iPhone or iPad into a wireless camera.
  • Teachers can also use a USB webcam or send the work to iMovie.

For schools

  • The App Store listing showed $9.99 and school licenses go through Apple School Manager.
  • School volume prices were not seen. Contact edu@boinx.com.
  • IT may need to deploy the app, and it is not a Chromebook app.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

iStopMotion has no AI. Students build sequencing skills by planning small changes from frame to frame. They learn that a movie is a long series of still images.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used for setup. The vendor site says it does not collect or share any student data. The App Store privacy label says the developer collects no data from the app. The research did not fetch the privacy policy page. A teacher should check where finished movies are saved. A teacher should also set rules for photographing classmates.

How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Make a short claymation clip of a character that changes shape.
  2. Animate a drawing, such as a flower growing across ten frames.
  3. Use onion skin layers to keep a character steady between frames.
  4. Create a looping GIF of a simple motion.
  5. Design a set and a character, then shoot a story of at least 30 frames.
How this can be used for Theater and Media Arts
  1. Plan a story with a storyboard before you shoot.
  2. Record a short voice track and use the sound waveform to time the animation.
  3. Use the Remote Camera app to shoot from a better angle.
  4. Export a video and add titles in iMovie.
  5. Show the movie to the class and ask for feedback on timing.

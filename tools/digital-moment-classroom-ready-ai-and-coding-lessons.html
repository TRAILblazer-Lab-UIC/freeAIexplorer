Digital Moment classroom-ready AI and coding lessons

Free K-12 lessons on coding and AI, with no coding experience needed.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Digital Moment offers free classroom-ready lessons for Kindergarten to Grade 12 on coding, AI, and cybersecurity. Most lessons take an hour or less and need no coding experience. The AI lessons are based on the Experience AI framework from the Raspberry Pi Foundation and Google DeepMind. Digital Moment is its exclusive Canadian partner. Featured lessons include Introduction to micro:bit, Sensing Data, AI Policy Website and Design, and Mandala Magic in Scratch.

Benefits

For students

  • Students try hands-on coding with Scratch or micro:bit.
  • Students learn about AI through lessons based on Experience AI.
  • Students can write an AI policy and design a website for it.

For teachers

  • Lessons need no prep or coding experience.
  • Most lessons take 60 minutes or less.
  • Teachers can pick lessons by grade.

For schools

  • The lessons are free.
  • Some lessons need micro:bit hardware.
  • Students use a browser. A teacher account is optional.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI lessons teach AI literacy and AI policy and do not run an AI tool. Students learn what AI is and how it affects people. They use the lessons to write an AI policy or to study sensor data with a micro:bit.

Privacy and safety

A free teacher account is offered, and the research did not confirm whether students need accounts. The materials do not state what student data is collected or where it goes. Tools vary by lesson and include micro:bit with MakeCode, Scratch and website building. The research did not review a privacy policy. A teacher should read the portal's privacy terms and check the tools each lesson uses before students sign in.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Build a simple Scratch project in the Mandala Magic lesson and share it with a partner.
  2. Program a micro:bit to sense light or motion and record the data.
  3. Look at the sensor data and decide how a machine could learn from it.
  4. Design a web page that states a school AI policy.
  5. Discuss three rules that make an AI tool fair and safe.

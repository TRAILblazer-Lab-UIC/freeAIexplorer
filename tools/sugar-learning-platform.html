Sugar Learning Platform

A free, child-friendly desktop of learning Activities for Linux computers.

Computing

Overview

Sugar is a child-friendly open source desktop environment first built for the One Laptop per Child XO-1 laptop. It is available in more than 25 languages and its Activities are used in schools in more than 40 countries, per the project. Activities cover writing, math, music, and Turtle Art programming. Students open an Activity, work in it, and find their work in a Journal.

Benefits

For students

  • Students use simple Activities for writing, math, music, and coding.
  • A Journal records their work.
  • The desktop is made for children and runs on most computers.

For teachers

  • Teachers get a ready set of Activities across several subjects.
  • The platform is available in more than 25 languages.
  • The classic XO-based Sugar has no GitHub release tag and development continues on master.

For schools

  • The software is free.
  • It installs on Debian, Ubuntu, or Fedora, or boots from Sugar on a Stick.
  • IT help is needed for a school fleet. There is no Chromebook install.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Sugar has no AI. Students use Activities that they control. They practice sequencing in Turtle Art and learn to give clear instructions.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The README describes a local desktop environment with no account step. The research read no privacy policy and did not verify that collaboration stays on the local network. The materials do not describe any data collection. A teacher should check that shared computers keep each student's Journal separate.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Write a short story in a writing Activity and save it to the Journal.
  2. Write a poem and read it to a partner.
  3. Review a classmate's Journal entry and add a comment.
  4. Illustrate a sentence from a story in an Activity.
  5. Create a class book from several students' entries.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Use a math Activity to practice counting or number facts.
  2. Explore patterns and record what you notice in the Journal.
  3. Make a game of number puzzles with a partner.
  4. Solve a set of problems and explain one answer.
  5. Compare answers with a classmate.
How this can be used for Music
  1. Make a short tune in a music Activity.
  2. Change the tempo and listen to the effect.
  3. Layer a rhythm and a melody.
  4. Describe your song in the Journal.
  5. Perform your tune for the class.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Move a turtle in Turtle Art to draw a square.
  2. Repeat a command to draw a pattern.
  3. Predict the shape before you run a program.
  4. Fix a program that draws the wrong shape.
  5. Share your drawing and explain the steps.

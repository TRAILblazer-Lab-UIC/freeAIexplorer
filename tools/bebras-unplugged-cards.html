Bebras Unplugged Cards

Printable computational thinking challenge cards in four levels for Years 3-10.

FREEComputingUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

Bebras Unplugged Cards are printable challenge cards adapted from the Bebras Challenge. There are four levels. They are Beginner for Years 3-4, Intermediate for Years 5-6, Advanced for Years 7-8, and Expert for Years 9-10. Each level is a downloadable PDF. A search result says each card gives a suggested answer and a key skill.

Benefits

For students

  • Students solve logic puzzles on paper at their own level.
  • Students learn skills such as decomposition and pattern recognition.
  • Students can reflect on their work with a reflection worksheet.

For teachers

  • Solutions and instruction cards come with the sets.
  • A separate card set explains six computational thinking skills.
  • Search results say teachers should stress that the cards are not a race.

For schools

  • The offline resources are free.
  • They need only a printer and scissors.
  • No devices or accounts are needed in class.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

These cards have no AI. They build computational thinking skills such as decomposition and pattern recognition. Students break a puzzle into parts and spot the pattern that solves it.

Privacy and safety

The cards run on paper and the research found no student data collection. The materials do not say whether the hub needs an account. The internet is used to download the PDFs. A teacher should check the download page and read each suggested answer before class.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Solve a card and name the key skill listed with the answer.
  2. Break a hard card into smaller parts and solve each part.
  3. Spot the pattern in a card and explain it to a partner.
  4. Compare your answer with the suggested answer and discuss any difference.
  5. Use the reflection worksheet to describe which card was hardest.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Use logic to rule out wrong answers on a card.
  2. Find the rule in a number or shape pattern.
  3. Count the steps in your solution and look for a shorter way.
  4. Solve a card that uses sorting or ordering.
  5. Write a card of your own with a pattern for a classmate.

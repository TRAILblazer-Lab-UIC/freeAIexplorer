Stanford CRAFT AI social studies collection

Six history and social science lessons about AI from Stanford CRAFT.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

This collection has six lessons from Stanford CRAFT that connect AI to history and social science. Topics include indigenous languages, the Nazca lines, AI misinformation, fact-checking AI search, AI in warfare, and AI bias in the Global South. The CRAFT overview describes the project as written for high school. Students read, discuss, and practice skills such as lateral reading.

Benefits

For students

  • Students practice checking AI search results with lateral reading.
  • Students look at real cases such as archaeology and language preservation.
  • Students discuss bias and harm in AI systems.

For teachers

  • Most lessons run 60 minutes and the warfare lesson runs 75.
  • Teacher guides come with the lessons.
  • Slides and printable organizers are provided for some lessons.

For schools

  • The lessons are free.
  • They need only a browser and a printer or projector.
  • IT does not need to install anything.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The lessons are about AI. They look at language processing, AI misinformation, bias, AI search results, and image generation. The lessons do not run AI themselves. Students use paper organizers and class discussion to examine AI critically. Some activities may ask students to try an outside tool.

Privacy and safety

The collection page does not say whether students use AI tools directly or need accounts. The fact checking lesson discusses ChatGPT and Perplexity. The research found no data handling statement. The lessons are mostly paper and slides. A teacher should check any outside AI search tool before students use it. A teacher should also preview each lesson. Topics such as warfare need care.

How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Read the indigenous languages lesson and discuss how AI could help save a language.
  2. Study the Nazca lines case and list what AI can and cannot tell researchers.
  3. Examine an AI generated image of a historical event and look for errors.
  4. Discuss how AI bias may affect people in the Global South.
  5. Debate the benefits and risks of AI in warfare after reading the lesson.
How this can be used for Library and Media Literacy
  1. Practice lateral reading by opening new tabs to check an AI search answer.
  2. Fill in a fact-checking organizer for one AI search result.
  3. Compare an AI answer with two library sources.
  4. Make a list of steps for checking any AI generated claim.
  5. Teach the lateral reading steps to a partner.

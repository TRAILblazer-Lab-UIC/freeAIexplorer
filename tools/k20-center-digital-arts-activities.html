K20 Center Digital Arts Activities

Three free digital art lessons that use Krita and explore art careers.

Computing

Overview

K20 Center Digital Arts Activities is a visual arts collection published in June 2023 by James Doyle and Shayna Pond. It has three lessons that help students explore art careers. The main software is Krita, a free painting program for Mac and PC. Students make digital images and then print them.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make monochrome images with Wacom tablets and screen print them.
  • Students design temporary tattoos and print them.
  • Students touch up photos and build collages.

For teachers

  • Each lesson is listed with a clear length. Ink Me Up takes about 5 class periods and Image Mash-up takes about 2.
  • The lessons are aimed at grades 9-12 and Secondary.
  • Krita is easy to learn.

For schools

  • The collection and Krita are free.
  • The tools run on the device and the internet is used only for setup.
  • IT needs to install Krita on Windows or Mac machines.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The collection has no AI. Students build skills in image making and design. They use layers and tools to shape a picture and learn the steps that artists follow.

Privacy and safety

The K20 pages do not mention accounts, and Krita installs from krita.org and runs locally. Student work stays on the device in Krita. Some lessons use videos online and need the internet for those. No privacy policy was reviewed for the collection pages. A teacher should check the printing supplies and ink for classroom safety. A teacher should also set rules for the images students use in collages.

How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Make a monochrome image on a tablet and screen print it, as the lesson Clearly, This Is Art does.
  2. Design a temporary tattoo that tells a story about the student.
  3. Touch up a photo and compare it to the original.
  4. Build a collage from three photos in Image Mash-up.
  5. Display the prints and explain the design choices.
How this can be used for Career and Technical Education
  1. Read about an art career and list the skills it needs.
  2. Interview an artist or designer about their daily work.
  3. Make a design brief for a client and create a draft in Krita.
  4. Compare a digital tool to a hand tool for the same task.
  5. Present a small portfolio of the three lesson products.

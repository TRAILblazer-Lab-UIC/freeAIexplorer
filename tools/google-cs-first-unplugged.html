Google CS First Unplugged

A screen-free booklet of three activities on how computer science helps people communicate.

FREEUnpluggedNo device needed

Overview

This is a screen-free booklet of three computational thinking activities that show how computer science helps people communicate. The Code Week listing says the activities can be done alone in any order and take about 1 hour. It lists a Network a Neighborhood map with counters and a Send a Secret Message cipher wheel. ShareMyLesson lists it for Grades 4-8 with English and Spanish versions.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how networks carry messages by playing with a map.
  • Students make and use a cipher wheel to send a secret message.
  • Students work offline with paper and simple tools.

For teachers

  • A teacher lesson plan comes with the booklet.
  • The activities can be done in any order.
  • Preparation takes only a few minutes.

For schools

  • The booklet is free. The license is listed on ShareMyLesson.
  • Google's CS First program shut down on June 30, 2025, and the booklet may now exist only on third-party mirrors.
  • Google recommends Experience CS from the Raspberry Pi Foundation for ages 8-14.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This booklet has no AI. Students learn how messages travel and how codes hide them. They build skills in data and pattern spotting.

Privacy and safety

The booklet is offline and no account is needed. The activities are on paper and the research found no student data collection. The internet is used to download it. The CS First site used Google accounts, was turned down on June 30, 2025, and Google deleted its stored data, according to Google's support page. The research did not read a privacy policy for this item. A teacher should check the mirror site and its license before downloading. A teacher should also supervise the pin or paper clip used for the cipher wheel.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Place counters on a neighborhood map to link houses into a network.
  2. Send a message across the network and trace its path.
  3. Build a cipher wheel and write a secret message.
  4. Trade messages with a partner and decode them with the wheel.
  5. Explain how a network helps people communicate.

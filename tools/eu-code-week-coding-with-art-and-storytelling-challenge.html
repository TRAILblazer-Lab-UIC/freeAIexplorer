EU Code Week Coding with Art and Storytelling challenge

Students build a story from an artwork and retell it with a robot or unplugged roles.

FREERoboticsUnpluggedStays on the device

Overview

This EU Code Week challenge by Maria Tsapara and Anthi Arkouli has students build a story from an artwork. They illustrate the story and then retell it by programming a robot such as a Bee-Bot across a grid. It can also be done unplugged with one child as the robot and one as the programmer. The page lists it as about 90 minutes at Intermediate level.

Benefits

For students

  • Students look closely at a painting and build a story from it.
  • Students write an algorithm with arrow cards.
  • Students test and debug in teams of three or four.

For teachers

  • The challenge works for ages 3 to 6 and 6 to 12.
  • A robot is optional because the unplugged version is included.
  • It links art, storytelling, and coding in one lesson.

For schools

  • The challenge is free, according to the batch notes.
  • A robot such as a Bee-Bot is a separate purchase.
  • Two resource files are Greek language Google Drive links.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

This challenge has no AI. Students write step lists for a robot or a partner. They build sequencing skills as they order the events of a story.

Privacy and safety

The activity needs no account and runs on paper or with a robot. The internet is used to open the page and download files. The Code Week site logs visitor IP addresses for up to one year, and JA Europe is the data controller under its privacy policy. Only Code Week account holders give an email address. Sharing work on Instagram is optional and goes to a third party platform. A teacher should decide whether to share student work publicly and check that the Greek Google Drive links open.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for English and Language Arts
  1. Look at a painting and write down what the people in it might say.
  2. Build a story with a beginning, middle, and end from the artwork.
  3. Retell the story aloud in order.
  4. Match each story event to a square on the grid.
  5. Share the story with another team and compare the endings.
How this can be used for Visual Arts
  1. Observe a painting for one minute and list what you see.
  2. Illustrate each event of your story on a card.
  3. Choose colors that fit the mood of the artwork.
  4. Place the illustrated cards on the class mat.
  5. Explain which part of the painting inspired each picture.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Write an algorithm with arrow cards to move a robot to the first event.
  2. Test the algorithm with a Bee-Bot or with a partner acting as the robot.
  3. Find the step that sent the robot the wrong way and fix it.
  4. Extend the algorithm to cover the whole story.
  5. Swap roles so each team member is the programmer once.

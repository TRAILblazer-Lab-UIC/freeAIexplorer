TERC INFACT computational thinking for neurodiverse learners

Computational thinking activities for grades 3 to 8 with executive function supports.

ComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

INFACT is a TERC program for grades 3 to 8 that teaches computational thinking with supports for executive function and social emotional learning. It was designed for neurodiverse learners such as those with ADHD, autism, or dyslexia and is meant to help all learners. Materials are organized into six sequence topics. Activities include movement, card and partner games, digital games, Scratch coding, and robots.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn decomposition, pattern recognition, algorithm design, abstraction, and debugging.
  • Students move, play games, and work with partners.
  • Students get supports for planning and working with others.

For teachers

  • Six sequence topics can be customized.
  • Each topic has Activation, Foundational, Applied, and Wrap-Up activities.
  • Free sample guides such as the Barrier Game are available.

For schools

  • The materials page says activities are freely available and lists no fees.
  • Most activities need no devices. Some use building bricks.
  • Activities that rely on Zoombinis may not be fully available.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

INFACT has no AI. Students practice computational thinking through games and partner work. They build skills in pattern spotting and in breaking big tasks into steps.

Many activities are physical or card based and need no accounts. Some use digital games, Scratch, or robots. The page does not list tool requirements or data handling. Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Play the Barrier Game and give a partner clear step by step instructions.
  2. Break a large task into smaller steps and put them in order.
  3. Find the pattern in a set of cards and describe the rule.
  4. Find and fix a mistake in a set of instructions.
  5. Choose the important details of a problem and ignore the rest.
How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Use a planning card to set a goal and the steps to reach it.
  2. Take turns in a partner game and name one thing that went well.
  3. Move around the room while you act out an algorithm.
  4. Use the wrap-up activity to say what you learned.
  5. Customize a sequence topic with a teacher for your own needs.

MIT App Inventor AI tutorials and curriculum (PICaboo)

A two-part tutorial where students train an image classifier and build a peekaboo app.

Overview

PICaboo is a two part App Inventor tutorial for grades 6 to 12. Students train a Personal Image Classifier and build a peekaboo game app. The baby on screen smiles when a face shows and cries when it hides. PICaboo is also used as a unit in the CoolThink@JC curriculum.

Benefits

For students

  • Students train a classifier with their own webcam images.
  • Students build a mobile app that uses the model.
  • Students see how training data changes the app's behavior.

For teachers

  • The tutorial has two clear parts.
  • It is rated intermediate.
  • Part one runs in a browser.

For schools

  • The tutorial is free.
  • Part two needs Android devices with cameras, per a search snippet.
  • Older devices should be tested beforehand.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI is an image classifier trained by the student. The vendor does not say where the training runs. Students collect webcam images, train a model, and then use it in a game app. They can add more images to make it more reliable.

Privacy and safety

The materials do not state which accounts students need or where training images go. The tutorial sends users to ai2.appinventor.mit.edu. The research did not confirm whether training and inference happen in the browser or on a server. A search snippet calls the Personal Image Classifier a web interface for training models with webcam images. Webcam training images and saved project files are the student data involved. The research read no privacy policy for this item. A teacher should check the App Inventor and classifier terms and keep faces out of images when possible.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Train a classifier with images of a face and an empty chair.
  2. Build the peekaboo app and make the baby react to the model.
  3. Add new training images and compare the results.
  4. Test the app in dim light and write down what changes.
  5. Change the baby's reactions to a new set of images.

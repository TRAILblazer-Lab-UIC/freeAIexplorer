Coding to the Beat (Georgia Tech CEISMC)

A middle school curriculum that teaches music technology and coding together.

FREEComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Coding to the Beat is a middle school curriculum of up to 18 weeks from Georgia Tech CEISMC. It has five units of about 2 to 3 weeks each. The units are Intro to Music Technology, Form and Rhythm, Melody, Harmony, and What Makes Music So Expressive. It is written for music teachers with strong music knowledge and little computing background.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make music with digital audio workstation tools and code.
  • Students learn music ideas such as form, rhythm, melody, and harmony.
  • Students practice computing in a music setting.

For teachers

  • The units suit music teachers new to computing.
  • The project site offers teacher training support through the EarSketch team.
  • Georgia standards for music technology and computer programming are listed.

For schools

  • The curriculum pages are free to view.
  • No cost is stated for the tools.
  • Some lessons come from the Georgia Department of Education and share the CC BY-NC-SA license.

How this can be used for Music
  1. Explore the parts of a song in the Intro to Music Technology unit.
  2. Build a form with sections that repeat and contrast.
  3. Create a rhythm pattern and loop it.
  4. Write a melody and add a harmony part.
  5. Discuss what makes a piece sound expressive and test a change.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Use code to repeat a rhythm pattern.
  2. Write a short program that plays a melody.
  3. Change a variable and hear the effect on the music.
  4. Debug a song program that plays notes out of order.
  5. Explain how a loop helps build a song.

ASU and Utah STEM Bee-Bot lesson plans

Teacher-written Bee-Bot math lessons for kindergarten to grade 3.

FREERoboticsStays on the device

Overview

These are teacher-written Bee-Bot lessons hosted on the ASU STEM Teachers site and the Utah STEM Action Center. In Number Bonds with Bee-Bots, kindergarten students code a Bee-Bot to show ways to break apart a whole number. In Guess My Number, students solve addition or subtraction equations and show answers by programming the Bee-Bot. The Utah page lists more lessons for kindergarten to grade 3.

Benefits

For students

  • Students program a robot to show number bonds and answers.
  • Students link counting and equations to movement on a mat.
  • Students see math ideas come alive on a number line.

For teachers

  • The lesson steps are in linked Google Slides.
  • The lessons match math tags for counting and algebraic thinking.
  • The two sites are separate, and the Utah page does not link the ASU lessons.

For schools

  • The lesson plans are free.
  • Schools must buy the Bee-Bot robots. The research did not check a price.
  • The robots run on their own. The lesson slides are opened online.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

These lessons have no AI. They build an AI-related skill, which is sequencing. Students press buttons to give the robot an ordered list of moves.

Privacy and safety

The Bee-Bot is a standalone robot and the research found no accounts or student data collection. The ASU lesson plan is a Google Slides deck that a teacher opens online. A teacher should check school rules for opening those links with students. Robots should stay on a clear floor so they do not roll off a table.

How this can be used for Math
  1. Program a Bee-Bot to show the parts of a number such as 5 as 2 and 3.
  2. Solve a Guess My Number equation and move the Bee-Bot to the answer.
  3. Move the Bee-Bot along a number line to add two numbers.
  4. Move the Bee-Bot back along the line to subtract.
  5. Draw the number bonds you found and compare them with a partner.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Press the Bee-Bot buttons to move it to a target square.
  2. Write down the moves in order before you press them.
  3. Debug a move list that sends the robot to the wrong square.
  4. Predict where the robot will stop before it runs.
  5. Trade a mat with another group and write moves for it.

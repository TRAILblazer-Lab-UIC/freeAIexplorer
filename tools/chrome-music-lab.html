Chrome Music Lab

Free Google music experiments in the browser, including Song Maker.

FREEComputingUnverified

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Chrome Music Lab is a set of free browser music experiments from Google, including Song Maker. It runs in a browser on computers, phones, and tablets with nothing to install. No account is needed, and Song Maker songs can be shared by link. Teachers have used it to link music to science, math, art, and dance.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make music by clicking and dragging.
  • Students try many experiments that show how sound works.
  • Students can share a song by link.

For teachers

  • Teachers can start a lesson in minutes.
  • No login is needed.
  • The experiments link music to science and math.

For schools

  • The tool is free, and the code is open source.
  • It runs on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, Linux, iOS, and Android.
  • IT does not need to install anything.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The vendor does not describe any AI in the materials read. The tools build the skill of pattern spotting, as students find rhythms and melodies that repeat. The internet is needed to load the site.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the experiments run in the browser with the internet. Song Maker songs are shared by link, and anyone with the link can open a song. The research did not confirm whether songs are stored on Google servers. Some experiments need microphone access. The site links the Google Privacy Policy without further detail. A teacher should check the Google privacy terms and decide which experiments use the microphone.

Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Music
  1. Make a short melody in Song Maker and play it back.
  2. Change the key or tempo and describe what happens.
  3. Build a rhythm with the Rhythm experiment.
  4. Compare two songs by two classmates.
  5. Share a song link and ask for feedback.
How this can be used for Science
  1. Use the experiments to see how sound waves look.
  2. Compare high and low notes and describe the waves.
  3. Explore how string length changes pitch.
  4. Record a sound with the microphone and look at its shape.
  5. Write a short explanation of how sound travels.
How this can be used for Math
  1. Find a repeating pattern in a song.
  2. Count beats in a bar.
  3. Make a rhythm using fractions of a beat.
  4. Compare two rhythms and describe the difference.
  5. Draw the pattern on a grid.

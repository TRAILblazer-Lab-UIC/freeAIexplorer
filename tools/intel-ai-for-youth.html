Intel AI for Youth

An Intel program that teaches students aged 8 to 19 both AI skills and AI ethics.

AIGood for AI beginners

The privacy details for this item are unverified. Check which tools and accounts students will use before class.

Overview

Intel's AI for Youth is a program for K-12 students aged 8 to 19 in schools or afterschool settings. The official page lists technical topics such as programming, data science, computer vision, natural language processing, generative AI, and large language models. It also lists social topics such as AI ethics, bias reduction, and critical thinking. No coding, math, or statistics experience is required.

Benefits

For students

  • Students learn how AI works and how it affects people.
  • Students need no earlier coding experience.
  • Students explore ethics and bias alongside technical skills.

For teachers

  • Teachers can use Intel Teaching AI pages for lesson plans and professional development.
  • The program has a technical track and a social track.
  • Search results report over 100 lesson plans, but the official page did not state a lesson count.

For schools

  • Search results describe the program as free, but the program page states no cost.
  • Search results report 250 hours of curriculum for grades 3-12.
  • The vendor does not say which devices are needed.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The program covers computer vision, natural language processing, generative AI, data science, and AI ethics. The vendor does not say where the AI tools run. Students learn the ideas and then use them to think about their own work.

Privacy and safety

The official page says some content requires signing in, and it does not say who needs an account. The materials do not say which tools students use, what data is collected, or where it goes. Access terms and data flow were not verified. No independent review was found. A teacher should check the privacy terms for each tool in a lesson.

Ideas by subject

Select a subject to see five classroom ideas.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Explore a computer vision example and describe how a model finds an object.
  2. Write a short prompt for a generative AI tool and judge the answer.
  3. Sort a small dataset by hand and compare it with a model's sort.
  4. Explain natural language processing to a younger student.
  5. Plan a simple AI project and list the data it would need.
How this can be used for Social Studies
  1. Discuss one example of bias in an AI system.
  2. List ways to reduce bias in a dataset.
  3. Debate whether a school should use AI to grade work.
  4. Interview a family member about how AI affects their job.
  5. Write a rule for fair use of AI in your community.

Avogadro

A free open source molecular editor for building and viewing molecules in 3D.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

Avogadro is a free open source molecular editor and visualizer. Users build molecules, optimize geometry with built-in force fields and view them in 3D. It reads many file formats and handles systems of thousands of atoms. It runs on Windows, macOS and Linux.

Benefits

For students

  • Students build molecules and see their 3D shapes.
  • Students clean up geometry with force fields.
  • Students can use the interface in 13 languages.

For teachers

  • The site has a Teach section with ten activities for general chemistry, organic chemistry and biochemistry.
  • A Hillsborough County lesson uses it in a VSEPR geometry lab.
  • The Teach page warns that some instructions and screenshots may be out of date.

For schools

  • The software is free.
  • It is not available on Chromebooks.
  • IT needs to install it on a desktop or laptop with 3D graphics.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

Avogadro has no AI. Students build skills in modeling and data. They build a structure and use force fields to see how it settles.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed and the internet is used at setup. Avogadro is a local desktop application. The research found no privacy policy and the site does not describe data collection. The Teach page warns that some instructions may be out of date. A teacher should test the install and the 3D graphics on a lab machine before class.

How this can be used for Science
  1. Build a water molecule and measure its bond angle.
  2. Build methane and compare its shape with water.
  3. Use the VSEPR activity to predict a molecular shape and check it.
  4. Optimize a molecule and describe how the shape changes.
  5. Make a short guide for a classmate on how to rotate and measure a molecule.

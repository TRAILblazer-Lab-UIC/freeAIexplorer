NVDA

A free, open source screen reader for blind and low vision users on Windows.

FREEComputingStays on the device

Overview

NVDA is a free, open source screen reader for blind and vision-impaired users on Windows. It reads screen content aloud through eSpeak NG or Windows voices and supports braille displays. The interface is translated into 56 languages. NV Access says over 250,000 people have used it.

Benefits

For students

  • Blind and low vision students can use a Windows computer by listening.
  • Students can use braille displays with the screen reader.
  • Students can pick a voice and a language that suit them.

For teachers

  • NVDA can run from a portable copy or be installed.
  • An add-on store lets teachers extend its features.
  • Windows OCR content recognition can read text in images.

For schools

  • The software is free and supported by donations.
  • It needs 64-bit Windows 10 or 11, at least 4 GB of RAM, and 500 MB of storage.
  • It does not run on Chromebooks, and IT approval may be needed on school machines.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

NVDA has no machine learning features confirmed in the pages read. It is an accessibility tool that reads screens aloud. Students and teachers can build a skill from it, which is how to use a computer through sound and keyboard commands.

Privacy and safety

No account is needed, and the internet is not needed to run it. On first launch NVDA asks whether to send usage statistics to NV Access, and the choice can be changed later under Privacy and Security. It checks for updates over the internet when that is enabled. The research did not check add-ons for AI or cloud features. A teacher should answer the statistics prompt, and check that add-ons come from the add-on store and ask IT to approve them.

How this can be used for Special Education and Accessibility
  1. Learn the basic NVDA keys by opening and closing a document.
  2. Listen to a short article read by NVDA and answer questions about it.
  3. Try two voices and choose the one that is clearest.
  4. Use NVDA to move through a web page by headings.
  5. Practice writing and checking a short paragraph with speech feedback.

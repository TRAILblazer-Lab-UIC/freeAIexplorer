KIBO Robot Kit

A screen-free robot that young children program with tangible blocks.

AIComputingRobotics

Overview

KIBO is a screen-free robot kit from KinderLab. Children program it by connecting tangible blocks. The company targets early childhood and elementary learners from PreK to grade 5. A free five lesson AI curriculum called Thinking with KIBO introduces AI ideas to ages 6 to 9.

Benefits

For students

  • Children program with their hands and need no screen.
  • Children learn sequencing by snapping blocks in order.
  • Children in the AI unit explore automation and sensing with a real robot.

For teachers

  • The kit needs no app and no account.
  • The Exploring with KIBO curriculum gives 60 hours of lessons for grades 3 to 5.
  • Thinking with KIBO gives five one-hour AI lessons at no cost.

For schools

  • Prices are on the KinderLab shop and were not read. The batch note says several hundred USD per kit.
  • The kit needs no internet and no Wi-Fi.
  • IT has no software to approve.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

KIBO has no AI of its own. The robot follows the block program that children build. The Thinking with KIBO lessons use the robot to introduce AI ideas. The five lessons are Automation, Sensing and Organizing, A KIBO Rover, KIBO's Personality, and KIBO Can Help Us.

Privacy and safety

The company page describes tangible blocks and mentions no app or tablet. No data collection was described, and the research read no privacy policy. The robot itself does not run AI. A teacher should ask the vendor for its privacy terms. A teacher should also plan time for children to share the blocks fairly.

How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Teach the robot to follow the steps in the Automation lesson. Describe why the robot repeats them.
  2. Arrange blocks in a new order and predict what KIBO will do.
  3. Use a sensor block to make KIBO react to its surroundings.
  4. Give KIBO a personality by choosing its sounds and moves.
  5. Plan a way KIBO could help the class and program it.
How this can be used for Engineering and Design
  1. Build a track and test where KIBO turns.
  2. Decorate the robot as a rover and test that it still moves.
  3. Fix a program that does not reach the goal.
  4. Compare two block programs and decide which is shorter.
  5. Draw the plan for a new robot job before building it.

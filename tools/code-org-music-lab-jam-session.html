Code.org Music Lab Jam Session

A 45 minute lesson to remix songs with code blocks and an AI beat helper.

AIComputingGood for AI beginners

Overview

Jam Session is a 45 minute Music Lab activity from Code.org. Students remix tracks by popular artists using code blocks. They learn sequencing and functions and can use an AI Beat block to make beats. Code.org lists it for grades 2 to 12 and built it with Amazon.

Benefits

For students

  • Students make music from code in a short lesson.
  • Students learn sequencing, functions, and events.
  • Students see how an AI block continues a beat they start.

For teachers

  • The lesson takes 45 minutes and needs no prep time.
  • Accounts are optional.
  • The lesson plan explains the AI block clearly.

For schools

  • The lesson is free and runs in a browser.
  • It works on Chromebooks, computers, and tablets.
  • Without accounts, the vendor collects only the IP address.

Getting started checklist

Where is the AI in this product?

The AI Beat block was trained on many beats. It continues a starting beat that a student makes. A temperature setting controls how close the new beat stays to the original. Students start a beat, let the AI continue it, and then change it to fit their own song.

Privacy and safety

Accounts are optional. Optional accounts store progress and projects, and without accounts the vendor collects only the IP address. The internet is needed at all times. The policy says it does not sell personal data or show ads. It says the nonprofit is not subject to COPPA and treats school directed accounts as FERPA student records. The research did not confirm whether the AI Beat block runs in the browser or on a server. A teacher should check the CodeAI privacy terms and decide whether students will sign in.

Ideas by subject

How this can be used for Music
  1. Remix a chart track by changing the order of its parts.
  2. Start a beat and let the AI Beat block continue it.
  3. Change the temperature and describe how the beat shifts.
  4. Add an effect to a track and describe how it changes the mood.
  5. Share a remix and tell a partner what you changed.
How this can be used for Computer Science
  1. Use a sequence of blocks to build a song from start to end.
  2. Make a function that plays a short pattern and use it twice.
  3. Trigger a sound with an event block.
  4. Explain what training data an AI beat block might have seen.
  5. Compare a beat you wrote by hand with one the AI made.

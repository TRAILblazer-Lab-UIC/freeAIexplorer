## Overview
This is a teacher pack for five 50 minute sessions called Robotics: Moving Intelligently. Students build and calibrate a cardboard computer controlled robot car driven by a micro:bit. The micro:bit comes preloaded with code for driving forwards, backwards, turning in circles, and following a line. Students calibrate a bias value, calculate the pause time for a full circle, and tune line following speed.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a robot car from cardboard.
- Students calibrate and measure as well as follow steps.
- Students fill in an industry style calibration sheet.

## Benefits for teachers
- The pack says it can run entirely without computers.
- The micro:bit code is preloaded.
- A student workbook and calibration sheet are provided.

## Benefits for schools
- The teacher pack is free.
- The masterclass supplied the vehicle kits. The kit price is not stated.
- No internet or accounts are needed.

## Where is the AI?
This pack has no AI. The car follows preloaded code and a line. Students build skills in sensors and data as they calibrate the car and compare results.

## Privacy and safety
The pack mentions no accounts and no data collection. No internet is needed. Code is preinstalled on the micro:bit. Students need no computer for the core tasks. Extension work needs computers. A teacher should check the batteries and keep the track area clear for the cars.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Build the cardboard car and attach the micro:bit.
2. Test the car on a straight path and note any drift.
3. Tune the bias value until the car drives straight.
4. Test line following on a black line track.
5. Fill in the calibration sheet like a technician.

### computer_science
1. Run the preloaded code in the forwards mode.
2. Switch to the turning mode and watch the car circle.
3. Try the line following mode and note what happens.
4. Change the bias value and compare the runs.
5. Describe in words how the car follows a line.

### math
1. Measure the time the car takes to make a full circle.
2. Calculate the pause time for a 360 degree turn.
3. Test the result and adjust the number.
4. Record the numbers in a table.
5. Explain how a small change in the bias changes the path.

## Overview
Tinkered is a browser based AI engineering platform for electronics. A user describes a device in text. The AI selects parts, draws and simulates the schematic, lays out the circuit board, writes firmware, and designs an enclosure. The site does not mention education or grade levels.

## Benefits for students
- Students see a whole electronics design from one idea.
- Students can test a circuit in a 3D simulation.
- Students can read and edit the firmware the AI writes.

## Benefits for teachers
- It supports Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi Pico, and STM32 boards.
- The vendor describes it as an alternative to Tinkercad Circuits.
- Teachers can export manufacturing files for services such as JLCPCB and PCBWay.

## Benefits for schools
- The site says it is free to start. Paid tiers are not described.
- It runs in a browser. No install is needed.
- The privacy policy excludes users under 13.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Tinkered website in a modern browser.
- [ ] Read the privacy policy and note the under 13 exclusion.
- [ ] Sign up or sign in with a teacher account.
- [ ] Describe a simple device such as a blinking light.
- [ ] Review the parts, schematic, and firmware the AI makes.
- [ ] Run the 3D simulation.
- [ ] Ask the vendor whether Chromebooks work.

## Where is the AI?
The AI generates circuits, picks parts, and writes firmware from a text prompt. The vendor does not say where the AI runs. Students describe a device, check the AI design, and fix errors. The checking step is where they learn.

## Privacy and safety
An internet connection is needed. The policy says it collects name and email for sign in, the designs and code users create, and usage data. It may share data with unnamed service providers for hosting and analytics. The policy says the service is not intended for children under 13 and does not mention COPPA or FERPA. The research did not confirm whether an account is required or which AI provider processes prompts. Check these points with the vendor and avoid student use under 13.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Describe a simple circuit in text and compare the AI schematic with your own sketch.
2. Run the 3D simulation and note what happens when a part is removed.
3. Ask for a device with a sensor and list each part the AI chose.
4. Review the board layout and explain why parts sit where they do.
5. Design an enclosure idea and describe how the circuit fits inside.

### computer_science
1. Read the firmware the AI writes and label what each section does.
2. Change a delay value and watch the simulation respond.
3. Find a mistake in generated code and fix it.
4. Write a prompt that adds a button to the device and test the new code.
5. Compare the code for two boards and note the differences.

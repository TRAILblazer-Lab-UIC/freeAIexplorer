## Overview
KAI is a kit for building a six-legged robot from over 100 pieces. Students train it with a phone or tablet. They record gestures with the camera and sounds with the microphone and link each one to an action. A school supplier lists the kit for grade 5 and up, and other sellers say ages 12 and up.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a walking robot from a 64-page manual.
- Students teach the robot with their own gestures and sounds.
- Students test the trained model in a play mode.

## Benefits for teachers
- The app builds the machine learning model from student samples.
- Five actions keep the training task simple.
- A manual control mode gives a backup when training needs another try.

## Benefits for schools
- Listings run from about 89.90 to 99.95 dollars, plus a smart device.
- A retailer says the app works offline, which the research did not verify.
- A retailer says student recordings are not stored as recognizable images or audio.

## Getting started
- [ ] Buy the KAI kit from Thames and Kosmos or a school supplier.
- [ ] Buy four AAA batteries, which are not included.
- [ ] Plan a full session for the build. One reviewer reported about three hours.
- [ ] Build the robot with the 64-page manual.
- [ ] Insert the batteries and install the companion app on a phone or tablet.
- [ ] Record gestures and sounds for walk forward, walk backward, turn left, turn right, and stop.
- [ ] Test the model in play mode and retrain any action that fails.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a supervised machine learning model that the app trains on student samples. It recognizes gestures and sounds. The model runs on the phone or tablet and the vendor says the app works offline. Students collect examples, test the results, and record better samples.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The app uses the device camera and microphone. Retailer text says the app works fully offline and does not store samples as recognizable images or audio. The research found only retailer claims. It did not find the app's privacy policy or permissions, and the Thames and Kosmos privacy page returned an error. Check the app permissions and test offline use before class. Small parts and batteries need adult supervision with younger students.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Record five gesture samples for walk forward and test them in play mode.
2. Add sounds for a second action and compare them with the gesture samples.
3. Record a poor sample on purpose and watch the robot make mistakes.
4. Retrain with better samples and explain what improved.
5. Write a list of the steps the app takes from sample to robot action.

### engineering
1. Build the six-legged body and test how the legs move.
2. Find a loose part by watching the walk and fix it.
3. Compare walking on a carpet and on a hard floor.
4. Design a short obstacle course for the robot.
5. Write build notes that a new team could follow.

### science
1. Predict how many samples the robot needs and test the guess.
2. Compare how well the robot recognizes a quiet voice and a loud voice.
3. Test whether a different student can control the robot with the same gestures.
4. Record results in a table and look for patterns.
5. Explain how learning from examples differs from following a fixed rule.

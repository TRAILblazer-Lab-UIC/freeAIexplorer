## Overview
KDE Step is an interactive 2D physics simulator. Students place particles, springs, rigid bodies, and forces such as gravity or Coulomb fields. They then watch the scene evolve. It also covers soft bodies, collisions, and molecular dynamics, with graphs, meters, and tracers.

## Benefits for students
- Students see physics ideas play out as they build a scene.
- Students use graphs, meters, and tracers to read the motion.
- Students can change a setting and run the scene again.

## Benefits for teachers
- A review recommends Step for visualizing simple harmonic motion.
- The tool is easy for students and moderate for teachers.
- Teachers can save scenes for demonstrations.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free under GPL-2.0 or later according to the official page.
- It runs on the device and the internet is used only for setup.
- The app pages list Linux only. IT must install it from a Linux source.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find KDE Step on the KDE Education pages.
- [ ] Ask IT to install it on a Linux computer.
- [ ] Open a simple scene with a spring and a mass.
- [ ] Add a graph or a meter to read the motion.
- [ ] Avoid zero mass objects. The official notes say they can crash the app.
- [ ] Note that gas particles do not interact with boxes.
- [ ] Save a scene for a class demonstration.

## Where is the AI?
KDE Step has no AI. It follows physics rules that students set. Students build skills in modeling and data as they test how changes affect a system.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the software runs on the device. KDE says its software generally does not send data unless the user takes an explicit action. Telemetry is opt in and off by default. Windows and macOS builds were not confirmed. A teacher should save scenes to a school folder and test them before class.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Build a mass on a spring and watch simple harmonic motion, as a review recommends.
2. Graph the position over time and describe the pattern.
3. Change the spring strength and compare the graphs.
4. Add gravity to a scene and track a falling object.
5. Collide two bodies and describe what happens to their speeds.

## Overview
This whitepaper is a guide from Central High School in Louisville and Boston Dynamics. It helps teachers teach robotics at secondary level with a Spot robot. The guide covers choosing a classroom use case, funding sources and safety protocols. It also has lesson plans and projects for operating and programming the robot. The authors are Gilbert, Brown, Canaday, Compton and Bringhurst (2023).

## Benefits for students
- Students learn advanced coding with a real walking robot.
- Students in a disabilities program drive Spot around campus.
- Students practice working safely with a large robot.

## Benefits for teachers
- The guide includes lesson plans for operating and programming Spot.
- It covers safety protocols and how to choose a use case.
- A teacher still needs strong support to run a robot of this size.

## Benefits for schools
- The guide lists funding sources for buying a Spot.
- The price of a Spot was not stated in the materials read.
- IT help is likely needed for setup and network access.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need, whether the internet is required, or where robot data goes. News coverage says the robot is driven with a tablet controller, and the research did not review the robot app. The whitepaper page does not say whether a contact form is needed to get it. Spot may use cameras and sensors. A teacher should ask IT and the vendor what data the robot and its app store. Follow the safety protocol and keep students at a safe distance during operation.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Read the guide and choose a classroom use case for Spot.
2. Plan a safe zone and write rules for working near the robot.
3. Study how the robot's legs and sensors help it move.
4. Compare Spot with a wheeled robot for a given task.
5. Write a short proposal for funding a robot for your school.

### computer_science
1. Operate Spot through a short course using the controller.
2. Learn how a program sends commands to the robot.
3. Write and test a simple command sequence.
4. Debug a route that sends the robot off course.
5. Explain to a partner how your code changed the robot's movement.

### special_ed
1. Drive Spot around campus with support from a teacher.
2. Choose a route that matters to you and plan it.
3. Share what you learned about the robot with the class.
4. Practice clear commands as part of a team.
5. Talk about how robots can help people in their daily lives.

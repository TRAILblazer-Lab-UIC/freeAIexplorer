## Overview
Blue-Bot is a programmable floor robot that adds Bluetooth and a free tablet or PC app to the Bee-Bot design. Children can program it with its buttons, with the optional TacTile Reader or with the app. TTS lists it for learners aged 3 and above. Students plan routes on mats and test them.

## Benefits for students
- Students can program with buttons, tiles or a screen.
- Students use repeats and 45 degree turns in the app.
- Students can try built-in games and challenges.

## Benefits for teachers
- The robot has a 256-step memory and audio recording.
- The TacTile Reader offers screen-free tile programming.
- It can detect other Blue-Bots or Bee-Bots.

## Benefits for schools
- The app is free and the robot works with no internet.
- TTS UK lists the robot at 87.99 GBP before VAT. US prices were not checked.
- The app runs on iOS, Android, Windows and Mac.

## Getting started
- [ ] Buy Blue-Bot from TTS or another seller and check the US price.
- [ ] Charge the robot.
- [ ] Set out a mat or a floor grid.
- [ ] Start with the buttons on the robot.
- [ ] Install the free app on a Bluetooth tablet or PC.
- [ ] Pair the robot with the app.
- [ ] Add the TacTile Reader later for tile programming.

## Where is the AI?
Blue-Bot has no AI. The robot follows the steps that children enter by buttons, tiles or the app. Students build skills in sequencing, repeats and debugging.

## Privacy and safety
The robot and TacTile Reader work without an app, and the app connects by Bluetooth. A district listing reportedly says no student account is needed, but the research did not verify this. The materials do not state what the app collects or where its data goes. The research could not read the TTS app privacy policy page and did not review its terms. A teacher should read the TTS app privacy policy before installing the app on student devices.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Program a route with the buttons and then repeat it with the app.
2. Use a repeat in the app to shorten a long route.
3. Predict where a 45 degree turn will send the robot and test the guess.
4. Build a tile program with the TacTile Reader and run it.
5. Debug a route that a partner built and explain the fix.

### math
1. Measure the length of one step and use it to plan a route.
2. Place numbers on the mat and drive the robot to add them.
3. Count the turns in a route and compare two routes.
4. Predict the number of steps to reach a goal and then check.
5. Build a route that traces a shape on the mat.

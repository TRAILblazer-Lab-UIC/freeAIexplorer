## Overview
AutoAuto is a self-driving car platform for teaching computer science and AI. It offers fully virtual courses with no hardware. A physical upgrade adds real AutoAuto cars. The site states 180 or more hours of curriculum, video lessons, and auto-graded challenges.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn Python through self-driving car projects.
- Students study image processing, sonar, classification, and neural networks.
- Students can start virtually and add a real car later.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can track student progress.
- Challenges are graded automatically.
- Open curriculum on GitHub has Beginner units 1 to 4 and Intermediate units 5 to 8.

## Benefits for schools
- Pricing is not published. The site directs buyers to a quote page.
- Virtual courses need no hardware.
- No independent classroom review was found.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the AutoAuto site and request a quote.
- [ ] Ask what the quote covers and whether cars are included.
- [ ] Read the privacy policy and ask about student data.
- [ ] Create teacher and student accounts for the course.
- [ ] Try a virtual lesson yourself.
- [ ] Decide whether to buy cars for physical projects.
- [ ] Check the grade fit. The vendor says grades 3 through college.

## Where is the AI?
The AI includes image classification, computer vision, and neural networks for driving. The vendor does not say where the AI runs. Students write Python, train a model, and test it on a virtual or real car. They then improve the model.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are required. The official page shows a login link and teacher progress tracking, which suggests vendor held student accounts. The research could not confirm this. The materials do not state what student data is collected or where it goes. The research reviewed no privacy policy. A teacher should read the privacy policy, ask the vendor about data handling, and check whether the internet is needed all the time.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Complete a beginner unit and write what the car learned.
2. Write Python code that reads a sensor and makes a car decision.
3. Train an image classifier and test it on new pictures.
4. Compare two models and explain which drives better.
5. Share a project with a classmate and explain the code.

### engineering
1. Describe how sonar finds an obstacle.
2. Test a car on a track and record the lap time.
3. Change a setting and observe how the car turns.
4. Design a track that is hard for a self-driving car.
5. Suggest how a real car could use more sensors.

## Overview
Growing with KIBO is a PreK to grade 2 curriculum with 60 hours of standards-aligned lessons. Exploring with KIBO is a grades 3 to 5 curriculum with 60 hours of cross-curricular activities. Themed guides add topics such as song and dance, art and literacy. The lessons use the screen-free KIBO robot and its extension sets.

## Benefits for students
- Students code with wooden blocks and no screen.
- Students link coding to math, science, social studies and English language arts.
- Students make art, songs and dances with the robot in themed guides.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each curriculum supplies 60 hours of ready lessons.
- A free Floor Map Activity Guide has 8 activities.
- Themed guides such as Showtime with KIBO offer extra units.

## Benefits for schools
- The guides are sold separately. One page listed Exploring with KIBO at 84 USD and another source said 67 USD.
- The Floor Map guide is free.
- The lessons need no internet, devices or accounts.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Growing with KIBO and Exploring with KIBO guides on the KIBO site.
- [ ] Download the free Floor Map Activity Guide.
- [ ] Obtain KIBO robot kits. The guides do not include the robots.
- [ ] Check which themed guides need extension sets.
- [ ] Choose the first lesson for your grade band.
- [ ] Run the lesson with small groups.
- [ ] Check the CSTA and ISTE mappings. The mappings for Exploring with KIBO were listed as coming soon.

## Where is the AI?
These guides have no AI. KIBO follows the sequence of blocks that children scan. Children build skills in sequencing and logic.

## Privacy and safety
KIBO is screen free and programmed with wooden blocks. The pages read do not describe data collection or accounts. A privacy policy was not reviewed. A teacher should confirm that the school owns the KIBO kits and extension sets that a guide needs. A teacher should also check the current price before ordering.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a block sequence that makes KIBO drive to a mark on a floor map.
2. Use a repeat block to shorten a long sequence.
3. Add a sensor block and make KIBO react to a hand.
4. Debug a sequence that sends KIBO the wrong way.
5. Teach a partner how to scan a program.

### ela
1. Retell a short story by programming KIBO to act out each scene.
2. Build a sequence that shows the beginning, middle and end of a story.
3. Write a caption for each robot move in a story map.
4. Use a literacy themed guide to make KIBO act out a character.
5. Read a short script aloud and match robot moves to the lines.

### visual_arts
1. Attach a marker and program KIBO to draw a design.
2. Build a costume or decoration for KIBO and describe it.
3. Plan a robot parade with a path for each robot.
4. Compare two drawings made by different programs.
5. Display the robot art in a class gallery.

### music
1. Program KIBO to dance to a short song.
2. Use sound blocks to make a short tune.
3. Choreograph a robot dance with a beginning, middle and end.
4. Match a robot move to each beat of a clapping pattern.
5. Perform a class show using the Showtime guide.

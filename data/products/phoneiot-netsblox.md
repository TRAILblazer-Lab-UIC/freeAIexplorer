## Overview
PhoneIoT is a free mobile app from Vanderbilt University that connects a phone or tablet to NetsBlox projects. NetsBlox is a networked block language built on Snap!. Student programs read phone sensors such as the accelerometer and gyroscope. Programs can also draw buttons and joysticks on the phone and receive messages from them.

## Benefits for students
- Students use a phone they already know as a sensor.
- Students build touch controls for their own programs.
- Students can control a robot remotely, such as with the RoboScape service.

## Benefits for teachers
- Block programming keeps the coding approachable.
- Teachers can use both a phone and a browser in one lesson.
- Setup needs a teacher to install the app and connect it.

## Benefits for schools
- The app and NetsBlox are free.
- Both devices need internet access at all times.
- The iOS app requires iOS 11.0 or later.

## Getting started
- [ ] Install the PhoneIoT app on a phone or tablet. The App Store lists a free iOS app, and search results report an Android app.
- [ ] Open NetsBlox in a browser.
- [ ] Ask IT to confirm both devices can reach the internet.
- [ ] Connect the app to a NetsBlox project through the PhoneIoT service blocks.
- [ ] Read one sensor and show the value on the stage.
- [ ] Decide whether students will use NetsBlox accounts. They are optional.
- [ ] Review the NetsBlox terms with IT.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in PhoneIoT. The app sends sensor readings to the student program. Students build sensor, data, and logic skills.

## Privacy and safety
The App Store label says Data Not Collected, which is a developer statement that Apple has not verified. Sensor readings travel to the NetsBlox project the student writes, and the service runs in the cloud. The internet is needed at all times. An account is optional. The research could not read the NetsBlox privacy policy and did not confirm where sensor data is stored. A teacher should read the NetsBlox terms and check what students save online.

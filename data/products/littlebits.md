## Overview
littleBits are magnetic electronic building blocks that snap together. Sphero owns the brand and still lists classroom packs and individual Bits for sale. Sphero ended cloudBit support and removed several littleBits apps from the app stores. Coding for the codeBit now goes through the littleBits Fuse app, and content is hosted in littleBits Classroom.

## Benefits for students
- Students snap blocks together to make circuits.
- Students invent working devices.
- Students learn how electricity moves through a circuit.

## Benefits for teachers
- littleBits Classroom offers lessons.
- The blocks need no soldering or wires.
- The materials rate the teacher learning curve as easy.

## Benefits for schools
- Individual Bits were mostly 10 to 36 USD on a Sphero reseller page.
- A single Invention Kit was 479 USD and a 15-kit Invention Class Pack was 7185 USD.
- Availability varies by kit.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find littleBits on the Sphero reseller page.
- [ ] Check each kit's current availability before buying.
- [ ] Unpack a kit and sort the Bits.
- [ ] Snap a power Bit to an output Bit.
- [ ] Open littleBits Classroom for lessons.
- [ ] Use the Fuse app for the codeBit.
- [ ] Check which apps still work on your devices.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in littleBits. The Bits follow the circuit that students build. Students build skills in logic and sensors by connecting inputs to outputs.

## Privacy and safety
The basic snap together blocks run locally and work without an app. The cloudBit servers were shut down. The data handling of the littleBits Classroom site and the Fuse app was not checked, and the materials do not say whether accounts are needed. A teacher should check the littleBits Classroom and Fuse app privacy terms. A teacher should also confirm that the apps run on school devices.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Build a circuit that turns on a light with a button.
2. Invent a device that solves a classroom problem.
3. Test two circuit designs and compare them.
4. Sketch a circuit before snapping it together.
5. Present the invention to the class.

### science
1. Build a circuit and describe the path of electricity.
2. Test which Bits make light, sound or motion.
3. Predict what happens when a Bit is removed.
4. Compare an open circuit and a closed circuit.
5. Record results in a simple chart.

### computer_science
1. Use the Fuse app to program a codeBit.
2. Change one line of code and observe the result.
3. Combine a sensor Bit and an output Bit with code.
4. Explain the code to a partner.
5. Debug a program that does not light the output.

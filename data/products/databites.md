## Overview
DataBites is a supervised learning simulation. Students sort food data to train and test a classifier. It shows how labeled data affects model performance and what happens when a model misclassifies. It needs printed ingredient cards, a worksheet, scissors, and an optional glue stick. It is one of several activities on the Northwestern AI Unplugged site.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a classifier by hand.
- Students see why labels matter.
- Students look at what goes wrong when a model misclassifies.

## Benefits for teachers
- The site provides a lesson plan and resource links.
- The materials are simple paper.
- A middle school research paper supports the activity.

## Benefits for schools
- The activity is free.
- It needs no computers and no accounts.
- No student data is collected.

## Where is the AI?
The activity has no software and no AI runs on any device. Students act as the classifier and learn from labeled cards. They then test it on new cards. This builds skills in supervised learning, labels, and spotting mistakes.

## Privacy and safety
The AI Unplugged site states that the activities need no computer and no accounts. The materials are printed, and the research found no student data collected. The internet is needed to download the files. A teacher should check the food cards for allergies or culture fit. A teacher should also tell students that this is a model of AI and not a real system.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Sort the food cards into labeled groups and write the rule for each group.
2. Test your sorting rule on new cards.
3. Find a card the rule misclassifies and explain why.
4. Change a label on purpose and see how the results shift.
5. Compare your sorter with a partner's sorter.

### science
1. Group foods by ingredients and explain what the groups share.
2. Test whether a new food fits an existing group.
3. Find a food that could belong to two groups.
4. Record how many cards each group sorted correctly.
5. Explain why scientists need clear labels for their data.

## Overview
Cubelets are magnetic robot blocks in Sense, Think and Act categories. They snap together to build robots without coding. The company lists 17 kinds of blocks for ages four and up. Free standards-aligned lesson plans, activity cards and training cover PreK to grade 12.

## Benefits for students
- Students build working robots by snapping blocks together.
- Students change a robot by swapping one block.
- Students learn how sensing, thinking and acting fit together.

## Benefits for teachers
- Free lessons are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 on the Educator Resource Hub.
- A free 10-day review kit is offered to educators.
- Older students can use optional Blockly and C coding through a free app.

## Benefits for schools
- Hats cost 19 to 24 USD and lesson bundles 199 USD each on the official site.
- No account is needed and no internet is needed.
- Pinellas County Florida reports Cubelets in every elementary science lab.

## Where is the AI?
Cubelets have no AI. Each block does one job and the robot follows the way students connect the blocks. Students build skills in systems thinking, sensors and logic.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed for the blocks, and the research found no student data collected by them. The optional apps connect through a Bluetooth block, and their data practices were not reviewed. No privacy policy was reviewed. A teacher should check the app privacy information and Bluetooth support before installing it.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Build a robot that reacts to light and describe what it senses.
2. Compare two robots that use different Sense blocks.
3. Test how a robot reacts to a flashlight and record the results.
4. Draw a diagram of the Sense, Think and Act parts of a robot.
5. Predict how a robot behaves before testing it.

### computer_science
1. Use a Cubelets challenge from the Introducing Computer Science unit.
2. Swap a Think block and describe how the robot changes.
3. Sketch the flow of information from a Sense block to an Act block.
4. Use the free app to view a robot's behavior.
5. Try Blockly in the app and change one rule.

### engineering
1. Build a robot that stops before a wall.
2. Design a robot for a simple task and test it.
3. Improve a failed build and record each change.
4. Compare two robot designs for one job.
5. Present a finished robot and explain each block.

## Overview
This is a one-hour workshop guide for coding a dance in Scratch. It uses the Imagine, Create, and Share structure. Students choose a dancer sprite, add music from the Sound library, and code a dance sequence. Printable Let's Dance cards go with the workshop.

## Benefits for students
- Students finish a creative project in one hour.
- Students pick their own dancer and music.
- Students learn sequence and loops through dance.

## Benefits for teachers
- A search snippet gives 10 minutes to Imagine and 40 to Create.
- Printable cards support students at their own pace.
- The guide is translated into other languages.

## Benefits for schools
- The workshop is free.
- Scratch runs in a browser or the desktop app.
- No IT involvement is listed.

## Where is the AI?
This guide has no AI. Students build sequencing and loop skills as they code a dance. They also use events to start the music.

## Privacy and safety
The guide works in the Scratch web editor or Scratch Desktop. An account is optional, and saving to the online editor needs a Scratch account. The research did not read the Scratch privacy policy. The materials do not state what data is collected or whether the internet is needed. A teacher should check whether students will save projects online and whether school rules allow it.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Choose a dancer sprite and code a short dance sequence.
2. Add a loop so the dance repeats.
3. Add a shadow effect and describe how it changes the look.
4. Trade projects with a partner and add one move.
5. Share the finished dance with the class and explain the blocks.

### music
1. Pick music from the Sound library that fits a dance mood.
2. Match each dance move to a beat in the music.
3. Compare two songs and explain which fits the dance better.
4. Start the music with a block and time the moves to it.
5. Describe how the tempo of a song changes the dance.

## Overview
JetAuto is a ROS robot car and JetHexa is a ROS hexapod. Both use an NVIDIA Jetson Nano and an Orbbec Astra+ 3D camera. Reported features include mapping, obstacle avoidance, navigation, color and tag tracking, and voice interaction. They suit advanced high school students. The vendor does not state a grade range.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with the same ROS tools used in real robotics.
- Students can try mapping, navigation, and tracking.
- Students train models with YOLO and run them with TensorRT.

## Benefits for teachers
- Control by Android and iOS apps or a wireless controller gives easy first demos.
- ROS tutorials guide the setup.
- A vendor article says Hiwonder STEAM products are used in over 2,000 schools. This is a vendor claim.

## Benefits for schools
- Prices were not found in the sources read.
- The AI runs on the robot and the internet is needed at setup only.
- A teacher will need strong Linux and ROS skills.

## Where is the AI?
The robots use AI for object tracking, mapping, voice commands, and human body recognition. Support lists OpenCV, MediaPipe, YOLO, and TensorRT. The AI runs on the Jetson Nano on the robot. Students write and tune the code that uses what the robot sees and hears.

## Privacy and safety
No account is described and the internet is used at setup. The article says processing runs on the Jetson on the robot. Newer Hiwonder products add ChatGPT features, but the article does not say these two models do. The research did not review the phone app or a privacy policy. The robot has a camera and a microphone array. A teacher should ask the vendor whether any feature uses the cloud. A teacher should also decide where the robot is used and what is recorded.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Run a ROS tutorial and describe how the robot builds a map.
2. Use color tracking to make the robot follow a colored object.
3. Read a ROS node and explain what it sends and receives.
4. Train a YOLO model on a small set of class objects.
5. Test the voice commands and list which words work best.

### engineering
1. Assemble the robot car and check each wheel.
2. Compare how the car and the hexapod move over the same floor.
3. Test obstacle avoidance on a course built by students.
4. Measure how accurate the 3D camera is at different distances.
5. Plan a delivery task for the robot and test it.

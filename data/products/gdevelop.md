## Overview
GDevelop is a no-code 2D game engine that uses visual events. It runs in a browser or as a desktop app. GDevelop for Education is a paid plan with a built-in curriculum, classroom organization, and anonymous student accounts. It suits middle and high school students who want to build games.

## Benefits for students
- Students build playable games with visual events and no typed code.
- Students learn logic by linking conditions and actions.
- Students can work together in real time.

## Benefits for teachers
- The education plan includes a built-in curriculum.
- Teachers organize classes inside the plan.
- Anonymous student accounts mean teachers need not collect student emails.

## Benefits for schools
- The engine is free. The education plan is paid and listed as very low per student, with a quote from sales.
- Each student gets 100 cloud projects.
- The plan supports a student data privacy agreement.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the GDevelop site and read the education page.
- [ ] Try the free engine in a browser first.
- [ ] Ask sales for a quote for the education plan.
- [ ] Ask IT to install the desktop app on school computers, or use the browser editor.
- [ ] Set up the teacher subscription and create a class.
- [ ] Give students anonymous accounts.
- [ ] Review the GDevelop education resources before the first lesson.

## Where is the AI?
GDevelop has no AI in the materials reviewed. Students build logic skills by writing events that react to actions in the game. The editor runs on the device or in the browser and cloud projects are stored on the vendor's servers.

## Privacy and safety
The education page says students get full anonymous accounts, with no email or personal data collected. That is a vendor statement. The plan supports a student data privacy agreement or a custom one. Cloud projects live on the vendor's servers and desktop projects stay on the device. The privacy policy page returned 404 and the research did not read it. The research also did not confirm whether the free tier needs accounts. A teacher should ask for the agreement in writing and confirm which projects are stored in the cloud.

3. Use a variable to keep score and show it on screen.

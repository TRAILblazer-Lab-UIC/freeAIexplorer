## Overview
Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy offers Certified K-12 Robotics Programs with matching teacher training. The program page says curricula for physical robots are free through the CS-STEM Network. Teacher courses run live online or asynchronously, and each is 36 hours. Courses cover Arduino ShieldBot, Jetson Nano JetBot AI, LEGO CS and AI, SPIKE, VEX, and REV DUO.

## Benefits for students
- Students can earn a certification.
- Students can use physical or virtual robots.
- Some courses cover AI vision.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can become CMRA Certified Instructors.
- Live and self-paced formats are offered.
- The program page says it is CSTA accredited.

## Benefits for schools
- Curricula for physical robots are free per the program page.
- Teacher course costs vary. A search snippet shows up to 1,299 USD.
- Teachers must be at least 18.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Certified K-12 Robotics Programs page on the Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy site.
- [ ] Choose the robot platform your school owns.
- [ ] Register through the CS2N digital store or by purchase order.
- [ ] Create a CS2N account.
- [ ] Read the CS2N privacy policy.
- [ ] Choose a live or self-paced course.
- [ ] Gather the robot hardware before the first session.

## Where is the AI?
AI is optional and appears in some courses, such as AI vision and Jetson Nano AI. The CS2N site runs in the vendor cloud. Students and teachers use the AI on their own robots and then program it to respond. Other courses teach robotics with no AI.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts and internet access are required. Teachers register through the CS2N store, and students use CS2N accounts that teachers can generate. CS2N collects name, email, username, group membership, course activity, progress, and uploaded evidence. Data goes to the vendor. The policy says it does not sell or rent personal information. It allows use by children under 13 with consent from a parent, school, district, or teacher. It does not name COPPA or say where data is stored. A teacher should read the CS2N policy and check what students upload.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Build a robot from a certified program and test its movement.
2. Follow a robotics curriculum and keep a design journal.
3. Compare two robot platforms and list their strengths.
4. Plan a robot task and measure how well it works.
5. Present a robot project to earn a student certification step.

### computer_science
1. Program a robot to follow a path with blocks or code.
2. Add a sensor and write code that reacts to it.
3. Use an AI vision course to teach a robot a new object.
4. Debug a program and describe what you changed.
5. Compare block code with text code for the same task.

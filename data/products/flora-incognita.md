## Overview
Flora Incognita is an AI supported plant identification app. It identifies more than 30,000 plant species from a photo. Users can save observations that help science. The site calls the app suitable for schools, universities, and nature conservation.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn the names of the plants around their school.
- Students read fact sheets on plant traits, protection status, and distribution.
- Students see that their own observations can help real research.

## Benefits for teachers
- The app is simple enough for a quick field lesson.
- Fact sheets give teachers ready facts about each plant.
- The citizen science link gives a real purpose to a nature walk.

## Benefits for schools
- The site says the app is totally free and has no ads.
- The site says the app works without a constant internet connection.
- Students need a smartphone with a camera, which a school may need to lend.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find Flora Incognita in the Android or iOS app store.
- [ ] Read the app privacy policy before classroom use.
- [ ] Install the app on a teacher phone and test it on a plant.
- [ ] Decide whether students will use school devices or their own.
- [ ] Choose a safe outdoor area and plan the walk.
- [ ] Have students photograph plants and read the fact sheets.
- [ ] Decide as a class which observations to save for science.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is plant image recognition. It looks at a photo and suggests a species. The vendor does not say whether the model runs on the phone or in the cloud. Students check each suggestion against the fact sheet and their own close look at the plant.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not say whether accounts are needed. They also do not say where photos and locations go. A batch note said photos are sent to servers, and the pages read do not confirm that. The site legal notice covers only the website and points to external privacy policies that the research did not read. Saved observations are shared for research. A teacher should find and read the app privacy policy and decide which students may save observations.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Photograph five plants on school grounds and record the species the app suggests.
2. Compare the app's answer with a field guide and note any mismatch.
3. Read a fact sheet and write a short profile of one plant, including its protection status.
4. Map where each plant was found and look for patterns in the habitat.
5. Save an observation for science and discuss how citizen scientists help researchers.

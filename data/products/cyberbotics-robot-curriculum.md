## Overview
This is a Wikibooks book on intelligent robots built on the Webots simulator and the e-puck robot. Part I covers what AI is, what robots are, and the e-puck and Webots. Part II covers computer setup, exercise chapters at several levels, and Cognitive Benchmarks. The Wikibooks page was last edited in 2018, and the SourceForge project was last updated in 2015.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a simulated robot with no hardware.
- Students move from beginner to advanced exercises.
- Students learn what AI and robots are in theory chapters.

## Benefits for teachers
- The content is free under open licenses.
- Exercises run at Beginner, Novice, Intermediate, and Advanced levels.
- The curriculum is older. Teachers should test the exercises first.

## Benefits for schools
- Content and software are free.
- Webots is open source under Apache-2.0.
- IT will need to install Webots on Windows, Mac, or Linux.

## Where is the AI?
The book teaches what AI is and covers cognitive benchmarks. The simulation runs on the local computer. Students program simulated e-puck robots and test how their code behaves. A real e-puck is optional.

## Privacy and safety
The materials state no account and no student data collection, but the research did not formally confirm this. The book is a static Wikibooks page, and Webots runs as a desktop application. The internet is used to read the book and to download Webots. No privacy policy was found. A teacher should check that the Webots install comes from the official source. A teacher should also test the exercises with a current version.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Read the chapter on what AI is and write a plain definition.
2. Program a simulated e-puck to avoid walls in a Beginner exercise.
3. Change a robot behavior and compare the two runs.
4. Work through a Cognitive Benchmark and describe what it tests.
5. Compare an Intermediate exercise with a Beginner one and list new ideas.

### engineering
1. Examine the e-puck design and list its sensors.
2. Test sensor readings in the simulator and record them.
3. Redesign a robot's behavior for a new task.
4. Compare two control strategies on the same course.
5. Explain how a simulation helps before building a real robot.

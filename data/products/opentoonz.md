## Overview
OpenToonz is a free open source 2D animation tool. It is based on Toonz, which Studio Ghibli customized. Users draw, scan, ink, and composite frame by frame or cutout animation. The official page says schools may use it at no cost.

## Benefits for students
- Students use the same kind of tool that professional studios use.
- Students can draw by hand and bring their pictures to life.
- Students can try both frame by frame and cutout animation.

## Benefits for teachers
- A teacher on the OpenToonz forum reported that elementary students picked it up quickly and stayed engaged.
- The tool has raster and vector drawing levels and many effects.
- The interface is large. Teachers should plan time to learn it.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free for school and commercial use.
- It runs on the local computer after the first download.
- IT will likely need to approve the install on managed Windows or Mac machines.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the installer from the official OpenToonz page on a PC or Mac.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install. No Chromebook or Linux build is offered on the official page.
- [ ] Check the computer needs. Windows 7 SP1 or later 64-bit, or macOS 10.14 or later, with 4GB RAM and 500MB of storage.
- [ ] Set up a drawing tablet if you have one. It is helpful.
- [ ] Open the program and draw a ball in the first frame.
- [ ] Draw a second and a third frame and play them back.
- [ ] Save the scene and export a short movie.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in OpenToonz. It is a drawing and animation tool. Students make each frame themselves and learn sequencing as they build motion one picture at a time.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the program runs on the local computer. The internet is used for setup. The research found no privacy policy and the official page says nothing about telemetry. The installer follows separate terms of use that the research did not read. A teacher should ask IT to read those terms and check how students save and share their finished movies.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Draw a bouncing ball in six frames and play it back, as elementary students did in the forum report.
2. Animate a character blinking in a short loop.
3. Ink a pencil sketch on a vector level and compare it with a raster drawing.
4. Create a cutout puppet and move its parts across the screen.
5. Make a short animated greeting card for a family member.

### theater_media
1. Storyboard a ten second animated scene and then build it in the program.
2. Add effects and compositing to place a drawn character over a background.
3. Export the animation as a movie and screen it for the class.
4. Time a mouth movement to a recorded line of dialogue.
5. Produce a short title sequence for a school video.

## Overview
This is a three-page Grade 5 lesson plan under the Ontario Human Health and Body Systems strand. Students code a micro:bit as a pedometer and as a BPM counter to imitate a smartwatch. They then run experiments that record changes in body systems with activity. The micro:bit has no heart rate hardware. The BPM tracker counts beats that the student enters while a timer runs.

## Benefits for students
- Students code a real device that they can carry.
- Students test how activity changes their pulse.
- Students discuss how wearable health technology works.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teacher coding sheets guide the pedometer and the BPM tracker.
- The plan links to Ontario Science and Technology A1.3, A2.1, and B1.2.
- Science North is an agency of the Government of Ontario.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- The class needs computers and a class set of micro:bits.
- The board price was not confirmed.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is working with sensor data. Students count steps and beats and compare the numbers. That practice shows how wearable devices gather data.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The lesson runs on the micro:bit and in the MakeCode browser editor, and the internet is used at setup. Measurements stay on the board. The research did not read the MakeCode privacy policy. Pulse counts are personal health information. A teacher should keep student pulse data within the class and let students opt out of any task.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Code a pedometer and compare steps for a walk and a run, as the Ontario strand suggests.
2. Record how body systems change with activity.
3. Compare class pulse data before and after exercise.
4. Write a claim about activity and pulse and support it with data.
5. Discuss how a smartwatch might measure the same things.

### health_pe
1. Count your pulse for a set time and enter the beats on the micro:bit.
2. Record your pulse at rest and after a short activity.
3. Compare resting and active BPM in a table.
4. Use the pedometer during a class walk and note the steps.
5. Write a short plan for staying active based on your data.

### computer_science
1. Code a pedometer in MakeCode.
2. Use button A to start a timer for the BPM tracker.
3. Add a counter that goes up with each button press.
4. Debug a program by running it and fixing one block.
5. Compare the pedometer and the BPM tracker and describe what each one counts.

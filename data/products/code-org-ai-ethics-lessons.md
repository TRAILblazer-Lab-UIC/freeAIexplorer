## Overview
This short Code.org unit centers on Lesson 1, Our AI Code of Ethics, which runs 45 minutes. Student groups research an AI ethics issue and then help write a class code of ethics. Teachers pick one of three final activities. The same lesson appears in CS Discoveries Unit 7 and in How AI Works.

## Benefits for students
- Students research a real AI ethics issue.
- Students practice writing and agreeing on shared rules.
- Students learn about algorithmic bias.

## Benefits for teachers
- A slide deck and student resources are ready to use.
- Teachers choose from three final activities.
- The lesson fits in one class period.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- It works on Chromebooks and other computers.
- A teacher account is optional.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no AI tool that students run. It teaches about AI ethics and algorithmic bias through research and discussion. The lesson content is hosted in the vendor's cloud. Students build the skill of weighing the impact of an AI system and writing rules for fair use.

## Privacy and safety
Student accounts are optional and store progress. Without an account only an IP address is collected. The data goes to the vendor, and the internet is needed throughout. The policy says the nonprofit is not subject to COPPA but adds protections, and it treats school directed accounts as student records. It lists language model providers as service providers. This lesson is research and discussion based. The AI tool data paths do not apply. A teacher should decide whether students need accounts. A teacher should also have students research with school approved sources.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Research how an AI system can show bias and report to the group.
2. Write one rule for building a fair AI system.
3. Compare two AI uses and decide which needs more oversight.
4. Present a group finding to the class in two minutes.
5. Revise a rule after hearing feedback from peers.

### social_studies
1. Research how AI is used in a community service such as policing or health.
2. Debate who should make the rules about AI.
3. Write a class code with rules that protect people.
4. Compare the class code with a real company code of conduct.
5. Interview an adult about how AI affects their work.

### ela
1. Write a clear rule for the class code in plain words.
2. Read an article about AI and summarize its main claim.
3. Draft a persuasive paragraph on why AI needs ethics rules.
4. Edit a classmate's rule for clarity.
5. Present your group's research with a short speech.

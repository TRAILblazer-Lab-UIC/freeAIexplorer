## Overview
Upscayl is a free, open source desktop app that enlarges and sharpens low resolution images. It uses AI models and supports batch upscaling. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux and needs a Vulkan compatible GPU. Students use it to improve images for art and media projects.

## Benefits for students
- Students can make a small image larger and clearer.
- Students can try different models and compare results.
- Students can upscale a whole folder of images in one batch.

## Benefits for teachers
- The app is simple to use after install.
- Custom models can be added by advanced users.
- A command line backend is available for technical use.

## Benefits for schools
- The desktop app is free.
- Images are processed on the school computer.
- A discrete GPU is needed, and most integrated GPUs are not supported. IT will likely need to install it.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Upscayl download page.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install.
- [ ] Check that the computer has a Vulkan compatible GPU.
- [ ] Download the installer for Windows, macOS, or Linux.
- [ ] Run the installer and open the app.
- [ ] Test it on one low resolution image.
- [ ] Avoid the separate paid Cloud service for student work.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is an image super resolution model built on Real-ESRGAN. It runs on the local GPU. Students choose a model, run it, and compare the result with the original. The Cloud service is separate and uploads images.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed for the desktop app and the internet is used at install. The privacy policy the research read covers only the Upscayl Cloud service. That service needs an account and uploads images to AWS S3, Firebase, and Google Cloud. The policy does not describe the desktop app and the research did not confirm desktop telemetry. The offline claim comes from independent coverage and not the official site. Keep students off Cloud. Ask IT to check the desktop app for network activity.

## Overview
This is a 60 minute in-person activity write-up from Actua for grades 2 to 7. It shows how machines learn from labeled data. Students sort images and use visual cues to train a machine by example. The facilitator uses a projector and slide deck, and learners use paper and pencils.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how a machine learns from labeled examples.
- Students work in pairs or groups of three or four.
- Students try the Quick, Draw! game to see a machine guess.

## Benefits for teachers
- The write-up and slide deck guide a full hour.
- A short OxfordSparks video of 2 minutes 19 seconds introduces supervised learning.
- Actua also publishes What is AI? write-ups and versions for ages 8 to 12.

## Benefits for schools
- The PDF is a free download.
- The sorting needs only paper and pencils.
- The internet is needed during the lesson for Quick, Draw! and the video.

## Where is the AI?
The sorting activity has no software. Quick, Draw! is a browser game that shows a machine guessing drawings. Students act as the machine and learn from labeled images. They build skills in pattern spotting and in training by example.

## Privacy and safety
The core sorting activity is on paper. The write-up also lists Google Quick, Draw! and an online video. Both need the internet and run on Google and video host servers. The write-up does not say whether an account is needed or how student data is handled for those tools. A teacher should check Quick, Draw! with IT and use a teacher computer for the demo.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Sort a set of images into two labeled groups and explain the clues you used.
2. Train a partner machine with five labeled examples and test it on a new image.
3. Draw a quick sketch in Quick, Draw! and see if the machine guesses it.
4. Compare a well labeled group with a messy one and see how the machine does.
5. Explain in a short talk how a machine learns by example.

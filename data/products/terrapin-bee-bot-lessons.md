## Overview
This is a library of lesson plans for the Bee-Bot and Blue-Bot floor robots from Terrapin. The About page counts 200 lesson plans and more than 600 activities across 10 subject areas for Pre-K through grade 2. Lessons are printable PDFs and link to mats in the Bee-Bot Online Emulator. Young students move a robot across a mat to practice a subject.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice counting, science, and health with a robot.
- Students learn to give step-by-step directions.
- Students can use an online emulator in place of a robot.

## Benefits for teachers
- The library lists lessons such as Counting and Number Match, Life Cycle of a Frog, and Power Up Electricity.
- Lessons are printable PDFs.
- The table of contents has a Lesson Standards section.

## Benefits for schools
- A one year site license subscription is needed. The About page lists no price.
- A 30 day free trial is listed.
- Bee-Bot or Blue-Bot hardware is sold separately.

## Where is the AI?
This library has no AI. Students press buttons to give the robot a list of steps. They build sequencing skills as they plan the path.

## Privacy and safety
The online product needs a license key that gives one year of access, and licensed teachers log in. The pages do not describe student data. The research did not review the Terrapin privacy policy or how the emulator handles data. The page does not say how much internet access is needed. Check the account terms and the privacy policy before use. Decide who creates the account for very young students.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Count backwards from 20 to 0 as the Bee-Bot moves on a number mat.
2. Skip count by twos by moving the robot to each stop.
3. Match each number card to a square and program the robot to go there.
4. Move the robot to shapes on the mat and name each shape.
5. Tell the time on a clock mat by driving the robot to the right hour.

### science
1. Move the robot through the life cycle of a frog in order.
2. Name the parts of a flower as the robot visits each one.
3. Program a path that connects a battery to a light on an electricity mat.
4. Talk about why the robot needs power to move.
5. Draw your own science mat and test it.

### health_pe
1. Drive the robot to each food group on a mat.
2. Sort healthy and less healthy foods and program a path to each pile.
3. Name a food in each group when the robot stops.
4. Plan a healthy plate and move the robot to each item.
5. Tell a partner why each food belongs in its group.

### computer_science
1. Press the arrow buttons to move the Bee-Bot to a square.
2. Predict where the robot will stop before pressing go.
3. Fix a path that sent the robot to the wrong square.
4. Give a partner a set of steps and watch them test it.
5. Try the same path in the online emulator.

## Overview
KiCad is a free, open source electronic design suite for schematic capture and printed circuit board layout. It runs on Windows, Linux, and macOS. It outputs Gerber and IPC-2581 files. A 2024 study used KiCad with 28 students aged 16 to 17 to design and simulate circuits in a physics course.

## Benefits for students
- Students use the same kind of tool that engineers use.
- Students draw a circuit and then lay it out on a board.
- Students export files that a board maker can use.

## Benefits for teachers
- A K-12 Fab Labs forum reply recommends KiCad for student board design.
- The study noted that students had difficulty designing and simulating circuits.
- The interface is dense for beginners, and teachers rate the learning curve as advanced.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free under GPL v3.
- It runs offline on desktop and laptop computers.
- Chromebooks are not listed as supported.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download KiCad from the official site.
- [ ] Ask IT to install it on a test computer.
- [ ] Open a sample project and look at the schematic.
- [ ] Draw a simple circuit with an LED and a resistor.
- [ ] Turn it into a board layout.
- [ ] Note that milling boards may need extra CAM software, according to the forum thread.
- [ ] Plan a short first lesson for students.

## Where is the AI?
KiCad has no AI. Students build skills in logic and design as they plan how parts connect. The tool checks the rules of a circuit that the student defines.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used for setup. Projects are files on the local computer. The About page does not mention telemetry or data collection, and the privacy page returned a 404. A teacher should supervise soldering and any board making.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Draw a schematic for a simple LED circuit.
2. Lay out the circuit on a small board.
3. Check the design for errors with the built in checker.
4. Export Gerber files and view them.
5. Design a small circuit for a class project and present it.

### science
1. Design a power supply circuit, as the physics course did for a phone.
2. Simulate a simple circuit and compare the results to a hand calculation.
3. Change a resistor value and see the effect.
4. Explain how current flows in the drawing.
5. Write a short report on the design choices.

### cte
1. Draw a circuit as a student technician would for a client.
2. Plan a board for a small product idea.
3. Follow a design checklist from schematic to layout.
4. Compare the size of two board layouts.
5. Present the design and the steps to build it.

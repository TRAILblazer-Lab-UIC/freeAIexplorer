## Overview
Coding as Another Language is a set of K-2 curriculum units from the DevTech group led by Marina Umaschi Bers. The units teach coding alongside literacy. They use ScratchJr on tablets or KIBO robots and mix unplugged games, storytelling, movement, singing and coding.

## Benefits for students
- Students link coding to stories and words.
- Students move, sing and play as they learn.
- Students choose a tablet path or a robot path.

## Benefits for teachers
- The curriculum is free and the ScratchJr version is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 3.0.
- A curriculum overview guides each unit.
- Unplugged games need only simple materials.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum and the ScratchJr app are free.
- KIBO robots are a paid purchase.
- No account is needed. The research did not confirm whether the download needs an email.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Coding as Another Language curriculum from the Boston College DevTech group.

- [ ] Choose ScratchJr on tablets or KIBO robots.
- [ ] Install the free ScratchJr app on tablets.
- [ ] Gather materials for the unplugged games.
- [ ] Read the overview for the first unit.
- [ ] Try one unplugged game with a small group.

## Where is the AI?
The curriculum has no AI. Children build programs in ScratchJr or on KIBO robots that follow the steps they set. Students build skills in sequencing and storytelling logic.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the vendor lists no student data collection. ScratchJr runs on the tablet and KIBO is a screen free robot. Work stays on the device. The research reviewed no privacy policy for the curriculum site. It did not confirm whether downloading the curriculum needs an email. A teacher should note that a research paper in a search snippet reports a 680 student trial. In that trial the curriculum did not significantly change literacy or math achievement, although growth in coding skill predicted both.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play an unplugged game that models a simple program.
2. Program a ScratchJr character to move across the screen.
3. Build a KIBO sequence with wooden blocks.
4. Fix a sequence that does the wrong action and tell why.
5. Show a program to a partner and explain each step.

### ela
1. Build a ScratchJr scene that retells a favorite story.
2. Program a character to say the story in order.
3. Sing a song that matches a coding sequence.
4. Act out a story with movement and then code it.
5. Write a short story and plan its coding steps with pictures.

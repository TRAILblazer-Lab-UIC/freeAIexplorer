## Overview
This listing comes from a 2023 news post about a free teacher course. The course has short videos and downloadable worksheets, and it includes ready made code. It covers temperature, sound, light, conductivity, and sound insulation. The post also points to more than 40 science projects.

## Benefits for students
- Students run fair tests on temperature, sound, light, and conductivity.
- Students collect real readings with a micro:bit sensor.
- Students can try projects such as a conductivity tester or a sound insulation meter.

## Benefits for teachers
- The ready made code means teachers need no coding skill.
- Short videos make the course easy to fit into a busy week.
- The worksheets are ready to download.

## Benefits for schools
- The course is free, and the boards are a paid purchase.
- The projects use micro:bit boards and MakeCode in a browser.
- The materials list no other costs, and a teacher can install the tools.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the course on Thinkific and check the sign up steps.

- [ ] Gather micro:bit boards for each group.

## Where is the AI?
This resource has no AI. It builds data skills, such as collecting readings and comparing them across trials. Students also practice planning a fair test.

## Privacy and safety
The course is hosted on Thinkific. The materials do not say whether the course needs an account, and the Thinkific page was not fetched. The research read no privacy policy. The projects run in a browser and on the board, and the internet is needed at setup. A teacher should check the Thinkific sign up page and what personal details it asks for before enrolling.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Measure the temperature of a room at three times of day and compare the readings.
2. Build the conductivity tester and test which materials let current pass.
3. Use the sound insulation meter to compare materials that block noise.
4. Log light levels near a window and in a dark corner.
5. Plan a fair test that changes one variable and keeps the rest the same.

### computer_science
1. Load the ready made code onto a board and describe what it does.
2. Read the data from the data logger and write down a pattern.
3. Change one value in the code and see how the result changes.
4. Match each block in the program to the sensor reading it takes.
5. Explain to a partner how a sensor turns a measurement into a number.

## Overview
Day of AI offers free AI literacy units for PreK through high school. The units are built for teachers without a technology background. They are tagged by subject, including ELA, social studies, science, arts, math, and computer science. They range from single lessons to courses of six or more lessons. The program began at MIT RAISE and became an independent nonprofit in 2024.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn AI ideas in units that fit their grade.
- Students try unplugged and browser based activities.
- Older students can work on a project based course.

## Benefits for teachers
- Units come as Google Slides and as downloadable Office files.
- Free teacher professional learning is offered.
- Teachers need no technology background.

## Benefits for schools
- The units are free.
- Students need only paper or a browser.
- IT has little to do. The vendor receives teacher account data.

## Where is the AI?
The units teach about AI. Topics include machine learning basics, training data, bias, chatbots, facial recognition, and AI policy. Some units use browser tools and others are unplugged. Students use the lessons to explain how AI works and to judge when it should be used.

## Privacy and safety
A teacher account is required, and teachers log in with an emailed link to view all unit resources. The account data goes to the vendor. The research did not read a privacy policy and did not confirm whether students need accounts. Some units use browser tools, and each lesson needs a check. A teacher should read the Day of AI privacy notice and the terms of any outside tool a unit uses.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Try the project based course on computer science and AI systems and design a small AI supported idea.
2. Sort cards into groups and explain the pattern you found.
3. Train a model in a browser tool and describe how the training data changed it.
4. List three ways an AI system could be biased and how to check each one.
5. Present a short talk on one AI topic from the units.

### social_studies
1. Join a Student Senate unit and draft a school AI policy.
2. Research how different groups feel about AI in schools.
3. Debate a draft policy and vote on changes.
4. Compare a school AI policy with one from another school.
5. Write a letter to the school board with your final policy.

### ela
1. Read a short text about AI and write a summary in your own words.
2. Write a persuasive paragraph about one rule for AI use in school.
3. Analyze the language in a chatbot reply and mark any human-like phrases.
4. Revise a classmate's argument and give two suggestions.
5. Write a short story about a helpful robot and a problem it must solve.

### science
1. Collect simple data in class and ask how a model could learn from it.
2. Compare how a scientist and a machine would classify plants.
3. Test whether an AI answer matches a measurement you took.
4. Discuss how AI helps researchers study large sets of data.
5. Record a prediction and check it against what happens.

### math
1. Count and sort data from a class survey and look for patterns.
2. Graph the results and describe what a model might predict.
3. Compare predictions and actual results and find the difference.
4. Use a table to track how many right answers a model gives.
5. Explain how a bigger sample might change a prediction.

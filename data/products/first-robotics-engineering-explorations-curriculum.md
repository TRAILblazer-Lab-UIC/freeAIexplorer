## Overview
This is a free introductory robotics engineering course for grades 7 to 12 from FIRST. It is described as a 90 hour course built on FIRST Tech Challenge or FIRST Robotics Competition materials. Topics are the engineering design process, engineering in society, safety, building a robot and computational thinking. Students design, build and program a robot for a challenge. The curriculum has teacher and student guides for six units as PDF downloads.

## Benefits for students
- Students design, build and program a real robot.
- Students learn safety and teamwork.
- Students practice the engineering design process.

## Benefits for teachers
- The course has a full teacher guide and an outline.
- Each of the six units has a teacher and a student guide.
- FIRST says the course is aligned to national standards.

## Benefits for schools
- The downloads are free.
- Schools must plan robot parts and a build space.
- IT help is likely needed for the programming computers.

## Where is the AI?
This course has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is computational thinking. Students program a robot to follow rules and respond to a challenge.

## Privacy and safety
The materials are public PDF downloads. The announcement page mentions no form or account, and the research found no privacy policy. The research did not open the unit PDFs. A separate FIRST Tech Challenge Class Pack needs registration and is a different offering. A teacher should open the PDFs first to check for any account or tool students would need. A teacher should also review the safety unit before students use tools and robot parts.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Work through the engineering design process on a classroom challenge.
2. Study a safety unit and write shop rules for your team.
3. Build a robot chassis and test it on the floor.
4. Discuss how engineering affects society.
5. Present your robot design to the class.

### computer_science
1. Break a robot task into small steps.
2. Write a program that moves your robot through a course.
3. Test and debug the program.
4. Add a second behavior and test the two together.
5. Explain how your code solves the challenge.

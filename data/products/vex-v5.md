## Overview
VEX V5 is a metal robotics platform used in classrooms and in competition. Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy offers a VEXcode based curriculum for classrooms, clubs, and teams. V5 serves the V5 Robotics Competition and RECF Achieve pathways. VEXcode V5 is a free desktop app and also runs in a browser.

## Benefits for students
- Students build strong metal robots.
- Students code in blocks or text, and VS Code is supported.
- Students can join competition pathways.

## Benefits for teachers
- The VEX Library lists grades 9 to college.
- CMU offers courses for classrooms, clubs, and teams.
- An optional AI Vision Sensor adds object detection.

## Benefits for schools
- A search snippet lists the V5 Classroom Starter Bundle of six kits for 12 to 18 students at 4,999.99 USD. This price is not verified.
- The desktop app is for Windows and Mac. The browser version works on Chromebook, Mac, and Windows.
- Windows 10 or later, 1 GB of free storage, and USB 2.0 are listed for the desktop app in a search snippet.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the V5 kits and VEXcode V5 on the VEX site.
- [ ] Confirm the current price with VEX or a reseller.
- [ ] Download VEXcode V5 for Windows or Mac, or use the browser version.
- [ ] Choose the right Mac installer for Intel or Apple Silicon.
- [ ] Charge the V5 Brain and connect it by USB.
- [ ] Run a first drive program in blocks.
- [ ] Read the VEX privacy policy before students create accounts.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is optional. The AI Vision Sensor detects objects and runs on the device. Students use its readings in their code. Without the sensor, the kit has no AI.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need. The internet is used at setup. A search snippet of the VEX software privacy policy says some apps are directed to children under 13 and that Google Analytics is used. The policy page could not be read. What data is collected, COPPA handling, and retention are unconfirmed. A teacher should read the policy directly before students create accounts.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow a CMU lesson to code the robot to drive a path.
2. Use a loop and a sensor to stop at a line.
3. Move a block program into text code and compare them.
4. Write a program that runs an autonomous routine.
5. Use the AI Vision Sensor to make the robot react to a target.

### engineering
1. Build a competition style robot and note each design choice.
2. Test a lifting arm with a light object.
3. Compare two drivetrain designs on the same field.
4. Record test results and pick the best design.
5. Present how the team improved the robot over several tests.

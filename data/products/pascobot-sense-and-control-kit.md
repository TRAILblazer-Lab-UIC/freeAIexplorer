## Overview
The PASCObot Sense and Control Kit includes a PASCObot body, a //control.Node, stepper motors, a line follower, a range finder, a gripper and servo motors. Students program it with Blockly in SPARKvue or PASCO Capstone. The lab collection has 7 experiments for middle and high school. They run from basic movement to line following and moving cups with the gripper.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a robot with blocks.
- Students use sensors such as a line follower and a range finder.
- Students work with science software that they may use in lab classes.

## Benefits for teachers
- Seven digital experiments come with the kit.
- Teacher resources need a free account.
- The robot connects by Bluetooth LE or USB.

## Benefits for schools
- PASCO lists the kit at 549 USD and 399 USD without the //control.Node.
- Setup can take days.
- IT should install SPARKvue or Capstone.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the PASCObot kit on the PASCO site.
- [ ] Ask IT to install SPARKvue or PASCO Capstone.
- [ ] Create a free account for teacher resources.
- [ ] Assemble the kit.
- [ ] Connect the //control.Node by Bluetooth or USB.
- [ ] Run the first experiment.
- [ ] Note that the Line Follower and Range Finder share a slot.

## Where is the AI?
The kit has no AI. It builds skills in sensors, logic and sequencing. Students use data from the sensors to decide how the robot moves.

## Privacy and safety
The robot runs on the device and the software runs on a computer. An account is optional and is needed for teacher resources. The materials do not state what data is collected or whether the internet is needed. The PASCO privacy policy pages returned 404 errors, and the research read no policy. A teacher should read the PASCO privacy terms and check Bluetooth rules with IT.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Assemble the PASCObot and test basic movement.
2. Program the robot to navigate obstacles, as in the documented STEM use.
3. Swap the Line Follower for the Range Finder and compare the two.
4. Use the gripper to move a cup and measure how often it works.
5. Write a short design report on the best sensor choice.

### computer_science
1. Build a Blockly program that drives forward and turns.
2. Use a loop for a repeated route.
3. Add a condition that reacts to the range finder.
4. Debug a program that follows the line badly.
5. Compare two ways to write the same route.

### science
1. Read accelerometer data in SPARKvue during a run.
2. Graph distance readings from the range finder.
3. Predict a robot's speed and measure it.
4. Compare the data from two runs.
5. Write a conclusion from the graphs.

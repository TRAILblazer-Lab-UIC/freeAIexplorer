## Overview
Tonybot is a humanoid robot kit sold by Hiwonder. It has 17 degrees of freedom and a 38 cm aluminum alloy body. Students program it with Scratch, Python, or the Arduino IDE. Optional ESP32-S3 AI vision camera and voice modules are available.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a walking humanoid from a kit.
- Students choose Scratch, Python, or Arduino to control it.
- Students can add vision and voice with optional modules.

## Benefits for teachers
- The Wonderbot app for Android and iOS pairs with the robot.
- A Windows program controls the servos.
- The vendor does not provide classroom documentation that was found. Teachers should plan their own lessons.

## Benefits for schools
- CNX Software lists 459.99 USD for the standard kit and 529.99 USD for the advanced kit.
- An 11.1V battery is included according to one article.
- Hiwonder's product page could not be read. IT should confirm details before buying.

## Where is the AI?
The base kit is a programmable robot. Optional AI vision and voice modules add vision and voice interaction. Hiwonder markets a version with multimodal large AI models. The vendor materials do not say where the AI runs. Students extend their code by reacting to what the modules sense.

## Privacy and safety
The research read no privacy policy and the Hiwonder product page could not be retrieved. Retailer text for related Hiwonder TonyPi listings describes ChatGPT based AI through the OpenAI API, which would send data to a third party. The research did not confirm whether Tonybot uses cloud AI. The materials do not state which accounts students need or whether the internet is needed. The app and AI modules may use a camera or microphone. Ask Hiwonder whether voice and vision data leaves the device and check the app permissions.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a Scratch program that makes the robot walk and wave.
2. Write a sequence of moves that makes the robot dance.
3. Switch a Scratch program to Python and compare the code.
4. Add the vision module. Then use what it sees to change what the robot does.
5. Plan a robot greeting and write each step in order.

### engineering
1. Assemble the robot and test each servo.
2. Balance the robot and record what makes it fall.
3. Compare two walking speeds and measure the distance.
4. Check the battery life during a long program.
5. Sketch a change to the body and explain how it might improve balance.

## Overview
Field Guide is an Android app that identifies plants, animals, fungi, rocks, minerals, gems, and clouds from photos. It uses Google's Gemini Nano model on the phone. According to AlternativeTo, it works without internet after a one-time download of about 1 GB. Users can add context, chat to refine results, and keep a local sightings journal that exports as a PDF.

## Benefits for students
- Students can identify things they find outdoors.
- Students can chat to refine an answer.
- Students keep a journal of their sightings.

## Benefits for teachers
- The app works offline after the download.
- The journal exports as a PDF.
- The Play listing warns that identifications can be wrong.

## Benefits for schools
- The model runs on the phone.
- The app needs a phone that supports Gemini Nano and about 1 GB of storage.
- The cost ranges from a limited free version to a one-time $60 purchase or a subscription of $5 to $30 per month.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is Google's Gemini Nano model, which identifies items from photos. It runs on the phone after the model is downloaded. Students take a photo, read the answer, and add context to refine it. They then check the result against a trusted field guide.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether an account is needed or what the app stores. Third party listings say the model runs on the phone after a one time download, and the journal stays on the device. The research could not read the Play Data safety section, and the developer is unknown. The Play listing says never to use identifications to decide whether plants or mushrooms are safe to eat or touch. A teacher should repeat that rule to students and read the Play Data safety section before installing.

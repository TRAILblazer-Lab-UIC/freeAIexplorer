## Overview
This health education lesson is for grades 7 to 12. Students list the technology they use and research the effects of social media and phones on teens. They compare class screen time with national averages and learn what AI is. One activity has each group use a school approved AI image generator to make a picture.

## Benefits for students
- Students think about their own technology habits.
- Students learn AI vocabulary on a worksheet.
- Students practice safe use of AI as a tool.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson comes as a PowerPoint and a worksheet.
- It fits Health Education I and II.
- The plan includes group work and a class screen time comparison.

## Benefits for schools
- The budget table lists no costs.
- Students need devices with internet.
- The school must choose an approved AI image generator.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson teaches what AI is and how it works. One short activity uses an AI image generator that runs on a vendor's cloud. Students make a group picture and then talk about safe use. The generator is the one the school chooses.

## Privacy and safety
Each student needs a device with the internet. Students visit socialharms.utah.gov. One activity uses a school approved AI image generator that runs in a vendor cloud. The materials do not state whether it needs an account or what data it collects. Groups upload images to a Google Form. Phone screen time is written on sticky notes and can be anonymous. Choose a generator that follows school rules and review each prompt before students submit it.

## Ideas by subject
### health_pe
1. List all the technology you use in one day and share the top three.
2. Research the effects of social media on teens and report one finding.
3. Compare class screen time with the national average.
4. Fill in the AI vocabulary worksheet and quiz a partner.
5. Write three safe use rules for AI tools.

### library_media
1. Check one claim from the research site and find its source.
2. Describe how an AI image is made and where its data comes from.
3. Compare a group made AI image with a photo and list the differences.
4. Discuss who owns an image made with AI.
5. Make a short guide on safe AI use for younger students.

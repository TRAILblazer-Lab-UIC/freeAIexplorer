## Overview
This is a hands-on middle school unit from Code.org. Students build a machine learning model from tabular data. They train classifiers in AI Lab and then import the model into a simulated App Lab app. The unit ends with students designing an ML app for a problem that matters to them. The page does not state the number of weeks.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a real classifier and see how data shapes it.
- Students put the model to work inside an app.
- Students design their own ML app for a personal problem.

## Benefits for teachers
- The unit follows the AI4K12 standards.
- Course pages are in English and Spanish.
- The unit is dated 2021, and Code.org has since released AI Discoveries and AI Foundations.

## Benefits for schools
- The unit is free.
- It runs in a browser. App Lab works best on a desktop or laptop.
- IT will likely need to approve Code.org.

## Where is the AI?
Students train classifiers on tabular data in AI Lab. The tools run in the vendor's cloud through a browser. Students then import the trained model into App Lab and design an app that uses it for their own idea.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are optional, and the internet is needed at all times. The vendor collects username, age, state, and project data for accounts. The policy says it does not sell personal data or show ads. It says it is not subject to COPPA as a nonprofit. The model training tools run in the vendor cloud. A teacher should check the Code.org privacy terms with the school. A teacher should also tell students not to use personal data in their training tables.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train a classifier in AI Lab and test it on new rows.
2. Import the model into an App Lab app and make it show a result.
3. Change the training data and see how the results change.
4. Design an ML app for a problem in your school.
5. Explain to a partner how the classifier makes a choice.

### math
1. Calculate the accuracy of a classifier from correct and wrong guesses.
2. Compare the accuracy for two sets of training data.
3. Plot two columns of tabular data and describe the pattern.
4. Decide which columns give the model the most useful clues.
5. Report how a larger data set changes accuracy.

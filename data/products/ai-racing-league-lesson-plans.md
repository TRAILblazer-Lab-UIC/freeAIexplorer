## Overview
The AI Racing League teaches machine learning by having students train small remote controlled cars to drive a track on their own. Each car uses a low cost Raspberry Pi or NVIDIA Nano computer with a camera, inspired by DonkeyCar. Students collect images and train a neural network that steers the car. The lesson plans serve fifth grade through college.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a camera car and train it to drive.
- Students collect real data and see how it shapes the model.
- Students practice Python, computer vision, and data science.

## Benefits for teachers
- Six week and 14 week sample curricula are provided.
- The content is open under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- The setup needs an experienced teacher or mentor.

## Benefits for schools
- Lessons and events are free. Per car cost was not stated.
- The program is run by CoderDojo Twin Cities under Code Savvy.
- The group said it lacked volunteers to move the courses online.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a neural network that learns to steer from camera images. Students drive the car, collect images, and train the model. The vendor does not say where training runs. The curriculum also covers computer vision, data science, and generative AI.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson site is static and the pages read do not say which accounts the software needs. They also do not say where model training happens. The internet is used at setup. The car camera captures images of the track as training data. The materials do not state how student data is handled. A teacher should check where training data is stored and keep faces out of the training images.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow the lesson plan to collect driving images and train a model.
2. Compare a model trained on 100 images with one trained on 1,000.
3. Write Python to display the images the car collected.
4. Explain how the neural network chooses to steer left or right.
5. Test generative AI ideas from the curriculum and discuss their limits.

### engineering
1. Build the car and test each part.
2. Tune the steering until the car drives straight.
3. Design a track with turns and test the car.
4. Record lap times and describe what helped.
5. Compare two camera angles and pick the better one.

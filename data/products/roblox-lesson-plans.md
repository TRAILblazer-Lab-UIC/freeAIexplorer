## Overview
These are free lesson plans from Roblox Education for teaching with Roblox Studio. The Roblox Developer 101 course has five sessions on Studio basics, coding fundamentals, and a three-part adventure game. Students use Roblox Studio on Windows or Mac and each needs a Roblox account. Handouts, a starter file, and a presentation are provided.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a game in a tool that many already know.
- Students learn Studio basics and then coding.
- Students save projects to their own Roblox account.

## Benefits for teachers
- Printed cheatsheets and a starter .rbxm file are included.
- Five sessions run 30 to 65 minutes each.
- A presentation guides each lesson.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson plans are free.
- Each student needs their own free Roblox account. Accounts are not managed by the school.
- IT may need to open ports and domains.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Roblox Developer 101 course on the Roblox Education site.
- [ ] Ask IT and school leaders to review the privacy terms and account rules.
- [ ] Ask IT to install Roblox Studio and open the needed ports and domains.
- [ ] Have students register accounts, ideally outside class.
- [ ] Print the handouts and the cheatsheets.
- [ ] Download the starter file and the presentation.
- [ ] Run Session 1 on Studio basics with a small group first.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson plans have no AI. They build an AI-related skill, which is logic. Students write code that decides what happens when a player touches an object. That practice shows how programs make decisions.

## Privacy and safety
Each student needs their own free Roblox account, and the internet is needed at all times. The setup page says accounts are individual and not managed by a school. It also says accounts cannot be created in batches. Registration asks for a birthday but not a name, and students under 13 get restricted accounts. A Roblox account holds a birthday, an optional email, and saved projects, which go to the vendor. The research did not read the Roblox privacy policy. A teacher should check the school's rules on student accounts and review what students publish.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow Roblox Studio Basics and place parts to build a small world.
2. Use the Coding Fundamentals session to write a script that changes a part's color.
3. Build the first part of the adventure game and describe the goal.
4. Add a rule that changes what happens when a player touches an object.
5. Test a classmate's game and write one bug report.

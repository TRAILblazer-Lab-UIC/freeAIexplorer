## Overview
This is a gamified 60 minute lesson in the AI and Society area of Stanford CRAFT. Students use Ethical Engine Cards to discuss scenarios in light of their own values. They also think about how personal and cultural backgrounds shape views on AI. A search result lists the lesson for grades 9 to 12.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice talking about AI ethics through scenarios.
- Students connect their own values to AI decisions.
- Students hear how other backgrounds shape different views.

## Benefits for teachers
- The page provides a teacher guide as a Google Doc.
- The lesson fits in one 60 minute class.
- Students need little technical background.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free to access.
- The cards are printed and few devices are needed.
- The materials include an AI Bill of Rights PDF and a school guide from NC State Friday Institute.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson is about AI ethics and AI policy and does not run an AI model. Students use cards on paper to discuss scenarios. They then build their own views on how AI should be used.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson uses printed cards. The materials are Google Docs and a PDF. The page shows a login link, and the research did not find whether an account is needed to open it. A teacher should check the scenarios before class. Some topics could touch on sensitive personal or cultural beliefs.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Read an Ethical Engine Card and decide how your values shape your view.
2. Compare your view with a classmate from a different background.
3. Read the AI Bill of Rights and pick the right you find most important.
4. Discuss how a community could set rules for AI use.
5. Write a short class agreement on fair use of AI.

### ela
1. Write a paragraph that defends your answer to a card scenario.
2. Practice a respectful reply to a view you disagree with.
3. Summarize the AI Bill of Rights in three sentences.
4. Hold a small group debate and then write a closing statement.
5. Reflect in writing on how your view changed after the discussion.

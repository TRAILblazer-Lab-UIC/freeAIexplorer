## Overview
GCompris is an educational software suite with more than 100 activities for children aged 2 to 10. The categories include computer skills, reading, arithmetic, science, geography, and games. Some activities are game based. Young students work through them on a school computer or tablet.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice reading and arithmetic through short activities.
- Students build computer skills such as using a mouse and keyboard.
- Game based activities keep young learners engaged.

## Benefits for teachers
- One program covers many early topics.
- No account is needed.
- Teachers can pick activities that match the lesson of the day.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free. Donations are invited.
- It runs offline after installation.
- It installs on Linux, Windows, Mac, and Android. IT will likely need to approve the install.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find GCompris from its project site.
- [ ] Choose the install source for your system, such as Flathub, Snap, a Linux package manager, the Microsoft Store, F-Droid, or Google Play.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install on school computers.
- [ ] Check store prices before buying. They were not checked here.
- [ ] Note that a macOS build is stated in the project brief but was not confirmed.
- [ ] Open the program and look through the activity categories.
- [ ] Test two or three activities before class.

## Where is the AI?
GCompris has no AI. The activities build reading, number, and computer skills. It runs as an app on the device.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the app runs locally after installation. The site links a mobile privacy policy, and the research did not read its text. The vendor lists no student data collection. A teacher should read that privacy policy and check the install source and the privacy policy of the app store used. A teacher should also test activities before students use them.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Use the arithmetic activities to practice adding to ten.
2. Play a counting game and record the score each day.
3. Compare two activities and say which one was easier.
4. Make a class chart of the activities students finished.
5. Pick one number activity and teach it to a partner.

### ela
1. Use the reading activities to match words with pictures.
2. Practice letters with a keyboard activity.
3. Read a short word aloud after finishing an activity.
4. Write a sentence about a favorite activity.
5. Sort words from an activity by their first letter.

### science
1. Try a science activity and tell a partner what happened.
2. Draw something learned from an activity.
3. Ask a question that an activity raised.
4. Match animals or objects in a science game.
5. Share one new fact with the class.

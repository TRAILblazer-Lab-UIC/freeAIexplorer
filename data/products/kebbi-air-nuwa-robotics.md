## Overview
Kebbi Air is a desktop social robot made by NUWA and sold in the US by RobotLAB. It is marketed for K-12, special education, higher education, healthcare, and hospitality. It offers facial recognition and voice interaction. A 2024 press release says students can design and program their own creations and mentions Nuwa conversational AI.

## Benefits for students
- Students talk with a robot that responds to their voice.
- Students can design and program their own creations.
- Students meet a friendly robot that may suit younger learners.

## Benefits for teachers
- The vendor markets the robot for special education.
- It ships with a charger, ears, a manual, and a quick start guide.
- Teachers can contact RobotLAB for help with deployment.

## Benefits for schools
- RobotLAB lists it at $5,000 and out of stock. An earlier note saw $1,390, which was not confirmed.
- The platforms and hardware needs were not listed on the reseller page.
- Contact RobotLAB for availability before planning a purchase.

## Where is the AI?
The AI includes facial recognition, voice interaction, and conversational AI. The materials do not say where it runs. Students talk with the robot, then use its coding tools to shape what it says and does.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. The listing does not say whether processing is local or in the cloud. A robot with face recognition and voice interaction collects biometric type data. No vendor privacy policy was read. A teacher should ask the vendor where face and voice data go and get permission before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### special_ed
1. Practice greetings with the robot and take turns speaking.
2. Use the robot as a calm partner for reading short sentences aloud.
3. Set up a daily routine that the robot announces with a student.
4. Let a student choose the robot's responses and watch the reaction.
5. Practice following spoken steps one at a time with the robot.

### computer_science
1. Design a simple creation for the robot and plan its steps on paper.
2. Test how well the robot recognizes a voice at three distances.
3. Change the robot's reply and record how a listener reacts.
4. Compare how the robot reacts to two different phrases.
5. Explain in a short talk how a robot can know a face.

## Overview
iNaturalist is a social network for recording and sharing observations of living things. It suggests species using computer vision and the community confirms them. Observations are public records of where and when an organism was seen. The terms say children 12 and younger need an adult's permission and a parent or guardian creates the account. Seek by iNaturalist is a separate app aimed at young people that does not share data by default.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn field observation skills.
- Students get species suggestions and feedback from a community.
- Students contribute real records to science.

## Benefits for teachers
- The educator guide suggests Seek for younger children.
- Forum guidance suggests that high school students learn field methods first and then use the apps.
- Teachers can plan a nature project around one site.

## Benefits for schools
- The platform is free and the code is open source.
- Students need a phone or camera for photos.
- Students 12 and younger need a parent created account.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is computer vision that suggests species from a photo. It runs in the vendor's cloud. Students take a photo, read the suggestion, and then ask the community to confirm it. They use the process to check what the AI proposed.

## Privacy and safety
Students need accounts, and the internet is always needed. Photos, observation location and time, and username go to the vendor, and observations are public records. Children 12 and younger need an adult's permission, and a parent or guardian creates the account with a one dollar donation to verify adulthood. The privacy policy returned an error during the research and was not read. The research did not confirm whether observations go to third parties. Common Sense Media rates it 13+ because of open sharing and messaging, according to a search snippet. A teacher should use the geoprivacy options to hide exact locations and choose Seek for younger students.

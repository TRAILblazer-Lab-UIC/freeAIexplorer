## Overview
This is a set of educator resources for teaching with Scratch. The Scratch Foundation page has a Learning Library of activity guides, videos, and tutorials for Scratch and ScratchJr. It also offers Teacher Accounts, an educator newsletter, and a stories blog. The old ScratchEd community at Harvard is an archive closed to new contributions.

## Benefits for students
- Students follow activity guides and tutorials at many levels.
- Students share projects in a moderated online community.
- Students work with blocks in a browser.

## Benefits for teachers
- The Learning Library gives ready-made guides and videos.
- Teacher Accounts let a teacher manage student accounts and projects.
- The educator newsletter shares new ideas.

## Benefits for schools
- All resources are free.
- Students use a browser on a Chromebook or computer.
- A teacher can manage accounts in one place.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Scratch Foundation site at scratchfoundation.org.
- [ ] Browse the Learning Library.
- [ ] Register for a Teacher Account if you want student accounts.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve student accounts.
- [ ] Choose one guide for the first class.
- [ ] Sign up for the educator newsletter.

## Where is the AI?
These resources have no AI. Scratch is a block coding tool. Students build sequencing, logic, and event skills with each guide.

## Privacy and safety
Browsing the resources needs no account. Teacher Accounts create student accounts, and Scratch accounts and projects are stored with the vendor. The internet is needed at all times. The page links a privacy policy and community guidelines that the research did not read. A teacher should check school rules on student accounts and review the online community settings.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Pick a guide from the Learning Library and build the project.
2. Watch a tutorial video and recreate the project from memory.
3. Change a finished project to add a new feature.
4. Share a project with the class and ask for feedback.
5. Compare a Scratch guide with a ScratchJr guide and list the differences.

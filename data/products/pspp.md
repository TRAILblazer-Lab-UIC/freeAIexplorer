## Overview
PSPP is a free statistical analysis program for sampled data. It is presented as a free software replacement for SPSS and looks very similar to it. Users work through a graphical interface or syntax commands. It runs descriptive statistics, t-tests, ANOVA, regression, factor analysis, and non-parametric tests.

## Benefits for students
- Students can analyze real data sets with professional tools.
- Students can import spreadsheets, text files, and databases.
- Students learn skills that carry to SPSS.

## Benefits for teachers
- The project FAQ says PSPP may be copied for students in an introductory statistics course.
- A PSPP Tutorial from NC State is available.
- The interface follows SPSS conventions and suits high school statistics or data science.

## Benefits for schools
- The software has no license fees, no expiry, and no limit on cases or variables.
- It runs on the local computer.
- IT will likely need to approve the install. It is not a Chromebook app.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download PSPP from the GNU site or a distribution package manager.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install on managed computers.
- [ ] Note that GNU and GNU/Linux are the preferred platforms. Other systems are reported to work.
- [ ] Follow the NC State tutorial.
- [ ] Open a small spreadsheet of class data.
- [ ] Run descriptive statistics on one column.
- [ ] Save the output for a lab write-up.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in PSPP. It runs statistical tests that students choose. They build data skills as they summarize and compare numbers.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The internet is used for setup. The official page describes a locally installed program, and data files stay on the computer where they are opened. No privacy policy was found, and the research did not verify this by code review. A teacher should check that class data sets contain no student names or other identifying details.

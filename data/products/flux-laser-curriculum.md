## Overview
This K-12 laser cutting curriculum from FLUX has three stages, kindergarten to grade 3, grade 4 to 7, and grade 8 to 12. The stages list 10, 9, and 10 courses, 29 in all. Examples include habitat design, modifying furniture, Pop Art etching, and tessellated puzzles. The projects need a FLUX laser cutter.

## Benefits for students
- Students design objects and then cut them.
- Students build projects such as puzzles and Pop Art etchings.
- Students learn from kindergarten through grade 12.

## Benefits for teachers
- Worksheets and design files are free downloads.
- A five chapter video course called Laser Cutter 101 teaches the Beam Studio software and machine operation.
- Courses are grouped by grade stage.

## Benefits for schools
- Curriculum files are free. The price of a FLUX laser cutter is not stated.
- The machine is a CO2 desktop laser cutter of 30W to 60W.
- IT staff should help install Beam Studio, and the room needs a ventilation plan.

## Where is the AI?
The curriculum has no AI. It builds design, measuring, and sequencing skills. Students plan a part and set the steps for cutting it.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the internet is used at setup to get Beam Studio and the files. Materials are hosted on FLUX pages and YouTube. Beam Studio also has a web version. The research did not review Beam Studio telemetry, and the materials do not say what data is collected. A teacher should ask IT to check Beam Studio telemetry before installing it. A teacher should also follow the safety guidance. Laser cutters need ventilation and PVC must not be cut.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Design a habitat model and cut its parts.
2. Modify a piece of furniture design and test the fit.
3. Make a tessellated puzzle and test its pieces.
4. Measure a cut piece and compare it with the design.
5. Write a short safety plan for using the machine.

### visual_arts
1. Etch a Pop Art design on wood using gradient controls in Beam Studio.
2. Create a pattern that repeats across a panel.
3. Design a puzzle with an image on its top.
4. Compare two designs and pick the stronger one.
5. Display finished pieces with a note about the design.

### cte
1. Follow a project from sketch to finished object.
2. Estimate the time for a cut and check it.
3. Plan a small product and list its materials.
4. Present a finished piece as a mock product.
5. Research jobs that use laser cutters.

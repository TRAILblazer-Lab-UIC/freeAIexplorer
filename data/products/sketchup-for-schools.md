## Overview
SketchUp for Schools is the SketchUp 3D modeler in a web browser and on iPad for primary and secondary schools. Schools must be enrolled with Google Workspace for Education or Microsoft Education. Students and teachers sign in with those accounts. Trimble offers K-12 lessons on topics such as 3D printing and modeling cells for biology.

## Benefits for students
- Students build 3D models in a browser or on iPad.
- Students sign in with the school account they already have.
- Students save projects to Google Drive or OneDrive.

## Benefits for teachers
- Trimble provides K-12 lessons.
- Lessons include 3D printing and modeling cells.
- A 2019 reviewer said some features from older education versions were dropped.

## Benefits for schools
- The service is free for enrolled schools.
- A school administrator must enable the app for the domain.
- Setup may take days.

## Getting started
- [ ] Confirm that the school uses Google Workspace for Education or Microsoft Education.
- [ ] Ask the school administrator to enable the app for the domain.
- [ ] Sign in with a school account.
- [ ] Try a Trimble K-12 lesson.
- [ ] Check that projects save to Google Drive or OneDrive.
- [ ] Plan a first lesson with a simple model.

## Where is the AI?
SketchUp for Schools has no AI. It is a 3D modeling tool. Students build skills in spatial thinking as they turn a plan into a model.

## Privacy and safety
A school account is required, and the internet is needed at all times. The FAQ says the app is COPPA compliant, stores no personally identifiable information and sends no user data to third party apps. Projects save to the school's Google Drive or OneDrive. The research did not read the full Trimble privacy policy. A teacher should check the privacy terms with the school administrator.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Model a small object and prepare it for 3D printing, as in the Trimble lessons.
2. Measure a desk and build it to scale.
3. Design a new chair and compare it with a classmate's design.
4. Redesign a model to use fewer parts.
5. Present a design and explain each choice.

### visual_arts
1. Design a room and choose colors for each wall.
2. Build a model of a landmark from a photo.
3. Create a sculpture from simple shapes.
4. Compare two designs for the same building.
5. Share a model and describe the design choices.

### science
1. Model a plant or animal cell and label its parts, following the Trimble biology lesson.
2. Build a model of a water molecule.
3. Create a model of the solar system and compare sizes.
4. Present a cell model and explain each part.
5. Compare models with a partner and note any differences.

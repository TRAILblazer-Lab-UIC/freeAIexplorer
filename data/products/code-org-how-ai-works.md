## Overview
These seven lessons for grades 6 to 12 each pair a short video with an activity. Topics are machine learning, computer vision, neural networks, chatbots and large language models, generative images, algorithmic bias, and an AI code of ethics. Code.org suggests showing all videos in one day. A teacher account is optional.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a robot and test a seven-segment display algorithm.
- Students use a diffusion widget and co-create text with an LLM.
- Students crop images to see how bias can appear.

## Benefits for teachers
- A one-day version is available.
- The videos can be shown without student accounts.
- The lessons are ready to use.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons are free.
- They work on browsers, Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and tablets.
- Little IT help is needed.

## Where is the AI?
The AI includes machine learning, computer vision, neural network, language model, and image generation demos. Most run in the browser, and some use the vendor's cloud. Students try each tool and use what they see to explain how AI works.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are optional and store progress. Without accounts only an IP address is collected. The data goes to the vendor, and the internet is needed throughout. The language model lesson has students co create text with AI. The policy lists language model providers as service providers and says free text entries may be processed and stored. It says the nonprofit is not subject to COPPA and treats school accounts as FERPA records. A review notes the videos may be blocked if a district blocks YouTube. A teacher should preview the language model lesson and review the Code.org privacy terms.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train the robot in the machine learning lesson and describe what it learned.
2. Test the seven-segment display algorithm and find where it fails.
3. Use the diffusion widget and explain how noise turns into a picture.
4. Co-create a short text with an LLM and mark what you changed.
5. Crop images to expose bias and write a note on the effect.

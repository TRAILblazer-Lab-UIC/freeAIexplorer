## Overview
Shotcut is a free open source video editor for Windows, macOS, and Linux. Users trim, cut, and arrange clips on a timeline. They add text, images, transitions, filters, and color correction. It can capture the screen, a webcam, and audio.

## Benefits for students
- Students edit video and audio on a timeline.
- Students add titles, transitions, and filters to polish a project.
- Students record the screen or webcam to make a video.

## Benefits for teachers
- Shotcut edits hundreds of formats without an import step.
- Leiden University presents it for educational videos and podcasts.
- The project gives no official education guidance and no vendor support.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and open source.
- It runs on school computers after install.
- IT must install it, and releases arrive about every two months.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the installer from shotcut.org.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install.
- [ ] Install Shotcut on one test computer.
- [ ] Set up a preset layout with the tracks and video mode you want.
- [ ] Copy the settings to classroom computers, as a forum post describes.
- [ ] Edit a short test clip and export it.

## Where is the AI?
Shotcut has no AI. It is an editor with filters and a timeline. Students build skills in sequencing scenes and spotting a good story rhythm.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. Files stay on the computer. The website privacy page says the site keeps no IP logs or analytics, apart from Google AdSense ads. The research did not confirm whether the desktop app has telemetry. A teacher should ask IT to confirm this and check that students save projects in a folder that the school backs up.

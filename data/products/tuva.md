## Overview
Tuva is a data literacy platform with datasets, a browser based data visualization tool, and lessons. The site lists Tuva Jr. for grades 2 to 5, and Tuva Math and Tuva Science for grades 6 to 12. Teachers sign up and students join a class with a join code. Students explore data and make graphs.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with real datasets and build their own graphs.
- Students join a class with a simple code.
- Students practice reading and explaining data.

## Benefits for teachers
- A lesson library covers math, science, and Tuva Jr.
- A Tuva OpenSciEd Companion supports science classes.
- Teachers can sign up for free and create a class in one sitting.

## Benefits for schools
- Free sign up is offered. A subscription and school quotes also exist, and prices are not listed on the pages read.
- The tool needs only a web browser, including on Chromebooks.
- The vendor collects student names. A privacy review is advisable.

## Getting started
- [ ] Go to the Tuva site and sign up as a teacher.
- [ ] Ask your school privacy lead to review the service before use.
- [ ] Create a class in your teacher account.
- [ ] Give students the class join code.
- [ ] Choose a lesson from the library for your grade.
- [ ] Try the data tool yourself on a sample dataset.
- [ ] Ask students to avoid uploading personal data.

## Where is the AI?
The site homepage mentions no AI features. This page treats Tuva as a tool with no AI. Students still build data skills. They spot patterns, compare variables, and explain what a graph shows.

## Privacy and safety
Student accounts are needed and the internet is always needed. The vendor collects first and last names, participation history, performance data such as response timing, uploaded datasets, and graphs. This data goes to the vendor cloud, hosted in the United States. Registrant information may go to contractual business partners and aggregated performance data may be used for marketing. The policy says Tuva will not knowingly collect identifiable information from children under 13 without consent from an adult account holder. That is a data rule. The policy has an inconsistency between its FERPA and sharing sections. Get school privacy approval before students join.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Graph a class survey and describe the shape of the data.
2. Compare two datasets and explain which one has the larger spread.
3. Use a scatter plot to look for a link between two measures.
4. Write a short claim about a dataset and back it with a graph.
5. Make a graph that misleads and then fix it.

### science
1. Explore a climate dataset and describe a trend over time.
2. Use a lesson from the OpenSciEd Companion to analyze data from a unit.
3. Graph a class experiment and compare the results with a partner.
4. Pick a variable from a dataset and predict how it changes another.
5. Write a conclusion that cites the data from a graph.

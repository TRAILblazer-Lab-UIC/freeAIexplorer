## Overview
This is a four-wheel-drive robot car chassis kit for a Raspberry Pi. It has independent wheels, line tracking, obstacle avoidance, and USB-C charging. A camera module streams video over WiFi and supports OpenCV projects. Students program it in Python and control it from a Windows, macOS, or Linux computer.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a robot car and then program it.
- Students try line following and obstacle avoidance.
- Students can explore computer vision with the camera.

## Benefits for teachers
- The kit gives a hands-on project with clear goals.
- Python is the main language.
- The vendor age note of 16 and up was not verified.

## Benefits for schools
- RobotShop lists the kit at 57.48 USD.
- The Raspberry Pi and two 18650 batteries cost extra.
- Setup takes days, and IT help is likely.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the kit on the RobotShop page and read the specifications.
- [ ] Buy a Raspberry Pi 3B, 3B+, 4B, or 5 and two 18650 batteries.
- [ ] Assemble the chassis and mount the Raspberry Pi.
- [ ] Install the vendor software on the Pi.
- [ ] Connect to the car from a computer over WiFi.
- [ ] Test the wheels and the camera.
- [ ] Ask the vendor about data handling. The pages read do not cover it.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is optional. Students can use OpenCV for computer vision on the camera feed. The processing runs on the Raspberry Pi. Students write Python code that finds a line or object and then steers the car.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state what data is collected or whether the internet is needed. No privacy policy was reviewed. The robot is controlled over WiFi from a computer and the camera streams video to that computer. A search note gives a vendor age of 16 plus, which the research could not verify. A teacher should check which network the car uses and who can see the video.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Assemble the chassis and list each part and its job.
2. Test the line tracking sensors on a track of tape.
3. Add an obstacle and tune how the car avoids it.
4. Measure how long the batteries last during a drive.
5. Redesign a mount for the camera and test the new view.

### computer_science
1. Write a Python program that drives the car forward and back.
2. Program the car to follow a line.
3. Use OpenCV to find a colored object in the camera view.
4. Stream the camera video to a computer and describe the delay.
5. Debug a program that makes the car turn the wrong way.

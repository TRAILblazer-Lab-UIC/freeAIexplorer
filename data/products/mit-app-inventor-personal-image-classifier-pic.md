## Overview
The Personal Image Classifier is a web tool from MIT App Inventor. Students create labels and capture example images with a webcam. They train a model, test it with new images, review a label correctness table and confidence graph, and retrain. They can then download the model for use in an app.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how training examples shape a model.
- Students read a confidence graph and a label correctness table.
- Students can retrain with better examples.

## Benefits for teachers
- The PICaboo tutorial is rated for grades 6-12 at intermediate level.
- Students typically work in pairs.
- A workshop used a facial expression classifier.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free and open source.
- It needs a computer with a browser and a webcam.
- A separate no-PII classroom version exists, per the App Inventor privacy page.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Personal Image Classifier through MIT App Inventor.
- [ ] Read the App Inventor Terms and Privacy page.
- [ ] Ask IT whether a Google sign-in is allowed.
- [ ] Open the classifier in a browser.
- [ ] Allow webcam access.
- [ ] Create labels, capture examples, train, and test.
- [ ] Download the model and plan a later app project.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is an image classifier that students train with transfer learning. The materials say it runs in the browser. Students capture examples, train, test, and retrain, and they then use the model in their own app.

## Privacy and safety
The App Inventor terms say a Google account is required to log in and apps are stored on MIT servers on Google App Engine. MIT tracks site activity, logs IP address, browser, and operating system, and uses cookies. The terms do not mention COPPA or FERPA, and they say MIT does not sign school contract addenda. They say a separate classroom version at code.appinventor.mit.edu collects no personally identifiable information. They do not mention the image classifier. The research did not confirm whether the classifier tool needs sign in or whether training stays in the browser. Webcam images are of students. A teacher should check the classroom version and consider using objects and not faces.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train a classifier on two hand gestures and test it with new images.
2. Work with a partner to train a facial expression classifier and evaluate its predictions.
3. Read the confidence graph and explain which label was hardest.
4. Retrain with better examples and compare the results.
5. Download the model and plan how an app could use it.

### science
1. Train a model to sort two kinds of classroom objects and record the accuracy.
2. Change one condition, such as lighting, and see how results change.
3. Collect data on how many examples a model needs.
4. Write a hypothesis about what makes the model fail and test it.
5. Present the results in a short chart.

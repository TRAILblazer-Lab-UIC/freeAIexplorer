## Overview
A news article from a Richmond, BC school district describes students in grades 1 to 3 making animated stories. Stories ranged from lunar eclipses to wizard adventures. Students used ScratchJr, a block-based coding app for early elementary learners. The article is a classroom report and does not publish a lesson plan.

## Benefits for students
- Students combine storytelling and coding.
- Students make characters move with blocks.
- Students share stories they invented.

## Benefits for teachers
- The article gives a real classroom example.
- ScratchJr is easy for young children.
- Teachers can adapt the idea to their own story units.

## Benefits for schools
- The discovery notes say ScratchJr is free.
- It runs on iOS and Android tablets.
- It is believed to need no internet after install. The article does not confirm this.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds skills in sequencing and storytelling. Students order blocks to tell a story from start to end.

## Privacy and safety
The article does not discuss data, accounts or the internet. ScratchJr is believed to store projects on the device and work offline, but the research did not confirm this from the article. No privacy policy was found. A teacher should check the ScratchJr privacy terms and whether tablets save student projects on shared devices.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Write a short story and animate it in ScratchJr.
2. Add a character who speaks the main events.
3. Retell a favorite tale with new characters.
4. Tell a story about an eclipse or a wizard with a beginning, middle, and end.
5. Share a story and describe the setting.

### computer_science
1. Use blocks to make a character move in order.
2. Add a loop to repeat a movement.
3. Change the speed of a character and describe the effect.
4. Fix a story in which the scenes are out of order.
5. Explain how the blocks in a project work.

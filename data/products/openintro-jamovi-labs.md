## Overview
These are nine statistics labs for jamovi, linked to chapters of OpenIntro Statistics. Topics are intro to jamovi, intro to data, probability, the normal distribution, foundations of inference, inference for categorical and numerical data, and simple and multiple linear regression. The inference lab uses a CDC survey of grade 9 to 12 students. The labs are written in RMarkdown.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with real survey data.
- Students move from basic data ideas to regression.
- Students use free, open source software.

## Benefits for teachers
- The labs follow the chapters of a statistics text.
- Each lab has a clear topic.
- A lab index lists all nine labs.

## Benefits for schools
- The labs and jamovi are free.
- Jamovi must be installed on student computers.
- IT may need to approve the install.

## Where is the AI?
These labs have no AI. They build data skills, such as summarizing data and testing claims. Students also practice reading statistical output.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the lab pages are static pages that need the internet. Students analyze data in jamovi, and the research did not evaluate how jamovi handles data. The inference lab uses a CDC survey on risk behaviors of high school students. A teacher should preview the survey data before use and ask IT to check jamovi.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Complete the Intro to data lab and describe the variables in a dataset.
2. Compute probabilities from a table and check them.
3. Check whether a variable follows a normal distribution.
4. Build a confidence interval and explain what it means.
5. Fit a simple linear regression and describe the slope.

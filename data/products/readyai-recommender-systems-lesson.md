## Overview
This is a ReadyAI lesson plan on how recommender systems work. Students use Facebook data to learn user-based collaborative filtering. They then do an unplugged exercise and discuss how these systems help or hurt people. The lesson is listed for middle and high school, but the vendor does not state a grade range.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how a platform can guess their interests from data.
- Students learn one real recommendation method called collaborative filtering.
- Students practice weighing the good and bad effects of an algorithm.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson comes with a free ReadyAI course page.
- The unplugged exercise lets teachers extend the idea without extra software.
- The discussion gives a clear way to connect AI to everyday apps.

## Benefits for schools
- The course is listed as free.
- The materials run in a browser and no install is listed.
- Students need Facebook data, and the course page says a substitute is allowed. Schools should decide which option fits their policies.

## Getting started
- [ ] Enroll in the free course on edu.readyai.org.
- [ ] Read the lesson called Recommender Systems - How Facebook Learns Your Interests.
- [ ] Decide whether students will use their own Facebook data or a substitute.
- [ ] Choose the substitute option for any student under 13.
- [ ] Ask your school leaders to approve the data choice before class.
- [ ] Run the collaborative filtering section and then the unplugged exercise.
- [ ] Finish with a class talk on how recommendations help or hurt people.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson is about recommender systems and collaborative filtering. The vendor does not say where any AI runs. Students study how these systems work by looking at data and doing an unplugged exercise. They then judge the results with their own words.

## Privacy and safety
An account is needed to enroll on the ReadyAI site. The lesson uses Facebook data, and the course page says students can use their own data if they prefer. The research did not find how that data is handled. Students under 13 cannot hold Facebook accounts. The research on this lesson is thin. A teacher should read the full lesson, check what data students bring and where it is stored, and consider the substitute option.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Explain in your own words how user-based collaborative filtering finds people with similar tastes.
2. Look at a set of interests assigned to a person and guess which data produced them.
3. Complete the unplugged exercise and predict what a friend would like.
4. Compare two users and count how many choices they share.
5. Write a short plan for how you would test whether a recommender gives good advice.

### social_studies
1. Discuss how a recommendation system could help a community find news and events.
2. List ways a recommendation algorithm could harm a person or group.
3. Debate whether platforms should tell users why an item was recommended.
4. Interview a family member about ads or videos they see and talk about why they appear.
5. Write a short class rule for fair use of personal data by apps.

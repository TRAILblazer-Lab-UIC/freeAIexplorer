## Overview
Experience AI offers free AI literacy resources from the Raspberry Pi Foundation, co-developed with Google DeepMind. A September 2026 blog describes Foundations of AI units and single Discovering AI lessons for ages 8 to 12 and 13 to 16. It also lists themed cross-subject resources. Materials include lesson plans, slides, and worksheets. The site also offers a free three-week online course for teachers.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how AI systems are trained.
- Students look at data, predictions, safety, and ethics.
- Students can use lessons in many languages.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each lesson has a plan, slides, and worksheets.
- A free three-week online course supports teachers.
- Teachers need no coding background.

## Benefits for schools
- The resources are free.
- Students need paper or a browser.
- The Foundation estimates the lessons have reached over 200,000 students, according to search results.

## Where is the AI?
The lessons teach how AI systems are trained and how data leads to predictions. Some lessons use online tools in the browser. Students use the lessons to explore how a model learns and where it can go wrong.

## Privacy and safety
Teachers need a free Raspberry Pi Foundation account to download the materials. The internet is needed for that download. Some lessons use online tools in the browser. The research did not review those tools, such as Machine Learning for Kids, and each has its own data practices. The materials do not state what student data is collected or where it goes. A teacher should check the tools used in each lesson and read their privacy terms before students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Sort examples into groups and explain how training data helps a model learn.
2. Train a simple classifier and test it on new examples.
3. Change the training data and note how the predictions change.
4. Name one limit of an AI system and suggest a test for it.
5. Draw a diagram that shows data, a model, and a prediction.

### social_studies
1. Discuss an AI safety question from the lessons and take a position.
2. List who might be affected by a biased AI system.
3. Write a short rule for fair AI use in school.
4. Research a news story about AI ethics and summarize it.
5. Debate who should decide how AI is used in a community.

## Overview
This is a memory matching card game for kindergarten to grade 6. Players flip two cards to match a word such as algorithm, bug, loop or variable with its definition. The PDF includes a vocabulary reference sheet with everyday examples. A student can play alone or with a partner.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn words such as sequence, conditional and debugging in a playful way.
- Students see everyday examples of each term on the reference sheet.
- Students earn one point for each matched card and can race their own score.

## Benefits for teachers
- The game was written by a Pinnguaq makerspace educator.
- The reference sheet gives plain examples a teacher can read aloud.
- The PDF is the first of a series, and the next planned activity is a Kriss Kross puzzle.

## Benefits for schools
- The download is free.
- Classrooms without a printer can write the cards by hand.
- The game uses no devices and collects no data.

## Where is the AI?
This game has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is knowing the words used in programming. Terms such as sequence, loop and variable appear in every coding tool students will meet later.

## Privacy and safety
The game runs on paper. No accounts are needed and no student data is collected. The internet is used only to download the PDF. A teacher should check that the word list fits the grade level and read each definition once before class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Match the word loop with its definition and give an everyday example.
2. Play the game with a partner and keep score at one point per card.
3. Use the reference sheet to act out the word sequence with a morning routine.
4. Pick three words and write a sentence about a robot using each one.
5. Find a bug in a set of directions and fix it.

### ela
1. Flip cards to match each vocabulary word with its meaning.
2. Sort the words into groups and explain how you chose them.
3. Make a new card pair for a word you want to add.
4. Say each definition in your own words to a partner.
5. Write a short story that uses at least four of the words.

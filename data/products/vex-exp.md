## Overview
VEX EXP is a metal classroom robotics platform. Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy calls it an excellent classroom platform for learning programming and robotics. Students build with metal parts and program in VEXcode EXP. EXP robots cannot be used in either V5 competition pathway, but skills transfer to V5.

## Benefits for students
- Students build sturdy metal robots.
- Students program in Blocks, Python, or C++.
- Students can add an AI Vision Sensor to detect objects and AprilTags.

## Benefits for teachers
- The VEX Library lists grades 8 to 12.
- VEXcode EXP runs on Android, iPad, Mac, Windows, and in a browser on Chromebook.
- Skills carry over to V5 for students who move on to competition.

## Benefits for schools
- A search snippet lists the EXP Education Kit at 789.99 USD. Classroom bundles start at 4,249.99 USD. These prices are not verified.
- VEX says images from the AI Vision Sensor stay on the student device. This is a vendor claim.
- Offline use was not confirmed. IT should test the install.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the VEX EXP kit and the VEX Library on the VEX site.
- [ ] Confirm the current price with VEX or a reseller.
- [ ] Build the robot and charge the EXP Brain.
- [ ] Install VEXcode EXP or open the web version.
- [ ] Program a first drive in blocks.
- [ ] Add the AI Vision Sensor after the basics work.
- [ ] Read the VEX privacy policy before students create accounts.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is optional. The AI Vision Sensor detects objects and AprilTags, and it runs on the robot. Students use what the sensor sees to guide the robot. Without the sensor, the kit has no AI.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need. The internet is needed at setup. VEX says AI Vision Sensor images stay on the student device and no personally identifiable information is collected. This is a vendor claim that has not been audited. A search snippet of the VEX software privacy policy says some apps are directed to children under 13 and that Google Analytics is used. The policy page could not be read. A teacher should read it directly.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow a VEX Library lesson to program the robot to drive a square.
2. Use a loop to repeat a movement and shorten the program.
3. Add a sensor condition so the robot stops near a wall.
4. Compare a block program with the same program in Python.
5. Use the AI Vision Sensor to make the robot react to an AprilTag.

### engineering
1. Build the robot and note each design choice.
2. Test the drivetrain on carpet and tile and record the difference.
3. Add an attachment and test whether it helps with a task.
4. Redesign the chassis to carry a load.
5. Present the final design and explain what changed.

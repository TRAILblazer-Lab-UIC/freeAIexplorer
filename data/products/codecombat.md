## Overview
CodeCombat is a game-based platform where students learn Python and JavaScript by writing code to control game characters. Teachers get a dashboard and school accounts. The privacy policy also covers CodeCombat AI League and AI HackStack. Student accounts for school use can only be created with a teacher's consent.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn real text code through game play.
- Students see their code move a character right away.
- Students can choose Python or JavaScript.

## Benefits for teachers
- A dashboard lets teachers manage classes.
- Students join with class codes or through Clever.
- Rostering works through Clever and Ed.link.

## Benefits for schools
- Batch notes say the first course is free and full access is paid. Prices were not confirmed.
- It runs in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
- Teacher consent is required for student accounts.

## Where is the AI?
The privacy policy covers the AI League and AI HackStack products. The materials do not describe what the AI does in the core coding game. The vendor does not say where the AI runs.

## Privacy and safety
Every student needs an account, and the internet is needed at all times. The vendor collects a first name or pseudonym, a last initial, optional email, and progress. Data is hosted on US servers at AWS and MongoDB Atlas, and processors include Clever, Ed.link, Cloudflare, and Google. Google Analytics applies to all users, and the policy states that third party behavioral tracking is in use. The policy says it follows FERPA, COPPA, and state laws and does not sell personal information. It does not describe AI data handling. A teacher should read the privacy policy with the school and use pseudonyms for student names.

## Overview
This is a free, self-paced online course for teachers about generative AI. Google built it with MIT RAISE. It takes about two hours. The announcement describes it for middle and high school teachers, and administrators can also take it.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn with a teacher who understands generative AI.
- Students may get clearer guidance on when AI tools fit a task.
- Students share no data with the course, which is made for teachers.

## Benefits for teachers
- The course is self-paced and takes about two hours.
- Teachers who finish receive a certificate.
- The certificate can go to the district for professional development credit, which depends on district and state rules.

## Benefits for schools
- The course is free.
- The course is hosted on the Grow with Google site and needs only a browser.
- Partner districts at launch included Albuquerque, Anaheim Union, Chicago Public Schools, Miami-Dade, and Sun Prairie.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the course on the Grow with Google site.
- [ ] Check whether a Google account is needed.
- [ ] Set aside about two hours.
- [ ] Work through the course at your own pace.
- [ ] Save the certificate when you finish.
- [ ] Ask your district whether it counts for professional development credit.
- [ ] Share one classroom idea with your team.

## Where is the AI?
The course teaches generative AI basics and ways to use generative AI tools in teaching. The course itself is hosted by the vendor in the cloud. Teachers take lessons and then apply ideas to their own planning and classroom work.

## Privacy and safety
The internet is always needed and the course is hosted by the vendor. The research did not confirm whether a Google account is needed. The announcement has no course specific privacy information and links only a general MIT Open Learning privacy statement. The course is for teachers, and no student data is involved. A teacher should read the Grow with Google privacy terms before signing in with a work account.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Explain in your own words what generative AI is after the teacher shares the course ideas.
2. As a teacher led demo, compare an AI generated answer with a textbook answer and list the differences.
3. Write a short class guide on good and poor uses of AI tools.
4. As a teacher led demo, test how a change in a prompt changes an AI answer.
5. Make a list of questions that a user should ask before trusting an AI answer.

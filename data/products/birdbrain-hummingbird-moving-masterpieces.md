## Overview
Moving Masterpieces is a BirdBrain project. Students choose a famous artwork and recreate it as a 3D piece that adds motion and light with a Hummingbird Bit robotics kit. They use servo motors and LEDs and sketch where light and motion will go. They write an artist statement and present to the class. The Bit page lists grade 8 and 6 or more hours. The Duo version lists grades 4 to 12.

## Benefits for students
- Students blend art, building and coding in one project.
- Students research an artist and explain their choices.
- Students share their work in a class gallery.

## Benefits for teachers
- The project is tagged for art, music, world languages and language arts.
- It lists standards from Pennsylvania, CCRA and NGSS.
- A shorter starter project called My First Moving Scene is available.

## Benefits for schools
- The project page is free.
- Schools must buy a Hummingbird Bit Premium Kit. The research did not check a price.
- Teacher materials need a free BirdBrain registration.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Moving Masterpieces project on the BirdBrain site.

## Where is the AI?
This project has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is controlling outputs. Students program servos and LEDs to respond to their own instructions.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need or whether the internet is required. The programming app used was not named on the project page and the research did not check its data flow. Teacher materials need a free registration. A teacher should check the BirdBrain software and school device rules first. Student artist statements should stay within school sharing rules.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Choose a famous artwork and research the artist.
2. Sketch where light and motion will go in your piece.
3. Build a 3D version from craft and recycled materials.
4. Explain how your motion and light fit the artist's goals.
5. Present the piece in a class gallery.

### engineering
1. Connect a servo and an LED to the Hummingbird Bit.
2. Test the motion and adjust the mount so it moves smoothly.
3. Program the servo to move at the right time.
4. Fix a part that jams or comes loose.
5. Write down what you would change in a second version.

### ela
1. Write an artist statement about your artwork.
2. Explain the artist's life and style in a short paragraph.
3. Practice a short talk about your piece.
4. Give feedback to a classmate using clear words.
5. Revise your statement after the presentation.

### world_languages
1. Research an artist from a place where the language is spoken.
2. Write a short label for your piece in the new language.
3. Learn the words for light, motion and color in the new language.
4. Present one sentence about your art in that language.
5. Compare names of artworks in two languages.

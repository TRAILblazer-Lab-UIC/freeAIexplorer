## Overview
This is a KS3 teachers guide with three activity sheets for programming micro:bit robot buggies around a fictional Auto City map. KS3 covers ages 11 to 14 in England. Route A is a simple linear route, Route B is a complex linear route, and Route C adds radio control and the accelerometer. The guide says the activities can be done as theory without buggies.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a buggy to follow a set route.
- Students move from simple routes to a radio-controlled traffic activity.
- Students learn about motors, sensors, driver assistance, radio, and radar.

## Benefits for teachers
- Three sheets rise in difficulty and can form a mini scheme of work.
- A printable Auto City map is included.
- A link leads to the Siemens Self-Driving Challenge online game.

## Benefits for schools
- The guide is free.
- The teacher must supply micro:bit buggies. Their price was not checked.
- Without buggies, students can code on paper and check against answer sheets.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Siemens Driverless Cars challenge teachers guide.
- [ ] Print the Auto City map and the activity sheets.
- [ ] Provide micro:bit robot buggies.
- [ ] Open the micro:bit Blocks editor in a browser.
- [ ] Try Route A yourself first.
- [ ] Run Route A with students.
- [ ] Move to Route B and then Route C.
- [ ] For Route C, set up two micro:bits as controller and receiver.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. Students write step-by-step code that the buggy follows. They build skills in sequencing and sensors as they plan routes and driver assistance rules.

## Privacy and safety
The guide describes no accounts or data collection. Each sheet needs the micro:bit Blocks editor in a browser, and students use internet research for some questions. A link leads to an online Siemens game. Programs run on the micro:bit. A teacher should decide whether students save projects to the device or download them, and should review the linked sites.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Program a buggy to follow Route A on the Auto City map.
2. Add turns and stops to complete the complex Route B.
3. Use radio blocks to send a drive command from one micro:bit to another.
4. Use the accelerometer to steer the buggy in Route C.
5. Write the code on paper first and check it against the answer sheet.

### engineering
1. Explain how motors and sensors help a driverless car.
2. Describe driver assistance technology, such as automatic braking, in your own words.
3. Plan a radio traffic control system for the Auto City map.
4. Test the buggy and record where it leaves the route.
5. Suggest one change to the buggy design that would improve the run.

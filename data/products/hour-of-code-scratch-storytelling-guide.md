## Overview
This is a 2022 facilitator guide for a one-hour workshop. Participants animate an interactive story in Scratch. Per search results it follows an Imagine, Create, Share flow with about 10 minutes of imagining and about 40 minutes of creating before the group shares. Facilitators set up a Scratch studio for sharing.

## Benefits for students
- Students plan a story and then animate it.
- Students learn how one character can speak and another can wait.
- Students share their stories in a Scratch studio.

## Benefits for teachers
- The workshop fits in one hour.
- The Imagine, Create, Share flow is simple to lead.
- An Hour of Code completion certificate is available through code.org.

## Benefits for schools
- The guide is free.
- Scratch runs in a browser and works on Chromebooks.
- IT has nothing to install.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Storytelling with Scratch facilitation guide in the Scratch Foundation learning library.
- [ ] Note that the old PDF link now redirects. The guide text was not read directly.
- [ ] Open Scratch in a browser.
- [ ] Create a Scratch studio and allow anyone to add projects.
- [ ] Practice the demo of one character speaking and a second character waiting.
- [ ] Plan about 10 minutes to imagine and about 40 minutes to create.
- [ ] Set time at the end for sharing.

## Where is the AI?
This guide has no AI. It builds sequencing and event skills. Students order blocks to make characters speak at the right time. Scratch runs in the browser.

## Privacy and safety
Scratch accounts are optional, and the account list says one is needed only to share to a studio. Scratch accounts and shared projects are hosted by the Scratch Foundation, and its privacy policy was not read. The internet is needed at all times for the online editor. The research could not open the guide itself. Its content comes from a search snippet. A teacher should decide whether students sign in to share and should review the studio so it holds only school safe projects.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Plan a short story with a beginning, a middle, and an end.
2. Animate two characters who speak in turns.
3. Add a choice so the reader picks what happens next.
4. Write the dialogue first and then code it.
5. Share the story in the class studio and write a comment on a classmate's story.

### computer_science
1. Make one sprite say a line of text.
2. Use a wait block to time a second character's line.
3. Use a broadcast so one character triggers another.
4. Fix a script where the lines appear in the wrong order.
5. Explain to a partner how the program runs step by step.

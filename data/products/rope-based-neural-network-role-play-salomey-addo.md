## Overview
Salomey Afua Addo designed this role play to teach neural networks without computers. One student is a supervisor, six are input units, two are hidden units and one is the output unit. Ropes show connection strength between units. Students act out forward propagation, evaluation and a simplified backpropagation using a crop disease example.

## Benefits for students
- Students feel how data moves from layer to layer.
- Students see connection strength as a rope that is added or removed.
- Students practice using a real example about crop disease.

## Benefits for teachers
- The materials include role instruction sheets and a data table for the supervisor.
- Cards carry data values between layers.
- The activity was designed for schools in Ghana without computers.

## Benefits for schools
- The activity is free.
- Ropes and printed cards are the only items needed.
- No devices or accounts are needed during the activity.

## Where is the AI?
The activity has no computer AI. Students act as the parts of a neural network. They pass data values on cards and use ropes for connection strength. Students then compare the output with the right answer and adjust the ropes, which models learning.

## Privacy and safety
The activity is on paper. No student data is collected and no accounts are needed. The internet is used only to get the materials. A teacher should check that ten students are available for one network and that the ropes are safe to handle.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Act as an input unit and pass a data value to a hidden unit on a card.
2. Add or remove a rope and describe how the output changes.
3. Compare the network output with the correct answer and name the error.
4. Draw the network from the role play and label each layer.
5. Explain backpropagation in your own words after acting it out.

### science
1. Record crop disease signs in the data table and pass them into the network.
2. Predict whether a plant is healthy or sick from the network output.
3. Discuss which plant signs should count most and adjust the ropes to match.
4. Compare the network answer with a farmer's judgment.
5. Design a new set of crop signs for the network to use.

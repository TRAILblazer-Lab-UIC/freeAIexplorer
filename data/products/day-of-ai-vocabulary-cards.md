## Overview
Day of AI Vocabulary Cards build AI literacy and academic vocabulary. Each card links a definition to a real-world example. Teachers can use the cards for warm-ups, centers, or short lessons of 5 to 15 minutes. The page lists ages 8 to 10, and the item notes say grades 3 to 12.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn terms such as data and model with real examples.
- Students practice the words in short, friendly activities.
- Students build the language they need for later AI lessons.

## Benefits for teachers
- The cards work as warm-ups, centers, or short lessons.
- An Educator Guide comes with the cards.
- The cards can supplement any foundational AI lesson.

## Benefits for schools
- The cards are free with a login.
- Teachers can print the cards or show them on a screen.
- The materials describe no student data collection.

## Where is the AI?
The cards have no running AI program. They teach the words people use to talk about AI. Students learn terms such as data and model and see them tied to real examples. A shared vocabulary helps students speak clearly about the AI tools they meet later.

## Privacy and safety
Teachers need an account to view all the resources. The cards can be printed or shown on a screen. The research did not examine the digital option for student screens, and its data handling is unknown. The research did not read the Day of AI privacy policy. A teacher should check how the digital cards load before students use them.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Match each AI word card to a real-world example and read the pair aloud.
2. Write a sentence that uses a new AI word correctly.
3. Draw a picture that shows the meaning of one vocabulary card.
4. Play a guessing game where one student describes a card without saying the word.
5. Build a class word wall from the cards and add one example for each word.

### computer_science
1. Sort the cards into groups such as data, learning, and tools.
2. Explain the word data to a partner using a classroom example.
3. Use the word model in a sentence about how a machine makes a prediction.
4. Find an example of each card word in a device the class uses.
5. Rank the cards from easiest to hardest and tell why.

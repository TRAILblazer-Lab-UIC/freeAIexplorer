## Overview
CodeHS Creative Computing is a first-year high school computer science course of about 65 hours. It has three modules. They are Web Design with HTML and CSS, Introduction to Programming with Karel the Dog in JavaScript, and JavaScript and Graphics. Students end with a personal portfolio website.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a website that they can show to others.
- Students learn programming with Karel the Dog.
- Students draw with JavaScript graphics.

## Benefits for teachers
- The course is planned as one semester.
- Teachers add it to My Courses and enroll students.
- A Spanish version of the content is listed.

## Benefits for schools
- The page lists no price and says to contact CodeHS.
- The notes say a paid school license applies.
- Data goes to the vendor's cloud, and students need accounts.

## Getting started
- [ ] Contact CodeHS for a school license.
- [ ] Add the course to My Courses.
- [ ] Enroll students.
- [ ] Read the course overview.
- [ ] Work through the first web design lesson yourself.
- [ ] Plan the semester across the three modules.
- [ ] Check the standards the course lists, such as NC and VA and WV standards.

## Where is the AI?
This course has no AI. It builds skills in sequencing, logic, and debugging. Students write HTML, CSS, and JavaScript and test the result.

## Privacy and safety
Student accounts are needed, and the internet is needed at all times. CodeHS collects name and email as the school chooses, coursework, saved code, IP address, and cookies. The policy says it acts as a school official under FERPA, relies on school consent for users under 13, and does not sell or rent personal information. It shares data with hosting, support, analytics, and optional integrations such as Clever and Google. A teacher should decide what name and email the school will share and read the CodeHS privacy policy.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Write commands that move Karel the Dog to pick up a ball.
2. Use a loop to shorten a long Karel program.
3. Draw a shape with JavaScript graphics.
4. Fix a program that has a planned bug.
5. Explain how one function in a program works.

### cte
1. Plan a personal portfolio website with a list of pages.
2. Build a home page with HTML and style it with CSS.
3. Add a page that shows a project and describes the skills used.
4. Ask a partner to review the site as a visitor.
5. Revise the portfolio after feedback.

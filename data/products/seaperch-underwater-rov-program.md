## Overview
SeaPerch is a project-based STEM program in which students build and operate a remotely operated underwater vehicle, or ROV. Standards mapping covers grades 5 to 12. Groups of 3 to 4 students share a kit. The program began in 2003 at the MIT Sea Grant College Program and is now run by RoboNation.

## Benefits for students
- Students waterproof motors, attach propellers, and build a control box.
- Students drive their own ROV in a pool or tank.
- Students can add extensions such as a hydrophone, a camera, or a depth gauge.

## Benefits for teachers
- A build manual, training videos, and lesson plans are available.
- The standards mapping names NGSS, Common Core, and P21.
- Training can be requested on site or online.

## Benefits for schools
- Prices are on the RoboNation shop and were not read for this entry.
- One tool kit is needed per five SeaPerch kits and is sold separately.
- No account or internet is needed after the order, and the research notes say some districts offer free training.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the SeaPerch program on the RoboNation website.
- [ ] Order kits and a tool kit online.
- [ ] Read the build manual and the Basic SeaPerch Build Guide.
- [ ] Watch the free training videos.
- [ ] Plan one adult mentor for each small group in elementary and middle school.
- [ ] Arrange a pool or tank to test the ROV.
- [ ] Build the control box and waterproof the motors.
- [ ] Test the ROV in water with students.

## Where is the AI?
This program has no AI. Students steer the ROV with a hand-built control box. They build skills in sensing and cause and effect as they test how the motors move the vehicle.

## Privacy and safety
The ROV is a tethered hardware build with no software or accounts. The seaperch.org site shows a cookie notice and links a privacy policy that the research did not read. Educators can join a mailing list. A teacher should plan water and electrical safety, with an adult watching each group near the pool.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Follow the build guide to assemble the ROV frame and control box.
2. Waterproof the motors and test each one before sealing.
3. Adjust the frame to make the ROV level in the water.
4. Steer the ROV through a small obstacle course.
5. Add an extension such as a camera and describe how it changed the design.

### science
1. Test how weight and air in the frame change buoyancy.
2. Predict whether the ROV will float, then check in water.
3. Record how long the ROV takes to cross the pool.
4. Discuss how scientists use ROVs to explore the ocean.
5. Build a hydrophone and listen for sounds under water.

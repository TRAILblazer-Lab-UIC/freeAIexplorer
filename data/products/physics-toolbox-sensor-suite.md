## Overview
Physics Toolbox Sensor Suite is a phone app that records data from built-in sensors. Modes include accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, barometer, light meter, and sound meter. It also has a tone generator, a color generator, and a stroboscope. Recordings export as CSV files for spreadsheets or Python.

## Benefits for students
- Students use their phones for real measurements.
- Students see live graphs and digital readouts.
- Students analyze exported data in a spreadsheet.

## Benefits for teachers
- A teacher blog calls it easier to pick up than phyphox.
- The blog also says it is less rigorous.
- Teachers can pick the sensor that matches each lesson.

## Benefits for schools
- The core app is free with optional Pro features.
- It works without an internet connection.
- Some features, such as the spectrogram, sit behind the paid version.

## Getting started
- [ ] Install the app from Google Play or the App Store.
- [ ] Check which sensors each student phone has.
- [ ] Open a sensor mode and watch the live graph.
- [ ] Record a short run and export it as a CSV file.
- [ ] Open the CSV file in a spreadsheet.
- [ ] Decide whether the free version is enough for your lesson.
- [ ] Ask IT to review the analytics note below.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in the app. It reads sensors and records the data. Students build data skills as they graph and compare measurements.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and sensor measurements stay on the device. The policy says measurements are never sent to the company. The apps send usage events, device information, and app instance identifiers to Google Analytics for Firebase and Crashlytics. The policy mentions COPPA only to say technical identifiers can count as personal information. The materials do not state a minimum age. A teacher should read the privacy policy and decide whether students may install the app on their own phones.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Record the accelerometer while a student walks and describe the pattern.
2. Measure the sound level in the classroom at different times.
3. Use the magnetometer to map a magnet's field near a desk.
4. Compare light meter readings by a window and in a dark corner.
5. Use the tone generator to test how pitch changes with frequency.

### math
1. Export a CSV file and make a graph in a spreadsheet.
2. Find the average of a set of sensor readings.
3. Compare two data sets and describe which varies more.
4. Predict the next value in a series and check it.
5. Write a short summary that uses numbers from your data.

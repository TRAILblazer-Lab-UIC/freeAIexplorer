## Overview
This is an unplugged online safety lesson of about 45 minutes in Code.org Course E, part of CS Fundamentals 2020-2021. Students stand or sit in response to statements about themselves and discuss why people share online. They watch a Common Sense Education video and sort examples such as a home address or favorite food as private or personal. They finish with a printed exit ticket and an online quiz.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn the difference between private and personal information.
- Students practice judging what is safe to share.
- Students take part in a movement warm-up.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson includes a slide deck, a video, an exit ticket, and a quiz.
- The page labels the standard as CSTA 1B-NI-05.
- An optional Share Jumper game and family activity are available.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It teaches students how to think about the information they share online. Students practice data thinking as they sort examples.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether an account is needed. The quiz is an online Google Form, and the optional Share Jumper game is online. The page does not say what data either one collects. A teacher should check the quiz terms with the school. A teacher should also note that the current test says information is personal when it would also be true for many other people.

## Ideas by subject
### library_media
1. Stand up or sit down in response to statements about yourself.
2. Sort a home address, a favorite food, and a pet's name as private or personal.
3. Explain to a partner why a home address is private.
4. Watch the video and list one tip for sharing safely.
5. Complete the exit ticket and share one idea with the class.

## Overview
Audacity is a free, open source audio recorder and editor for Windows, macOS, and Linux. Students record, cut, mix, and export audio for podcasts, interviews, and music projects. It supports plugins and a spectrogram view. The current release is Audacity 4.0.2.

## Benefits for students
- Students can record and edit a podcast or interview with a free tool.
- Students can layer narration over background tracks and cut pieces of music into new versions.
- The spectrogram view lets students see sound as a picture.

## Benefits for teachers
- The tool is easy to learn and works for many subjects.
- Teachers use it to record podcasts, lessons, and language practice.
- Files export to WAV, MP3, FLAC, and Ogg.

## Benefits for schools
- Audacity is free and open source.
- The 3.x privacy notice makes the random ID opt-in. Update checks are on by default and can be turned off.
- IT will likely install it. The tool runs on the computer and needs no student accounts.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download Audacity from the official site.
- [ ] Ask IT to install it on school computers.
- [ ] Open Preferences and turn off update checks if the district requires it.
- [ ] Leave telemetry and error reports off unless IT approves them.
- [ ] Plug in a microphone and record a short test.
- [ ] Practice cutting, mixing, and exporting.
- [ ] Install the optional Intel OpenVINO AI plugins only if the school wants them.

## Where is the AI?
Audacity itself is audio editing software. Optional Intel OpenVINO plugins add local AI effects such as speech transcription, noise suppression, music separation, and music generation. The plugins run on the device and work on Windows only, per the Audacity blog. Students can use them to clean up a recording or get a transcript.

## Privacy and safety
Audacity needs no account and works on the device. Update checks are on by default and send the operating system, version, and country from an anonymized IP address. They can be turned off in Preferences. Error reports are reviewed before sending and a random ID is created only on opt-in. Audio.com sharing is optional and sends audio to a vendor service. The notice the research read covers version 3.x and the Audacity 4 notice was not read. A teacher should check the update and sharing settings on each computer.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Record a short melody and cut and paste the best parts into a new version.
2. Layer a narration over a background track and balance the volume.
3. Record an instrument and view its sound in the spectrogram.
4. Make a remix from three short clips and export it as an MP3.
5. Compare a noisy recording with a cleaned version and describe the change.

### theater_media
1. Record and edit a podcast episode with an intro and an outro.
2. Interview a classmate, cut out the pauses, and export the result.
3. Add sound effects to a short scene and time them to the action.
4. Record a radio ad and trim it to exactly 30 seconds.
5. Use a transcript from a plugin to check your edit against the words spoken.

## Overview
LaserGRBL is a free, open source Windows program that sends images and G-code to GRBL based laser engravers. It converts images with grayscale, dithering, and vectorization. It also offers jogging, a job time preview, and GRBL configuration tools. Design work needs separate software.

## Benefits for students
- Students turn a picture into an engraving.
- Students see a time preview before the job runs.
- Students learn how a machine reads instructions.

## Benefits for teachers
- The tool is free and works with Grbl v0.9 and v1.1 controllers.
- Teachers set up a job with a few steps.
- Teachers rate the learning curve as advanced.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free. The developer asks for donations.
- A GRBL laser engraver is a separate purchase.
- Laser use needs IT and safety approval.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download LaserGRBL from lasergrbl.com or GitHub releases.
- [ ] Ask IT and your safety lead for approval.
- [ ] Install it on a Windows computer.
- [ ] Connect the laser controller by USB or WiFi.
- [ ] Plan safety glasses and adult supervision for every job.
- [ ] Test a small image on scrap material.
- [ ] Note that Chromebooks are not supported.

## Where is the AI?
LaserGRBL has no AI. It converts images by fixed rules. Students build sequencing and design skills as they plan how a machine will draw a picture.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used for setup. The site and README describe local use with a USB or WiFi link to the laser controller. No privacy statement was found, and network behavior beyond that is not described. A laser needs safety glasses and supervision. A teacher should get approval from IT and the safety lead before any student use.

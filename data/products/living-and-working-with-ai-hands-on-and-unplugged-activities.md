## Overview
This CSTA resource library session shows how to teach AI with interactive demos, unplugged activities, and coding projects. The activities come from the open access AI4GA curriculum. Researchers and middle school teachers co-designed and tested the lessons. Participants explore self-driving vehicles, face recognition, and how machines learn and decide.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how self-driving vehicles and robots work.
- Students explore how computers recognize faces.
- Unplugged activities let students learn AI ideas without a device.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lessons were tested by middle school teachers.
- The curriculum gives interactive demos and coding projects.
- The AI4GA material is described as open access.

## Benefits for schools
- The session costs nothing, but the slides need a CSTA membership.
- The vendor does not state hardware needs.
- Unplugged activities lower the device load.

## Where is the AI?
The materials teach how AI works through examples such as self-driving vehicles and face recognition. The vendor does not describe where any tool runs. Students learn how machines learn and decide, then apply the ideas in their own activities.

## Privacy and safety
The slides need a CSTA membership login. The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. They do not say where any demo runs or where student data goes. The research read no privacy policy for this item. A teacher should check each AI4GA activity for accounts, webcams, and face data before class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Act out how a self-driving car senses its surroundings and chooses an action.
2. Sort pictures into groups to show how a computer might learn to recognize faces.
3. Role-play a machine that learns from examples and corrects its mistakes.
4. Discuss what could go wrong when a machine decides without a person.
5. Draw a flowchart of the choices a robot makes when it meets an obstacle.

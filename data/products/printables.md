## Overview
Printables is a free 3D model library run by Prusa Research. Users download model files and pre made G-code for 3D printers. Uploading and community features need an account. Prusa's education program invites teachers to share classroom projects on Printables.

## Benefits for students
- Students can find ready models to print.
- Students can see how others design and share objects.
- Students can browse without any account.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can download models for a class project.
- The education program supports sharing classroom projects.
- Each model has its own license. Check it.

## Benefits for schools
- The site is free to use.
- It runs in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
- A 3D printer and a slicer are needed to print.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open Printables in a browser and browse models.
- [ ] Download a model without an account.
- [ ] Check the license on each model.
- [ ] Open the model in a slicer and prepare it for your printer.
- [ ] Create an account only to upload or follow.
- [ ] Look at the Prusa education program pages.
- [ ] Ask IT to review the site before student use.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in Printables. It is a library of files. Students build skills in logic and sequencing as they move from a model to a printed object.

## Privacy and safety
The research could not read the privacy pages. Search snippets say browsing and downloading need no account and uploading does, and the research did not verify this. The materials do not state age rules or data practices. Uploads and community content go to the vendor's cloud. A teacher should check the site terms and community content with IT before students create accounts.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Download a model and print it, as classroom projects in the Prusa education program do.
2. Compare two models of the same object and pick the better design.
3. Measure a printed part and compare it with the model.
4. Modify a downloaded model in a design program.
5. Write a short review of a model and its print quality.

### cte
1. Practice the full workflow from a model to a printed part.
2. Sort models by use and build a class parts library.
3. Check the license on three models and explain what each allows.
4. Prepare a batch of parts for a classroom project.
5. Present a finished print and list the steps you followed.

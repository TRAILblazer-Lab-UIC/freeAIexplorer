## Overview
Duckietown offers university-level classroom resources for teaching robot autonomy. Modules cover autonomy basics, modeling and control, computer vision, estimation, planning, multi-vehicle systems, machine learning, and safety. Each module has slides, notes, videos, and learning activities for simulation or a Duckiebot. The site says the materials have been used at more than 300 universities.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how a self-driving robot senses, plans, and acts.
- Students can practice in simulation before they touch a robot.
- Students can follow a self-driving cars course on edX.

## Benefits for teachers
- Every module bundles slides, notes, short videos, and activities.
- The site lists example courses from several universities.
- An instructor guide suggests that students watch the course at home and use class time for activities.

## Benefits for schools
- The page lists no prices. It links to pricing for learners, universities, and schools.
- Simulation activities need no robot. Physical activities need Duckiebot kits.
- The materials are aimed at university level. A high school needs advanced students and IT support.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Duckietown educational resources page and browse the module list.

## Where is the AI?
The AI includes computer vision, machine learning, planning, and autonomous driving. It runs on the Duckiebot or in a simulation on a laptop. Students work through activities that build up a robot's driving abilities and then change the code to test their own ideas.

## Privacy and safety
The page links login and sign-up at the Duckietown hub, and the research did not confirm whether the free materials need an account. The research did not read the privacy terms, and the materials do not state what data the hub account collects. The internet is needed for setup. A teacher should check the account terms before students sign up.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Work through the computer vision module and describe how a robot finds lane lines.
2. Run a simulation of a robot driving and record when it leaves the lane.
3. Compare two planning methods in the planning module and explain the difference.
4. Watch a short module video and write three questions about machine learning.
5. Adjust a control setting in simulation and chart how the robot's path changes.

### engineering
1. Follow the modeling and control module and sketch how a robot turns.
2. Assemble a Duckiebot and test its wheels and camera.
3. Tune the robot's control values to reduce wobble on a straight road.
4. Test the safety module's ideas by listing how a robot should stop.
5. Run a multi-vehicle activity and note how the cars avoid each other.

## Overview
Zumi is a small self-driving car kit built around a Raspberry Pi Zero and a Pi Camera. Students learn about computer vision, face and object detection, gesture learning, and sign detection. They also train a data model. Students code with Blockly or Python, and lessons are hosted on Robolink's Basecamp site.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a car and then teach it to see.
- Students move from blocks to Python at their own pace.
- Students train a data model and watch the car use it.

## Benefits for teachers
- Lessons for grades 6 to 12 and up are ready on Basecamp.
- The kit needs only a device with a browser to connect.
- The classroom set includes 30 minutes of professional development.

## Benefits for schools
- A single kit is 179.99 USD. A classroom set of 10 kits is 1799.90 USD. Some resellers charge more.
- The car works with Chromebooks, Macs, Windows computers, iPads, and Android devices.
- The vendor describes the AI as running on the car. Offline use was not confirmed on a vendor page.

## Getting started
- [ ] Order the Zumi kit from Robolink or a reseller.
- [ ] Build the car and charge the battery.
- [ ] Plan for about 40 minutes to 1 hour of battery life.
- [ ] Connect a device with a browser to the car.
- [ ] Open the lessons on Robolink Basecamp.
- [ ] Run the first driving lesson in blocks before trying Python.
- [ ] Test the face and sign detection lessons with a small group.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is computer vision that runs on the car itself. It detects objects, faces, gestures, and signs from the camera. Students collect examples and train a data model. They then test how well the car uses what it learned.

## Privacy and safety
The vendor page does not describe data handling or account rules. The brief says the camera and models run on the robot's Raspberry Pi, with students connecting over its own Wi-Fi, and the research did not confirm this on a vendor page. The materials do not state what student data is collected. A teacher should confirm with Robolink how face images are handled and decide whether students may use real faces in a class activity.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a Blockly program that drives the car around a taped course.
2. Train the car to recognize a sign and test it at different distances.
3. Switch a block program to Python and compare the two versions.
4. Test face detection with a class photo and list when it fails.
5. Write down the steps the car follows to decide when to stop.

### engineering
1. Assemble the Zumi kit in a team and test each part.
2. Design a road with signs and turns for the car to navigate.
3. Measure how long the battery lasts during driving and record it.
4. Improve the track layout after the car gets stuck.
5. Pitch a new feature for a self-driving car and sketch how it would work.

## Overview
Pixelorama is a free open source pixel art and sprite editor built with the Godot engine. It has an animation timeline with layers, frames, onion skinning, and tags. Students draw and animate sprites, tilesets, and palettes. A browser version runs from the itch.io page, and desktop builds exist for Windows, macOS, and Linux.

## Benefits for students
- Students draw and animate sprites in one tool.
- Students can build tilesets for rectangular, isometric, and hex tiles.
- Students export to PNG, GIF, spritesheet, and video.

## Benefits for teachers
- The tool is easy to teach.
- The browser version starts in minutes.
- Community extensions add extra features.

## Benefits for schools
- Users name their own price on itch.io. A free download is available. It is also on Steam.
- The browser version needs no IT install.
- Student work stays on the device.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the web version from the itch.io page.
- [ ] Choose a free download or a desktop build if you want one.
- [ ] Ask IT about a desktop install on managed computers.
- [ ] Test the browser version on a Chromebook. The researcher did not test it.
- [ ] Draw a small sprite and add a second frame.
- [ ] Turn on onion skinning and play the animation.
- [ ] Export a GIF or a spritesheet.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in Pixelorama. Students draw every pixel by hand. They build pattern spotting and sequencing skills as they plan how a sprite changes across frames.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed to use the editor. An itch.io login is only for comments or following. The internet is used to open the web version or download a build. The page describes no data collection, but the research found no privacy policy. The research did not confirm whether the browser version works offline. A teacher should check where students save and share their exported files.

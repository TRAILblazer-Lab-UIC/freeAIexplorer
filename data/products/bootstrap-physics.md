## Overview
Bootstrap:Physics is a Physics First course from a collaboration of AAPT, AMTA, Bootstrap, and STEMteachersNYC. Students learn the Pyret language and then build computational models of energy, constant velocity, uniform acceleration, balanced forces, and unbalanced forces. The module targets ninth grade physics. A student workbook is available as PDFs.

## Benefits for students
- Students build models of motion in code.
- Students test ideas about forces by running the model.
- Students keep a workbook as they learn.

## Benefits for teachers
- Six units run from a Pyret introduction to unbalanced forces.
- Standards named include Physics First and NGSS.
- Teachers can print or share the workbook PDFs.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free.
- Students use a browser editor, and Chromebooks are listed.
- Students sign in with Google, which stores work in Google Drive.

## Where is the AI?
The course has no AI. It builds the skills of modeling and sequencing, as students code a physical system step by step. Students also practice reading data from a model.

## Privacy and safety
Students use the code.pyret.org editor, and the internet is needed throughout. A Google account is used to save programs, and the research did not confirm whether the editor runs without sign in. The editor receives the Google identity and email and logs usage events and errors. It does not log program contents, which are saved in the student's own Google Drive. The privacy page does not mention children or how long usage data is kept. The research did not find a Bootstrap privacy page. A teacher should confirm that the school allows Google accounts for students.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Code a model of constant velocity and compare it with a real cart or walker.
2. Change the acceleration in a model and describe the result.
3. Model balanced forces and show that the object does not speed up.
4. Add an unbalanced force and predict the motion.
5. Track energy in a model and explain where it goes.

### computer_science
1. Write a first Pyret function in Unit 0.
2. Test a function with two examples.
3. Change a number in a model and record the result.
4. Find an error in a short program.
5. Explain a model's code in plain words.

## Overview
This is a media literacy lesson plan for high school and junior college students. It shows how writing choices shape what readers think about AI. Students learn to spot hype and personification in AI coverage. They learn to prefer process verbs such as generates or outputs over verbs that imply belief. Aspen Digital developed it with the National Association for Media Literacy Education, aiEDU, and high school educators.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn to read AI news with a careful eye.
- Students practice choosing precise verbs.
- Students learn to separate hype from reality.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no software and no AI runs on any device. It teaches AI literacy and media literacy. Students read AI news and watch for words that make a machine sound human. They build skills in careful reading and in describing what AI systems really do.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson is a document based lesson about news articles. No account is needed and the research found no student data collection. Students may read articles online and the page does not specify this. A teacher should choose news articles that suit the age of the students. A teacher should also read the four criteria for strong AI writing before class.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Highlight words in an AI news story that make a machine sound like a person.
2. Rewrite a sentence using a process verb such as generates or outputs.
3. Apply the four criteria for strong AI writing to a short article.
4. Write a headline about an AI tool that avoids hype.
5. Trade articles with a partner and give feedback on word choice.

### library_media
1. Compare two news stories about the same AI tool and note the differences in tone.
2. Build a list of hype words and personifying verbs found in coverage.
3. Check a claim in an AI article against a second source.
4. Create a poster that helps readers spot hype in AI news.
5. Lead a class talk on how a reader can tell hype from facts.

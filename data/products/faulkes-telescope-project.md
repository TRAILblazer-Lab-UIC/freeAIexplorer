## Overview
The Faulkes Telescope Project gives schools access to robotic telescopes for astronomy observing. The official address now redirects to the Schools' Observatory, run by Liverpool John Moores University with the Dill Faulkes Educational Trust. Registered users can request observations on the Liverpool Telescope and the Las Cumbres Observatory network of more than 20 telescopes. The service offers viewing and analysis software, an observing archive, and over 100 activities.

## Benefits for students
- Students request images of real objects in the sky.
- Students analyze their own observations.
- Students can use an archive of past observations.

## Benefits for teachers
- Educator resources and over 100 activities are offered.
- Free software is provided for viewing and analyzing images.
- Registration takes under five minutes.

## Benefits for schools
- The Schools' Observatory states its resources are free.
- Registration is free.
- The service runs through the vendor cloud and needs an account.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in this project. The project builds data and measurement skills. Students request images and measure what they show.

## Privacy and safety
An account is required and the service is always online. The teacher registers, and the research did not confirm whether students can hold their own accounts. The site shows no privacy policy, only Terms and Conditions and a cookie notice. It lists SchoolsObs@ljmu.ac.uk as the contact for data questions. The materials do not say what student data is collected. Free access appears aimed at educators in the UK, Ireland, and partner European organizations, and US school eligibility is unconfirmed. A teacher should ask that contact about student data and eligibility before registering.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Request an observation of a bright object and describe the image.
2. Compare two images of the same object taken on different dates.
3. Use archive images to study a planet or a star cluster.
4. Plan an observing night and say what to look for.
5. Write a short report on what was found.

### math
1. Measure the size of an object in an image.
2. Compare brightness values for two stars.
3. Plot measurements on a graph.
4. Estimate error in a measurement.
5. Use ratios to find a scale for an image.

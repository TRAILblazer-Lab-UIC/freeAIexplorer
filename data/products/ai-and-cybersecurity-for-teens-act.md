## Overview
AI and Cybersecurity for Teens, or ACT, is a high school activity sequence that teaches machine learning through cybersecurity topics. Students program in the block-based NetsBlox environment and explore data in CODAP. The sequence runs about 20 class periods of 45 to 50 minutes. Topics include Caesar ciphers, chat apps, denial of service attacks, sentiment analysis, bot detection, and adversarial examples.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a working chat app and add encryption to it.
- Students train and test classifiers, then see how an attacker can fool them.
- Students use real topics such as bot detection and cyberbullying to learn about data.

## Benefits for teachers
- The curriculum is free online and comes with ready activities.
- Teachers can pick single activities from the sequence.
- A printed cipher wheel gives a hands-on start to the cryptography lesson.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum costs nothing, but Activity 3 needs micro:bit boards.
- The school supplies one board per student plus 2 to 4 server boards.
- Each student needs a computer with a browser and a NetsBlox account.

- [ ] Open CODAP and test the data files for the bot detection activity.
- [ ] Plan about 20 class periods across the unit.

## Where is the AI?
The unit teaches classification, training data, sentiment analysis, bot detection, adversarial examples, generative adversarial networks, and gradient descent. Some work runs in the browser and some uses cloud services, including outside sentiment services. Students build and test their own models and then try to fool them.

## Privacy and safety
Nearly all activities need a student NetsBlox account and an internet connection. The research did not review the NetsBlox privacy terms. The materials do not state what student data NetsBlox keeps. The sentiment activities call outside services named ParallelDots and Genius. The materials do not say whether API keys are needed. A teacher should review the NetsBlox terms and the outside services before students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build the simple chat app in NetsBlox and send messages between two student projects.
2. Add Caesar cipher encryption to the chat app and test it with a partner.
3. Train a sentiment classifier and list three messages it gets wrong.
4. Explore bot detection data and describe the traits that mark a fake account.
5. Create an adversarial example that fools a classifier and explain why it worked.

### math
1. Use the printed cipher wheel to encode a message and explain the shift as a number rule.
2. Count letter frequencies in coded text in CODAP and use them to crack a cipher.
3. Graph the accuracy of a classifier and describe what the pattern shows.
4. Compare two data sets in CODAP and decide which better separates bots from people.
5. Calculate how many keys a Caesar cipher has and discuss why that is easy to break.

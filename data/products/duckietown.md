## Overview
Duckietown is a learning ecosystem for robotics and AI that grew out of an MIT course. It offers Duckiebot robots, Duckiedrones, city mats, a simulator, teaching materials, and online courses. The site says it is designed for university-level education. Several high schools have used it, and advanced high school students program the robots to drive through a small city.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how a robot senses a road and decides where to steer.
- Students can test their code in the simulator before they run it on a real Duckiebot.
- Students join a real engineering project that started in university courses.

## Benefits for teachers
- The open documentation, called Duckumentation, is free to read.
- Teaching guides for instructors come with the project.
- A self-driving cars online course gives a teacher a ready path.

## Benefits for schools
- The cost is high. Store pages list a Classroom Kit for 12 at 5999 USD and a Class-in-a-box for 30 at 19029 USD.
- The docs say no readily available high school curricula exist. Staff time to adapt the material is a real cost.
- The robots run on the device. IT will likely need to help with installation.

## Getting started
- [ ] Visit the Duckietown website and read the Duckumentation.
- [ ] Contact Duckietown to discuss pricing and your school's situation.
- [ ] Order the kits and city mats.
- [ ] Assemble the Duckiebots with students.
- [ ] Install the software on a Linux, Windows, or Mac computer.
- [ ] Decide whether students need hub accounts. The accounts are optional.
- [ ] Stay online for setup and then run the first driving lesson.
- [ ] Try the self-driving cars online course before you teach it.

## Where is the AI?
The AI handles autonomous driving, perception, and navigation. The robot camera sees the road, and the software decides how to steer. The code runs on the robot and on a school computer. Students write and change that code to improve how the robot drives.

## Privacy and safety
The robots run on the device, and the internet is needed at setup. Accounts on the Duckietown hub are optional for courses and challenges. The research did not read a privacy policy. The data collected by hub accounts is unknown. The data flow is mixed between the device and the hub. A teacher should check the account terms before students sign up.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Run the simulator and watch how the robot follows a lane. Describe each step the software takes.
2. Change one setting in the driving code and predict how the robot will behave.
3. Compare the robot's path in the simulator and on the real mat. Write down the differences.
4. Work through the self-driving cars online course in small teams.
5. Explain in a short talk how the robot uses its camera to perceive the road.

### engineering
1. Assemble a Duckiebot from its parts and test each component.
2. Build a city mat layout and test how the robot handles turns.
3. Find a loose or weak part on the robot and propose a fix.
4. Plan a route through the city and measure how long the robot takes.
5. Document the build in a short guide for the next class.

## Overview
These are two demos listed on the AI4K12 site. Hand Pose Detector shows real-time palm and finger detection from webcam images. FaceMesh shows a real-time face mesh from a webcam. AI4K12 lists them as suitable for all ages. Students watch the model draw landmarks over their own hand or face.

## Benefits for students
- Students see computer vision work on their own hands and faces.
- Students learn that a computer sees points and not a picture of a person.
- Students can try the demos in a few minutes.

## Benefits for teachers
- The demos need only a browser and a webcam.
- Teachers can run a live demonstration for a whole class.
- No setup or lesson prep is needed.

## Benefits for schools
- The demos are free to use.
- No install or account is mentioned.
- IT needs to allow webcam access and the hosted demo pages.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a computer vision model that finds hand and face landmarks. The page addresses suggest TensorFlow.js models, but the pages do not confirm the technology. The vendor does not say where the model runs. Students move their hand or face and watch the landmarks follow.

## Privacy and safety
No account is mentioned and the internet is always needed. The demos use the webcam, and the pages do not state whether video stays in the browser. The AI4K12 privacy statement covers the website contact form and Google Analytics cookies. It does not mention the demos, webcams, or camera data. It says the AI4K12 site is not intended for children under 13. The hosted demo pages were not opened. A teacher should open each demo first and check what it says about video. A teacher should also tell students to stop the webcam when they finish.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Hold up one hand and count the landmarks that the model draws.
2. Move the hand closer and farther and note how the landmarks change.
3. Cover part of a face and describe how the mesh responds.
4. Try the demo in dim light and describe any failures.
5. Explain in a sentence how a computer can find a hand in a picture.

### science
1. Compare how your eyes and the model find a face.
2. Test which hand positions the model finds easily and which are hard.
3. Draw a diagram of how light gets from a hand to a camera and into the model.
4. Explain how perception differs between people and computers.
5. Record a short observation of what the model sees well and what it misses.

## Overview
This collection has six science experiments built around the micro:bit. They test light through materials, evaporation, sound over distance, a human circuit, heat insulation, and solar panel placement. The page says no coding or expensive equipment is needed. The projects were co-designed with White Rose and rated Intermediate.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn to use the micro:bit data logging function.
- Students run real tests and collect numbers.
- Students compare results from different conditions.

## Benefits for teachers
- The projects work alongside the White Rose primary science scheme of work.
- The MakeCode projects are ready to load. Teachers need no coding.
- Each experiment has a clear set of steps.

## Benefits for schools
- The projects are free, and a micro:bit board is a paid purchase.
- The projects need a micro:bit V2.
- Some projects need a battery pack and a sealed bag.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the White Rose science collection and pick one experiment.
- [ ] Gather micro:bit V2 boards, USB cables, and any battery packs.
- [ ] Load the provided MakeCode project onto a board.
- [ ] Run the experiment.
- [ ] Plug the board into a computer by USB and open MY_DATA.
- [ ] Copy the data to a spreadsheet to compare results.
- [ ] Repeat with the other five experiments.

## Where is the AI?
This collection has no AI. It builds data skills, such as logging readings and comparing them. Students also practice planning a fair test.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The pages read do not discuss data handling. A search snippet says logged data is stored in a MY_DATA file on the board and copied to a computer. The research read no privacy policy for this item. A teacher should check where students save copied data. A teacher should also check the safety steps for the human circuit and the sealed bag before students begin.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Test how much light passes through different materials and rank them.
2. Measure evaporation from two containers and compare the logs.
3. Log how sound volume changes as a speaker moves farther away.
4. Compare materials that keep heat in and draw a graph of the results.
5. Find the best place to put a solar panel and defend the choice with data.

### computer_science
1. Turn on data logging on a board and explain what it records.
2. Open the MY_DATA file and describe what each column holds.
3. Copy the log to a spreadsheet and make a simple chart.
4. Compare two data sets and write one pattern you see.
5. Explain why a sensor reading is a number a computer can store.

## Overview
This is a five-day introductory AI package developed for a middle school computer science project. Students explore Google Quick, Draw! and Code.org AI for Oceans to see how models learn from training data. They study data quality, review bias examples, and use generative AI tools to redesign a book cover. The package has a lesson plan overview, an educator key concepts document, and a slide deck.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how training data shapes what a model learns.
- Students look at examples of algorithmic bias.
- Students redesign a book cover with a generative AI tool.

## Benefits for teachers
- The package comes with plans, key concepts, and slides.
- An educator guide covers access to AI tools with privacy and district policy in mind.
- Files are DOCX and PPTX, which teachers can edit.

## Benefits for schools
- The package is free.
- Students need a browser and internet.
- A teacher should check district policy and pick an approved generative AI tool.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is machine learning in AI for Oceans, a doodle network in Quick, Draw!, and a generative AI tool for the final project. These run in companies' clouds. Students train and test models, notice bias, and write prompts to make their own book cover.

## Privacy and safety
The lessons run in the browser and need the internet at all times. The AI tools run in company clouds, and student input goes to third party tools. The materials do not state which accounts students need. The generative AI tools are not named. Their data handling is unknown. The educator guide covers privacy and district policy. A teacher should choose a generative AI tool the district has approved and review prompts and generated covers.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play Quick, Draw! and describe how the network learns from examples.
2. Train a model in AI for Oceans and note how the data changes the results.
3. Test a model with unfair data and describe the bias it shows.
4. Compare a model trained on varied data with one trained on narrow data.
5. Write a short summary of why data quality matters.

### visual_arts
1. Sketch a book cover by hand before using any tool.
2. Write a prompt for a new book cover and refine it three times.
3. Compare the AI cover with your sketch and decide what to keep.
4. Discuss who owns the art that an AI tool makes.
5. Present your final cover and explain each choice you made.

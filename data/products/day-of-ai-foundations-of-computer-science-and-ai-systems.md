## Overview
This Day of AI unit runs about 25 hours for ages 11 to 18 and is recommended for Grades 7 to 12. It has five modules, from how AI systems think to learning from data, generative AI, designing intelligent systems, and careers. Students finish by identifying a community need. They then design, build, and present an AI supported solution.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how AI systems learn from data.
- Students look at what generative AI can do and where it falls short.
- Students design and present a solution to a real community need.

## Benefits for teachers
- Slide decks, educator guides, and student resources are included.
- Pacing is flexible across the five modules.
- A teacher registers or logs in to see the full guide.

## Benefits for schools
- The unit is free.
- Students need a browser.
- The materials do not say which accounts students need. IT should check.

## Where is the AI?
The course teaches how AI systems work. Modules cover learning from data, generative AI, model training, and designing intelligent systems. Students work in the browser on their own devices. They use what they learn to design an AI supported solution for a community need.

## Privacy and safety

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Describe how a simple AI system takes in data and makes a choice.
2. Train a small model and explain how more data changes its answers.
3. Test a generative AI tool and list where it makes mistakes.
4. Design a flow chart for an intelligent system that solves a school problem.
5. Present a final project and explain how the AI learned from data.

### cte
1. Explore AI careers in Module 5 and write a short profile of one job.
2. Interview a worker about how AI changes their job.
3. List skills a person needs to work with AI.
4. Create a career poster that shows the human judgment each job still needs.
5. Debate which tasks a person should keep doing even when AI can help.

### engineering
1. Identify a community need and write a short problem statement.
2. Sketch a design for an AI supported solution.
3. Build a simple prototype and test it with a classmate.
4. Revise the design after testing and record the changes.
5. Present the solution and explain its limits.

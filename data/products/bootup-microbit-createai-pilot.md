## Overview
This BootUp pilot runs for the 2026-2027 school year for educators of grades 4 to 8. It offers professional learning and classroom-ready lesson plans, slides, and worksheets built on micro:bit CreateAI. The goal is to support 300 educators across all 50 states. No prior AI or coding experience is required.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a model on their own movement data.
- Students see how training data shapes what a model can do.
- Students use a micro:bit and a browser to test their model.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers get professional learning along with the lessons.
- The lesson plans, slides, and worksheets are ready to use.
- No prior AI or coding experience is needed.

## Benefits for schools
- No participation fee is mentioned. One listing says participants receive free micro:bit devices.
- The pilot is closed. Applications closed on June 30, 2026.
- The materials do not say what IT must do.

## Where is the AI?
Students train a machine learning model on their own movement data with micro:bit CreateAI. The model is built in the browser and used with the micro:bit. Students collect the movements, train the model, and test how well it tells them apart.

## Privacy and safety
The announcement does not cover accounts, student data, or internet needs. The research did not verify these points. The CreateAI tool trains a model from movement data and is described as running in the browser. A teacher should check the CreateAI privacy terms before students record movement data. A teacher should also ask whether the pilot sets its own data rules.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Record movement samples for two actions and train a model to tell them apart.
2. Test the model with a classmate and list where it gets confused.
3. Add more training data and compare the new results with the old.
4. Explain to a partner what training data means in your own words.
5. Plan a game that reacts to a movement the model recognizes.

### engineering
1. Fix a micro:bit to a wrist strap and test how the sensor reads a wave.
2. Compare readings from two board positions and choose the better one.
3. Design a small wearable that signals a recognized move with lights.
4. Test the wearable with three different users and record the results.
5. Improve the design after the test and describe the change.

## Overview
This is a four page teaching and learning story for Grades 3 to 5 in Applied Design, Skills, and Technology. A class discussed AI, ethics, bias, and school rules with a slide deck, short videos, and image examples. Students then played an unplugged game. One student acted as the human and the other as an AI that follows rules and learns from mistakes. The story records what one class did and includes teacher reflections.

## Benefits for students
- Students play a game that shows how AI can learn from mistakes.
- Students discuss ethics, bias, and school rules for AI.
- Students work in pairs with simple printed materials.

## Benefits for teachers
- The story shows a real class so a teacher can copy the plan.
- It includes teacher reflections.
- It lists learning cards, game boards, and score sheets.

## Benefits for schools

## Where is the AI?
The game has no software and no AI runs on any device. One student acts as an AI that follows rules and learns from mistakes. This shows the basic idea of machine learning improving over time. Students build skills in rules, feedback, and fair play.

## Privacy and safety
The story describes a slide deck, short videos, and printed game materials. It does not describe accounts or student data. The materials do not state whether an account is needed. The internet is used at setup and for the optional short videos. A teacher should preview the videos and images before class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play the human versus AI game and record how the AI changes after each mistake.
2. Write the rules the AI player follows and test them.
3. Add one new rule and see whether the AI does better.
4. Count how many rounds the AI needs to stop making the same mistake.
5. Explain in a drawing how feedback helps a machine learn.

### social_studies
1. Discuss a school rule about AI use and why it exists.
2. Look at two images and talk about who might be left out.
3. Create a class list of fair ways to use AI at school.
4. Talk about a time when a rule was unfair and how it was fixed.
5. Role play a classroom debate on whether AI should help with homework.

## Overview
The JetBot Mini is a small robot car built around an NVIDIA Jetson Nano 2GB or 4GB computer. Students in high school program it in Python 3 through JupyterLab. The vendor lists face detection, object and color recognition, and gesture detection. A phone app controls it, and the battery lasts about 180 minutes.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a small robot car in Python.
- Students try face, object, color, and gesture recognition.
- Students control the car from a phone or a notebook.

## Benefits for teachers
- A preloaded microSD image speeds up setup.
- Python examples are in a Yahboom code repository.
- The car works with ROS for students who want to go further.

## Benefits for schools
- The kit without a board costs near 118 dollars on the Yahboom store.
- A bundle with a 4GB board is near 590 Canadian dollars at one retailer.
- The internet is needed to download tutorials, and the Jetson Nano 2GB is an older board.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the JetBot Mini on the Yahboom store.
- [ ] Buy a Jetson Nano board if the kit does not include one.
- [ ] Insert the preloaded microSD image.
- [ ] Charge the 18650 battery pack.
- [ ] Ask IT to connect the car to the school network.
- [ ] Open JupyterLab in a browser.
- [ ] Run the face detection example first.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a set of vision programs for face detection, object recognition, color recognition, and gesture detection. They run on the Jetson Nano on the car. Students run the Python examples, change them, and extend the car with their own ideas.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the internet is used for setup and for tutorials. The listing says the vision models run on the Jetson board. The Yahboom store policy covers website customers only and does not address children or data location. The research did not review the privacy practices of the phone app that controls the car. A teacher should check the app permissions and where the camera points. No independent hands-on review was found.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Run the face detection example and describe what it shows.
2. Change the color the car looks for.
3. Test gesture detection with five hand signs.
4. Write a short program that makes the car react to a face.
5. Compare how well the car sees in bright and dim light.

### engineering
1. Assemble the car and check the wheels and battery pack.
2. Test how long the car runs on one charge.
3. Move the camera and compare detection results.
4. Build a simple track for the car.
5. Write a care guide for the 18650 battery pack.

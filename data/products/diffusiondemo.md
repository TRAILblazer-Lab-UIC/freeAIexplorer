## Overview
DiffusionDemo lets students experiment with the Stable Diffusion text-to-image method and learn about latent image spaces. The AI4K12 site lists it for grades 6 to 12 and links a 52 page activity guide. A search result says the demo is hosted at the Indiana Supercomputing Center and must be opened with http. The site refused the connection during research.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how a text prompt becomes an image.
- Students explore latent space, the hidden map that the model uses.
- Students follow a long activity guide with steps.

## Benefits for teachers
- The activity guide has 52 pages and was written by Touretzky and Adithya Kameswara Rao.
- The demo runs in a web browser.
- A teacher should test that the site is working before class.

## Benefits for schools
- The demo is free.
- No device beyond a browser is needed.
- The vendor does not say where student prompts go.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is the Stable Diffusion model that makes images from text. It runs on a remote server. Students type prompts, look at the images, and explore how nearby points in latent space change the result. They use the results to explain how an image model works.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether accounts are needed or where student prompts go. A search snippet says the demo runs on university hosting in Indiana over http only. The site refused the connection during research, and no privacy policy was found. A text-to-image model will try to draw whatever students type. A teacher should check the host, review prompts, and avoid personal details in them.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow the first activity in the guide and write down what changes.
2. Change one word in a prompt and describe the new image.
3. Explore two points in latent space and describe what lies between them.
4. Explain in your own words how a model might start from noise and make a picture.
5. List two limits of the model that you found while testing.

### visual_arts
1. Write a prompt that describes a painting style and judge the result.
2. Compare an AI image with a drawing you made of the same idea.
3. Create a set of images that tell a short story.
4. Edit a prompt five times and note which words had the most effect.
5. Discuss how artists should be credited when AI helps.

## Overview
Search results describe the Cortic AI Toolkit as open-source software for learning AI on a Raspberry Pi 4B. It is aimed at beginners and uses a drag-and-drop interface from Cortic Technology. Retailer pages say it runs with an OAK-D Lite camera. Luxonis once listed it as a community project, but its current education page no longer mentions it.

## Benefits for students
- Students can try computer vision and gesture recognition with a drag-and-drop screen.
- Students can see AI run on a small computer.
- The camera makes the projects visual.

## Benefits for teachers
- The toolkit is described as beginner friendly.
- Teachers should check that the toolkit still exists before planning a lesson.
- The Raspberry Pi gives a hands-on build.

## Benefits for schools
- The software price was not found.
- The setup needs a Raspberry Pi 4B and likely an OAK camera. Prices were not checked.
- A teacher will likely need to install it, and the GitHub repository returned 404 during research.

## Where is the AI?
The toolkit is described as offering computer vision and gesture recognition. It runs on the Raspberry Pi. Students drag blocks into place to build a small vision project and then test it with their own gestures.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not say whether accounts are needed, whether the internet is needed, or where data goes. The toolkit is described as running on a Raspberry Pi, but the research did not verify the data flow. No privacy policy was found. The toolkit uses a camera. A teacher should keep it away from faces unless the school approves. A teacher should also confirm that the toolkit is still available before students use it.

## Overview
OpenWhispr is a dictation app for macOS, Windows, Linux, and iOS. It types spoken words into any app. It can transcribe with models on the device or through optional cloud transcription. Students talk and the words appear as text.

## Benefits for students
- Students who find typing hard can speak their ideas instead.
- Students can dictate drafts into any app they already use.
- Students can try meeting notes and text cleanup.

## Benefits for teachers
- The app works in any program. There is little to teach.
- Local models work offline.
- Teachers can use it to produce notes from meetings.

## Benefits for schools
- The free plan includes unlimited local dictation and 2,000 cloud words each week.
- Pro costs $6.67 per user per month or $80 per year. Business pricing is listed by the vendor.
- Local models need disk space, for example 672 MB for the recommended model. Chromebooks are not listed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the OpenWhispr site and its open source repository.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install. The app listens through the microphone.
- [ ] Install the desktop app.
- [ ] Download a local model for offline use.
- [ ] Choose local mode and leave cloud features off.
- [ ] Test dictation in a word processor.
- [ ] Note that audio stays on the device for 30 days by default.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a speech-to-text model that turns speech into text, plus an optional cleanup step for the text. It runs on the device with local models. Cloud transcription is an option and runs in a third party company's cloud. Students speak a draft and then edit the text into their own writing.

## Privacy and safety
An account is optional and is needed for cloud features. The research could not tell whether local only use needs one. Local mode keeps audio on the device, and dictation audio is kept there for 30 days by default. Cloud mode sends audio to third parties such as OpenAI, Groq, and Deepgram. Cloud logs record audio length and word counts. The policy does not address children or COPPA. A teacher should set local mode only for students.

1. Dictate a short journal entry and edit the text with a helper.
2. Dictate answers to three reading questions and read them back.
3. Practice speaking in short sentences to get clean text.
4. Compare the speed of dictating and typing the same paragraph.
5. Dictate a to-do list for the day and check the words.

### ela
1. Dictate a first draft of a personal narrative, then revise it by hand.
2. Dictate a paragraph and mark where punctuation needs fixing.
3. Speak a story aloud, then turn the text into a polished version.
4. Dictate notes from a class discussion and sort them into main points.
5. Compare a spoken draft with a typed draft and describe the differences in voice.

## Overview
Sigmoid is a design project for a paper toolkit for teaching AI in K-12. The author's prototype is an input and output machine. A paper ball is the data and pipes form the network with adjustable weights. The toolkit draws on ideas such as order and chaos, pattern recognition, ethics and bias.

## Benefits for students
- Students can see data move through a network as a physical ball.
- Students learn that weights change what comes out.
- Students meet ideas of pattern recognition and bias.

## Benefits for teachers
- The idea is made from paper and is easy to picture.
- The project write-up gives a clear model of a network.
- The article names ethics and bias as part of the design.

## Benefits for schools

## Where is the AI?
The kit models how a neural network passes data through weighted connections. No computer AI runs in it. Students push a paper ball through pipes and adjust the weights. They then see how the output changes.

## Privacy and safety
The kit is made of paper, and the article mentions no electronic devices or software. The article lists no accounts. The materials do not say how a teacher gets the kit or whether the internet is needed. A teacher should check the source and any reuse rights before building copies.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Drop a paper ball through the machine and name the input and output.
2. Change one weight and describe how the output changes.
3. Draw the pipe network as a diagram with labels.
4. Look for a pattern in what the machine does with different inputs.
5. Discuss how a biased weight could give an unfair result.

## Overview
This MediaSmarts lesson by Matthew Johnson teaches how recommendation algorithms work. Students red team the downsides of goals such as watch time and engagement. They sort explicit and implicit inputs and find privacy settings. They then design an algorithm for an app they choose, with fewer harms.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn what a recommendation system watches and why.
- Students practice weighing the harms of optimization goals.
- Students design a less harmful algorithm of their own.

## Benefits for teachers
- A downloadable kit has handouts, overheads, and backgrounders.
- The lesson works with paper handouts.
- It links to the MediaSmarts Use, Understand and Engage framework.

## Benefits for schools
- The download is free.
- Most of the lesson needs no software.
- The privacy settings activity may need student devices.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson teaches about recommendation algorithms. It runs on paper and no AI tool runs in the lesson. Students learn how training inputs and goals shape what an app shows. They then build their own design for a better algorithm.

## Ideas by subject
### library_media
1. Examine a video app and list what it shows and what it may leave out.
2. Sort explicit and implicit inputs that a recommender might use.
3. Refresh an app's recommendations and describe what changed.
4. Find the privacy settings in an app and explain each choice.
5. Write a short guide to help younger students understand their feeds.

### social_studies
1. Red team a goal such as watch time and list who could be harmed.
2. Debate whether apps should aim for engagement.
3. Discuss how recommendations may shape what people believe.
4. Compare privacy settings across two apps and write a recommendation.
5. Draft a class statement on fair design for recommendation systems.

### computer_science
1. Sketch the inputs and outputs of a recommendation algorithm.
2. Design an algorithm for an app that reduces one harm.
3. Explain how changing a goal changes the results.
4. Trace how one click may change the next recommendation.
5. Present your algorithm design to the class and invite questions.

## Overview
Kdenlive is a free, open source, multi-track video editor. Students cut and arrange footage from phones, cameras, and screen recordings. They add titles, transitions, effects, and audio. It also offers automatic transcription for subtitles.

## Benefits for students
- Students build films with several video and audio tracks.
- Students add titles, effects, and motion graphics.
- Students can make subtitles with automatic speech to text.

## Benefits for teachers
- The editor has proxy clips that help older computers.
- It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
- Teachers can use it for class videos and screen recordings.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and donations are optional.
- It works offline after install, apart from speech to text models that may download on first use.
- The main GitHub license is GPL-3.0, and IT may need to approve the install.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Kdenlive website and download the installer.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install if required.
- [ ] Install it on a computer with enough memory for video.
- [ ] Open a project and import a short clip.
- [ ] Try proxy clips for slow computers.
- [ ] Test the subtitle transcription on a short clip.
- [ ] Save the project and export a video.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is optional. Kdenlive offers automatic speech to text for subtitles. The vendor does not say where the transcription runs. Students edit the generated text to fix mistakes and finish accurate subtitles.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used for setup. KDE says its software generally sends nothing unless the user acts, and telemetry is opt in. Speech to text models may be downloaded on first use, and whether transcription runs fully on the device was not confirmed. A teacher should check that students have permission to film people. A teacher should also test the transcription feature to learn where it runs.

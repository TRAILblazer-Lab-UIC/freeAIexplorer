## Overview
Create@School is a version of Pocket Code adapted for schools. It was released in September 2016 for the No One Left Behind project. It was revised to work more like Scratch, with teacher guidelines, game templates, and training resources. Pocket Paint is the Catrobat image editor app.

## Benefits for students
- Students build games and quizzes with blocks.
- Students can draw their own images in Pocket Paint.
- Teachers in a pilot found ideas such as collision detection easier to explain.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teacher guidelines and templates give a starting point.
- A quiz template was adapted for physics and arts lessons in an Austrian pilot.
- Only six of eleven pilot teachers continued the project.

## Benefits for schools
- The apps are free and the code is open source.
- The Catroid repository is AGPL v3.
- Whether Create@School is still a separate app is not confirmed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Catrobat apps on Google Play.
- [ ] Check whether Create@School is still available as its own app.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the apps on Android devices.
- [ ] Read the teacher guidelines and templates.
- [ ] Try the quiz template yourself.
- [ ] Read the Catrobat privacy policy with IT.
- [ ] Open Pocket Paint and draw a simple image.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in these apps. Students build programs from blocks and draw images. They build sequencing and logic skills.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are needed for the share.catrob.at community, and the research did not confirm whether the apps work without one. The Catrobat privacy policy says a username and optional email are stored. It says usage data such as crash data and visited pages goes to Google and Dynatrace in the USA with IP addresses anonymized. Users under 14 need guardian consent under that policy. The policy never names Create@School. Which apps it covers is unconfirmed. A teacher should check the policy with IT and confirm consent rules before use.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Explain collision detection by building a game where two sprites touch.
2. Use a game template and change the rules.
3. Add a score block to a game.
4. Debug a program and describe the fix.
5. Present your game to a partner.

### science
1. Adapt the quiz template to ask questions about a physics topic.
2. Build a short quiz about the states of matter.
3. Add images from Pocket Paint to the quiz.
4. Test a classmate's quiz and review the answers.
5. Write a rule that gives feedback on a wrong answer.

### visual_arts
1. Draw a character in Pocket Paint and use it in a game.
2. Build a quiz about famous artists with your own drawings.
3. Design a background for a game scene.
4. Change an image and compare the result in a program.
5. Present a finished piece and explain your art choices.

## Overview
IoT4Ag K-12 lessons are classroom lessons on the Internet of Things in agriculture. The four topics are Ag Technology, Limiting Resources, Plants and Planetary Health, and Scientific Inquiry. The lessons use the 5E format. Students build sensors, study water use, and code Cutebot robots with blocks.

## Benefits for students
- Students build their own water sensor probes.
- Students see how data from a sensor can guide farming choices.
- Students try block coding with Cutebot robots.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lessons follow the 5E format and align to STEELS, NGSS, and Common Core.
- The lessons are ready to use and are shared as documents.
- Search results say the lessons can be adapted for grades 4-12.

## Benefits for schools
- The vendor does not state a cost.
- The hardware parts lists were not on the pages read. Budget time to check them.
- Setup is likely to take days and needs a teacher to install and prepare parts.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Use the water use lesson to measure how much water a plant needs.
2. Build a DIY water sensor probe and test it in dry and wet soil.
3. Plan a fair test in the Scientific Inquiry topic and record the results.
4. Compare plant growth under two watering plans and graph the results.
5. Explain how limited water changes what farmers can grow.

### cte
1. Read the intro document and list ways sensors help a farm.
2. Interview a local grower about the tools they use and compare notes with the lessons.
3. Set up a sensor and write steps that another student could follow.
4. Present a plan for a small farm that uses sensor data.
5. Describe jobs in precision agriculture that use these tools.

### engineering
1. Build a water sensor probe and improve it after the first test.
2. Study the solar powered IoT gateway and sketch how the parts connect.
3. Test how well a probe works in different soils and record the data.
4. Design a stand or case to protect a sensor outdoors.
5. Present a design choice and explain the trade offs.

### computer_science
1. Use block coding to move a Cutebot robot along a path.
2. Add a loop so the robot repeats a pattern.
3. Read sensor values on a dashboard and describe how they change.
4. Write steps that tell a robot how to react to a reading.
5. Debug a block program with a partner and explain the fix.

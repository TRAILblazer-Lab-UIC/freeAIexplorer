## Overview
Stop Motion Studio is an animation app for making stop-motion movies with LEGO figures, clay, paper, and everyday objects. Students capture frames with a device camera, edit them on a timeline, add sound, and export a video, GIF, or flipbook. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android, Windows, Chromebook, and Amazon Fire. The vendor lists lessons for ages 5 to 14 and up.

## Benefits for students
- Students bring figures and clay to life with a device they already know.
- Students edit frames and add sound to polish their movie.
- Students can export as video, GIF, or flipbook.

## Benefits for teachers
- The vendor schools program offers ready-made lessons in science, math, social studies, and language arts.
- The app works offline after activation.
- Teachers can deploy it with VPP or MDM tools.

## Benefits for schools
- A free version exists with an in-app upgrade. A third-party listing puts Pro at about 4.99 dollars.
- No student accounts are needed.
- IT can deploy it through VPP, Google Admin, or Windows tools.

- [ ] Browse the vendor's education lessons for your grade.

## Where is the AI?
Stop Motion Studio has no AI. Students take pictures one frame at a time and build movement by hand. This builds sequencing. Each small change must follow the last.

## Privacy and safety
No student accounts are needed, and the app works offline after activation. Projects, photos and recordings stay on the device unless the user shares them. Crash reports may send error type, app version, device model and OS version, and license activation is checked on Windows and Mac. The policy says the vendor does not collect personal information from children or students and has signed the California student data privacy agreement. Movies can show faces and voices. A teacher should decide where student movies are saved and shared.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Animate a clay figure that changes shape across ten frames.
2. Make a paper cutout scene and film it moving across the background.
3. Add a soundtrack and sound effects to the finished movie.
4. Export a GIF and compare it to a video of the same scene.
5. Use LEGO figures to tell a short story of your own.

### science
1. Animate the states of matter for a lesson with students ages 7 to 9.
2. Show how erosion changes a landscape in a short film, as in the erosion lesson for ages 12 to 13.
3. Film the steps of a life cycle with clay figures.
4. Add voice narration that explains each stage.
5. Share the movie and ask classmates what they learned.

### math
1. Animate shapes that turn from squares into rectangles, as in the vendor lesson for ages 5 to 6.
2. Film a counting story where objects appear one at a time.
3. Show a pattern growing across ten frames.
4. Add a voice that names each shape.
5. Compare two movies and describe the shapes you see.

### social_studies
1. Reenact a historical battle with small figures, as in the vendor lesson for ages 14 and up.
2. Plan the scenes on a storyboard before filming.
3. Add captions that name the people and places.
4. Film a day in the life of a community with paper characters.
5. Present the film and explain which sources shaped each scene.

### ela
1. Write a short script and film it, as in the Sneak Peek lesson for ages 14 and up.
2. Retell a favorite story in five scenes.
3. Add voice acting and sound to show character feelings.
4. Edit the timeline to improve the pacing of the story.
5. Trade films with a partner and write feedback on the plot.

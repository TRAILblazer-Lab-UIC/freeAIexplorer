## Overview
Wings 3D is a free, open source polygon subdivision modeler. It has modeling tools, lights, materials, and built in AutoUV mapping. It has no animation support. No classroom use was documented, but students could use it for simple 3D modeling.

## Benefits for students
- Students shape 3D forms by pushing and pulling polygons.
- Students can add lights and materials.
- Students learn how models are built from faces and edges.

## Benefits for teachers
- The interface is customizable.
- The tool is free for personal and commercial use.
- The tool suits art and design classes.

## Benefits for schools
- It is free and open source.
- It runs on Windows 10 or later, macOS Sequoia 15 on Apple silicon, and Ubuntu 24.04.
- It is not usable on Chromebooks. IT may need to install it.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the installer from the Wings 3D downloads page.
- [ ] Ask IT to install it on school computers.
- [ ] Check that each computer supports OpenGL 2.1.
- [ ] Open the program and try the basic shapes.
- [ ] Practice extruding and smoothing a model.
- [ ] Try lights and materials.
- [ ] Save models in a shared folder.

## Where is the AI?
This tool has no AI. Students shape every model by hand. They build spatial reasoning and design skills as they plan forms in three dimensions.

## Privacy and safety
Wings 3D runs on the computer and needs no account. The research found no telemetry statement and did not review a privacy policy. The internet is needed only to download it. A teacher should ask IT to confirm the network behavior. A teacher should also check that school computers meet the system needs before planning a lesson.

## Overview
This lesson idea is on the Australian Digital Technologies Hub for Years 5 to 8. It comes from the Queen Mary University of London cs4fn project. It explores what artificial intelligence is, learning from mistakes, and how the brain works. The hub page describes the activities only in general terms.

## Benefits for students
- Students ask what makes a machine intelligent.
- Students think about learning from mistakes.
- Students compare machines with how the brain works.

## Benefits for teachers
- The hub links the lesson to Australian Curriculum Digital Technologies codes.
- A cs4fn booklet offers activities such as An Intelligent Piece of Paper.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no software and no AI runs on any device. The activities ask what AI is, how rule following machines work, and how people and machines learn from mistakes. Students build skills in following rules and in judging when rules are enough.

## Privacy and safety
The hub page lists no materials or devices. The Unplugged tag suggests no devices but the page does not confirm this. The materials do not state whether an account is needed or whether student data is collected. The internet is used at setup. A teacher should read the booklet activities before class because the hub describes them only in general terms.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow a set of rules on paper and decide whether you showed intelligence.
2. Write rules for a simple game that a classmate follows without thinking.
3. Find a rule that fails and fix it.
4. Discuss whether a machine that only follows rules is intelligent.
5. Compare a rule following machine with one that learns from mistakes.

### science
1. Draw how a brain sends a message from the eye to the hand.
2. Describe a time you learned from a mistake and what changed.
3. Compare how a person and a simple machine fix an error.
4. Build a model of neurons passing a message with a class line.
5. Discuss which parts of the brain a machine copies and which it does not.

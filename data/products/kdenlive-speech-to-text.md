## Overview
Kdenlive is a free, open source video editor that includes speech recognition for subtitles and transcripts. Users choose between the VOSK and Whisper engines. The Automatic Subtitling dialog places subtitles on the timeline. The Speech Editor transcribes clips and lets users build clips from selected text.

## Benefits for students
- Students add subtitles to their own videos.
- Students edit video by selecting words in a transcript.
- Students can translate speech with Whisper.

## Benefits for teachers
- Subtitles make class videos more accessible.
- Whisper can autodetect language and translate to English.
- An optional SeamlessM4T model translates between languages.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
- No account is needed, and the speech models appear to run on the device.
- The SeamlessM4T model is about 9 GB. Chromebooks are not supported.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download Kdenlive from kdenlive.org.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install.
- [ ] Open the Speech to Text configuration.
- [ ] Choose VOSK or Whisper and install the models while online.
- [ ] Avoid version 21.04.2. The manual says speech to text is broken there.
- [ ] Run Automatic Subtitling on a timeline zone.
- [ ] Check the subtitles for errors.
- [ ] Go offline and test a short clip.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is speech recognition and speech translation. It appears to run on the local computer with the VOSK or Whisper models. Students record or load a clip, run the tool, and then correct the text by hand. They use the transcript to edit and caption their own video.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used to download the models on first use. The manual does not state outright that recognition runs locally. It warns that translation can use all of the RAM, CPU, and disk, which suggests local processing. No privacy policy was found at the address tried. A teacher should remind students to get permission before recording voices. A teacher should also review each subtitle before sharing a video.

## Ideas by subject
### theater_media
1. Add automatic subtitles to a short student film and fix the mistakes.
2. Use the Speech Editor to cut a clip by selecting words in the transcript.
3. Silence detect with VOSK and trim the dead air from an interview.
4. Compare VOSK and Whisper on the same clip.
5. Export a captioned video and show it to the class.

### world_languages
1. Record a short speech in the target language and read the transcript.
2. Use Whisper to translate the clip to English and compare with your own translation.
3. Find the words that the tool misheard and list them.
4. Try the SeamlessM4T model to translate between two languages.
5. Make a captioned video in two languages.

### special_ed
1. Caption a class video so every student can follow it.
2. Compare captions with and without edits and discuss clarity.
3. Read a transcript while watching and note where the tool made mistakes.
4. Make a short lesson video with clear captions for a younger class.
5. Ask classmates what caption style helps them most.

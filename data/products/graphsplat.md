## Overview
GraphSplat is a browser-based graphing tool from DrawSplat. It offers bar, line, pie, scatter, bubble, and radar charts, picture graphs, coordinate planes, a function grapher, a geometry tool, and Equation Studio. The page links to TEKS-aligned science and math lesson plans. It suits grades 3 to 8.

## Benefits for students
- Students make many chart types in one tool.
- Students graph functions and plot points on a coordinate plane.
- Students can export charts as PNG, SVG, JPEG, PDF, or CSV.

## Benefits for teachers
- No account is needed.
- Lesson plans include a Schoolyard Biodiversity Snapshot for grades 3 to 5.
- A Campus Microclimate plan for grades 6 to 8 compares shade and sun.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is listed as free. The official page links to pricing but did not state it.
- It runs in a browser and needs no install.
- It is part of the SplatWorks suite with GridSplat and SplatLMS.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open GraphSplat from DrawSplat in a web browser.
- [ ] Try a bar chart with sample data.
- [ ] Try the coordinate plane and function grapher.
- [ ] Open the TEKS-aligned lesson plans from the page.
- [ ] Export a chart as PNG and a data table as CSV.
- [ ] Save a project as a JSON file.
- [ ] Check whether the tool works offline on your network. The vendor does not say.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe the AI features in the materials reviewed, although the privacy page mentions optional AI features. Students build data skills. They choose a chart type, enter data, and explain what the graph shows. The tool runs in the browser.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The GraphSplat page says data, expressions, and uploaded pictures stay in the browser and nothing is sent to DrawSplat servers. The privacy controls page says drafts are kept in browser storage. It says AI features need a key held only in the current tab with approval for each provider. It says connected processing for children under 13 needs school or parent authorization. Whether the tool needs the internet after loading was not stated. A teacher should test the tool on school devices and keep AI features off.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Graph class survey results as a bar chart and a pie chart and compare them.
2. Plot points on a coordinate plane to draw a shape.
3. Graph a function and change one number to see what moves.
4. Make a picture graph of favorite foods.
5. Use Equation Studio to write an equation and export it.

### science
1. Record schoolyard biodiversity counts and graph them as in the Biodiversity Snapshot plan.
2. Measure temperature in sun and shade and plot the results as in the Campus Microclimate plan.
3. Make a line graph of plant growth over two weeks.
4. Compare two data sets on a scatter plot.
5. Write a claim that a chart supports.

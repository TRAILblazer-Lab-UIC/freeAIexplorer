## Overview
This is a free self-paced course for K-12 teachers on how AI works and how to use it in teaching. It has five modules of about one hour each, around five hours in total. Topics include bias, reliability, misinformation, and responsible use. A companion video series from 2023 is also available.

## Benefits for students
- Students gain from a teacher who understands AI.
- Students hear a clear message about bias and reliability.
- Students get guidance on responsible AI use.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each module can be taken on its own.
- The course fits into a few short sessions.
- It is a teacher course and not a student lesson set.

## Benefits for schools
- The course is free.
- The notes say no district approval is needed.
- Nothing needs to be installed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the course from code.org/ai101.
- [ ] Sign in if the site asks.
- [ ] Take the first module.
- [ ] Plan about one hour for each module.
- [ ] Take notes on bias and reliability.
- [ ] Watch the companion videos if you want more.
- [ ] Share one idea with your team.

## Where is the AI?
The course teaches about AI and does not use an AI tool. It covers how AI works, bias, misinformation, and responsible use. The course is hosted in the vendor's cloud. Teachers use what they learn to guide their own planning and to help students judge AI output.

## Privacy and safety
The course launches from a Code.org Studio link, and the research did not confirm whether an account is needed. The course needs the internet. It is for teachers and asks for no student data. The Code.org privacy policy says it does not sell personal data or show ads. It also says free text entries may be processed and stored. A teacher should check the Code.org privacy terms before signing in with a school account.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Explain in a short talk how an AI learns from examples.
2. Find an example of bias in a data set and describe the harm.
3. Check an AI answer for mistakes and write a note on its reliability.
4. Discuss one rule for responsible AI use at school.
5. Make a short quiz on one module for classmates.

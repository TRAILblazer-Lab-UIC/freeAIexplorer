## Overview
The JetRacer AI Kit is a small racing car built around an NVIDIA Jetson Nano. It has an 8MP wide angle camera and supports the DonkeyCar self driving project. The product page names high school AI education as a use. Students assemble the car, collect driving data, and train it to follow a road.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a car and then teach it to drive.
- Students collect their own driving examples and train a model.
- Students tune the car and watch the results on a track.

## Benefits for teachers
- Notebooks run in Jupyter Lab through a web browser.
- DonkeyCar and JetRacer programs give a clear path through the work.
- ROS is offered in separate JetRacer ROS kits and is not in this kit.

## Benefits for schools
- Prices range from about 213.99 to 761.99 dollars based on the controller and card options.
- The Jetson Nano is optional and the 18650 batteries are not included.
- The internet is needed only for setup, and IT will likely need to help.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the JetRacer AI Kit on the Waveshare site and read the vendor wiki.
- [ ] Choose a kit option and buy 18650 batteries separately.
- [ ] Assemble the car.
- [ ] Ask IT to flash the JetPack 4.5 based image with Etcher.
- [ ] Connect the car to Wi-Fi.
- [ ] Set the Jetson Nano to 5W power mode for the battery pack.
- [ ] Run the Jupyter Lab notebooks in a browser.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a self driving model that follows a road using the camera. It is trained with the DonkeyCar notebooks and runs on the Jetson Nano on the car. Students collect driving data, train the model, and test how well the car drives.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the internet is used only for setup. The product page describes local processing on the Jetson Nano and no cloud service. The research found no policy or classroom use reports. A teacher should check that the camera records only the track. A teacher should also set battery handling rules for the lithium cells.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Drive the car by hand and record a set of laps as training data.
2. Train a self driving model and compare it with the manual laps.
3. Collect data with more examples and see how driving improves.
4. Read a notebook cell and explain it in plain words.
5. Describe in a short report how the car follows the road.

### engineering
1. Assemble the car and check each wheel and the steering.
2. Build a track with tape and test the lap time.
3. Move the camera and compare how the car sees the road.
4. Test the car on battery power and note how long it runs.
5. Document the assembly steps for a new team.

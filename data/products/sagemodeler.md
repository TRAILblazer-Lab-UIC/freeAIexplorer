## Overview
SageModeler is a free web-based system dynamics modeling tool. The Concord Consortium and the CREATE for STEM Institute at Michigan State University developed it. Students build and run models by dragging variables and defining how one affects another. It works with the CODAP graphing and data analysis platform. It targets middle and high school science.

## Benefits for students
- Students build models with drag and drop and no equations.
- Students run a model and watch how variables change.
- Students graph results with CODAP.

## Benefits for teachers
- Documented units include a high school evaporative cooling model and a middle school carbon cycle model.
- Students can debate and revise relationships in their models.
- The source code is MIT licensed.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free.
- It runs in a browser and needs no install.
- Saving options were not described on the pages found.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open SageModeler at sage.concord.org in a browser.
- [ ] Ask IT to check the site and review the privacy terms.
- [ ] Check whether students need an account. The materials do not say.
- [ ] Build a small model with two variables.
- [ ] Run the model and open the graph in CODAP.
- [ ] Find out how students can save their models before class.
- [ ] Plan a unit such as a carbon cycle model for a middle school group.

## Where is the AI?
SageModeler has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is working with data and models. Students define how variables affect each other and test the model against evidence. That practice shows how any model, including an AI model, rests on assumptions.

## Privacy and safety
SageModeler runs in the browser and needs the internet at all times. The research did not confirm whether an account is needed or where student models are saved. The Concord Consortium policy says student data is confidential, is not sold and is not used for targeted advertising. It names FERPA and COPPA and says Concord signs data privacy agreements with schools. The policy also lists identifiers, IP address, cookies and Google Analytics usage data. The policy covers the whole organization and the product pages add no statement. A teacher should ask IT to review the policy and the saving rules.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Build a model of the carbon cycle and debate the relationships, as a middle school unit did.
2. Model evaporative cooling and revise the model after new evidence.
3. Change one variable and predict what happens to the others.
4. Compare your model with a classmate's and list the differences.
5. Write a short explanation that uses the model as evidence.

### math
1. Define how one variable affects another and describe the relationship in words.
2. Run a model and open the graph in CODAP.
3. Compare two graphs from two runs and describe the change.
4. Describe a relationship as increasing or decreasing and test it.
5. Predict the shape of a graph before you run the model.

## Overview
Dr. Geo is free interactive geometry software for school-age children. It is part of the GNU project. Students build geometric sketches by hand and move the parts to see what changes. The sketches can also be scripted and animated in Smalltalk.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and move geometric sketches with their own hands.
- Students can script and animate a sketch in Smalltalk.
- Students see geometry and a little coding in one place.

## Benefits for teachers
- The software is free and falls under the GNU GPL.
- One author who teaches middle school suggests it for introducing coordinate geometry.
- The same author suggests drawing a house on a grid to join art and math.

## Benefits for schools
- The software costs nothing.
- It needs no installer. Users unzip the download and run it from the folder.
- It runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux. IT should plan on helping with the first setup.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Dr. Geo download on the GNU project pages and choose the ZIP file for your system.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve running the program from a folder on one test computer.
- [ ] Extract the ZIP file into a folder.
- [ ] Run the program from that folder.
- [ ] Draw a simple sketch and move its points to see how it reacts.
- [ ] Try the grid house idea from the middle school article.
- [ ] Copy the folder to other computers once the test works.

## Where is the AI?
Dr. Geo has no AI. It builds pattern spotting and logic skills. Students watch how a sketch changes as they move its parts. They can also write Smalltalk to make a sketch move.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the program runs from an unzipped folder on the computer. The research could not read the official GNU page, and no privacy policy was read. No source describes accounts or data collection. The internet is used for the download. A teacher should check that the download came from the official project page. Chromebook support is not documented.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Build a sketch by hand and move its points to see which measures stay the same.
2. Introduce coordinate geometry by placing points on a grid and reading their positions.
3. Draw a triangle and drag one corner to describe what happens to the angles.
4. Construct a circle and a line and explore where they meet.
5. Write a short Smalltalk script that animates a point along a path.

### visual_arts
1. Draw a house on a grid using lines and polygons.
2. Create a repeating pattern from one shape and describe the geometry behind it.
3. Make a symmetrical design and check it by moving the parts.
4. Combine circles and lines to sketch a simple logo.
5. Animate a small scene and explain which shapes move.

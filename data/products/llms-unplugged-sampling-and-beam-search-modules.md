## Overview
These are two extension modules in LLMs Unplugged. Sampling is for ages 7 and up and takes 20 to 30 minutes, or 60 with the poetry slam. It uses one model to make different outputs by changing temperature, truncation, and your own rules. Beam Search is for ages 9 and up and takes 30 to 40 minutes. It keeps several candidate sentences and scores each by the likelihood of the whole sentence.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how settings such as temperature change a model's text.
- Students learn that the most likely sentence is not always the best one.
- Students write and share poems in the poetry slam.

## Benefits for teachers
- The two modules can run on separate days.
- The modules use a grid, a ledger, and booklets.
- The poetry slam gives a creative finish.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free.
- The modules need no computers and no accounts.
- No student data is collected.

## Where is the AI?
The modules have no running AI program. They show how a language model chooses the next word and how settings change the choice. Students experiment with temperature and truncation by hand. They then compare candidate sentences in Beam Search and score each one.

## Privacy and safety
The modules run on paper. The pages read mention no devices or accounts. The research read no privacy policy for this item. The license text differs between sources. The research did not confirm it. A teacher should confirm the license on the GitHub repository before sharing the sheets. A teacher should also review any poems before the poetry slam.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Change the temperature setting on the grid and compare the sentences you get.
2. Truncate the list of word choices and see how the output changes.
3. Write your own rule for picking words and test it.
4. Keep three candidate sentences alive and score each one.
5. Explain why the likeliest sentence is not always the best.

### ela
1. Generate lines of text with the sampling rules and pick the most poetic one.
2. Edit a generated line into a stronger line.
3. Compose a short poem from several sampled lines.
4. Perform a poem in the poetry slam and tell the class how you made it.
5. Compare a sampled poem with one you wrote yourself.

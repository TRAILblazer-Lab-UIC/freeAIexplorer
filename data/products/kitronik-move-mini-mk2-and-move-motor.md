## Overview
The :MOVE mini MK2 is a small two-wheeled buggy kit driven by a BBC micro:bit, which is sold separately. It includes a servo:lite board, a three AAA battery holder, five RGB LEDs, two continuous rotation servos and a laser-cut plastic chassis. Students build it and program it in the MakeCode editor. The :MOVE Motor was not researched beyond its name.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a buggy from a laser-cut chassis.
- Students program servos and lights in MakeCode.
- Students can mount a pen in the spare holes on the chassis.

## Benefits for teachers
- The kit includes batteries and a battery holder.
- Line following needs a separate sensor add-on.
- A reviewer says younger builders may need help with assembly.

## Benefits for schools
- The Raspberry Pi Magazine review lists the mini MK2 at 27.54 GBP without the micro:bit.
- The research found no student data collection described.
- The buggy performs well on smooth floors and poorly on rough terrain.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the :MOVE mini MK2 on the Kitronik site.
- [ ] Buy a micro:bit for each buggy.
- [ ] Gather a small screwdriver.
- [ ] Assemble the chassis and board.
- [ ] Clamp in the micro:bit.
- [ ] Calibrate the servos with the trimmer.
- [ ] Open MakeCode and look for the servo blocks under pins.

## Where is the AI?
The :MOVE mini has no AI. It follows the code that students write. Students build skills in sequencing and logic by planning a route for the buggy.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The buggy runs its program on the micro:bit with no network. The MakeCode editor is a Microsoft web service whose privacy terms were not reviewed. No Kitronik privacy policy was reachable. A teacher should help younger builders with assembly. A teacher should also test the buggy on a smooth floor before class.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Assemble the buggy and name each part.
2. Calibrate the servos until the buggy drives straight.
3. Mount a pen on the chassis and test a drawing.
4. Compare driving on a smooth floor and on carpet.
5. Redesign one part of the buggy and test the change.

### computer_science
1. Program the buggy to drive forward and stop.
2. Use the RGB LEDs to signal a turn.
3. Program a square path and measure how close it comes.
4. Find the servo blocks and explain what they do.
5. Trade programs with a partner and test each other's code.

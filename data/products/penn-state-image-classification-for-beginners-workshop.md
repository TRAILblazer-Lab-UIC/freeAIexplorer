## Overview
This is a hands-on workshop that uses Python and Google Colab to train and test a simple image classification model. It uses user-submitted iNaturalist photos. It covers preparing training images and measuring how often the model names the right species. It took place on April 22, 2026 and was part of the INSECT NET beginner series.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a real image classifier.
- Students measure the accuracy of their model.
- Students practice preparing training images.

## Benefits for teachers
- The workshop shows a complete small machine learning project.
- It uses real nature photos from iNaturalist.
- Teachers need Python skills to adapt it.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is an image classification model that students train on labeled photos. It runs in Google's cloud through Colab. Students write Python code, test the model on new photos, and use the accuracy number to improve their own work.

## Privacy and safety
Colab runs in Google's cloud and needs a Google account. Code and photos go to Google's servers. The materials do not state what student data is collected. The official page could not be read, and no privacy policy was found. A teacher should check district rules for Google accounts and keep photos free of students' faces.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Collect iNaturalist photos of two insect species and sort them into folders.
2. Test the trained model on a new photo and explain whether it was right.
3. Compare accuracy for a common species and a rare one.
4. Remove blurry photos and report how accuracy changes.
5. Write a short report on how a classifier could help a citizen science project.

### computer_science
1. Read each step of the Colab code and describe its job in plain words.
2. Change the number of training photos and record the accuracy.
3. Split photos into training and test sets and explain why that matters.
4. Find a photo the model gets wrong and suggest a fix.
5. Draw a diagram of the steps from raw photos to a prediction.

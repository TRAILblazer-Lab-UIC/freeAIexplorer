## Overview
CoderZ is a cloud platform for learning coding and robotics with virtual robots. It is sold to schools by Coderz Technologies Ltd. and its affiliate Intelitek. The discovery notes describe it as aimed at grades 4 and up. The official site blocked fetching. Feature details are limited.

## Benefits for students
- Students code virtual robots in the browser, per the discovery notes.
- Students use a school account to save their work.
- Students can practice robotics ideas with no physical kit.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers create teacher and student accounts for the school.
- The vendor sells the platform as a school license.
- Teachers should confirm features on a live demo. Only privacy pages were visible in the research.

## Benefits for schools
- The notes describe a paid school license. The price was not found.
- No robot hardware is needed for virtual robots.
- IT should check browser and network needs with the vendor.

## Getting started
- [ ] Contact Coderz Technologies Ltd. or Intelitek to ask for a demo.
- [ ] Ask for the price of a school license.
- [ ] Ask what the platform teaches at each grade.
- [ ] Ask what student data is collected and where it is stored.
- [ ] Create teacher accounts first.
- [ ] Create student accounts only after reading the privacy terms.
- [ ] Test one lesson on a student device.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in the pages that were visible. Students code virtual robots that run through the vendor cloud. A teacher should ask the vendor whether any AI features exist.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are required, and the school creates them. The internet is needed at all times, and the platform runs in the vendor cloud. The research could not read the official site. Search snippets of the COPPA policy say Coderz Technologies Ltd. and Intelitek follow COPPA, and a school may act as the parent's agent and give consent. A separate FERPA policy says clients provide student personal information when they sign up. The research did not verify the data fields, retention, or any third party analytics. A teacher should read the privacy pages and ask how student data is protected.

3. Write a program that reacts to a virtual sensor.
4. Compare two programs for one task and pick the shorter one.
5. Explain a finished program to a partner.

## Overview
LMMS is a free, open source music production program for Windows, macOS, and Linux. Users compose, sequence, mix, and automate songs. It has a Beat+Bassline editor, a piano roll, and 16 built-in synths. Students in grades 6-12 build tracks and mix them.

## Benefits for students
- Students make full songs with beats, bass, and melody.
- Students learn how a piano roll shows pitch and time.
- Students can plug in a MIDI keyboard to play notes.

## Benefits for teachers
- The program supports MIDI, VST instruments, SoundFonts, and LADSPA effects.
- The site hosts a sharing platform and a forum.
- Reviewers report a learning curve and occasional crashes. Save often.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free.
- It runs on the device and needs no account.
- IT should install it. Browser-based BandLab is suggested as a classroom alternative.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download LMMS for your system from the LMMS website.
- [ ] Ask IT to install it on lab computers.
- [ ] Check that each computer has audio output and headphones.
- [ ] Open the Beat+Bassline editor and make a simple drum pattern.
- [ ] Add a synth in the piano roll and write a short melody.
- [ ] Save the project often.
- [ ] Connect a MIDI keyboard if one is available.

## Where is the AI?
LMMS has no AI. The vendor describes no AI features. It builds skills in sequencing and pattern spotting. Students arrange repeating patterns and layer sounds in time.

## Privacy and safety
LMMS runs on the device and needs no account. The internet is used for download and setup. The website offers an optional community sharing platform and forum. The research read no privacy policy. Student songs stay on the computer unless a student shares them. A teacher should check where students save project files and tell them not to post work to the community site.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Build a four-bar drum loop in the Beat+Bassline editor.
2. Write a melody in the piano roll and play it with two different synths.
3. Layer bass, chords, and melody into a short song.
4. Use automation to make a sound fade in over time.
5. Present a finished track to the class and describe the choices.

## Overview
The Adafruit EdgeBadge is a credit card sized board with a color display, buttons, lights, a microphone, an accelerometer, and a light sensor. It can run TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers. Adafruit demos recognize word pairs such as yes and no. Students can also program it with CircuitPython or MakeCode Arcade.

## Benefits for students
- Students run a real AI model on a tiny device.
- Students use the microphone and motion sensor for projects.
- Students can wear the badge and show it to others.

## Benefits for teachers
- Ready demos cover keyword spotting and gestures.
- Several programming tools work with the board.
- A USB cable and a computer are all that setup needs.

## Benefits for schools
- The price was 35.95 USD for one to nine units with volume discounts.
- The board was out of stock on the Adafruit page when checked.
- AI runs on the board. Adafruit.io data applies only if a device is connected to that service.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the EdgeBadge page on the Adafruit site and check the stock.
- [ ] Gather a USB cable and a computer.
- [ ] Install the Arduino IDE or the CircuitPython tools.
- [ ] Load the TensorFlow Lite demo.
- [ ] Flash the board over USB.
- [ ] Stay online during the downloads.
- [ ] Test the yes and no demo in a quiet room.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a small TensorFlow Lite model that runs on the board itself. It listens through the microphone or reads the accelerometer. Students load a demo, test it, and then study how it works. The board does the work on the device.

## Privacy and safety
The AI runs on the board and no account is needed. The internet is used at setup. The research lists no student data collected. Adafruit.io data applies only if a device is connected to that service. The Adafruit store policy says it does not knowingly sell or share personal information of minors under 16. That is a data rule and not a minimum age. A teacher should check the policy before students use any Adafruit online service. A teacher should also test the microphone demo in a quiet space.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Run the yes and no demo and record how often it is right.
2. Test the keyword demo with different voices and note the results.
3. Read the demo code and label the part that listens.
4. Try a gesture demo with the accelerometer and describe the motions it knows.
5. Write a short CircuitPython program that shows a message on the display.

### engineering
1. Identify each sensor on the board and say what it measures.
2. Test the light sensor in bright and dim spots and chart the readings.
3. Design a case or lanyard for the badge.
4. Measure how long the battery lasts on a demo.
5. Compare the board with a larger computer and explain the trade offs.

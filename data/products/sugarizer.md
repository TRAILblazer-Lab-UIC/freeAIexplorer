## Overview
Sugarizer is a free learning platform for children roughly ages 6 to 12. It comes from the Sugar environment built for One Laptop per Child. It offers about 50 activities, a Journal, and over 1,000 educational videos. Students open an activity and their work is recorded in the Journal. An optional school server saves work and gives teachers a dashboard.

## Benefits for students
- Students choose from about 50 activities, including reading, writing, and math.
- Students find their work in a Journal.
- Students can watch more than 1,000 educational videos.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can build activities into lessons on tablets or classroom computers.
- Teachers can review student work through a server.
- The Exerciser tool builds exercises.

## Benefits for schools
- The platform is free and the code is open.
- It has apps for Windows, Mac, Linux, Android, and iOS, plus a browser app.
- The optional server needs IT help and supports Docker.

## Getting started
- [ ] Install a native app or open the web app.
- [ ] Try several activities on one device.
- [ ] Decide whether you need saved work and class features.
- [ ] Ask IT to set up or join a Sugarizer Server.
- [ ] Review what the server stores before students use it.
- [ ] Check the README of each activity. Licenses vary.
- [ ] Plan three activities for a first lesson.

## Where is the AI?
Sugarizer has no AI. Students run activities that they choose and control. They build reading, math, and science skills through practice.

## Privacy and safety
Without the server, work stays on the device. With a server, it stores a student's user name, colors, favorites and activity work and history. Accounts are optional. The site states no advertisements and no tracking tools, but the research did not read its privacy policy. The web app needs a constant connection to a server. A teacher should check who runs the school server and who can see student work.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Write a short story in a writing activity and save it in the Journal.
2. Read a text activity and retell it to a partner.
3. Build a word game and trade it with a classmate.
4. Write a caption for a picture.
5. Share a Journal entry with the class.

### math
1. Practice number facts in a math activity and record your score.
2. Solve a puzzle and explain your steps.
3. Make a pattern and ask a partner to continue it.
4. Use the Exerciser tool to build five math questions.
5. Compare your answers with a classmate.

### science
1. Watch an educational video and write three facts.
2. Use an activity to explore a science topic.
3. Draw what you learned in a Journal entry.
4. Ask a question and find the answer in a video.
5. Teach a classmate one fact from your video.

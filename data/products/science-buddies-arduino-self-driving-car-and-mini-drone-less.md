## Overview
These are free lesson plans from Science Buddies. Students build circuits and code an Arduino-based self-driving car and a mini drone. The car unit has eight parts and about 12 hours for grades 8 to 12, for groups of 2 to 4. The official pages returned an error to the research tool. These details come from search snippets.

## Benefits for students
- Students build circuits and code a working car.
- Students design obstacle avoidance and lane-following with sensors.
- Students work in groups of 2 to 4.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each part has a video and a supplemental PDF with objectives and assessments.
- A companion mini drone lesson is available.
- A search snippet recommends an introductory Arduino lesson first.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons are free.
- Arduino kits, a chassis, and drone hardware are separate purchases. Prices were not found.
- IT will likely need to install the Arduino IDE.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Arduino Self-Driving Car lesson on Science Buddies.
- [ ] Gather Arduino boards, sensors, and the chassis or drone parts.
- [ ] Install the Arduino IDE on student computers.
- [ ] Download the PDFs or play the videos.
- [ ] Start new students with an introductory Arduino lesson.
- [ ] Build the first circuit.
- [ ] Test each sensor before wiring the full car.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson is not an AI lesson in the machine learning sense. Self-driving here means sensor-based rule coding. Students build skills in sensors and logic as they write rules for the car.

## Privacy and safety
The programs run on the Arduino. The internet is used at setup. The research could not read the lesson pages. It did not confirm account rules for downloads and videos. The materials do not state what the site collects. A teacher should check the Science Buddies site terms and watch battery and wiring safety.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Build the car chassis and mount the sensors.
2. Test the car on a lane marked with tape.
3. Design an obstacle course for the car.
4. Compare two sensor placements and record the results.
5. Build the companion mini drone and list how it differs from the car.

### computer_science
1. Write code that makes the car stop at an obstacle.
2. Add a rule that steers the car back into its lane.
3. Change a sensor threshold and predict the effect.
4. Debug a program that makes the car turn the wrong way.
5. Explain how the car decides what to do in plain words.

### science
1. Test how a sensor reads a surface at different distances.
2. Build a simple circuit and measure the voltage.
3. Record sensor readings in a table.
4. Graph the readings and look for a pattern.
5. Explain how the sensor sends a signal to the board.

## Overview
These are two courses on the NVIDIA Jetson Nano with the JetBot platform. Applied AI and Robotics has five units, from Getting Started with JetBot to Localization with AprilTags and ROS. Autonomy Foundations covers networking, collision avoidance, AprilTag navigation, and SLAM. Students collect and label data and use classification and regression on the robot. CS2N delivers both courses.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a real robot to avoid collisions.
- Students try reinforcement learning for racing.
- Students learn how AI and robot navigation fit together.

## Benefits for teachers
- The five units follow a clear order.
- Courses are delivered through CS2N.
- Search results describe training for high school and college educators.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum price is not stated.
- Hardware is the main cost. A search snippet lists onsite teacher training at 1,299 USD.
- Setup takes days and IT help is needed.

## Where is the AI?
The AI runs on the Jetson Nano on the robot. Students collect and label data, train models, and run them. The courses cover classification, regression, transfer learning, and reinforcement learning. The CS2N site runs in the vendor cloud.

## Privacy and safety
Course pages say access and tracking go through CS2N, and some data goes to the vendor. The CS2N policy lists name, email, username, group membership, and course activity. It does not name COPPA or say where data is stored. It allows use by children under 13 with consent from a parent, school, district, or teacher. The robot AI runs on the Jetson Nano, but the pages do not state data handling. The materials do not state whether an account is needed or how much the internet is used. The robot camera records images that students label. A teacher should read the CS2N policy and decide where images are saved.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Collect and label images to train a collision avoidance model.
2. Compare classification and regression on the robot.
3. Train an autonomous racing model and review how it improves.
4. Test the robot on a new floor and describe where it fails.
5. Explain how a labeled picture teaches a model.

### engineering
1. Build the JetBot and list each part.
2. Test basic navigation on a taped course.
3. Use AprilTags to guide the robot to a waypoint.
4. Tune the robot speed and compare the lap times.
5. Design a mount that gives the camera a better view.

## Overview
These free lesson plans teach engineering design and the science behind underwater ROVs. Topics include Newton's laws, buoyancy, air pressure, and Archimedes' principle. Students design, build, and test an ROV with shipwreck exploration as the theme. The unit is written for grades 6 to 8 and adapts to grades 9 to 12.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a low-cost ROV and test it.
- Students connect forces and buoyancy to a real design task.
- Students learn how NOAA scientists and maritime archaeologists explore shipwrecks.

## Benefits for teachers
- The unit fits in about three 45-minute blocks and can be expanded.
- Teams of 2 to 4 share a kit.
- The PDF lists science standards and Ocean Literacy Principles 6 and 7.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons are free.
- The PDF says the materials are simple and inexpensive. No prices are given.
- The PDF says kits can be borrowed.

## Where is the AI?
This unit has no AI. Students build and test a physical ROV. They practice testing and pattern spotting as they compare designs.

## Privacy and safety
The materials describe no student data collection and list no student tools. They do not state whether accounts or the internet are needed. The ROV build is a hands-on activity. A teacher should confirm that the PDF is the current version. A teacher should also plan for water and electrical safety near the pool.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Explain Newton's laws using the ROV's motion.
2. Test how added weight changes buoyancy.
3. Predict whether the ROV will sink or float and check it.
4. Explain Archimedes' principle in your own words.
5. Discuss how air pressure changes as an ROV goes deeper.

### engineering
1. Sketch an ROV design for exploring a shipwreck.
2. Build the ROV from the kit materials.
3. Test the ROV in water and record the results.
4. Change one design feature and test again.
5. Present the final design to the class.

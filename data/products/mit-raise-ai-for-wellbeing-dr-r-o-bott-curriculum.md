## Overview
AI for Wellbeing is a 30-hour middle school curriculum on conversational AI and design justice. It has four modules on intro to AI, text classification and algorithmic justice, chatbots, and a final project. Students use the browser-based RAISE AI Playground to program a Jibo social robot or a virtual Jibo. A virtual Jibo is included. A class can work without the robot.

## Benefits for students
- Students build chatbots and text classifiers with blocks.
- Students study fairness and design justice.
- Students program a virtual Jibo in the browser.

## Benefits for teachers
- The materials include Google Slides, Kahoot assessments, and a Semantris game.
- The curriculum is built around four modules.
- A pilot ran the curriculum with middle school students through the STEAM Ahead Learning Academy.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials appear to be free. No Jibo price is given.
- Jibo robots may be hard to obtain, but the virtual Jibo avoids that.
- It runs in a browser and needs little IT work.

## Where is the AI?
The AI includes text classification, algorithmic bias, chatbots, and command recognition. It runs in the browser and on the Jibo robot. Students program the AI Playground blocks, train a classifier, and design chatbot talk. They then test the results for fairness.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need or where their work is stored. The page names Google Slides, Kahoot, and the Semantris game as outside tools. The research did not review the data flow of the AI Playground or the virtual Jibo. A teacher should check the AI Playground sign-in and data terms. A teacher should also review any text students use to train classifiers.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a text classifier in the AI Playground and test it on new sentences.
2. Program a virtual Jibo to answer three questions.
3. Add examples to a weak class and compare the results.
4. Find a sentence the classifier gets wrong and explain why.
5. Create a chatbot for the final project with a clear goal.

### social_studies
1. Discuss how algorithmic bias could harm a community.
2. Interview a classmate about a time a system treated them unfairly.
3. Write a design justice rule for a chatbot.
4. Compare two classifiers trained on different data and describe who each serves.
5. Present how a school could check a system for fairness.

### ela
1. Write a chatbot script with a greeting, three replies, and a goodbye.
2. Revise the script to sound friendly and clear.
3. Rewrite one reply for a different audience.
4. Role play the chatbot with a partner and note where the talk breaks down.
5. Write a short reflection on how word choice changes a chatbot's tone.

## Overview
Sonata is an add-on for NVDA, a screen reader for Windows. It adds a speech driver built on neural text to speech models and supports Piper voices. It serves blind and low vision students who already use NVDA. Students listen to their computer read text aloud in a more natural voice.

## Benefits for students
- Students hear screen text read in a neural voice that sounds more natural than a basic synthesizer.
- Students can choose among several downloaded voices and pick the one they find easiest to follow.
- Students keep using the same NVDA tool they already know.

## Benefits for teachers
- The add-on works inside NVDA. Teachers have no new program to teach.
- A built in voice manager lists voices and downloads them with a few key presses.
- Teachers can install a voice from a local file when a student device is offline.

## Benefits for schools
- The add-on is free and uses the GPL-2.0 license. NVDA is also free.
- The speech runs on the student's own Windows computer.
- IT may need to approve the add-on install on managed machines.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Sonata release page and download the add-on package.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the add-on install on a managed Windows computer.
- [ ] Open the package to install it in NVDA and restart NVDA.
- [ ] Open the voice manager from the NVDA menu or from the prompt.
- [ ] Download at least one voice. The README recommends low or medium quality voices for faster response.
- [ ] Choose Sonata as the speech synthesizer in NVDA settings.
- [ ] Go offline and test that the chosen voice still reads aloud.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a neural text to speech model that turns written words into spoken audio. It runs on the student's own computer through the Sonata Rust speech engine. The add-on ships with no voices. Users download them once. Students then use the voice to listen to their own writing and their reading.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The internet is used to download the add-on and the voices. The README describes Piper as a local text to speech system and describes no data collection. It does not say outright that the add-on works fully offline after voices are installed. The research found no privacy policy. A teacher should test the add-on offline and check that the chosen voice responds quickly.

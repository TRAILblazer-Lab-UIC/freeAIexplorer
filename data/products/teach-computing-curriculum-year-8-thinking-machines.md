## Overview
This is Lesson 5 of the Year 8 Computing Systems unit in the Teach Computing Curriculum. Learners define artificial intelligence and look at problems it has handled. They compare machine learning with conventional programming. They also train a model in Google Teachable Machine and discuss moral dilemmas.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a real model and see how data shapes the result.
- Students learn the steps of gathering data, training, and testing.
- Students discuss fair and unfair AI decisions.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson package is free and ready to teach.
- The lesson covers the main ideas in one class period.
- Teachers do not need coding skills.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson costs nothing.
- Students need a device with a webcam or microphone.
- The materials do not state what data the tools collect.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson teaches machine learning. Students train a model in Google Teachable Machine, which runs in the web browser. They gather data, train the model, and test it. Students then compare this approach with writing rules by hand.

## Privacy and safety
Students use Google Teachable Machine in the browser and the internet is needed during the lesson. Teachers may need a free NCCE account to download the package. The research did not read the Teachable Machine privacy policy or verify where its training data goes. The materials do not state what data the tools collect. Students use a webcam or microphone. Check what Teachable Machine does with that input and decide whether students use their own faces or other safe images.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train a Teachable Machine model on two kinds of objects and test it.
2. Compare a machine learning approach with a rule-based program for the same task.
3. Add poor data to a model on purpose and describe how the results change.
4. List the steps of gathering data, training, and testing in your own words.
5. Discuss a moral dilemma about an AI decision and share a position.

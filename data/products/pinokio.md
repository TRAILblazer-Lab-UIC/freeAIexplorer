## Overview
Pinokio is a desktop app that launches and manages local AI and creative apps. It runs installer scripts from public Git repositories. Each app is installed and started with a click. Pinokio is not a model runner itself. It is a tool for teachers and IT staff, with advanced high school students as possible users.

## Benefits for students
- Students can try open source AI apps on a school computer.
- Students see how local AI differs from cloud AI.
- Each app installs in its own local folder.

## Benefits for teachers
- A directory and feed list many apps.
- One-click installs reduce command line work.
- Apps can be reached from other devices.

## Benefits for schools
- Pinokio is free and open source.
- Each app has its own hardware needs, often a GPU.
- Downloads can pass 6 GB, and scripts run arbitrary commands. IT approval is advisable.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the Pinokio desktop installer for your system.
- [ ] Ask IT to review the app before installing.
- [ ] Install Pinokio on one capable computer.
- [ ] Browse the app directory and pick one small app.
- [ ] Check the hardware needs, such as an NVIDIA GPU with 6 to 8 GB of VRAM or an Apple silicon Mac.
- [ ] Review the script before running it.
- [ ] Review each app's data flow separately.

## Where is the AI?
Pinokio is a launcher for third-party AI apps. The AI belongs to each installed app and runs locally for most of them. Students can try an app, change its settings, and compare the results. The vendor does not describe AI inside Pinokio itself.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The Pinokio Registry policy covers the Registry website and API. That service collects account, community, and check-in data, including platform, GPU, RAM, and a system fingerprint. The policy does not apply to the third party apps you install. Installer scripts can run any command and download files. The research did not confirm whether the desktop app sends telemetry. A teacher or IT staff should review every app before students use it.

## Overview
TIP-Toy is an open source tactile toolkit from UCL for teaching computing concepts through music to children with mixed visual abilities. Passive 3D printed blocks carry QR code markers. An overhead webcam reads them on a Raspberry Pi running custom Python software. It targets ages 6 to 11. It is a research prototype and the paper was published at ASSETS 2020.

## Benefits for students
- Children arrange blocks by touch to make music.
- Children with full sight and children who are blind can play together.
- Children learn sequencing as the blocks play in order.

## Benefits for teachers
- The design welcomes children from full sight to total blindness.
- The code is open source.
- The toolkit is a research prototype. Teachers should expect to adapt it.

## Benefits for schools
- Users print the blocks and supply a Raspberry Pi and webcam.
- The cost was not verified.
- It needs no internet and no accounts.

## Where is the AI?
TIP-Toy has no AI. The webcam reads the QR markers on the blocks and plays the music in order. Children practice sequencing by arranging blocks to build a tune.

## Privacy and safety
The system runs locally on a Raspberry Pi and needs no account or internet. No privacy policy exists. The research could not read the paper. The study data handling is unknown. ## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Arrange blocks to play a short tune and listen to it.
2. Change the order of the blocks and describe how the song changes.
3. Make a rhythm with two blocks repeated.
4. Compose a tune for a partner to guess.
5. Perform the tune for the class.

### computer_science
1. Line up blocks as a sequence and predict the sound.
2. Swap two blocks and predict the new result.
3. Find the block that makes the tune go wrong.
4. Describe in words the order of a tune.
5. Teach a partner how a sequence works.

### special_ed
1. Explore the blocks by touch before you play.
2. Work with a partner and describe each block aloud.
3. Build a tune that a classmate can follow by touch.
4. Play a game where a partner finds the missing block.
5. Share what you made and how it felt to build it.

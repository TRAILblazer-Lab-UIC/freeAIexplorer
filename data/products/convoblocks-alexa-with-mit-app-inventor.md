## Overview
ConvoBlocks is a block-based environment inside MIT App Inventor for building conversational agents. Agents can be tested in the browser or deployed to Alexa devices such as the Alexa mobile app or an Echo. Built-in tutorials include Alexa Hello World and Alexa Calculator. A research review reports a five day workshop with 47 students aged 11 to 18 and 9 teachers.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a voice app with blocks and no typed code.
- Students can test the agent in the browser.
- Students can deploy to Alexa and talk to their own app.

## Benefits for teachers
- Tutorials run from beginner to advanced.
- Teachers can follow the model of the five day workshop.
- The tool is free.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free. Deploying to Alexa needs an Amazon developer account.
- It runs in Chrome, Safari, or Firefox. An Alexa device is optional.
- IT will likely need to approve the sign-in and the Amazon account.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the ConvoBlocks site and sign in.
- [ ] Check that browsers meet the App Inventor needs, such as Chrome 55 or later.
- [ ] Follow the Alexa Hello World tutorial.
- [ ] Test the agent in the browser.
- [ ] Ask IT before creating Amazon developer accounts for students.
- [ ] Deploy to an Alexa device only if the school approves.
- [ ] Try the Alexa Calculator tutorial.

## Where is the AI?
The tool builds conversational agents that use speech interaction. The AI runs in the vendor's cloud and in the Alexa service. Students write the blocks that decide what the agent says and then test how it understands speech.

## Privacy and safety
Students need an App Inventor account, and the internet is needed at all times. Deploying to Alexa needs an Amazon developer account. Voice interactions on Alexa devices go through Amazon cloud services, per discovery notes. A search snippet says no Amazon device is required. The research read no privacy policy, and the materials do not say what student data is kept. A teacher should check the Amazon and App Inventor privacy terms. A teacher should also check whether student voices are recorded on a shared device.

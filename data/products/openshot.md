## Overview
OpenShot is a free open source video editor. It offers timeline editing across unlimited tracks, keyframe animation, transitions, chroma key, and color grading. It suits middle and high school students who make videos. Students cut clips, add titles and effects, and export a finished film.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn the basics of a real video editing timeline.
- Students can add transitions, titles, and green screen effects.
- Students can animate objects with keyframes.

## Benefits for teachers
- The interface is easy to pick up for most teachers.
- Teachers can use one tool for many kinds of video projects.
- It reads many file formats through FFmpeg.

## Benefits for schools
- The software costs nothing and the code is open source.
- Student projects stay on the computer.
- IT will likely need to approve the install. Video files need enough memory and disk space.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the installer from openshot.org. The current version listed is 4.1.1.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install on managed computers.
- [ ] Check that the computer has enough memory and disk space for video files.
- [ ] Wikipedia lists ChromeOS support, but the official page does not confirm it. Test before you plan a Chromebook lesson.
- [ ] Import a short clip and drag it onto the timeline.
- [ ] Add one transition and one title.
- [ ] Export a short test video.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in OpenShot. The tool works through manual editing on a timeline. Students make every cut and effect choice themselves.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and projects stay on the computer. The internet is used for the download. The privacy policy covers the website only. It does not mention software analytics or crash reports, and the website uses cookies and Google AdSense. A teacher should ask IT to check network activity. A teacher should also check permission before students film classmates or use music and images they do not own.

## Overview
This LEGO Education kit serves four students and combines building, block coding in the Coding Canvas app, and AI computer vision. It has 276 bricks, a single motor, a color sensor, a connection card, a USB cable, and a storage box. The page lists 30 lessons across six units, with an AI and Data unit. The Teacher Portal provides presentations and facilitation notes.

## Benefits for students
- Young students build and code in small groups.
- Students meet computer vision in simple activities.
- Some lessons are screen-free and use only the bricks.

## Benefits for teachers
- The free Teacher Portal gives presentations and notes.
- Students join lessons with a PIN.
- Group work lets one device serve four students.

## Benefits for schools
- The product page lists 339.95 USD for one kit and 2249 USD for a 24 student bundle.
- Professional learning is priced separately.
- The internet is needed at setup only.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the kit on the LEGO Education site.
- [ ] Sign up for the free Teacher Portal.
- [ ] Install the Coding Canvas web app or iOS app while online.
- [ ] Charge the motor with the USB cable.
- [ ] Build the first model with a group of four.
- [ ] Share the PIN so students can join the lesson.
- [ ] Review the privacy terms before students sign in.

## Where is the AI?
The kit uses computer vision, pose detection, and classification. The AI runs on the device in the Coding Canvas app. Students use it to make a model respond to what a camera sees. The vendor material available here does not describe the youngest lessons in detail.

## Privacy and safety
Students join lessons with a PIN and need no account. Teachers sign up for the free Teacher Portal. LEGO states that all AI features run locally on the student device, that no data goes to the LEGO Group or third parties, and that no models are trained on student data. It says only pose data is kept locally. The statement names Google MoveNet as the engine and does not say whether any data goes to Google. The internet is needed for first install, updates, and language changes. A teacher should read the LEGO privacy terms before teachers or students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Open the AI and Data unit and sort a few objects into groups.
2. Use a body movement to make the camera model react.
3. Put coding blocks in order to make the motor spin.
4. Tell a partner what the camera sees and what the model guessed.
5. Fix a block program that does not do what it should.

### engineering
1. Build the first model and test how it moves.
2. Change one brick on the model and watch what happens.
3. Test the color sensor with different colored bricks.
4. Draw the model before building it.
5. Show the finished model to the class and say how it works.

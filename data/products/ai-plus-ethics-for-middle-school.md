## Overview
This open curriculum teaches middle school students about AI technical concepts and their ethical effects. It has eight activities, teacher guides, and assessments, and takes about 13 hours. Students train a simple classifier, study the stakeholders of YouTube, redesign its recommendation algorithm, and add to a class mural of hopes and concerns about AI.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a classifier and see how bias enters a model.
- Students look at who gains and who loses from a platform they use.
- Students share their own hopes and concerns about AI on a class mural.

## Benefits for teachers
- The curriculum includes teacher guides and assessments.
- Each activity lists a time, from 30 minutes to about 4 hours.
- Teachers should use the materials as published. The license forbids changes.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free to access.
- Several activities are paper based and need only printing.
- Three activities need Chromebooks with webcams and internet access.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a simple classifier that students train in the browser. Other activities use paper to explain recommendation systems, algorithmic bias, and ethics. Students train the model, test it, and then change the training data to see what shifts. The curriculum also asks them to redesign a recommendation algorithm.

## Privacy and safety
The materials list no student accounts and no student data collection. Five of the eight activities need no device or run on paper. The classifier activity uses Google Teachable Machine with webcams and the internet. Other activities use online AI demos and YouTube, and the materials come as a Google Doc with datasets in Google Drive. The research did not review those tools' data practices. A teacher should check how Teachable Machine handles webcam images before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train a simple classifier and test it on new examples.
2. Play AI Bingo to learn where AI shows up in daily life.
3. Write an Algorithms as Opinions note that shows how design choices shape results.
4. Complete the YouTube Redesign and sketch a new recommendation algorithm.
5. Explain how a biased training set changes a classifier's answers.

### social_studies
1. Fill in an Ethical Matrix that lists the people affected by an AI tool.
2. Run the YouTube stakeholder analysis and name who gains and who loses.
3. Hold a class talk on who should decide how recommendations work.
4. Write a speculative fiction story that shows the social impact of an AI tool.
5. Add notes to the class mural of hopes and concerns about AI.

## Overview
Smart Cutebot and Cutebot Pro are wheeled robot cars driven by a micro:bit. They have motors and four-way line tracking, and the Pro adds more sensors. Students program them with MakeCode or Python. The micro:bit is sold separately. The ELECFREAKS wiki lists 15 case lessons for the Smart Cutebot.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a car that follows a line.
- Students work in pairs with one car each.
- Students can use blocks first and Python later.

## Benefits for teachers
- The wiki has 15 case lessons for the Smart Cutebot.
- A Liverpool outreach lesson plan for Years 7 to 9 runs 60 to 90 minutes with one Cutebot per pair.
- The assembly is simple.

## Benefits for schools
- Shop sale prices were 29.90 USD for the Cutebot and 49.90 USD for the Cutebot Pro, both without a micro:bit.
- Purdue lists a price of 71.99 USD with a micro:bit.
- The kit may ship without batteries. Plan to buy them.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Smart Cutebot or Cutebot Pro on the ELECFREAKS shop or a reseller.
- [ ] Buy a micro:bit for each car.
- [ ] Buy batteries if the kit does not include them.
- [ ] Assemble the car and insert the micro:bit.
- [ ] Open MakeCode in a browser and add the Cutebot extension.
- [ ] Download a first program over USB.
- [ ] Draw a line with dark tape and test line tracking.

## Where is the AI?
The Cutebot has no AI. It follows the code students write and reads its line sensors. Students build skills in sensors and logic by deciding what the car should do on each side of a line.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed for the robot. Programs are flashed to the micro:bit over USB, and the robot runs on board. The vendor policy covers website orders only. The MakeCode online editor is a Microsoft tool whose policy the research did not read. A teacher should check which editor students use. A teacher should also check the battery type and test the track before class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow one of the wiki case lessons and make the car drive forward.
2. Program the car to turn at a corner and stop at a finish line.
3. Use the line sensors to follow a taped track.
4. Work with a partner and swap who types and who tests.
5. Change a speed value and describe how the car behaves.

### engineering
1. Tape a track with curves and test how well the car stays on it.
2. Record how fast the car finishes with two different speed settings.
3. Compare line tracking on dark tape and on light tape.
4. Sketch where an extra sensor could go on the car.
5. Redesign the track layout to make it harder and test the car again.

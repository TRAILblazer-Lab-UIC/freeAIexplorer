## Overview
Easel is browser software from Inventables for designing and carving projects on CNC machines such as X-Carve and Carvey. Inventables runs an education program that offers one year Easel Pro licenses to qualifying educators. Students design in the browser and send the job to a connected CNC machine.

## Benefits for students
- Students see a design on screen turn into a carved object.
- Students use a project library as a starting point.
- Students practice careful measuring and planning.

## Benefits for teachers
- Qualifying educators can apply for a free one year Pro license.
- The software runs in a browser.
- The project library gives ready ideas.

## Benefits for schools
- Easel has a free tier.
- A CNC machine is needed to carve projects, and the education page directs schools to request a quote.
- Accounts and design files are stored with the vendor.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find Easel on the Inventables website and create an account.
- [ ] Apply for the free Pro educator license with a school email address.
- [ ] Read the school privacy terms for student accounts.
- [ ] Confirm that your CNC machine is a supported model.
- [ ] Connect the machine and try a project from the library.
- [ ] Decide how students will make accounts and share files.
- [ ] Set a safety routine for using the machine.

## Where is the AI?
Easel has no AI. It builds design and sequencing skills. Students plan a part and set the order of cuts.

## Privacy and safety
Easel is always online, and accounts are required. Student designs are stored with the vendor. The education page links to a privacy policy that the research did not read, and no student data statement was found. Teachers on forums mention students using their own free accounts and sharing files. A teacher should read the privacy terms and decide whether students use their own accounts or shared files.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Design a small part in the browser and carve it on the CNC machine.
2. Measure a finished carving and compare it to the design.
3. Change one dimension and predict how the finished piece will differ.
4. Plan a project that has two parts that fit together.
5. Write a short step list for safe machine use.

### cte
1. Practice digital fabrication by taking a project from design to finished product.
2. Compare two designs for the same object and pick the better one.
3. Estimate the time to carve a project and check it against the real time.
4. Present a finished piece as a mock product to the class.
5. Describe jobs that use CNC machines.

### visual_arts
1. Start from a project in the library and change its look.
2. Design a sign or nameplate with letters and shapes.
3. Create a pattern that repeats across a board.
4. Sketch an idea on paper and then rebuild it in Easel.
5. Display the finished carvings and write a short artist note for each.

## Overview
Patterns All Around is an eight-lesson KIBO unit on mathematics for PreK to grade 2. Lessons cover sturdy building, what a robot is, programming, sensors, repeats and ifs. The unit ends with a patterns project and a class quilt. Robotic Animals is a companion unit that links biology and robotics.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn pattern ideas by programming a robot.
- Students use warm-up games, pair work and free exploration.
- Students meet sensors, repeats and conditionals in early lessons.

## Benefits for teachers
- The PDFs are free.
- Each lesson follows a clear routine with a warm-up, a challenge and a technology circle.
- The materials rate the teacher learning curve as easy.

## Benefits for schools
- The PDFs cost nothing, but a KIBO robot is needed.
- The units need no internet, devices or accounts.
- The PDF is copyright 2016 from the DevTech Research Group, which has moved to Boston College.

## Where is the AI?
These units have no AI. KIBO follows the blocks that children scan. Children build skills in sequencing and pattern spotting.

## Privacy and safety
The PDFs are static documents, and the robot is screen free. The research read no privacy policy and found no account needs. The Animals PDF was not read in full. A teacher should check that the school has enough KIBO kits for small groups. A teacher should also check that the 2016 PDFs still match the current kit.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Build a repeating pattern of blocks and run it with KIBO.
2. Identify the unit that repeats in a pattern.
3. Predict the next step in a pattern before KIBO runs it.
4. Make a quilt square that shows a pattern from the unit.
5. Count and sort robot steps in a growing pattern.

### science
1. Choose an animal and list how it moves.
2. Program KIBO to move like that animal.
3. Compare a robot's sensor to an animal's sense.
4. Draw a habitat and program KIBO to travel through it.
5. Present what the robot animal can and cannot do.

### computer_science
1. Build a program with a repeat block.
2. Use a sensor block and test what the robot does.
3. Use an if block to make the robot choose a path.
4. Explain what a robot is in your own words.
5. Debug a program and share the fix.

## Overview
CircuitVerse is a free open source online simulator for digital logic circuits. Users build circuits from simple gates to sequential designs, plot timing diagrams, generate circuits from truth tables, and explore standard ICs. Teachers can create groups, post assignments with deadlines, and grade student work, per search results. Most documented use is in university courses.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and test logic circuits on screen.
- Students see timing diagrams for their designs.
- Students can learn from an interactive book on digital electronics.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can create groups and assignments.
- Teachers can grade student work.
- CircuitVerse Learn tutorials are available.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free and open source.
- It runs in a browser, and Chromebooks are listed.
- Accounts are optional but needed to save work.

## Where is the AI?
CircuitVerse has no AI. It builds the skill of logic, as students combine gates to make a circuit follow a rule. It also builds pattern spotting through truth tables.

## Privacy and safety
Data goes to the vendor cloud, and the internet is needed throughout. Accounts are optional but are needed to save circuits and to join teacher groups. The policy lists email, name, profile data, and sign in data from Google or Facebook, plus cookies and logs. It says personal information is not sold. It also says user content may be public and indexed by search engines, and that Google Analytics is used. The policy does not mention children or students and does not say where data is stored. A teacher should read the privacy policy and decide whether students need accounts.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build an AND gate and test every input, as in a truth table.
2. Generate a circuit from a truth table.
3. Make a circuit that follows a simple rule.
4. Read a timing diagram and explain it.
5. Compare a circuit with a short program that does the same thing.

### engineering
1. Design a circuit for a door alarm.
2. Build a half adder from gates.
3. Test a circuit and fix a wrong output.
4. Explore a standard IC and describe its job.
5. Present a design to the class.

## Overview
PhET provides free research-based interactive science and math simulations. They are written in HTML5 and can run online or be downloaded. Wikipedia reports over 125 simulations in physics, chemistry, biology, earth science, and math. Students change settings and watch what happens.

## Benefits for students
- Students can see ideas that are hard to see in real life.
- Students can test many changes quickly.
- The simulations are translated into dozens of languages.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers use the simulations as lecture demonstrations in physics, chemistry, and biology.
- The site offers teacher activities.
- No student account is needed.

## Benefits for schools
- The simulations are free on the web and in the offline installer.
- The offline installer can download the whole site for use with a weak connection.
- IT work is light. A Chromebook needs the latest Chrome.

## Getting started
- [ ] Go to the PhET site and browse the simulation catalog.
- [ ] Try a simulation in the browser.
- [ ] Check device needs. Use the latest Chrome on a Chromebook, Safari 15 or later on iPad, and macOS 14 or later on a Mac.
- [ ] Download the offline installer for schools with weak internet.
- [ ] Pick a teacher activity that matches your lesson.
- [ ] Tell students that no account is needed.
- [ ] Read the privacy policy with IT.

## Where is the AI?
PhET has no AI. It builds data and pattern spotting skills. Students change a variable and look for a pattern in the results.

## Privacy and safety
Students need no account, and PhET collects no personal information from them. The website and simulations use Google Analytics. PhET collects anonymous run data such as simulation name, language, device type, browser, and an anonymized IP address. Teacher accounts collect a name, an email, a school, and demographics. Users must be 13 or older to register an account. The research did not confirm whether the offline installer still sends analytics, and older Java simulations can send run data unless it is turned off. A teacher should read the privacy policy and decide which version to use.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Use a simulation as a demonstration and ask students to predict what will happen.
2. Change one variable in a physics simulation and record the results in a table.
3. Explore a chemistry simulation and sketch what happens to particles.
4. Compare a biology simulation with a diagram in the textbook.
5. Write a short lab report that explains what a simulation shows.

### math
1. Explore a PhET math simulation and describe the pattern you see.
2. Test three values and graph the results.
3. Predict an answer and check it in the simulation.
4. Create two questions about the simulation for a classmate.
5. Explain a math idea to a partner using a simulation as your example.

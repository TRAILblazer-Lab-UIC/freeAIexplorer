## Overview
This lesson is called Outsmarting AI-Driven Political Misinformation. Students learn to detect AI generated images, video, and online content using visual, contextual, and metadata cues. The agenda has a guess the fake opener, image analysis stations, a video verification challenge, a tool demo, and a reflection. The source says it was designed for academic librarians and faculty, but it may suit high school.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice reading images for visual clues.
- Students check the source, date, and location of a video.
- Students try tools such as TinEye, InVID, or Snopes.

## Benefits for teachers

## Where is the AI?
The lesson is about detecting AI generated media. It does not run AI itself. Students use public checking tools and their own judgment. They build skill in questioning images and videos.

## Privacy and safety
The tool demo has students try third party fact checking sites on phones or laptops. Those sites receive whatever images or links students submit, and the research did not review their privacy terms. The materials do not state accounts or data rules. Choose one tool and review its terms before class. Screen the images and videos because the topic is political misinformation.

## Ideas by subject
### library_media
1. Play the guess the fake game and explain your choices.
2. Look at an image for visual clues and fill in the detection worksheet.
3. Check the source, date, and location of a video clip.
4. Run a reverse image search with one of the tools.
5. Write a short guide on how to verify media before sharing.

### social_studies
1. Discuss how fake media can change public opinion before an election.
2. Study a past case of political misinformation and find how it spread.
3. Debate who should be responsible for stopping fake media.
4. Compare a verified photo with a faked one from the same event.
5. Write a message that urges neighbors to check before they share.

### ela
1. Evaluate the source of a claim and rate how much you trust it.
2. Compare how a real and a faked item use language to persuade.
3. Write a short reflection on what you learned about spotting fakes.
4. Rewrite a misleading caption so it is accurate.
5. Explain the difference between an opinion and a verified fact.

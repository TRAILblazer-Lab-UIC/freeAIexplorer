## Overview
SciServer is a free web platform for analyzing large science datasets. It gives free virtual machines running Jupyter notebooks. The Big Data in the Classroom program offers lesson plans built on Sloan Digital Sky Survey data. Students work in hosted notebooks with real astronomy data.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with real data from a sky survey.
- Students write Python code in a hosted notebook.
- Students need no software on their own device beyond a browser.

## Benefits for teachers
- Template educational notebooks support short labs and independent research.
- The platform holds public datasets of over 2 PB.
- The teacher learning curve is advanced.

## Benefits for schools
- The basic level is free.
- Students need only a browser and internet.
- The materials do not say how classroom accounts are managed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the SciServer login portal and register for a free account.
- [ ] Open the education notebooks.
- [ ] Run one template notebook yourself.
- [ ] Check with IT that the site is open on school networks.
- [ ] Ask the vendor how classroom accounts work before enrolling students.
- [ ] Pick a short lab for the first class session.

## Where is the AI?
SciServer has no AI. It is a Python and Jupyter data analysis environment. Students build data skills as they load, filter, and plot real measurements.

## Privacy and safety
An account is required, and the materials do not say who creates it. Student work and data live on the vendor cloud, and the internet is needed at all times. The homepage shows no terms or privacy policy, and the research found none. The materials do not state the minimum age or what student data is collected. A teacher should ask the vendor how classroom accounts and data are handled before students sign up.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Open a template notebook and plot the positions of stars from the survey data.
2. Compare the colors of two groups of objects and describe the difference.
3. Ask a question about the data and test it in a short lab.
4. Write a short summary of what the data shows about the sky.
5. Present an independent research question to the class.

### computer_science
1. Run a notebook cell and explain what the code does.
2. Change a Python variable and describe how the output changes.
3. Filter a table of data to a smaller group.
4. Make a chart from a column of data.
5. Add comments to a notebook that explain each step.

## Overview
Snap! is a free block-based programming language that runs in a web browser. It grew out of BYOB and was created by Jens Moenig with design input from Brian Harvey of UC Berkeley. Students build animations, games, and stories. They can also work with first-class procedures and lists.

## Benefits for students
- Students build animations, games, and stories with blocks.
- Students work with procedures and lists.
- Students can move on to deeper topics in the same tool.

## Benefits for teachers
- Berkeley uses Snap! to teach The Beauty and Joy of Computing.
- New York City adopted that course as an AP Computer Science Principles course in 2014.
- Setup takes only minutes.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and open source.
- It runs in the browser or offline as a PWA.
- No IT involvement is listed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open snap.berkeley.edu in a browser.
- [ ] Try a short project yourself.
- [ ] For offline use, install it as a PWA or unzip the GitHub release and open snap.html.
- [ ] Remember that offline projects are exported as XML files.
- [ ] Decide whether students will use cloud accounts.
- [ ] Leave the opt-in JavaScript block off unless it is needed.

## Where is the AI?
Snap! has no AI. Students build sequencing, logic, and data skills with blocks. They also learn how procedures and lists work.

## Privacy and safety
Cloud accounts are optional. They collect an email address, with a parent or guardian email for students under 13. Server logs record IP address and pages requested, and Google Analytics collects general usage statistics. Programs are not recorded unless saved to the cloud, and cloud projects are private unless shared. The servers are in the United States and cloud storage runs on a commercial provider. Some libraries can call web services. A teacher should decide whether students use cloud accounts or save files on the device.

## Overview
Model AI Assignments is a peer-reviewed collection of AI teaching assignments hosted at Gettysburg College. The one assignment read is When Your Neighbor is a Zombie: Zombie KNN by Yim Register and Dan Schneider. It is a 45 minute unplugged lesson for grades 6 to 12 that teaches the K-nearest neighbor algorithm. Students use features such as noise level to predict zombie counts at a new location and discuss accuracy.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how a nearest neighbor model makes predictions.
- Students use averaging skills to make and check predictions.
- Students discuss how accurate their predictions were.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson has a printable map and answer keys.
- It needs only basic averaging skills.
- Code.org runs a version of the same activity in its AI and Machine Learning unit.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free and printing is the only cost.
- The lesson needs no computers.
- The research team did not read the rest of the collection.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no running AI program. It teaches the K-nearest neighbor algorithm, a method that predicts from the closest known examples. Students use features of nearby places to predict a zombie count. They then check how accurate the prediction was.

## Privacy and safety
The Zombie KNN lesson runs on paper, and the research found no account needs, no internet need, and no student data collection. The internet is used only to download the printed materials. Other assignments in the collection may use software, and the research did not review them. A teacher should check the data needs of each assignment before use. The zombie theme also needs a look to see that it suits the class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Use noise level and other features to find the nearest neighbors of a new location.
2. Predict the zombie count for the new location from its neighbors.
3. Compare your prediction with the real count and judge accuracy.
4. Change the number of neighbors and see how the prediction changes.
5. Explain to a partner how nearest neighbor prediction works.

### math
1. Average the zombie counts from the nearest locations.
2. Find the difference between a prediction and the real count.
3. Compare the average of two neighbors with the average of three.
4. Make a table of predictions and errors for several locations.
5. Find which feature gives the best predictions and explain why.

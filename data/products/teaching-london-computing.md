## Overview
Teaching London Computing is a resource hub for computing teachers, run with CAS London and the cs4fn project. It offers mainly unplugged classroom activities, puzzles, booklets, workshops, and teacher training courses. Activities include machine learning, sorting algorithms, 20 Questions, and magic tricks. It covers primary through A-level.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn computing ideas through games and puzzles.
- Students try a machine learning activity without a computer.
- Students practice sorting and logic with cards and tricks.

## Benefits for teachers
- Many activities can be printed and used right away.
- Free workshops and CPD courses are offered.
- A 2017 evaluation found most interviewed teachers positive.

## Benefits for schools
- Many resources are described as free.
- Free cs4fn magazines go to UK schools that register.
- Prices for every course are not listed. Some activities need materials such as cards.

## Where is the AI?
One listed activity teaches machine learning without a computer. The activities run on paper and do not use an AI tool. Students learn how a system can improve by training on examples, and they build skills in algorithms and logic.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed to read the activities, and the internet is used only to download them. The paper activities collect no student data. Some items on the site use software such as JFLAP, Python, and Arduino. The research did not review how that software handles data. Registering for free cs4fn magazines is for schools in the UK. Check any software and any workshop sign-up against school policy.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Try the unplugged machine learning activity and describe how the system improves.
2. Sort a set of numbers with a sorting algorithm using cards.
3. Play 20 Questions and discuss how each question narrows the choices.
4. Perform a magic trick and explain the rule behind it.
5. Work through a puzzle booklet and share one solution.

### math
1. Sort a set of cards and count the swaps needed.
2. Solve a puzzle that needs logic and write down the steps.
3. Explain why a magic trick works using numbers.
4. Compare two sorting methods by counting steps.
5. Play 20 Questions and work out the best first question.

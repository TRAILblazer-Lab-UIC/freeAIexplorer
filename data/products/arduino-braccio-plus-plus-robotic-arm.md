## Overview
Braccio++ is a six servo robotic arm kit for advanced high school and university students aged 16 and over. The kit has an Arduino Nano RP2040 Connect, a Braccio Carrier with LCD and joystick, and six smart servo motors. It comes with an online platform with 4 lessons and 3 projects. A retailer lists it as retired and replaced by Braccio.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and control a real robotic arm.
- Students program in Arduino IDE text code.
- Students use motors that report data back to the board.

## Benefits for teachers
- Online lessons and projects come with the kit.
- Setup and teaching need advanced skills.
- No independent classroom review was found.

## Benefits for schools
- The page gives no price. Launch coverage reported 599 USD or 499 EUR.

## Getting started
- [ ] Check availability. A retailer lists the kit as retired.
- [ ] Assemble the arm.
- [ ] Connect it to a computer by USB C.

- [ ] Run the first lesson.

- [ ] Plan for several days of setup and practice.

## Where is the AI?
This kit has no AI. It runs the code students write. Students build logic and control skills as they plan servo moves and read data from the motors.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are optional. The online lesson platform needs an internet connection, and educators set up a classroom at classroom.arduino.cc, according to a search snippet. The materials do not say what student data is collected. Arduino policy says people under 18 need parental consent and Junior Accounts apply under 14. A teacher should check the platform terms and keep fingers clear of moving joints.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Assemble the arm and test each joint.
2. Move the arm to pick up a light object.
3. Measure the reach of the arm and record it.
4. Read data from the smart servos and describe it.
5. Design a task for the arm and test it.

### computer_science
1. Write code that moves one servo to a set angle.
2. Combine several moves into a pickup routine.
3. Use the joystick as an input to control the arm.
4. Use a loop to repeat a motion.
5. Debug a program that moves the arm too far.

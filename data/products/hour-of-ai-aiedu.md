## Overview
Hour of AI is a global initiative run by CodeAI and partners as an evolution of the Hour of Code. Activities take about one hour and are self-guided. They can run on laptops, tablets, or with no devices. The aiEDU page is listed as classroom curricula on ethics, fairness, and responsibility of AI, but the direct page returned a 404. Its activities could not be read.

## Benefits for students
- Students try an AI activity in about one hour.
- Students can join an unplugged activity with no device.
- Students think about fairness and responsibility.

## Benefits for teachers
- Most activity cards carry teacher notes.
- Teachers can choose an activity for any grade from K-12.
- The aiEDU Curriculum Portal offers Elementary Explorations for grades 3-5.

## Benefits for schools
- Hour of AI activities are described as free.
- Activities run in a browser or on paper.
- The site is a hub of partner activities, and each partner has its own data practices.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Hour of AI site and the aiEDU Curriculum Portal.
- [ ] Choose an activity that fits the grade.
- [ ] Read the teacher notes on the activity card.
- [ ] Check the partner's privacy policy for that activity.
- [ ] Decide between a browser activity and an unplugged one.
- [ ] Run the activity in one class period.
- [ ] Finish with a short discussion.

## Where is the AI?
The activities teach AI literacy and ethics. The vendor does not say where the AI runs, and it varies by partner. Students try tools or unplugged tasks and then discuss fairness and impact. They use the discussion to judge real AI tools.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are optional, and the internet is always needed. Teachers need a free account to download aiEDU curriculum portal materials, and student login rules are not stated. The aiEDU policy says it does not knowingly collect personal information directly from children under 13, which is a data rule. It says a school directing students to aiEDU resources is responsible for parental consent. The hourofai.org policy says that site is not intended for children under 13 and collects contact details and analytics through cookies. Each partner activity has its own data practices. A teacher should read the privacy policy for the chosen activity before students begin.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Run one Hour of AI activity and list what the AI did well and poorly.
2. Try an unplugged activity that sorts items and explain the rule.
3. Write a short definition of AI in your own words.
4. Test a tool and note one example of a wrong result.
5. Make a class list of questions about how AI works.

### social_studies
1. Discuss who could be treated unfairly by an AI tool and why.
2. Write three rules for responsible AI use in school.
3. Compare how two groups might view the same AI tool.
4. Research an AI story in the news and describe its impact.
5. Role play a town meeting about whether to use AI in a public service.

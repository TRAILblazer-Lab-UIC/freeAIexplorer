## Overview
Grounded is an introductory course in QGIS geographic information software for secondary students. Search results say the student manual targets Transition Year students and fits a Transition Unit. It has separate student and teacher manuals and YouTube video tutorials. No prior QGIS knowledge is needed.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn to make real maps on a computer.
- Students follow a student manual at their own pace.
- Students can watch video tutorials.

## Benefits for teachers
- A separate teacher manual is provided.
- No prior QGIS knowledge is needed.
- It was devised by Fiona Stapleton of QGIS School and funded by iCRAG and SFI.

## Benefits for schools
- The course is described as free of charge.
- QGIS runs offline on Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- IT will likely need to install QGIS.

## Where is the AI?
This course has no AI. It builds spatial thinking skills. Students layer map data and read patterns on a map. It runs on the local computer.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the internet is used only for setup. QGIS is a local desktop program and map projects stay on the device. The research could not reach the course pages. Every course fact comes from search snippets. It did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should read the manuals before class and choose map data that suits the age of students.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Make a map of the school neighborhood with several layers.
2. Add points for local landmarks and label them.
3. Compare two maps of the same area from different years.
4. Use a map to answer a question about where people live.
5. Present a map and explain each layer.

### science
1. Map local rock types or soil from sample data.
2. Layer a river map over a land height map.
3. Plot field sample sites on a map.
4. Compare a map of weather data across regions.
5. Write a short note about what the map shows about the land.

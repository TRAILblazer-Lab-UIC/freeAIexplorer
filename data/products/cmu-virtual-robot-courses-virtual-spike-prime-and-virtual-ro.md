## Overview
These are browser courses with a simulated robot. A class needs no hardware. Coding and Computational Thinking with a Virtual Robot has 9 units. Virtual SPIKE Prime has 8 units, and RoboCamp with Virtual SPIKE Prime is a shorter introduction. Students in grades 3 through 12 use block coding in over 70 virtual environments.

## Benefits for students
- Students code a simulated robot with block programming.
- Students try design challenges in many virtual environments.
- Students can repeat a challenge as many times as they like.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teacher groups and progress tracking are built into CS2N.
- Unused seats do not expire.
- Chromebooks are supported and Chrome is recommended.

## Benefits for schools
- No robot kits are needed.
- Licenses are one seat per learner and are sold through the CS2N store or by purchase order.
- The index page lists no prices. A 2021 announcement for a related course listed 9 USD or 12 USD per seat.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in these courses. The simulated robot runs in the browser and follows the blocks that students place. Students build skills in sequencing, sensors and logic.

## Privacy and safety
Every student needs a CS2N account, and the internet is needed throughout. CS2N stores the name, email, username, group membership, course activity, saved code, and progress. The policy says it does not sell or rent personal information. It allows use by children under 13 with consent from a parent, school, district, or teacher. That is a data rule and not a minimum age. It does not name COPPA or say where data is stored. A teacher should read the CS2N privacy policy before assigning seats.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a block program that moves the virtual robot to a target.
2. Use a loop to shorten a repeated movement.
3. Read the sensor values on the screen and use them to stop the robot.
4. Compare two programs for one challenge and pick the better one.
5. Explain a finished program to a partner using the correct terms.

### engineering
1. Solve a design challenge in one of the virtual environments.
2. Test a plan in two environments and record the results.
3. Sketch a plan before coding and compare it to the final program.
4. Improve a failed run and describe each change.
5. Share the best solution with the class and explain the steps.

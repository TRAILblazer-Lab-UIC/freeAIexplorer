## Overview
This CS Unplugged topic teaches searching algorithms through games. The page lists How many guesses? and Divide and conquer for ages 5-7. It adds The Great Number Hunt for ages 8-10. The classic Battleships activity teaches linear search, binary search, and hashing. Students play the games with paper and pens.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn why some ways of searching find an answer faster.
- Students play guessing games that use numbers and sorted lists.
- Students see how computers find data using ideas such as binary search.

## Benefits for teachers
- The topic lists literacy integrations such as Where is my hat? and Biography.
- The page covers more than one age range.
- A search result lists 30 to 45 minutes for the classic version.

## Benefits for schools
- The activities are free and open source.
- They need only paper, pens, and optional numbered cards.
- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Where is the AI?
This topic has no AI. It builds an understanding of data and search. Students learn that a sorted list lets a computer find an item in far fewer guesses.

## Privacy and safety
Everything runs on paper and no accounts were seen. The page does not state a privacy policy. The research found no student data collection. The internet is used to download the sheets. A teacher should check the game sheets and pick the version that suits the age of the class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play a guessing game and count how many guesses a linear search takes.
2. Repeat the game with a halving strategy and compare the counts.
3. Act out a search for a hidden card in a sorted row.
4. Explain to a partner why sorted lists help a search.
5. Play the classic Battleships game and describe the search steps you used.

### math
1. Guess a secret number and use higher or lower clues to narrow the range.
2. Record the number of guesses for ten rounds and find the average.
3. Order a set of number cards and then find one card quickly.
4. Compare searching a sorted row with searching a shuffled row.
5. Predict the best first guess for a range of 1 to 100.

### ela
1. Act out the Where is my hat? story and describe the search steps.
2. Write a short biography and search it for a key fact.
3. Retell a story and list the places searched in order.
4. Write a clue-based riddle that narrows down an answer.
5. Present a search strategy to the class in a short talk.

## Overview
Inkscape is a free and open-source vector graphics editor that uses SVG as its main format. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux. It is used for logos, illustrations, and diagrams. A Raspberry Pi Foundation Year 8 media unit is tailored to Inkscape and has students design an illustration, logo, or icon set.

## Benefits for students
- Students draw shapes and paths that stay sharp at any size.
- Students learn skills used in real design work.
- Students can import and export PDF, EPS, and PNG files.

## Benefits for teachers
- The Raspberry Pi unit has lessons from Get into shapes to Showcase.
- The unit resources can be adapted to other vector editors.
- Trace Bitmap turns images into vectors.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free.
- It runs offline on Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- It is not confirmed as a Chromebook app. School IT may need to approve the installer.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download Inkscape from inkscape.org.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the installer.
- [ ] Open the app and draw a shape.
- [ ] Try the Raspberry Pi Media vector graphics unit.
- [ ] Practice with paths and multiple objects.
- [ ] Decide where students save SVG files.
- [ ] Test the tool on one computer first.

## Where is the AI?
Inkscape has no AI. It builds design skills. Students combine shapes, paths, and color to make clear images. It runs on the local computer.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used for setup. The desktop editor works on local files and has no accounts. The inkscape.org pages returned errors during the research. No privacy policy was read. Telemetry status was not confirmed from a primary page. A teacher should choose a folder for student files and review the content of any images students import.

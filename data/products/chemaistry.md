## Overview
ChemAIstry is an online tool that teaches K-8 students how machine learning models are trained. Students choose everyday items they think are safe to bring into a chemistry lab. Their choices train a decision tree classifier that then sorts new items. It was tested with 40 students aged 7 to 14 at a public K-8 school.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how their own choices become training data.
- Students watch a model sort new items.
- Students talk about lab safety.

## Benefits for teachers
- The tool opens in a browser with no login.
- Training is nearly instant.
- It supports a short lesson on how models learn.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free to use online.
- The code is open.
- The page asks for a first name and grade. Code suggests results stay in the browser.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a decision tree classifier. It trains in the browser on the student's own choices. Students pick items, watch the model learn, and then see how it sorts new items. They use the results to talk about training data and safety.

## Privacy and safety
No login is needed, and the internet is needed throughout. The page asks for a first name, a grade level, and the items chosen. It shows no privacy notice, and the research found no privacy policy. Code in the repository suggests results stay in the browser's local storage, but this is an inference and not a vendor statement. The research version recorded student selections and made screen and audio recordings of study sessions. The repository still holds scripts that save data files. A teacher should tell students to use a nickname. A teacher should also clear the browser data on shared computers.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Pick items for the lab and watch the tree form.
2. Compare your tree with a classmate's tree.
3. Add an item that confuses the model and explain why.
4. Change one training choice and see how the tree changes.
5. Write a sentence that explains what training data is.

### science
1. Decide which household items are safe in a chemistry lab and give a reason.
2. Make a class list of lab safety rules.
3. Test the model on items such as a candy bar and glass jar.
4. Discuss why a model can be wrong about safety.
5. Write a safety poster that uses the model's best rules.

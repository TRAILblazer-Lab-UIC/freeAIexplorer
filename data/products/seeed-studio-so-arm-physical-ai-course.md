## Overview
This free course has four stages and 22 chapters built around the open source SO-ARM100 and SO-ARM101 robot arms. It starts with equipment and assembly, then moves to imitation learning with LeRobot and vision-language-action models. It suits advanced high school students and teachers. Students build the arms, record demonstrations, and train robot policies.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and calibrate a real leader and follower robot arm.
- Students record their own demonstrations and train a policy from them.
- Students explore advanced ideas such as vision-language-action models.

## Benefits for teachers
- The course gives a clear path from basics to training in 22 chapters.
- It is offered in English, Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, and Portuguese.
- Stage 4 has a no-code SenseCraft path for teachers who want a gentler option.

## Benefits for schools
- The course is free, but the arm hardware must be bought or built.
- The materials do not give kit prices.
- The GR00T chapter implies a training GPU, and IT will likely need to help.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Seeed Studio SO-ARM Physical AI Course on the Seeed site.
- [ ] Read the course introduction and Chapter 2 on the SO-ARM hardware.
- [ ] Buy or build a SO-ARM100 or SO-ARM101 leader and follower pair.
- [ ] Ask IT to help set up the Seeed fork of LeRobot on a Windows or Linux computer.
- [ ] Work through Stage 2 to assemble the arms and control the servos.
- [ ] Record a few demonstrations in Stage 3 before any training.
- [ ] Plan GPU access before starting the GR00T stage.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a set of robot policies. Stage 3 trains imitation learning policies with LeRobot, and Stage 4 covers vision-language-action models with Isaac GR00T. The vendor does not say where training runs in every chapter. Students show the arm what to do and then watch it repeat and extend the task.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether an account is needed or where data goes. A teacher should check which tools and accounts students need, including the GR00T tools and SenseCraft. Camera recordings of demonstrations may include faces. A teacher should decide where cameras point. The course text is not open licensed.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Read the Stage 3 chapters and explain imitation learning in plain words.
2. Record twenty demonstrations of a pick and place task and train a policy.
3. Compare a policy trained on five demonstrations with one trained on twenty.
4. Try the no-code SenseCraft path and compare it with the LeRobot path.
5. Write a short summary of what a vision-language-action model does.

### engineering
1. Assemble the follower arm following the Stage 2 chapters.
2. Calibrate the servos and record how far each joint moves.
3. Teleoperate the follower with the leader arm and time a simple task.
4. Redesign a gripper idea and describe how it would help the task.
5. Keep a build log of problems and how the team fixed each one.

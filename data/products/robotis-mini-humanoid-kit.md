## Overview
ROBOTIS MINI is a small programmable humanoid robot kit that students assemble from servo and frame parts. It uses 16 DYNAMIXEL XL-320 servos and an OpenCM9.04 controller. Students control it from a phone app or program motions on a computer with RoboPlus software, R+ Block, or the Arduino IDE. Retail listings give a grade level of 7 to 12 and age 12 and up.

## Benefits for students
- Students assemble a humanoid robot from parts.
- Students control it by phone with touch, gesture, and remote controls.
- Students program motions with blocks or Arduino code.

## Benefits for teachers
- The kit has a rechargeable battery that charges over USB.
- It comes with an M2 screwdriver for assembly.
- Teachers can start with the phone app and add programming later.

## Benefits for schools
- Retail listings range from about 456 to 560 USD, and RobotLAB starts at 499.
- The e-Manual notes that the servo gears wear over time.
- No independent classroom review was found, and IT may need to install the Windows software.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the ROBOTIS MINI e-Manual on the ROBOTIS website.
- [ ] Assemble the robot with the included M2 screwdriver.
- [ ] Charge the battery over USB.
- [ ] Pair the BT-210 Bluetooth module.
- [ ] Install the phone app or the RoboPlus software on a Windows computer.
- [ ] Check the e-Manual for iOS needs. It says iOS requires the BT-410 adapter.
- [ ] Try a first motion from the phone app.
- [ ] Program one motion in R+ Block.

## Where is the AI?
This kit has no AI. Students program fixed motions and tasks. They build skills in sequencing and logic as they combine motions into a routine.

## Privacy and safety
The kit needs no account, and the internet is used at setup. Programs run on the robot and the computer. The robot pairs with a phone or computer over local Bluetooth. The phone app has a voice recognition feature, and the research did not check how it handles data. No privacy policy for the app was found. A teacher should check the app's permissions and voice settings before students install it.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Assemble the frame and servos by following the e-Manual.
2. Test how each servo moves the robot's limbs.
3. Compare the robot's body with a human body.
4. Find a part that wears over time and suggest a fix.
5. Document the build in a short class guide.

### computer_science
1. Control the robot from the phone app and list the commands.
2. Program a motion in R+ Block.
3. Combine three motions into a short routine.
4. Change one value and predict the new motion.
5. Try a simple Arduino sketch for the controller.

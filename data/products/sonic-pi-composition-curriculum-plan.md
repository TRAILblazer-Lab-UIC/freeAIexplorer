## Overview
This is a six-lesson unit called Algorithmic Composition with Sonic Pi by Chris Petrie, last updated in June 2018. It was designed for Year 8 students in New Zealand and developed as part of a master's thesis. Students write music by programming and compose for short films on topics such as climate change and ocean plastic. The unit has a group project and an individual project.

## Benefits for students
- Students compose music with algorithms.
- Students score short films about real topics.
- Students complete both a group and an individual project.

## Benefits for teachers
- The unit has six lessons with a clear order.
- Lessons cover scales, samples, algorithms, synthesisers, and effects.
- The author's site holds both sources.

## Benefits for schools
- The unit is free.
- Preparation is 5 to 10 minutes per lesson. Lesson 6 may need over an hour.
- IT must install Sonic Pi and may need to supply video editing software.

- [ ] Start with Lesson 1, Getting comfortable.

## Where is the AI?
This unit has no AI. Students write algorithms that make music. They build pattern skills as they use loops, rules, and randomness.

## Privacy and safety
Sonic Pi runs locally, and no account is needed for it. The unit needs computers with internet access. It uses Google Drive and Google Docs for handouts, YouTube for videos and GitHub Gists for syntax references. The unit says nothing about student accounts for those services. A teacher should check which of those services students will use. A teacher should also check which video software students use and where films are saved.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Write a scale in code and play it, as in the second lesson.
2. Compose a short piece with samples and a synthesiser.
3. Add effects and compare the sound before and after.
4. Write an algorithm that builds a melody.
5. Perform a composition for the class.

### computer_science
1. Write a loop that repeats a pattern of notes.
2. Use a rule to choose the next note.
3. Add randomness and compare two runs.
4. Fix a bug in a program that plays out of order.
5. Explain how an algorithm makes music.

### theater_media
1. Compose music for a short film about climate change.
2. Match the music to the mood of each scene.
3. Edit the film and add the soundtrack.
4. Write a short note about the film for an audience.
5. Present the film and explain the sound choices.

## Overview
This is a master's thesis by Nada Hussein, advised by Cynthia Breazeal, at MIT in September 2021. It describes a middle school curriculum on how humans and machines perceive sound. The thesis builds a music audition extension for Scratch that uses Google Magenta. Students can create and analyze their own music.

## Benefits for students
- Students compare human hearing with machine hearing.
- Students make music with Scratch and Magenta.
- A pilot reported better understanding of signal properties.

## Benefits for teachers
- The thesis describes a full unit and its pilot.
- Teachers can borrow ideas for lessons on sound.
- The thesis is free to read.

## Benefits for schools

## Where is the AI?
The AI is Google Magenta, which generates music or accompanies student music, and the unit studies machine audition. The materials do not say where it runs. Students create music, analyze it, and compare how a machine and a person perceive sound.

## Privacy and safety
This is a research thesis. The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. The research did not confirm where the Scratch extension and the Magenta models run. The availability of the extension is not confirmed. Only the thesis record page was read. A teacher should check whether the extension needs a login and whether audio leaves the device.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Compose a short melody and compare how a person and a machine describe it.
2. Ask Magenta to accompany a melody and judge how well it fits.
3. Change one note and discuss how the machine responds.
4. Record a favorite rhythm and describe it in words a machine could use.
5. Present a short talk on how the accompaniment felt to a listener.

### computer_science
1. Build a Scratch project that reacts to a sound.
2. Explain in steps how a machine turns sound into numbers.
3. Compare the machine's analysis of two melodies.
4. Test where the machine's analysis differs from human hearing.
5. Write a short guide to the blocks in the music extension.

### science
1. Describe pitch and volume as signal properties of a sound.
2. Record a sound and sketch what its signal might look like.
3. Compare how two instruments differ in pitch and loudness.
4. Test how distance changes loudness and record the results.
5. Explain why a machine and an ear may hear the same sound differently.

## Overview
Marty is a walking robot from Robotical, an Edinburgh company. Students progress from unplugged commands through the Marty Controller, Blocks Jr, Scratch-based Blocks, and Python. The site places Marty in two bands. Ages 4 to 11 cover introductory coding. Ages 12 to 18 and up cover advanced coding, hardware expansion, and AI and machine learning.

## Benefits for students
- Young students start with no screen.
- Students can climb from simple blocks to Python on the same robot.
- Older students can explore AI and machine learning.

## Benefits for teachers
- The Robotical App is free and runs on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, iPads, and Android tablets.
- Free lesson plans and a knowledge base are provided.
- Schools can request a free two-week trial and free teacher training, per a search snippet.

## Benefits for schools
- The site shows no single price. A ten-robot bundle was 3999 USD per a search snippet.
- The code is open and 3D printing files are provided.
- The robot pairs by Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.

## Getting started
- [ ] Visit the Robotical website and read about the two age bands.

- [ ] Open the Robotical App or the web app.

## Where is the AI?
AI is optional and the vendor does not describe which AI tools exist. The site says older students can explore AI and machine learning. Without that the robot has no AI, but students still build sequencing and logic skills. The vendor does not say where any AI runs.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and most activities work without registering. The research did not confirm whether the internet is needed after setup. Settings and projects stay on the device. Optional cloud saves can be loaded by anyone with the link. Users are told to avoid student personal information. Analytics need consent and can include device and robot identifiers. Service providers include Google Firebase, Amazon Web Services, Slack, Sentry, and Zoho, with possible processing in the EEA and United States. Warranty registration collects email, school name, and robot serial number. The policy has no COPPA statement. A teacher should turn off analytics, avoid cloud saves with student details, and test pairing on school devices.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Use unplugged commands to plan a walk, then check it on the robot.
2. Build a Blocks Jr program that makes Marty dance.
3. Move the dance into Scratch-based Blocks and add a repeat.
4. Write a short Python program to make Marty walk and turn.
5. Compare the four coding levels and say which you prefer.

### engineering
1. Watch Marty walk and describe how its legs move.
2. Test how the floor surface changes the walk.
3. Print a 3D part from the provided files and fit it.
4. Add a sensor if you have one and test it.
5. Plan a course that Marty must finish.

## Overview
This teacher resource from the University of Manchester uses hexapawn to show reinforcement learning. Martin Gardner invented the game. Search results describe a physical learning machine made of drawers, one per board position. They also describe a class variant in which students act as the machine. ## Where is the AI?
The resource has no running AI program. It shows reinforcement learning with a physical machine. Each drawer stands for a board position and holds the choices for that position. Students watch the machine improve as losing moves are removed.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Act as the learning machine and pick moves from a drawer of choices.
2. Remove a losing move after each loss and record the change.
3. Play several games and describe how the machine improves.
4. Explain why each board position needs its own drawer.
5. Compare this machine with a computer program that learns from experience.

### math
1. Play hexapawn against a partner and look for a winning strategy.
2. Count how many board positions the machine must cover.
3. Draw the first three moves of a game as a tree.
4. Track wins and losses over ten games and find the share of wins.
5. Explain why the number of drawers differs for black and for white.

## Overview
Guess My Passcode is a game-based lesson on AI and backpropagation. It was written by Evan Shieh, Princewill Okoroafor, and Thema Monroe-White of the Young Data Scientists League. Students try to guess the passcode to their teacher's special phone. It was tested for grades 6 and above and lines up with Common Core math for grades 4 to 6.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how a model improves by correcting its guesses.
- Students meet gradient descent and the update rule through play.
- The page says no programming experience is needed.

## Benefits for teachers
- The materials include a lesson plan, sample slides, and extra credit challenges.
- The game is a Python notebook that the teacher can open in Colab.
- The page notes the game takes upfront time to learn.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free, and the authors used the Colab free tier.
- It runs in a web browser and needs the internet.
- The game may not fit periods shorter than 50 minutes.

## Where is the AI?
The AI idea is backpropagation, which is how a neural network adjusts after each wrong answer. The game runs in a Python notebook on a cloud service such as Colab. Students make guesses, see the error, and update their next guess. The lesson shows the same steps a model takes when it learns.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether accounts are needed or where student data goes. The game runs in a Python notebook on a cloud service. The authors tested it on the free tier of Google Colab, which suggests a Google account. The research did not review Colab data practices. The internet is needed throughout. A teacher should check whether students need a Google account and review the school's rules for Colab.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Use the lesson's elementary math to guess a passcode and record each error.
2. Compare two guesses and say which one moved closer to the target.
3. Chart the error after each round and describe the pattern.
4. Explain the update rule in plain words using a number example.
5. Try an extra credit challenge and explain the answer to a partner.

### computer_science
1. Play the game and describe how backpropagation changes a guess.
2. Explain gradient descent as a ball rolling downhill.
3. Change one setting in the notebook and note how the results change.
4. Draw a diagram of the steps in one round of learning.
5. Compare the game to how a neural network trains on data.

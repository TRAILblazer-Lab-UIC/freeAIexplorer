## Overview
The Makeblock AI Camera 2.0 is a vision module for the mBot2 robot and CyberPi. It has nine built-in AI functions, which cover face, color, gesture, tag, object, path, posture, text, and voice. Students code it with mBlock 5 in blocks or Python. They can also train custom models on the mTraining web platform.

## Benefits for students
- Students use nine ready AI functions in robot projects.
- Students can train their own model on mTraining.
- Students code in blocks first and can move to Python.

## Benefits for teachers
- The product page lists nine project-based lessons.
- The module has a 2.4 inch touchscreen and a 12 MP camera.
- It includes a 32 GB microSD card.

## Benefits for schools
- The module alone costs $129.99. The mBot2 AI Learning Kit costs $279.99.
- It needs a CyberPi or mBot2. It does not work as a generic USB webcam for PCs.
- Inference runs on the device and the internet is needed only at setup.

## Getting started
- [ ] Buy the AI Camera 2.0 or the mBot2 AI Learning Kit from Makeblock.
- [ ] Attach the module to the mBot2 or CyberPi through the mBuild port.
- [ ] Install mBlock 5 or open it in a browser.
- [ ] Try a built-in function such as face recognition.
- [ ] Follow the first project-based lesson.
- [ ] Ask IT to review the mTraining terms before students train models.
- [ ] Train a custom model with a small set of images.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is nine pre-trained functions that run on the camera. It uses a RISC-V processor with a 1 TOPS accelerator. Custom model training uses the mTraining web platform in the vendor's cloud. Students pick a function, code a reaction, and train their own model to extend the work.

## Privacy and safety
An account is optional and the internet is needed at setup. Inference runs on the device. Custom training uses the mTraining web platform, which likely receives uploaded images. The research did not confirm this, and it did not read the mTraining or mBlock data handling. The Makeblock store policy says it does not directly or knowingly collect personal data from children under 13. That is a data rule and not an age limit. Vendors for hosting, analytics, payments, and support receive data, including Shopify, Azure, and Google Analytics. The policy covers the store and accounts only. A teacher should check the mTraining terms and keep faces out of student training images.

## Overview
Unplugging Artificial Intelligence is a brochure by Annabel Lindner and Stefan Seegerer. It has five unplugged activities on AI. They are Classification with Decision Trees, #deeplearning, Reinforcement Learning, Back to the Roots, and The Turing Test. It builds on CS Unplugged and needs no computer.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn AI ideas by playing paper games.
- Students try the Good-Monkey-Bad-Monkey decision tree game.
- Students explore reinforcement learning and the Turing test.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each activity lists a target group, the big ideas, materials, and steps.
- The brochure covers primary and secondary school.
- Print templates are on aiunplugged.org.

## Benefits for schools
- The brochure is a free PDF in English and German.
- The activities need no computers.
- No accounts are needed and no student data is collected.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Unplugging Artificial Intelligence brochure and download it.
- [ ] Download the templates from aiunplugged.org.
- [ ] Choose one of the five activities.
- [ ] Cut out the monkey cards for the decision tree game.
- [ ] Prepare a board with magnets or a pinboard.
- [ ] Read the steps and the big ideas for the activity.
- [ ] Run the activity with a small group first.

## Where is the AI?
The activities have no software and no AI runs on any device. They teach ideas such as decision trees, training and test data, deep learning, reinforcement learning, and the Turing test. Students build skills in classification and in learning from rewards.

## Privacy and safety
The activities are on paper and no account is needed. The research found no student data collection. The internet is used to download the brochure and the print templates on aiunplugged.org, which the research did not fetch. A teacher should check that the chosen activity fits the age of the students. The authors' email is on the brochure for questions.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play the Good-Monkey-Bad-Monkey game and build a decision tree from the training cards.
2. Test the tree on the test cards and count how many it gets right.
3. Act out reinforcement learning by rewarding a partner for good moves.
4. Try the Turing test game and decide which answers came from a machine.
5. Explain in a short talk the difference between training data and test data.

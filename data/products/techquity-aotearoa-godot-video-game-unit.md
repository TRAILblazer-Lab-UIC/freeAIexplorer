## Overview
This Level 1 NCEA digital technologies unit comes from a New Zealand teacher group in Otautahi Christchurch. Students learn Godot 4 and the Piskel pixel art editor. They then design, develop, and program their own games. The unit runs about 12 weeks in three 4 week sprints. It covers GDScript programming and a design portfolio.

## Benefits for students
- Students make a complete game from design to code.
- Students make their own pixel art in Piskel.
- Students can show exemplar student games as models for their own.

## Benefits for teachers
- The site links tutorials, assessment standards, and exemplar games.
- Teachers set the timing of the sprints.
- Optional nationwide game jams run on itch.io.

## Benefits for schools
- The site shows no price and Godot is free, per a search snippet.
- The unit is aligned to New Zealand NCEA assessments and may need changes for US classes.
- Students install Godot 4, and IT help is likely. The page tells Godot 3.5 users to find other resources.

## Where is the AI?
The unit has no AI. Students write GDScript, a text language, to control game objects. They practice logic, debugging, and design as they plan how a game responds to a player.

## Privacy and safety
Godot runs on the device and no account is needed for the core work. Accounts are optional. The page mentions itch.io for playtesting, which a school may not allow. The materials do not state how much internet access the unit needs or what data it collects. The research did not review how Godot or Piskel handle data. Check whether students will post games on itch.io. Review any shared game before it is posted.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow the Basic Video Game Skills tutorials and build a moving character.
2. Write GDScript that adds a score when the player collects an item.
3. Plan a game in a design portfolio before you code it.
4. Test a classmate's game and report one bug.
5. Finish a Level 1 game project across the three sprints.

### visual_arts
1. Draw a character in Piskel using a limited set of colors.
2. Animate a character with a few frames.
3. Design a game background in pixel art.
4. Create an icon for your game.
5. Compare your art with the exemplar games and note what to improve.

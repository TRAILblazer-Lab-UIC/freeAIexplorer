## Overview
AI Unlocked is a five-part video series with lesson plans for grades 6 to 12. Each plan runs about 50 minutes. Students think, pair, and share, watch a video with pauses for talk, and finish with an exit ticket. Other lessons cover ethical AI use, recognizing AI generated content, evaluating AI tools, and finding facts with AI search.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn what generative AI is in plain terms.
- Students discuss bias and misinformation.
- Students practice talking in pairs and groups.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each plan includes pause points in the video.
- Lessons list Common Core ELA, ISTE 1.2 and 1.3, and suggested NGSS standards.
- Printable lesson plan PDFs are on Google Drive.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons are free.
- They need a screen and internet to show the video.
- No IT work is needed and no accounts are required.

## Where is the AI?
No AI tool runs in these lessons. They teach about generative AI, bias, misinformation, and how to recognize AI generated content. Students watch, talk, and write, and they build the skill of questioning what AI produces.

## Privacy and safety
The lessons are video and discussion, and the internet is needed to show the video. The research documented no account requirement and no student data collection. The printable PDF sits on Google Drive. The research did not confirm whether any lesson needs a live AI tool. A teacher should preview each video and check that the school allows the video host and Google Drive.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Think, pair, and share about a time you saw AI in daily life.
2. Write an exit ticket on one benefit and one challenge of AI.
3. Discuss a video clip at each pause and record a main idea.
4. Write a short claim about whether AI helps writers and support it with evidence.
5. Compare two sources on generative AI and note which seems more reliable.

### library_media
1. List signs of misinformation in a sample post.
2. Discuss how bias can enter an AI tool and how to spot it.
3. Check an AI claim against two trusted sources.
4. Make a poster that shows steps for checking AI output.
5. Practice finding facts with AI search and record where you checked them.

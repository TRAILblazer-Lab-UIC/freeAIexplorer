## Overview
Facing Off with Facial Recognition is a short Common Sense Education lesson. Students learn what facial recognition is and then take a position on a school lunch-line scenario. The collection lists it for grade 8, and some pages list grades 6 to 12. It takes about 20 minutes and uses slides, a student handout, and a teacher handout.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn the words artificial intelligence, facial recognition, and privacy.
- Students practice weighing the benefits and risks of a technology.
- Students defend a position with reasons in a low-stakes school scenario.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson fits in one short class period.
- A teacher handout and slides guide the whole discussion.
- Teachers need no technical background.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free with a free teacher sign-in.
- Students need no devices. The teacher projects the slides.
- IT has no work to do.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson is a discussion and does not run an AI tool. Students talk about how facial recognition could work in a school lunch line. They think about privacy and about what the system would collect. The lesson builds the skill of spotting how AI uses data about people.

## Privacy and safety
Students do not need accounts. Teachers sign in for free, and the lesson page needs an internet connection. The materials do not describe any student data collection. A teacher should preview the scenario and check that students feel comfortable discussing their own images.

## Ideas by subject
### library_media
1. Use the lunch-line scenario to start a talk about what personal data schools collect.
2. Write a short note that explains facial recognition to a younger student.
3. Make a poster of three questions to ask before a school adopts a new tool.
4. Compare two news articles about facial recognition and list their main claims.
5. Create a vocabulary card for privacy with an example from school life.

### social_studies
1. Take a side on whether the school should use facial recognition in the lunch line and explain why.
2. Hold a class vote and then discuss how a community should decide on new technology.
3. List who is helped and who is at risk in the lunch-line plan.
4. Draft a short school policy about cameras and student images.
5. Interview a family member about when they feel comfortable being scanned and share the results.

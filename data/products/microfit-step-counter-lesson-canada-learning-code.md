## Overview
This lesson by Jen Perry for Canada Learning Code is for ages 7 and up and takes about three hours. Part one is an unplugged Code.org variables activity. Part two has students program a micro:bit that adds one to a steps variable on each shake. Part three has students design a step counter strap from craft materials.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn what a variable is before they code one.
- Students program a working step counter.
- Students design a wearable strap with craft materials.

## Benefits for teachers
- The three parts mix unplugged, coding, and making.
- The lesson lists links to Canadian physical education and technology curriculum connections.
- Students should know Scratch or Blockly first, which sets a clear starting point.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- Each student needs a micro:bit, and the strap needs craft materials such as fabric, tape, cardboard, and elastic.
- A teacher should know the micro:bit, and IT help is limited.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds the skill of using a variable, which is a named place that stores a number. Students also practice using a sensor input, the shake.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The pages read do not discuss data handling. The step count lives in a variable on the board. The internet is used at setup. The research read no privacy policy for this item. A teacher should check the MakeCode privacy terms and that the boards pair with the school computers before class.

## Ideas by subject
### health_pe
1. Walk a set distance while wearing the step counter and record the count.
2. Compare the micro:bit count with a hand count and discuss why they differ.
3. Design a strap that holds the board in a safe place on the body.
4. Set a class step goal and track progress over a week.
5. Graph the steps from different activities and write about what they show.

### computer_science
1. Play the unplugged variables activity and explain what a variable stores.
2. Program a variable that adds one each time the board shakes.
3. Show the steps number on the display.
4. Test the program with ten shakes and check the total.
5. Add a reset button that sets the steps back to zero.

## Overview
Bee-Bot is a small floor robot for young learners. Children press arrow buttons on its back and then press GO to run the sequence. It moves forward, moves back, and turns left or right. Students in kindergarten through grade 2 use it on grid mats.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn to plan a route and test it.
- Students practice debugging when the robot misses its goal.
- Students work with no screen and no reading demands.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers use it for sequencing, math and literacy.
- No app or account is needed.
- Many lesson plans exist, including some from SFUSD and the Digital Technologies Hub.

## Benefits for schools
- Terrapin lists the robot at 99.95 USD. Class packs and mats cost extra.
- The Terrapin page lists no app or account for the robot.
- A grid made from butcher paper with 6 inch squares can replace a mat.

## Getting started
- [ ] Buy Bee-Bot from Terrapin or another seller.
- [ ] Charge the robot.
- [ ] Set out a floor mat or a butcher paper grid with 6 inch squares.
- [ ] Try the free online Bee-Bot emulator to practice.
- [ ] Place story cards or a number line on the mat.
- [ ] Show the class how to clear and press GO.
- [ ] Let pairs plan and test a short route.

## Where is the AI?
Bee-Bot has no AI. It follows exactly the steps that children enter and nothing more. Students build skills in sequencing and debugging.

## Privacy and safety
The robot has buttons only and the Terrapin page lists no app or account for it. No account is needed and the research found no student data collection. The research reviewed no privacy policy for the physical robot. A teacher should check that the mat or grid is flat and clear of loose items.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Plan a route on the mat and use words such as algorithm and debug to describe it.
2. Predict where the robot will stop and then test the guess.
3. Fix a route that misses the target and tell a partner what changed.
4. Write a route on paper with arrows before entering it.
5. Use the online emulator to practice a route before using the robot.

### math
1. Use a number line on the mat to add by moving the robot forward.
2. Subtract by moving the robot back along the number line.
3. Count the squares the robot crosses on a route.
4. Predict the number of steps to reach a goal and then check.
5. Make a route that covers a set number of squares.

### ela
1. Place story cards on the mat and move the robot through the story in order.
2. Retell a familiar book by sending the robot to each scene.
3. Place word cards on the mat and drive the robot to nouns or verbs.
4. Create a new story ending and program the robot to act it out.
5. Order a set of event cards and program the robot to visit them.

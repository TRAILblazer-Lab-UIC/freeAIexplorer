## Overview
This 2024 resource pack by Nik Peachey and Ross Crichton has 43 activities for English language teachers. It is a PDF of about 40 pages and is organised into ten areas of AI use. The activities use general chat tools, and the pack names Gemini, ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity, and Poe. It is aimed at English language teaching and not at K-12 in particular.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice English with an AI practice partner.
- Students get models and examples of language.
- Students work with materials adapted to their level.

## Benefits for teachers
- The pack has guiding principles and tips for writing prompts.
- It covers privacy and age restrictions.
- It includes activities for teacher development.

## Benefits for schools
- The PDF is free to download.
- The AI tools named have their own free and paid tiers.
- No install is needed for the pack itself.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a general chat tool such as Gemini, ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity, or Poe. These run in the vendor's cloud. Teachers use them to build models, activities, and practice for learners. Students then use the results to extend their own language practice.

## Privacy and safety
The PDF download needs no account, and the pack collects no student data itself. The activities use chat tools such as Gemini, ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity, and Poe. Prompts go to those third party companies, and the internet is needed all the time. The pack warns teachers not to enter identifiable personal data and to check each tool's terms. It notes that most registration platforms bar users under 13 and often require parental consent for ages 13 to 18. The pack is aimed mainly at teachers using AI for preparation. A teacher should check each tool's age rule and the school policy before students use one.

## Ideas by subject
### world_languages
1. Ask a chat tool for a short model dialogue and mark any odd phrases.
2. Practice a conversation with the chat tool as a partner and note useful words.
3. Compare an AI written paragraph with a student paragraph.
4. Write a prompt that asks for a level-appropriate reading text.
5. Evaluate an AI answer for accuracy and correct it.

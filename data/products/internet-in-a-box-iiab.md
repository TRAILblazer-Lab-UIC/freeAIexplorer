## Overview
Internet-in-a-Box is a portable offline server that creates a local Wi-Fi hotspot serving stored content to nearby phones, tablets, and laptops. It runs on a Raspberry Pi or a Linux PC and offers almost 40 apps, including Kolibri, Moodle, Nextcloud, Sugarizer, and WordPress. Content packs come from Kiwix, OER2Go, and Archive.org. It grew out of the One Laptop per Child school server.

## Benefits for students
- Students read and learn without an internet connection.
- Students use phones, tablets, and laptops with only Wi-Fi and a browser.
- Students reach content libraries such as Kiwix.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers choose which apps and content packs to install.
- Teachers can serve one library to a whole room.
- A Columbia SIPA case study compares IIAB with RachelPLUS in Peru.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free. A recommended Raspberry Pi costs about 35 USD.
- Total cost can range up to about 500 USD, based on the listed range.
- A server admin must install and maintain it.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find Internet-in-a-Box at download.iiab.io.
- [ ] Plan hardware such as a Raspberry Pi 4, 5, 400, or 500 with at least 2 GB RAM and a microSD card.
- [ ] Flash the image to the card, or install on a Linux PC.
- [ ] Stay online during setup to choose apps and content packs.
- [ ] Change the default credentials on the apps you enable.
- [ ] Connect a test phone to the box over Wi-Fi.
- [ ] Review the data settings of each app before students use it.

## Where is the AI?
Internet-in-a-Box has no AI. It builds information access and library skills. Students find and read stored resources without the internet. It runs on a school server.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are optional and depend on the installed apps. The FAQ says data stays on the local server, and Nextcloud stores students' files on the box. No privacy policy exists, and what student data an app collects depends on the app. The FAQ documents default credentials such as Admin with changeme. It also says the hotspot is generally open to all unless password protected. A teacher or admin should change the default passwords and review each app, such as Kolibri or Moodle, before students connect.

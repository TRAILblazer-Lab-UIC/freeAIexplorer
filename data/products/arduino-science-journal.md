## Overview
Arduino Science Journal is a free app that turns a phone or tablet into a science lab. Students record readings such as light, motion and sound. They graph the data, add notes and photos and export it. Extra sensors can connect over Bluetooth, such as Arduino boards.

## Benefits for students
- Students use a device they know to run experiments.
- Students graph data and add notes and photos.
- Students can link outside sensors over Bluetooth.

## Benefits for teachers
- The app aligns with NGSS and the UK National Curriculum.
- Learning materials are available from Arduino.
- A search result mentions an optional paid Teacher Plan, which is unconfirmed.

## Benefits for schools
- The app is free.
- It needs Android 5, iOS 12 or a Chromebook that runs Android apps.
- Teachers need to install it on student devices.

## Getting started
- [ ] Install the app from Google Play or the App Store.
- [ ] Open the Arduino learning materials.
- [ ] Try a light or sound experiment on the device.
- [ ] Pair a Bluetooth sensor board if wanted.
- [ ] Practice exporting data.
- [ ] Read the Arduino privacy policy.
- [ ] Plan where student data will be saved.

## Where is the AI?
The app has no AI. Students build skills in data and graphing. They collect readings, plot them and write what the data shows.

## Privacy and safety
No account is listed as required. Experiment data, notes, and photos are stored in the app. The product page says experiments can be saved locally or synced to Google Drive. Current Drive behavior is unclear. Arduino policy says it may analyze app usage and download location, except for Junior Account users. It collects device type, OS version, IP address, and timestamps and keeps identifying data for 30 days. A teacher should read the policy and avoid photos of students in notes.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Record the light level in the classroom at three times of day.
2. Measure sound at the playground and the library and compare.
3. Graph motion data from a rolling cart.
4. Add notes and photos to explain an experiment result.
5. Export the data and write a claim based on it.

### engineering

2. Test how well a phone sensor measures a known value.
3. Design an experiment with a sensor and a plan for the data.

## Overview
CRAFT stands for Classroom-Ready Resources About AI for Teaching. It is a co-design project of the Stanford Graduate School of Education and the Institute for Human-Centered AI. The library has free, adaptable AI literacy resources for high school. They range from short activities to multi-day lesson series.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn what AI is and how to question it.
- Students see AI in art, math, English, and history.
- Students join activities that range from 15 minutes to several days.

## Benefits for teachers
- Resources are ready to use and can be adapted.
- Lesson stacks give a plan for several days.
- A professional development page supports teachers who are new to AI.

## Benefits for schools
- The resources are free.
- They run in a web browser, including on Chromebooks.
- IT does not need to install anything.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Stanford CRAFT resource library on the Stanford GSE site.
- [ ] Browse the library and choose a lesson for your subject.
- [ ] Read the lesson and its teacher notes first.
- [ ] Check whether the lesson uses an outside tool and plan for it.
- [ ] Ask IT to confirm the outside tool is allowed.
- [ ] Adapt the activity to your class.
- [ ] Look at the professional development page for next steps.

## Where is the AI?
The materials teach about AI. They help students critique AI tools and understand how they work. Some lessons point to outside AI tools, which run on those companies' servers. Students use the lessons to question AI and to build their own judgment.

## Privacy and safety
The home page has a login link, and the research did not find what it is for. The materials do not state what data the site collects or which accounts students need. Some lessons use Observable notebooks in the browser, and some point to outside AI tools that run on those companies' servers. A teacher should check each outside tool and the school's data rules before students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Try a 15 minute activity that asks how an AI system learns from examples.
2. Critique an AI output and list what it got right and wrong.
3. Work through a lesson stack over several days and keep a learning journal.
4. Debate where AI should and should not be used in schools.
5. Gather class ideas on AI ethics from the gamified ethics lesson.

### ela
1. Read an AI written paragraph and mark parts that sound human or machine made.
2. Compare AI feedback on an essay with a peer's feedback.
3. Discuss who is the author of a text made with AI help.
4. Write a short reflection on how AI changes the way people write.
5. Revise an AI draft by hand and explain each change.

### social_studies
1. Study how AI is used to tell a history story and ask who made the choices.
2. Look for bias in an AI answer about a historical event.
3. Discuss a social science case where AI affects people differently.
4. Compare an AI summary with a primary source.
5. Write a class guide on trusting AI answers about history.

### library_media
1. Check an AI answer against two reliable sources.
2. Make a checklist for judging AI generated information.
3. Practice spotting the signs of weak or invented sources.
4. Write a short note that tells younger students how to question AI.
5. Collect examples of good and poor AI answers for a class display.

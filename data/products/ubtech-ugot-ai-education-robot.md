## Overview
uGOT is a modular 7-in-1 robotics kit from UBTECH for AI education. The main controller has a quad-core CPU, a 1 TOPS NPU, 2 GB of RAM, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth. UBTECH says it covers computer vision, speech, and robot kinematics. Students in grades 6 to 12 program it with uCode blocks, uPython, or the UGOT app.

## Benefits for students
- Students build different robots from one modular system.
- Students program with blocks or with Python.
- Students work with vision, speech, and movement in one kit.

## Benefits for teachers
- The kit works with Micro:bit, Arduino, and Raspberry Pi.
- A teacher can start with blocks and move to code later.
- The official page names in-school and institution classrooms as the use.

## Benefits for schools
- The official page gives no price.
- The battery lasts about 2.5 hours.
- The vendor does not say whether the internet is needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the uGOT page on the UBTECH site and ask the vendor for a price.
- [ ] Ask IT to review the app, the account needs, and the Wi-Fi use.
- [ ] Build a first robot from the modular parts.
- [ ] Charge the 11.1V battery and power the controller.
- [ ] Connect over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.
- [ ] Install the UGOT app on a phone or tablet.
- [ ] Start with uCode blocks before trying uPython.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is vision and speech recognition that runs on the controller's NPU, along with motion control. The vendor does not say clearly where each function runs. Students program the robot to see, hear, and move, and then extend its behavior with their own code.

## Privacy and safety
The research found no privacy policy. The page describes an onboard NPU and Wi-Fi but does not state where speech or vision processing happens. The materials do not state whether an account is needed or where data goes. Check which accounts students need and what the camera and microphones send. Check the privacy policy with IT before use.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Write uCode blocks that make the robot react to a face.
2. Program a voice command that starts a movement.
3. Move the same program from blocks to uPython and compare them.
4. Test how well speech recognition works in a noisy room.
5. Connect a Micro:bit and explain what each device does.

### engineering
1. Build a wheeled robot and a walking robot from the same parts.
2. Use the distance sensor to stop the robot before a wall.
3. Test the battery by timing one mission and noting the result.
4. Design a robot for a task, such as carrying a light object.
5. Compare how the modular parts lock together and suggest an improvement.

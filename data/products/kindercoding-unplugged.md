## Overview
Kindercoding Unplugged is a 184 page book by Deanna Pecaski McLennan, published by Redleaf Press in September 2020. It is written for ages 3 to 8. Each activity has a materials list, instructions, observations, and next steps. Examples include directing a figurine across a coding grid, designing Rube Goldberg machines, making ASCII bead bracelets, and sorting dice with a diagram.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn coding ideas with figurines, beads, dice, and blocks.
- Students connect computational thinking to art, literacy, math, and community.
- Students stay off screens.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each activity lists materials, instructions, observations, and next steps.
- A May/June 2021 NSTA review calls the activities engaging, very low cost, and easy to implement.
- The author is a full-day kindergarten teacher in Ontario, Canada.

## Where is the AI?
This book has no AI. It builds sequencing, algorithms, and binary thinking with hands-on materials. Students direct a figurine, string beads, and sort dice using clear rules.

## Privacy and safety
The activities are physical, and no student data is collected. No account and no internet are needed. A teacher should watch young children with small items such as beads. The vendor materials do not describe other safety steps.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Direct a wolf figurine across a coding grid to reach plastic fruit.
2. Write the steps for a partner to follow on the grid.
3. Fix a path that does not reach the fruit.
4. Build a simple Rube Goldberg machine and describe each step.
5. Sort dice with a diagram and explain the rule.

### math
1. Sort dice by number and record the groups.
2. String binary beads to show two states, such as on and off.
3. Count the steps in a path on a grid.
4. Compare two paths and say which is shorter.
5. Make a pattern with beads and extend it.

### visual_arts
1. Make an ASCII bead bracelet to connect binary and art.
2. Choose two bead colors to stand for 0 and 1.
3. Design a bracelet that spells an initial.
4. Compare bracelets and describe the patterns.
5. Share a bracelet and explain the code.

### ela
1. Sequence the parts of a story with picture cards.
2. Retell a story in order using beginning, middle, and end.
3. Tell a story about the wolf figurine crossing the grid.
4. Act out a story as a set of steps.
5. Fix the order of a story that is mixed up.

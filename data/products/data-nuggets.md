## Overview
Data Nuggets are free classroom activities that bring real research data into K-12 and college classes. Scientists and teachers designed them together. Students form hypotheses, visualize data, and make evidence-based claims. The site lists a searchable table, Digital Data Nuggets, DataVersify, and a Spanish collection.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with real data from real scientists.
- Students practice making claims with evidence.
- Students can read activities in English or Spanish.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers search the table and download an activity.
- Activities can be printed or shared.
- One example is A Tail of Two Scorpions about grasshopper mice.

## Benefits for schools
- Activities are free.
- Printed copies need no devices.
- Printed activities collect no student data. The digital versions were not reviewed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Data Nuggets website.
- [ ] Search the table by theme.
- [ ] Download an activity that fits your class.
- [ ] Print or share it with students.
- [ ] Try the Digital Data Nuggets for a screen version.
- [ ] Look at the Spanish collection for multilingual learners.
- [ ] Plan time for students to share their claims.

## Where is the AI?
This resource has no AI. It builds skills in data and pattern spotting. Students look at real numbers and decide what they show.

## Privacy and safety
The site did not mention accounts, and the activities are downloadable documents. The research did not review Digital Data Nuggets or DataVersify. Their accounts and data handling are unknown. No privacy policy was reviewed. The internet is needed to download the files. A teacher should check any linked tool before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Read the scorpion and grasshopper mice activity and state a hypothesis.
2. Graph the data from an activity and describe the pattern.
3. Write a claim and support it with two pieces of evidence.
4. Compare the claims of two groups and discuss the gap.
5. Ask a new question that the data could not answer.

### math
1. Draw a graph from a data table.
2. Compare the averages of two groups.
3. Describe a trend in a set of measurements.
4. Explain which graph type fits a data set best.
5. Use the data to make a prediction and check it.

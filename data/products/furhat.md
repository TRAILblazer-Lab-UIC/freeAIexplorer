## Overview
Furhat is a social robot with a customizable back-projected 3D face and multilingual voice synthesis. It is sold in research, development, education, and commercial packages. Applications are built with the Furhat SDK, which includes a virtual Furhat that runs on a desktop. University labs use it for human-robot interaction research and conversation practice.

## Benefits for students
- Students build dialogue skills that the robot speaks aloud.
- Students can customize the robot's face and voice.
- Students can practice a spoken language with the robot. One outreach activity used it for online French classes with ages 10 to 14.

## Benefits for teachers
- The free SDK has a virtual Furhat. A class may try it without the robot.
- FurhatAI Creator uses large language models to prototype conversations.
- A Realtime API lets advanced users plug in their own AI models.

## Benefits for schools
- The vendor site lists no prices. A 2023 academic brochure suggests a package near $27,000.
- The robot needs a network connection and an account.
- A reseller page noted that the premium edition may be unavailable. IT needs to plan the network setup.

## Getting started
- [ ] Request a trial or package from the Furhat vendor.
- [ ] Ask IT to review the account and network needs.
- [ ] Install the Furhat SDK on a Windows, Mac, or Linux desktop.
- [ ] Open the virtual Furhat.
- [ ] Build a short skill with the SDK.
- [ ] Try FurhatAI Creator to prototype a conversation.
- [ ] Connect a physical robot after IT confirms the network setup.

## Where is the AI?
The AI handles conversational dialogue, speech synthesis, and personalities driven by large language models. It runs on a mix of the robot hardware and the vendor's cloud. Students write the dialogue and test how the robot answers. They then change the design to improve the conversation.

## Privacy and safety
An account is needed, and the internet is always needed to connect a physical robot. The published privacy policy covers only the furhatrobotics.com website. It does not cover the robot, the SDK, or the Realtime API. It says personal information may go to ad services and analytics providers. It does not mention students or children. The site does not name which language model or speech providers the robot uses. The research could not confirm where conversation audio and text go. A teacher should ask the vendor about data handling before students talk to the robot.

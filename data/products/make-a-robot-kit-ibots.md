## Overview
The Make A Robot Kit, called M.A.R.K., is a build-it-yourself robot kit for ages 12 and up. The Tindie listing says it ships disassembled with tools. It includes an aluminum body, a 2.4 inch color screen, a pan-tilt camera, an ultrasonic sensor, and an electromagnet. Students program it with Codecraft, a Scratch 3.0 based tool that can also show MicroPython.

## Benefits for students
- Students assemble a robot from parts.
- Students train the onboard camera and save models to an SD card.
- Students code in blocks and can move to MicroPython.

## Benefits for teachers
- The robot recognizes colors, shapes, and objects out of the box.
- A Colab notebook is available for custom model training.
- STL files for 3D printed covers are available. Documentation and curriculum were not read.

## Benefits for schools
- Tindie lists the kit at $260. The seller ships from Xiamen, China.
- A SparkFun listing is retired. It is unclear whether the kit can be ordered now.
- The robot needs 6 AA batteries and an SD card. Plan for days of build time.

## Getting started
- [ ] Check the Tindie listing from iBots to see whether the kit can be ordered.
- [ ] Gather 6 AA batteries and an SD card.
- [ ] Assemble the kit from the parts with the included tools.
- [ ] Open Codecraft in a browser or desktop app.
- [ ] Try the preloaded color recognition.
- [ ] Train the camera on a new object and save the model to the SD card.
- [ ] Try the Colab notebook after the basics work.

## Where is the AI?
The AI recognizes colors, shapes, and objects and can follow lanes. Students train it on the onboard camera and the models stay on the SD card. Custom training can also use a Colab notebook, which runs online. Students test how well each model works on the robot.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. The research found no privacy policy for the kit. The listing says recognition and training happen on the robot. The Colab notebook for custom training runs in Google's online service and would send training images to a third party. Codecraft is a separate online and desktop tool, and the research did not check its policy. The research did not confirm whether Codecraft needs the internet or an account. A teacher should check school rules for the Colab notebook and review the images students train on.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Code a Codecraft program that reacts to a recognized color.
2. Train the camera on two shapes and test how often it is right.
3. View the MicroPython version of a block program and describe what each line does.
4. Compare a preloaded model with one the student trained.
5. Write a rule for when the robot should turn on its electromagnet.

### engineering
1. Assemble the robot and test each sensor.
2. Print a custom cover from the STL files.
3. Measure the ultrasonic sensor's range against a ruler.
4. Use the electromagnet to lift a small metal object.
5. Adjust the pan-tilt camera to give the best view.

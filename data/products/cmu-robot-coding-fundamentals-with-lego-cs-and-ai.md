## Overview
This is a grades 6 to 8 coding course for the LEGO Education Computer Science and AI kit, listed on CS2N. Its first two units are Getting Started and Remote Control. Teacher training prepares educators to teach it. Students code with blocks and use features such as pose detection and classification.

## Benefits for students
- Students code a real LEGO build with blocks.
- Students try pose detection and classification.
- Students learn how AI uses data.

## Benefits for teachers
- Self-paced teacher training comes with CS2N access.
- The training was free while in development, with payment planned later.
- Training works on Windows, Mac, or Chromebook.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum price is not stated.
- The kit costs about 529.95 USD per a search result.
- A search snippet says LEGO states that the AI runs on the student device and the camera does not record or send images.

## Where is the AI?
The kit includes pose detection and classification that run on the student device. Students build a model and use it in a project. The camera does not record or send images, per LEGO. Students test and improve how the model reacts.

## Privacy and safety
CS2N tracks student progress, and that data goes to the vendor. The CS2N policy lists name, email, username, group membership, and course activity, and it does not name COPPA or say where data is stored. A search snippet of LEGO's AI and Safety page says the AI features run locally, the kit uses no logins, and no data goes to LEGO or third parties. The research did not read that page in full. The materials do not state whether a CS2N account is needed or how much the internet is used. A teacher should read the CS2N policy and the LEGO privacy statement. A teacher should also test the camera features before class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Complete Getting Started and describe how blocks control the build.
2. Use Remote Control to drive a model and list the commands you used.
3. Train a classifier on two kinds of gestures and test it.
4. Use pose detection to make a model move when you move.
5. Explain in your own words how the kit knows what the camera sees.

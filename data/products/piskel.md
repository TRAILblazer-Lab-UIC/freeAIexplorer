## Overview
Piskel is a free online editor for pixel art and animated sprites. It gives a live animation preview with adjustable frame delay. Work exports as an animated GIF or a spritesheet. A Piskel for Kids version is child friendly.

## Benefits for students
- Students see their animation play as they draw.
- Students export a GIF to share.
- Students can use the Kids version if they are younger.

## Benefits for teachers
- A Vermont computing teacher built lessons around Piskel for grades 4 through 8.
- It opens in a browser with no install or account.
- The tool is easy to learn.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free, and the website shows ads.
- Offline apps exist for Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- It runs on Chromebooks.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open piskelapp.com in a browser.
- [ ] Try the Piskel for Kids version for younger students.
- [ ] Draw one frame and add a second.
- [ ] Adjust the frame delay in the preview.
- [ ] Export a GIF.
- [ ] Download an offline app if school internet is unreliable.
- [ ] Review the ads and analytics with IT.

## Where is the AI?
Piskel has no AI. It builds sequencing and pattern spotting skills. Students plan how a picture changes frame by frame.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The policy says creations are stored locally in the browser and not sent to a server. The policy is inconsistent about whether cookies store creations. The service sends anonymous usage data and error reports to Sentry, and no pixel art is sent. The site uses analytics and advertising cookies. The ad partner Ezoic may collect an IP address, device data, and a hashed email. Rejecting cookies does not remove ads. A teacher should check that the ads are suitable for students and consider the offline apps.

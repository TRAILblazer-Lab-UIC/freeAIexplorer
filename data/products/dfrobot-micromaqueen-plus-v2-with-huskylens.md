## Overview
micro:Maqueen Plus V2 is a micro:bit based robot car from DFRobot. Some bundles include a HuskyLens AI camera. The Mechanic kit version adds an ultrasonic sensor, a line-tracking map, and servos. Students build the car and program it with Mind+ or MakeCode.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a real robot car and then give it vision.
- Students program in MakeCode blocks or Mind+.
- Students see the camera learn objects and then steer the car.

## Benefits for teachers
- DFRobot publishes a wiki and tutorials for AI car projects.
- MakeCode runs in a web browser. No large install is needed.
- The car supports ultrasonic and line-tracking tasks without the camera.

## Benefits for schools
- DFRobot lists the HuskyLens bundle at $104.80 and with a micro:bit V2 at $127.20. Prices vary by retailer.
- The AI runs on the camera and needs no internet.
- Some bundles do not include the micro:bit V2 or batteries. Plan for those extra items.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the micro:Maqueen Plus V2 bundle with HuskyLens on the DFRobot site.
- [ ] Check whether the bundle includes a micro:bit V2 and batteries.
- [ ] Assemble the robot and insert the micro:bit and batteries.

- [ ] Open MakeCode or Mind+ and connect the micro:bit by USB.
- [ ] Teach the HuskyLens an object.
- [ ] Follow the DFRobot AI car tutorial.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is object recognition, object tracking, and line tracking that run on the HuskyLens camera. The camera learns objects on the sensor itself. Students write code on the micro:bit that reacts to what the camera sees. Chromebook use may work through the MakeCode web editor, but it was not confirmed.

## Privacy and safety
The robot and camera work without accounts, and recognition stays on the camera. MakeCode is a web editor and Mind+ is desktop software, and the research did not check their data practices. The DFRobot store privacy policy covers store accounts only and says the service is for people over 18. A teacher should check that school computers can run Mind+ or reach the MakeCode editor.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Code the car in MakeCode blocks so it drives forward and stops at an obstacle.
2. Teach the camera an object and program the car to follow it.
3. Program the car to read the line-tracking map and stay on the path.
4. Write a flowchart of what the car does when the camera sees a known object.
5. Compare MakeCode blocks with Mind+ for the same program.

### engineering
1. Build the Maqueen car and test that every wheel and sensor works.
2. Mount the HuskyLens at an angle that gives the best view of the floor.
3. Design a short course with turns and measure how fast the car finishes it.
4. Add the ultrasonic sensor and tune the stopping distance.
5. Document the wiring and mounting in a build guide for another team.

### science
1. Measure how the car's speed changes on different floor surfaces.
2. Test how light level changes what the camera recognizes.
3. Record how the car senses an object and compare it with how animals sense the world.
4. Plot the distance reading of the ultrasonic sensor against real distance.
5. Predict the car's path on a curved line and then test the prediction.

## Overview
This lesson for Years 5 to 6 teaches how natural language processing assesses and categorises online comments. In the unplugged step, students classify online reviews as positive, neutral, or negative on a worksheet. They write a review and discuss which word combinations cause wrong results. The plugged step has three levels with an online tool, a Scratch program, and the My Computer Brain anti-bullying experiment.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how a computer guesses the mood of a comment.
- Students find word combinations that fool a sentiment tool.
- Students build a Scratch program that reacts to keywords.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson has an unplugged step and three plugged levels.
- Remixable Scratch samples save prep time.
- A teacher can pick the levels that fit class time.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- Linked third party tools may have their own terms.
- Levels one to three need browsers and the internet.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is sentiment analysis, which is a kind of natural language processing. Students test it with an online tool that runs in the vendor's cloud. They also build a keyword program in Scratch in the browser. Students sort reviews by hand first, then test the tool and judge where it fails.

## Privacy and safety
The internet is needed for levels one to three. In level one, students type review text into third party sentiment tools. The page does not state how those tools handle data. It also does not say whether Scratch or My Computer Brain accounts are needed. The unplugged step uses a printed worksheet. A teacher should check each linked tool and use made up reviews. A teacher should also tell students to avoid personal details in any text they enter.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Sort a set of reviews as positive, neutral, or negative and explain each choice.
2. Write a review and ask a partner to guess its tone.
3. Find word combinations that sound positive but mean the opposite.
4. Rewrite a mean comment in a kinder way.
5. Discuss how companies might use the tone of reviews.

### computer_science
1. Test a review in an online sentiment tool and compare it with your sort.
2. Build a Scratch program that reacts to keywords in a comment.
3. Remix a sample program to add three new keywords.
4. List the cases where the keyword program gives a wrong answer.
5. Explore the My Computer Brain experiment and describe what it learns.

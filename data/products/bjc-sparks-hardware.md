## Overview
BJC Sparks Hardware is the hardware unit of the BJC Sparks middle school computing curriculum from UC Berkeley. It has eight labs in which students use the micro:bit with craft materials and toys, LEDs, servo motors, and sensors. The unit covers toys and games, e-textile circuits with conductive thread, NeoPixels, micro:bit to micro:bit messages, and a closing Rube Goldberg chain reaction project. Five middle school CS teachers piloted it in Spring 2022.

## Benefits for students
- Students build physical things that move, light up, and sense.
- Students tinker with craft materials, toys, and e-textiles.
- Students build a chain reaction machine with classmates.

## Benefits for teachers
- The eight labs give a clear path through the unit.
- Summer PD workshops are mentioned on the BJC Sparks page.

## Where is the AI?
This unit has no AI. It builds the skills of sensors and logic, as students program devices that respond to the world. The wider BJC Sparks curriculum includes an intro to machine learning in Unit 2, but this hardware unit does not.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. The page names Snap! for programming and the micro:bit as the controller. It does not discuss accounts or data. The research could not verify where student work goes. A teacher should check the programming editor, any accounts, and the privacy terms before the first lab.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Program a micro:bit to show a message when a button is pressed, as in the toys and games lab.
2. Send a message from one micro:bit to another.
3. Use a sensor reading to decide what an LED does.
4. Light NeoPixels in a repeating pattern.
5. Debug a classmate's program and explain the fix.

### engineering
1. Stitch an e-textile circuit with conductive thread and an LED.
2. Add a servo motor to a craft project and make it move.
3. Plan a Rube Goldberg chain reaction on paper before building it.
4. Test a toy with a sensor and note how it reacts.
5. Improve a design after one round of testing and record what changed.

## Overview
This is a lesson for ages 7 to 11 from the BBC micro:bit playground survey. Pupils copy an activity tracker file to a micro:bit and wear it on the wrist during break and lunchtime. They start and stop logging with buttons A and B. Afterward they open the MY_DATA file, graph the data, and vote on how accurate it was.

## Benefits for students
- Students collect data about their own movement.
- Students graph real data.
- Students judge how accurate a model is.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson takes one break and one lunchtime.
- The tracker file is ready to use.
- The lesson fits health and math.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- A micro:bit V2, a battery pack, and a wrist strap are needed.
- Data stays on the board.

## Getting started

- [ ] Plug the board into a computer to read MY_DATA after break.
- [ ] Copy the data first. Transferring a new hex file erases it.

## Where is the AI?
The hex file includes a machine learning model trained on children's movement. It runs on the micro:bit and estimates activity from the accelerometer. A search result says the model came from the Aarhus University tool. Pupils wear the board, view the graphs, and then judge how well the model matched what they did.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. Movement readings and estimated activity are saved in MY_DATA on the board. Viewing needs a computer, or a tablet with the micro:bit app. The research did not confirm whether the app needs an account. The page does not say where the model runs. The model sits in the hex file, which suggests on device inference. The research read no privacy policy. A teacher should check the pairing before break and keep activity graphs inside the class.

## Ideas by subject
### health_pe
1. Wear a logger during break and describe your busiest moment.
2. Compare your graph with a classmate who played a different game.
3. Predict which activity will look most active and check the graph.
4. Set a goal for more movement and measure it next week.
5. Discuss how the model decides what counts as active.

### math
1. Read a graph of logged data and find the highest value.
2. Compare two sets of data and find the difference.
3. Vote on the accuracy of the model and make a bar chart of the votes.
4. Estimate the time spent active and check it with the graph.
5. Explain one thing that makes the data unreliable.

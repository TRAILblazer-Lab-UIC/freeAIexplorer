## Overview
Modular Robotics publishes more than 50 free standards-aligned lessons and mini-units for Cubelets. They cover PreK through grade 12. The company's pages say the lessons are editable Google Docs under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike license. Students build robots from magnetic blocks, and building the robot is the programming. The lessons need Cubelets kits, which are not free.

## Benefits for students
- Students snap blocks together and see a robot work at once.
- Students test ideas without typing code.
- Students solve challenges such as a maze or a scavenger hunt.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lessons are editable. Teachers can adapt them.
- Lessons span many ages and grades.
- A STEAM teacher describes a maze challenge and a robot scavenger hunt for grades 3 to 8.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons are free.
- Schools must buy Cubelets kits.
- The robots run without the internet. The lessons are Google Docs that need the internet.

## Where is the AI?
These lessons have no AI. They build an AI-related skill, which is logic. Students combine sensor, think and action blocks. The robot then reacts to its surroundings.

## Privacy and safety
The robots run on their own, and no accounts are needed for them. The lessons are documents hosted on Google Docs. Opening them needs the internet. The optional app is separate, and its privacy was not reviewed. The research found no student data collected by the blocks. A teacher should check school rules for opening Google links and review the app before use. Keep small magnetic blocks away from young children who might swallow them.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a robot that reacts to light and describe what each block does.
2. Change one block and predict how the robot will change.
3. Make a robot that solves a maze challenge.
4. Write the logic of your robot as simple if and then statements.
5. Explain to a partner how building the robot is the same as programming.

### engineering
1. Build a robot for a scavenger hunt.
2. Test the robot and note what fails.
3. Redesign the robot to fix one problem.
4. Compare two designs and say which works better.
5. Build a robot that moves toward a goal.

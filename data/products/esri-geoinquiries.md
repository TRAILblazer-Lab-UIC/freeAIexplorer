## Overview
Esri GeoInquiries are short web GIS map activities for grades 4 to 12. Per search results they take about 15 minutes each and use interactive maps in ArcGIS Online. Topics include Earth science, environmental science, human geography, mathematics, and world geography. The official page redirects to a gallery that showed no readable text. Details come from search results.

## Benefits for students
- Students explore interactive maps in a short activity.
- Students practice reading and questioning data on a map.
- Students try analysis tools at Level 2.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each activity is short, at about 15 minutes.
- Bundles are grouped by topic.
- Level 1 activities need no special account.

## Benefits for schools
- The activities are free per Common Sense Education.
- US accredited K-12 schools may request a free ArcGIS Online subscription per a search snippet.
- Level 2 needs a school organization account with Publisher permissions. The classic format was being retired for newer map viewer activities.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the GeoInquiries gallery from Esri in a browser.

## Where is the AI?
The materials describe no AI in GeoInquiries. The activities build data and map reading skills. Students look at layers on a map and draw conclusions.

## Privacy and safety
The activities are online and run in the vendor cloud. The internet is always needed. Level 1 maps open without an account, according to search results. Level 2 needs an ArcGIS Online organization account for teachers and students, and that account stores user content with Esri. The research did not read a privacy policy. What student data is collected is unknown. Esri says classic activities stop working by Q1 2026. A teacher should check Esri's terms and which activities still work before giving students accounts.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Use a map activity to compare two regions and describe their differences.
2. Explore a human geography map and name one pattern.
3. Locate a country on a world map and list three facts about it.
4. Compare a map from two points in time and describe the change.
5. Write a short summary of what a map shows.

### science
1. Open an Earth science map and describe where an event happens most.
2. Map an environmental topic and note one cause.
3. Compare two layers and say how they relate.
4. Predict where a change will happen next and explain why.
5. Share a finding with the class.

### math
1. Measure the distance between two places on a map.
2. Read a map layer that shows numbers and describe the range.
3. Compare two areas using a number from the map.
4. Make a simple chart from a map table.
5. Explain how a map scale helps with measuring.

## Overview
Two Teacher Magazine articles describe unplugged AI classroom activities. The facial recognition article is dated 21 March 2018. Students act out feature extraction and database search with printed Disney princess pictures. A CSIRO volunteer scientist and the school's ICT teacher ran it with Year 6 at a girls' school in Sydney.

## Benefits for students
- Students answer feature questions about a printed character.
- Students learn that matching depends on database size and quality.
- Students see how a simple count can model facial recognition.

## Benefits for teachers
- The activity fits in one lesson.
- The setup is seven printed images with a list of features on the back.
- The second article covers machine learning, but its content is unconfirmed.

## Benefits for schools
- The articles are free to read.
- Printing is the only cost.
- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Where is the AI?
The activity models facial recognition by hand with no computer. Students extract features from a character and search a database for matches. One student counts matching features to score each match. They then talk about why accuracy depends on the database.

## Privacy and safety
The facial recognition activity is on paper and the dossier records no student data collected. No accounts are needed. The internet is used only to read the articles and a printer makes the cards. ## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Answer yes or no feature questions about a printed character.
2. Match an unnamed character against classmates by counting shared features.
3. Compare results when the database has only a few characters.
4. Add a new feature to the list and see whether the match improves.
5. Explain how a real facial recognition system might use the same steps.

### visual_arts
1. Look closely at a character and list its shapes, colors and details.
2. Draw a new character with features that are easy to tell apart.
3. Change one feature and see whether classmates can still match the drawing.
4. Compare how different art styles change which features stand out.
5. Design a card set with a clear list of features on the back.

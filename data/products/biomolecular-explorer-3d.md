## Overview
BioMolecular Explorer 3D is a UMass Amherst web resource for high school biology. It gives teachers interactive 3D structures of important molecules such as DNA, hemoglobin, lactase, antibodies, lipids, carbohydrates, proteins, vitamins, and water. Students open a molecule and rotate it to study its shape. Molecules are grouped by topic such as the immune system, digestion, and genetics.

## Benefits for students
- Students rotate and inspect real molecule models on screen.
- Students connect a molecule's shape to its job in the body.
- Students can explore at their own pace.

## Benefits for teachers
- The page offers background material for teachers.
- Molecules are grouped by biology topic, which helps with planning.
- Links lead to guided tutorials and other MolViZ.Org resources.

## Benefits for schools
- The resource is free to use online.
- Students need only a browser, and no install is listed for the current version.
- The pages are static with no sign in. The research found no data collection statement.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the BioMolecular Explorer 3D site.
- [ ] Note that version 1 is the older site and version 2 is the current release.
- [ ] Pick a molecule and click its link to open the 3D display.
- [ ] Test the display on a student computer before class.
- [ ] Test any tutorial that needs the MDL Chime plugin. That plugin is obsolete in modern browsers.
- [ ] Read the Frequently Asked Questions page.
- [ ] Prepare a short list of questions for students to answer from the model.

## Where is the AI?
This resource has no AI. It builds the skill of reading a model and spotting structure. Students also practice describing patterns in a three dimensional shape.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the pages are static university pages with no sign in. The research found no privacy policy or data collection statement. The internet is needed for each visit. A teacher should test the 3D viewer in the school browsers. Older tutorials rely on plugins that may not work.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Rotate the DNA model and describe what the twisting shape looks like.
2. Compare hemoglobin with an antibody and note one difference in shape.
3. Open the lactase model and link its shape to digestion.
4. Find a lipid and a carbohydrate and sketch what each looks like.
5. Pick one molecule and write three facts that the 3D view helped reveal.

## Overview
Bloxels EDU is a game-building app for K-12 students that needs no coding. Students create characters, art, animations, backgrounds, and games, and add narrative and logic. Teacher EDU plans add a Class Library for private sharing and an EDU Hub where teachers view student work and control sharing. Physical Bloxels boards and blocks are optional.

## Benefits for students
- Students make pixel art and animations.
- Students build a game with a story.
- Students can capture physical creations with the app.

## Benefits for teachers
- The EDU Hub lets teachers see student work and control sharing.
- Educator Guides and video tutorials match standards.
- The Class Library keeps sharing private to the class.

## Benefits for schools
- EDU plans are paid, with a discounted per-student price.
- Prices are not listed. Schools request a trial, a quote, or a purchase order.
- It runs on Chromebooks, iOS, Android, Kindle, and the web.

## Getting started
- [ ] Choose an EDU plan and request a trial, a quote, or a purchase order.
- [ ] Sign in to the EDU Hub as a teacher.
- [ ] Have students log in at play.bloxels.com or in the iOS, Android, or Kindle app.
- [ ] Review the sharing and arcade settings before students begin.
- [ ] Try the physical board and camera capture before class. A review says it was hard to get working.
- [ ] Read the Educator Guides.
- [ ] Start with a small art or character task.

## Where is the AI?
Bloxels EDU has no AI. It builds the skills of logic and sequencing, as students add rules to a game. It also builds storytelling through the narrative tools.

## Privacy and safety
Bloxels EDU needs student logins, and student work goes to the vendor cloud. The research did not find a privacy policy, and the education page has no privacy text. It could not confirm what student data is collected or whether the internet is needed all the time. Common Sense Education says its privacy team has not rated the product. A teacher should ask the vendor for the privacy terms and check the EDU Hub sharing settings before students publish games.

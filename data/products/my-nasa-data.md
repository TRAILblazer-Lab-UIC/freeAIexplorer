## Overview
My NASA Data is a NASA site of Earth science resources for grades 3 to 12, aligned to the Next Generation Science Standards. Content is organized by Earth system sphere. Each sphere offers mini lessons, interactives, lesson plans, data resources, GLOBE connections, and career connections. The site also has story map collections, flipbooks, and an Earth System Data Explorer.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with real NASA data.
- Students explore Earth systems through mini lessons and story maps.
- Students practice reading graphs and thinking in systems.

## Benefits for teachers
- Mini lessons can be used in Google Classroom and remote classrooms.
- A Data Literacy Cube and systems thinking organizers support planning.
- Common Sense Education notes that student sense making with graphs needs teacher guidance.

## Benefits for schools
- The site is free.
- It runs in any browser, including on Chromebooks.
- No install or IT work is needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the My NASA Data site in a browser.
- [ ] Browse by sphere or by phenomenon.
- [ ] Try the Air Quality phenomenon.
- [ ] Look at the 5E Earth System StoryMap collection.
- [ ] Open the Earth System Data Explorer and its tutorials.
- [ ] Prepare Excel or NASA Worldview for activities that open datasets.

## Where is the AI?
This site has no AI. It builds data skills, such as reading graphs and spotting patterns in Earth data. Students learn to ask questions of real data.

## Privacy and safety
The research found no account requirement, but the materials do not say whether accounts are used. The internet is needed at all times. The page links to the NASA privacy policy. The research did not confirm what data the site collects or whether analytics run. A teacher should read that policy and guide students in reading the graphs.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Study an Air Quality story map and list three causes of poor air.
2. Use the Earth System Data Explorer to compare two places.
3. Read a flipbook on an Earth system variable and draw a diagram.
4. Use the Data Literacy Cube to ask questions about a graph.
5. Build a systems map that shows how two spheres affect each other.

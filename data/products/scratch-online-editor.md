## Overview
Scratch is a block-based visual programming editor and online community. Students make stories, games, and animations. The editor runs in the browser and can be used without an account. Saving to the cloud and sharing need an account. Wikipedia gives a target age of about 5 to 16, mainly 8 to 16.

## Benefits for students
- Students snap blocks together to make games and stories.
- Students can share projects and browse studios.
- Students can add extensions such as text to speech.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can create student accounts in bulk with a teacher account.
- The editor is easy to learn.
- It needs no install.

## Benefits for schools
- Scratch is free, and the source code is on GitHub under AGPL-3.0.
- It runs on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- IT work is small.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open scratch.mit.edu in a browser.
- [ ] Try the editor without an account.
- [ ] Request a teacher account to manage student accounts.
- [ ] Read the privacy policy, which was updated 2026-03-27.
- [ ] Create student accounts in bulk.
- [ ] Choose which extensions students may use.
- [ ] Teach students to keep personal details out of projects and comments.

## Where is the AI?
Scratch has optional extensions such as text to speech and translate. These call online services in the vendor's cloud. Students can use them to add speech to a project. The privacy policy also mentions AI backend providers and AI tool training. The editor itself has no AI.

## Privacy and safety
The editor can be used without an account, and signing in stores projects and account data at MIT. Scratch also records approximate location from the IP address. Servers are in the U.S. Teacher-created student accounts store a username, password, birthdate, gender and country. The privacy policy updated 2026-03-27 lists service providers for hosting, analytics, email and AI backend support. It says personal information is used to train AI tools and that de-identified data may be shared for research and AI model training. Shared projects and comments are public. Deleting an account hides personal information from view but does not remove it from servers. The policy does not mention COPPA, and it says Scratch does not collect FERPA education records. A teacher should read the policy before creating student accounts.

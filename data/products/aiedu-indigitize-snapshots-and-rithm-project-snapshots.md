## Overview
These are two variant sets of aiEDU AI Snapshots. Indigitize is a nonprofit focused on Indigenous youth and educators. It adapted the Snapshots for classrooms that mainly serve Indigenous students. The Rithm Project partnership made two high school ELA lessons on how AI can support or erode human connection.

## Benefits for students
- Students discuss AI through Indigenous perspectives in the Indigitize set.
- Students think about how AI affects friendships and human connection.
- Students practice speaking and writing about AI in ELA class.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lessons are short, about 20 minutes according to search results.
- Teachers get a slide deck for each lesson.
- The Rithm lessons fit directly into a high school ELA plan.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free to educators.
- Search results say the Rithm materials use a Creative Commons license and the Indigitize deck uses CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- No IT install is needed.

## Where is the AI?
The lessons teach AI literacy and do not run an AI tool. The slide deck uses scenarios about AI and human connection. Students discuss what AI can support and what it might wear away. The lessons build the skill of judging AI's role in daily life.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Read a short scenario about an AI companion and discuss whether it can be a real friend.
2. Write a short reflection on how AI could support a human connection.
3. Compare two views on AI and friendship in a small group.
4. Write a letter to a friend about using AI wisely.
5. Debate whether AI can erode human connection and cite examples.

### social_studies
1. Discuss the Indigitize adaptation and what Indigenous perspectives add to AI talks.
2. Name who should decide how AI is used in a community.
3. Compare how different groups might view the same AI tool.
4. Research a community value and tie it to a rule for AI use.
5. Write a statement about how AI should respect a community.

## Overview
PBS LearningMedia is a free library of videos, interactives, and lesson plans for K-12. Its computing materials include the PBS KIDS ScratchJr collection with lesson plans for ages 5 to 8. Other items tie to shows such as Lyla in the Loop, Odd Squad, and Cyberchase. No single official computational thinking collection page was found.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn through shows and characters they may already know.
- Students can do screen free activities from a printable PDF.
- Students practice computational thinking in short, playful tasks.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can browse resources without an account.
- A free teacher account can save items.
- The materials are easy to use for most teachers.

## Benefits for schools
- Everything listed is free.
- It runs in a browser and needs no install.
- The ScratchJr app needs an iPad or Android tablet.

## Getting started
- [ ] Go to PBS LearningMedia and search for the Elementary School Computational Thinking list.
- [ ] Browse without an account first.
- [ ] Pick one item such as Human Robot from PBS KIDS ScratchJr.
- [ ] Print the screen free PDF for the activity.
- [ ] Decide whether to make an optional free teacher account.
- [ ] Check that the site loads on your school network. The main page loaded without content for the researcher.
- [ ] Plan a short lesson of one activity.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in these resources. The activities build skills in sequencing, loops, and logic. Students practice giving clear step by step directions.

## Privacy and safety
Resources are free to browse, the site runs in the vendor cloud, and an optional account is for teachers. The PBS Student Privacy Notice says interactives may collect usernames, submissions, IP addresses, device identifiers, and cookie data. It says student data is not sold or used for targeted ads. It says educators who create student accounts must confirm parental consent. The research could not load the main page to confirm account details. A teacher should use the site without student accounts and read the privacy notice.

## Overview
Sugar on a Stick is a Fedora Spin that features the Sugar Learning Platform. It boots from a USB stick or SD card of 2 GB or more. The host computer stays unchanged. It works on 64-bit computers with 1 GB of RAM, a 32-bit TOAST version, Raspberry Pi 2 or 3, or a virtual machine. Students use Sugar Activities for writing and Turtle Art programming.

## Benefits for students
- Students get a child-friendly desktop on any supported computer.
- Students keep their work on the stick with persistent storage.
- Students can write and program with Turtle Art.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can try Sugar without installing anything.
- The stick can move from one computer to another.
- The wiki lists Sugar 0.118 based on Fedora 35 as the latest version.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and needs only a USB stick.
- IT may need to allow USB booting.
- The wiki dates suggest slow maintenance. Check the project's status.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the ISO from the Sugar Labs wiki.
- [ ] Get a 2 GB or larger USB stick or SD card.
- [ ] Write the ISO to the stick. Windows uses Fedora Live USB Creator, Linux uses livecd-iso-to-disk, and macOS uses hdiutil and dd.
- [ ] Ask IT to allow booting from USB.
- [ ] Boot the computer from the stick using the boot menu.
- [ ] Test Turtle Art and a writing Activity.
- [ ] The internet is needed only at setup.

## Where is the AI?
Sugar on a Stick has no AI. Students run Activities and write simple programs. They build sequencing skills by giving a turtle clear steps.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The wiki covers only downloading and making the stick. It does not say whether the running system needs the internet or where student files are stored. A batch note says files stay on the stick, which is plausible with persistent storage but not stated. A teacher should keep the stick safe. It may hold student work.

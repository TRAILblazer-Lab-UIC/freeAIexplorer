## Overview
The Makey Makey lessons page offers 16 classes in a Beginner course and an Intermediate Maker course. Teachers get embeddable Google Slides. Classes include a banana piano, playable drawings, Scratch and MakeCode Arcade coding, a poem generator, and an invention challenge. The Alligator Clip Academy catalog lists 108 lessons from Pre-K to 12+.

## Benefits for students
- Students make music, stories, and games with everyday objects.
- Students learn circuits and coding together.
- Students meet lessons that fit many ages.

## Benefits for teachers
- Two courses of eight classes each give a clear plan.
- Slides can be embedded in a teacher's own materials.
- The materials are mostly household items plus a Makey Makey board.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons appear free, but kits are a paid purchase.
- The Academy page does not state its cost.
- Most classroom use evidence comes from the vendor.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Makey Makey lessons page.
- [ ] Choose the Beginner course or the Intermediate Maker course.
- [ ] Gather Makey Makey boards, alligator clips, and household conductive items.
- [ ] Open the slides for the first class.
- [ ] Use Scratch or MakeCode Arcade where a class needs them.
- [ ] Try the banana piano as a first class.
- [ ] Browse the Alligator Clip Academy for more ideas.

## Where is the AI?
These lessons have no AI. The vendor describes no AI features. Students build circuits and write code. They practice sequencing and logic.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether accounts are needed or where data goes. The lessons page does not say accounts are needed. The Academy page has a login link and does not explain access. Scratch based lessons use MIT online tools, and some classes use MakeCode Arcade. The research read no privacy policy. A teacher should check the privacy terms of those tools and the Academy before students use them.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Draw a playable instrument and play it with a piano app.
2. Build a banana piano and perform a short tune.
3. Blend music and STEM as Mesa Public Schools do and create a class band.
4. Compose a short melody and play it on touch pads.
5. Record the performance and describe how it works.

### science
1. Test which objects conduct electricity and record the results.
2. Draw the circuit that links a person to the board.
3. Predict which materials will work and check the prediction.
4. Compare a wet and a dry object as a switch.
5. Explain how a closed circuit works.

### ela
1. Use the poem generator class to write a poem with touch-based lines.
2. Craft an interactive story with several touch points.
3. Create a story map that links each touch to a scene.
4. Read a classmate's story and give feedback.
5. Perform the story for the class.

### computer_science
1. Code a Scratch project that reacts to key presses.
2. Build a MakeCode Arcade game with a Makey Makey controller.
3. Use a variable to keep a score.
4. Fix a program that does not respond.
5. Explore how a digital system takes input and gives output.

### engineering
1. Complete the invention challenge with a clear goal.
2. Design a switch from household items.
3. Test the switch and improve it.
4. Plan a design on paper first.
5. Present the final design to the class.

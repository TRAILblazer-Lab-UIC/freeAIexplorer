## Overview
myPhysicsLab is a free website of about 60 interactive physics simulations written in JavaScript. Visitors watch real-time animations and drag objects or change settings such as gravity. The simulations cover springs, pendulums, rigid body collisions, roller coasters, and a vibrating string. The site explains the math and the Runge-Kutta numerical method behind them.

## Benefits for students
- Students drag objects and change settings live.
- Students see the math behind each simulation.
- A share button makes a URL that recreates a setup.

## Benefits for teachers
- No account or install is needed.
- Pages explain the differential equations and numerical methods.
- The text heavy pages suit high school or above.

## Benefits for schools
- The site is free.
- It works in browsers on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- A downloadable zip allows offline use.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the myPhysicsLab website in a browser.

## Where is the AI?
This site has no AI. It builds skills in modeling and in reading how equations drive motion. Students test how changing a setting changes an outcome.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the simulations run in the browser. The internet is needed for the hosted site, and a downloadable zip allows offline use. The research found no formal privacy policy, and the site describes no analytics tools. A teacher should ask IT to check the site's network activity and preview the pages. The reading level is high.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Change gravity on a pendulum and record the period.
2. Compare a spring and a pendulum and describe the motion.
3. Watch a collision and predict where each object goes.
4. Test a roller coaster and find the point of greatest speed.
5. Share a setup by URL and ask a partner to explain it.

### math
1. Read the equation for a spring and name each variable.
2. Compare a simple numeric method with the one the site uses.
3. Graph position against time for a pendulum.
4. Change a parameter and predict the effect on the graph.
5. Explain in your own words what a differential equation describes.

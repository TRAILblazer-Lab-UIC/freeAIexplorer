## Overview
FreeCAD is a free, open-source parametric 3D modeler for designing real-life objects. Editing parameters in the model history lets users change a design later. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux with no sign-up. A 2025 article by Litecka and Mitalova examines using FreeCAD in lower secondary technology lessons in Slovakia.

## Benefits for students
- Students model real objects in 3D.
- Students change a design by editing its parameters.
- Students make technical drawings.

## Benefits for teachers
- The official site says it is a good place to learn CAD.
- It has technical drawing and sheet metal tools.
- An add-on ecosystem extends it.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and open source.
- It needs a desktop or laptop and is not a Chromebook app.
- IT approval may be needed for each computer.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download FreeCAD from freecad.org.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install.
- [ ] Install it on one test computer.
- [ ] Make a simple part and edit its parameters.
- [ ] Make a technical drawing of the part.
- [ ] Install on other computers.
- [ ] Update to the latest release as IT allows.

## Where is the AI?
FreeCAD has no AI. It builds design and spatial skills. Students plan a part and change its measurements to see the effect.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the internet is used only at setup. Models stay on the computer. The site says there is no sign-up. It links a privacy policy that the research could not read. The materials describe no student data collection. A teacher should confirm that IT approves the install and that students save files in the right place.

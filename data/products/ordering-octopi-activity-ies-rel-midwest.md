## Overview
Ordering Octopi is a 15 to 20 minute card activity from the REL Midwest ENACT computational thinking summer institute. Pairs get a set of octopus cards and choose a feature to order them by. The feature can be number of octopi, size or color. Students then explain their reasoning to the class.

## Benefits for students
- Students choose their own sorting rule and defend it.
- Students hear that one set of cards can be ordered in many ways.
- Students practice naming what matters and what can be ignored.

## Benefits for teachers
- The activity fits into a short slot of 15 to 20 minutes.
- Facilitators pick pairs with different rules to share, which starts a rich discussion.
- A related ENACT handout defines pattern recognition, abstraction, decomposition, debugging and algorithms.

## Benefits for schools
- The handout is a free download from IES.
- Color printing helps, but black and white works too.
- The activity needs no devices and no IT help.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Introduction to Abstraction and Pattern Recognition handout from IES REL Midwest.
- [ ] Download the handout.
- [ ] Print one set of octopus cards for each pair.
- [ ] Use color if you can. Black and white cards can still be ordered by number or size.
- [ ] Cut the cards apart and put each set in an envelope.
- [ ] Give pairs a few minutes to order the cards and prepare an explanation.
- [ ] Choose pairs who used different rules to share.

## Where is the AI?
This activity has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is spotting patterns and choosing which features matter. Machine learning systems also sort things by chosen features.

## Privacy and safety
The activity runs on paper, and the research found no account needs and no student data collection. The internet is used to download the handout. The official PDF returned a 403 error. The research relied on search snippets. A teacher should read the facilitator notes first and check the grade fit for the class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Order the octopus cards by one feature and explain the rule to a partner.
2. Reorder the same cards by a second feature and compare the two orders.
3. Name which details you ignored and why that made the task easier.
4. Spot a pattern in the cards and describe it in one sentence.
5. Listen to another pair's rule and guess what feature they used.

### math
1. Order the cards by number of octopi from least to most.
2. Order the cards by size and describe the pattern as the sizes grow.
3. Count the octopi on each card and make a tally of the totals.
4. Group cards that share the same number and tell how many groups you made.
5. Write a comparison sentence using more than, fewer than or equal to.

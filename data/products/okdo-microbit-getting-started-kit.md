## Overview
This is a starter kit around the BBC micro:bit board. Retail listings describe the board, a dual battery holder with a switch, a clear PVC case, a 1 m USB cable and a getting started guide. The listings differ on exact contents. Students program the board with an online micro:bit editor.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a small board and see the result at once.
- Students can carry the board in its case with a battery holder.
- A Spanish edition exists for Spanish-speaking learners.

## Benefits for teachers
- The kit comes with a getting started guide.
- The micro:bit works with common computers, including Chromebooks.
- Setup takes about an hour.

## Benefits for schools
- Listings show about 21.90 USD at a US retailer.
- The kit needs no accounts and no software license.
- The online editor needs the internet while students program.

## Getting started
- [ ] Buy the kit from RS or another retailer and check the contents list.
- [ ] Choose the English or Spanish edition.
- [ ] Insert batteries into the holder.
- [ ] Connect the board to a computer with the USB cable.
- [ ] Open an online micro:bit editor in a browser.
- [ ] Run the first example from the guide.
- [ ] Test the board on battery power.

## Where is the AI?
The kit has no AI. It builds skills in sequencing and logic through block coding. Students also learn about inputs such as buttons and outputs such as lights.

## Privacy and safety
The board runs programs on the device. The listings describe no accounts and no student data collection. Students program it with an online micro:bit editor, which needs the internet during use. The research did not review that editor, and data flow depends on the editor chosen. A teacher should check which editor the school will use and what terms that editor has.

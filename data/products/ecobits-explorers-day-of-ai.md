## Overview
EcoBits Explorers is a six lesson unit of about 4 to 5 hours for ages 11 to 18. Students set up micro:bits with environmental sensors and analyze example data. They design an investigation and log readings to Google Sheets with an MIT App Inventor app. They then build a mobile dashboard and compare local data with global climate records.

## Benefits for students
- Students collect real readings from their own surroundings.
- Students build a mobile dashboard for their findings.
- Students compare local data with global climate records.

## Benefits for teachers
- Materials include a slide deck, an educator guide, and student resources.
- The lessons run 45 to 60 minutes each.
- The unit is free.

## Benefits for schools
- The unit costs nothing, but micro:bits and sensors must be supplied.
- Students need phones or tablets and a Google account for Sheets.
- Full resources need a Day of AI login.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in this unit. The unit builds data skills. Students collect sensor readings, chart them, and compare them with climate records.

## Privacy and safety
A Day of AI login is needed to view the full resources, and the internet is required. Readings are logged from a phone or tablet app to Google Sheets, which sends data to a third party. The materials do not state whether students need Google accounts or what student data is collected. The research did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should decide which Google accounts students use and avoid personal details in logs.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Log temperature and light readings in the classroom and describe the pattern.
2. Compare readings from two spots in the school and explain the difference.
3. Design an investigation with a question, a prediction, and a plan.
4. Compare local data with global climate records and write one finding.
5. Present a dashboard to the class and answer questions about it.

### computer_science
1. Program a micro:bit to read a sensor.
2. Build an App Inventor app that logs readings to a sheet.
3. Create a mobile dashboard that shows the latest reading.
4. Fix a program that logs the wrong values and explain the change.
5. Add a second sensor to the logging program.

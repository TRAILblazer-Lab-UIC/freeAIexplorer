## Overview
This MiTechKids grade 4 lesson teaches stop motion basics. Students make a short movie with Lego minifigures or premade clay figures. The intro takes about 20 minutes and students need one or two more class periods. The lesson uses the Stop Motion Studio app.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how still photos make motion.
- Students make a movie with figures they can touch.
- Students retell a story or show a science or math idea.

## Benefits for teachers
- A walk-cycle tutorial helps teachers teach the first skill.
- A suggested rubric guides grading.
- It lists content ideas in ELA, math, science, and social studies.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- The app is free with in-app purchases, and one source mentions about $4.99 for a Pro upgrade.
- Lego or clay figures may need to be bought.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Stop Motion Animation lesson on the REMC MiTechKids site.
- [ ] Ask IT to install Stop Motion Studio on classroom tablets or phones.
- [ ] Gather Lego minifigures or clay figures.
- [ ] Review the tutorial videos.
- [ ] Practice the walk-cycle tutorial once.
- [ ] Print the suggested rubric.
- [ ] Plan about 20 minutes for the intro and one or two class periods for the movie.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is sequencing. Students take photos in order and see how the order creates motion. That practice helps them understand how a series of frames becomes a story.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. Photos are captured on the device. The lesson uses Stop Motion Studio, which is free with in-app purchases. The research did not review the app's privacy policy or any account prompts. The page lists iOS and Android only. Chromebook support is unconfirmed. A teacher should check the app settings and save student movies in a school location.

## Ideas by subject
### theater_media
1. Make a short stop motion movie with a Lego minifigure, as the lesson describes.
2. Follow the walk-cycle tutorial to make a figure walk.
3. Plan a storyboard before taking photos.
4. Add a title card to the movie.
5. Show the movie and use the rubric to give feedback.

### ela
1. Retell a book scene with a stop motion movie.
2. Write the script for a short scene before filming.
3. Show a character's feeling through movement.
4. Add a title and a closing line.
5. Share the movie and explain how the retelling stays true to the book.

### math
1. Animate a math concept with clay, as the lesson suggests.
2. Show a shape growing in equal steps.
3. Make a movie that shows a fraction being split.
4. Count the frames in a clip and work out the number of seconds.
5. Explain the math in the movie to a classmate.

### science
1. Demonstrate a science concept with stop motion, as the lesson suggests.
2. Show the water cycle with clay figures.
3. Film the stages of a life cycle.
4. Plan each frame so the order shows cause and effect.
5. Present the movie and explain the science in it.

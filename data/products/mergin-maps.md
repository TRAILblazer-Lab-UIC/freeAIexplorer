## Overview
Mergin Maps is a field data collection tool. A mobile app for iOS and Android captures location data on a phone or tablet. A plugin for the desktop GIS program QGIS syncs that data to a project in the Mergin Maps cloud. It is a professional tool and was not designed for children.

## Benefits for students
- Students collect real data on a field trip.
- Students see their points on a map.
- Students can work offline in the field, per the feature list.

## Benefits for teachers
- Forms can be customized for a project.
- Projects have a history of changes.
- Setup is advanced and takes days.

## Benefits for schools
- The app is open source under GPL-2.0 and a free option exists. Paid plans are priced in euros.
- The company, Lutra Consulting, offers an Academic Plan for universities, and the official page says private schools can apply.
- IT will need to help with QGIS and accounts.

## Getting started
- [ ] Review the Mergin Maps education page and the pricing page.
- [ ] Create a teacher account.
- [ ] Install QGIS and the Mergin Maps plugin.
- [ ] Prepare a small project and sync it to the cloud.
- [ ] Have students install the mobile app and log in.
- [ ] Download the project and collect a few test points.
- [ ] Apply for the Academic Plan only if your school qualifies. It needs employment verification.

## Where is the AI?
Mergin Maps has no AI. The vendor describes no AI features. Students collect and map data. They build skills in data and pattern spotting.

## Privacy and safety
Project data synced to the service is stored on Mergin Maps cloud servers run by Lutra Consulting. Cloud sync needs an account, and the research did not confirm whether the app works without one. The privacy policy text could not be read. The cookie banner lists essential, marketing, personalization, and analytics cookies. The research found no student specific privacy terms. The pricing page lists an on premise option for keeping data on school infrastructure. Location data is collected as part of the work. A teacher should check the privacy terms, set up accounts with IT, and decide what location data students may collect.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Collect plant observations in the schoolyard and map them.
2. Record litter or water quality readings at set points.
3. Compare two areas of the school grounds using the map.
4. Add a form field for each observation and test it.
5. Write a short report that uses the map as evidence.

### social_studies
1. Map places of local importance and add a photo and note for each.
2. Survey a street for features such as bus stops and ramps.
3. Compare the map to a historic map of the area.
4. Plan a walking tour and mark the stops.
5. Present the map and explain what it shows about the community.

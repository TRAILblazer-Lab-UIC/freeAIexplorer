## Overview
The CSER resources page lists one unplugged Hour of Code activity, Challenge 1 Lego Building. It takes 30 to 60 minutes for primary aged students. A related Digital Technologies Hub lesson has pairs build a LEGO model. They then give instructions so a partner builds a replica. The page lists a Micro:bit simulator as the plugged option.

## Benefits for students
- Students give clear instructions and follow a partner's instructions.
- Students build with LEGO or similar blocks.
- Students see why precise wording matters.

## Benefits for teachers
- The activity takes 30 to 60 minutes.
- The Hub lesson targets Years 3-4.
- The page pairs the activity with a plugged micro:bit simulator option.

## Benefits for schools
- The page is free.
- The school may need to supply LEGO or similar building blocks.
- No devices are needed for the unplugged part.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the CSER Adelaide resources page and open the Hour of Code section.
- [ ] Read the Challenge 1 Lego Building instructions.
- [ ] Open the related Digital Technologies Hub lesson.
- [ ] Gather LEGO or similar blocks for each pair.
- [ ] Check the page for license or account details. None are listed.
- [ ] Have each pair build a model that stays hidden.
- [ ] Hold a class discussion on which instructions worked best.

## Where is the AI?
This activity has no AI. It builds sequencing and clear instruction writing. Students give step-by-step directions and fix directions that fail.

## Privacy and safety
The unplugged activity runs on building blocks, and the research found no student data collected. The CSER page does not state account needs, and the research found no license or privacy policy. The micro:bit simulator is a separate plugged option that students would use in a browser, and the research did not review it. A teacher should check the page terms before sharing materials and review the simulator before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a small LEGO model and write step-by-step instructions for a partner.
2. Follow a partner's instructions and compare the replica with the original.
3. Find the unclear step that caused a difference.
4. Rewrite the instructions to fix the unclear step.
5. Try the micro:bit simulator option and compare it with the LEGO task.

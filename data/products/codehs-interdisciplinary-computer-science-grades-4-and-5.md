## Overview
These CodeHS courses fold coding into other subjects. The 4th grade course has 8 units and 44 lessons, with coding in math, science, ELA, and social studies. Students use Scratch inside CodeHop. The general classroom teacher is meant to teach it about once a week. The 5th grade page was not read.

## Benefits for students
- Students use Scratch to explore ideas from their other classes.
- Students make projects such as digital flashcards and cause-and-effect models.
- Students can animate a poem reading.

## Benefits for teachers
- The classroom teacher can teach it, with no special coding background.
- Lessons run about once a week.
- The course lists state CS standards and CSTA.

## Benefits for schools
- The page lists no price and asks schools to get a quote.
- Students use a browser on Chromebooks, Windows, or Mac.
- The course data goes to the vendor.

## Where is the AI?
This course has no AI. It builds skills in sequencing and cause and effect. Students arrange blocks to tell a program what to do.

## Privacy and safety
The course page does not say whether accounts are required, and the internet is needed throughout. CodeHS lists name and email as the school chooses, coursework, and saved projects. Data goes to the vendor. The policy says it acts as a school official under FERPA, relies on school consent for users under 13, and does not sell or rent personal information. A teacher should confirm with CodeHS which accounts students need and how young students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a Scratch project that uses a loop.
2. Make a sprite respond to a key press.
3. Create a cause-and-effect model with blocks.
4. Debug a Scratch project that a partner has changed.
5. Describe the steps of a program in order.

### ela
1. Animate a poem reading in Scratch.
2. Build a character that speaks lines from a story.
3. Create a digital flashcard set of vocabulary words.
4. Tell a short story with two sprites.
5. Record and share a favorite sentence in an animation.

### math
1. Apply a grade 4 math idea in a Scratch program.
2. Make a program that quizzes a partner on facts.
3. Draw shapes with equal sides using blocks.
4. Use a variable to count points in a game.
5. Check a program's answer with a paper solution.

### science
1. Build a cause-and-effect model of a science idea.
2. Animate the steps of a life cycle.
3. Create a program that shows day and night.
4. Test one change in the model and describe the result.
5. Explain what the model leaves out.

### social_studies
1. Make a digital flashcard set about a community.
2. Animate a short event from history.
3. Build a map project with clickable places.
4. Present a model of a community that uses a program.
5. Write steps for a cause-and-effect story from history.

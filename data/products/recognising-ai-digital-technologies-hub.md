## Overview
Recognising AI is a lesson sequence from the Digital Technologies Hub for Years 5 to 8. Most tasks are unplugged and use printable card sets. Students sort examples, work through input, process and output, and decide whether something is an AI application. Some steps use online tools or Scratch programs.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a shared vocabulary for AI through a word wall.
- Students practice sorting and justifying whether an example uses AI.
- Students learn that every system takes input, does a process, and gives output.

## Benefits for teachers
- The sequence has five clear steps with printable handouts and cards.
- The page lists curriculum codes AC9TDI6P06 and AC9TDI8P10.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free to download.
- Most work is on paper and few devices are needed.
- Devices are needed only for the online tools in step 2 and the Scratch activities in steps 4 and 5.

## Where is the AI?
The sequence teaches about AI and does not run an AI model itself. Students classify AI systems and study input, process and output. Some tasks run in a browser on the device. Students use an AI flow chart to decide whether an example is AI and then explain their choice.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need, and the lesson page does not describe data collection. Paper tasks keep student work in the classroom. Step 2 may use online tools such as Quick, Draw! and smartphone assistants, and the research did not review their data practices. Some steps use Scratch. A teacher should check each online tool before class and see whether it asks for an account.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Sort a set of cards into uses AI and does not use AI, and explain each choice.
2. Label the input, process and output of a vending machine and a voice assistant.
3. Build a word wall of AI terms with a short meaning for each word.
4. Use the flow chart to test a new example from daily life.
5. Change a Scratch program from the lesson and describe how the output changes.

### ela
1. Write a paragraph that defends why one example is or is not AI.
2. Complete the pre-lesson handout and revisit it after the sequence to show what changed.
3. Present a card sort result to a partner and answer questions.
4. Write a one-sentence definition of AI and improve it after class talk.
5. Create a short glossary entry for three words from the word wall.

## Overview
This DIY.org challenge frames a dance routine as an algorithm. Students pick four to six moves, give each a symbol, and lay out eight cards as a sequence. A repeated group marked x2 stands for a loop. Learners can then build matching commands in any block coding app, run them, and fix the sequence.

## Benefits for students
- Students turn their own dance moves into symbols and a sequence.
- Students practice loops, testing, and debugging.
- Older learners can try nested loops and conditionals.

## Benefits for teachers
- The page gives different suggestions for ages 4 to 6, 7 to 11, and 12 plus.
- The digital step works with Scratch, MakeCode, or Tynker.
- The materials are paper, index cards, scissors, tape, and a timer.

## Benefits for schools
- The challenge page is free.
- DIY.org sells a Plus subscription per search results.
- The paper steps need no devices.

## Where is the AI?
This challenge has no AI. It builds sequencing, loops, and debugging. Students plan a routine as a set of steps, repeat a group of moves, and fix any step that fails.

## Privacy and safety
The paper steps keep student work in the room. Sharing a result on DIY.org sends it to the company, and the page does not say whether an account is required. DIY.org describes itself as a kids community where only an avatar and username are public. The research did not read a current privacy policy. A teacher should review the DIY.org terms and decide whether students post at all.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Choose four to six dance moves and give each a symbol.
2. Lay out eight cards as a sequence and perform it.
3. Mark a repeated group with x2 and explain the loop.
4. Build matching commands in a block coding app and run them.
5. Test the sequence, find a bug, and fix it.

### health_pe
1. Perform a routine written by another group.
2. Make a routine with a loop and count the repeats.
3. Change the tempo and note how the dance feels.
4. Teach your routine to a partner using only the symbol cards.
5. Reflect on how a clear plan helps a group dance together.

## Overview
Student teams trace an enlarged Iditarod trail map and pick at least five checkpoints. They research each checkpoint and plan an action for the Ozobot, such as a zig-zag for slipping on ice. The lesson plan was written by Jim Deprez and Eric Lane for grades 2 to 8. Teams draw Ozobot color codes on the map, write a script, and film the run.

## Benefits for students
- Students link geography and research to real robot behavior.
- Students practice planning a sequence of actions.
- Students tell the story of the race through a script and a short film.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson covers social studies and technology in one project.
- The plan names Ohio Technology Standards for ICT and Design and Technology.
- The setup is a short list of printing, charging, and map tracing.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free to use.
- The school needs Ozobots, iPads, butcher paper, and markers.
- The plan assumes a parent volunteer helps copy the map each year.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Ozobot Iditarod Mapping and Coding lesson plan by Jim Deprez and Eric Lane.
- [ ] Practice the Ozobot color codes with students first.
- [ ] Trace the year's race map onto butcher paper for each group.
- [ ] Print the Ozobot coding sheets.
- [ ] Charge the robots.
- [ ] Set up iPads for filming.
- [ ] Have each team choose five checkpoints and plan an action for each.
- [ ] Let teams test the run and film it in iMovie.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. The Ozobot follows color codes that students draw on paper. Students build sequencing skills as they order the codes along the trail.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson needs no account, and the Ozobot runs offline from drawn codes. The internet is used at setup and to open the map and checkpoint list on Iditarod.com. Students film on iPads with iMovie or a similar app. The videos stay on the filming device unless the teacher shares them. A teacher should decide where the iPad videos are saved and who can view them.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Research five Iditarod checkpoints and write a short fact card for each.
2. Mark each checkpoint on the trail map and explain why the route goes there.
3. Plan a robot action that matches a trail hazard, such as slipping on ice.
4. Write a reader's theater script that tells the story of the race.
5. Present the map and film to the class and answer questions about the route.

### computer_science
1. Practice the Ozobot color codes on a small test track.
2. Draw a code sequence on the map that makes the robot zig-zag.
3. Test the run and fix any code that sends the robot the wrong way.
4. Trade maps with another team and predict what their robot will do.
5. Film the robot run and explain each code to the viewer.

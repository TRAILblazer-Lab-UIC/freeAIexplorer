## Overview
Universe Sandbox is a commercial physics based space simulator. It combines gravity, climate, collision, and material interactions. Users create and destroy planets, stars, and systems and recreate historical events. Students run experiments and watch what happens.

## Benefits for students
- Students can test what happens when planets collide.
- Students see gravity and climate effects in motion.
- Students can ask their own what if questions.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can run live demonstrations of space ideas.
- The company offers education licenses per seat, per classroom, or site wide.
- Each license includes an offline copy and updates.

## Benefits for schools
- The company gives no education list price. Schools request a quote by number of computers.
- The offline copy is DRM free.
- It runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux. It does not run on tablets or Chromebooks currently.

## Getting started
- [ ] Contact the company for a quote and state the number of computers and users.
- [ ] Ask IT to check the hardware requirements in the company FAQ.
- [ ] Install the offline copy on school computers.
- [ ] Check whether accounts or the internet are needed. The materials do not say.
- [ ] Try a simple orbit demo yourself.
- [ ] Plan one short guided activity.
- [ ] Let students run their own experiments.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI. The simulator uses physics rules. Students build data and pattern skills as they compare outcomes across runs.

## Privacy and safety
The education page says the license includes an offline DRM free copy, which suggests no required connection. The research did not confirm this and read no privacy policy. The materials do not state which accounts students need or what data is collected. Ask the company about accounts, connection needs, and data before purchase.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Model the orbits of the planets and describe the pattern.
2. Change the mass of a planet and record how the orbit changes.
3. Recreate a moon forming collision and discuss what the model shows.
4. Test how a planet's distance from its star changes its climate.
5. Write a prediction before each experiment and compare the result.

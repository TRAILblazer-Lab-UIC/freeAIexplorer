## Overview
The Code.org home page lists free CodeAI resources. They include AI Foundations for grades 9-12, AI Discoveries for grades 6-8, and How AI Makes Decisions for grades 3-5, which runs one week. It also lists Hour of AI activities and a 30 minute educator module with Microsoft. The page did not list a How AI Works video series.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how AI works with age-fitting lessons for each grade band.
- Students study machine learning and bias with hands-on activities.
- Students get short Hour of AI activities for a first taste.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can create sections to follow student progress.
- The courses come in English and Spanish.
- The standards listed are CSTA.

## Benefits for schools
- All the resources are free.
- The courses run in a browser.
- IT will likely need to approve the Code.org site and student sign-in.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Code.org site and find the CodeAI resources.
- [ ] Choose the course for your grade band.
- [ ] Create a teacher account and a section.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve Code.org for student use.
- [ ] Preview the first lesson yourself.
- [ ] Try the 30 minute educator module.
- [ ] Share the course link or section code with students.

## Where is the AI?
The courses teach how AI works, how machine learning learns, and how bias appears. The tools run in the vendor's cloud through a browser. Students try activities with data and models and then talk about what the results mean.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are optional, and the internet is needed at all times. The vendor sends account and activity data to its own servers. The policy lists display name, username, age, state, and progress data. It says it does not sell personal data or show ads. It says it is not subject to COPPA as a nonprofit, and it treats school created accounts as Student Records. AI supported curricula use third party language model providers, and the policy does not describe their retention. The Common Sense page showed no privacy rating. A teacher should check the privacy terms with the school and decide whether students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Try a How AI Makes Decisions lesson and explain how a computer picks an answer.
2. Train a simple model on example data and test it with new examples.
3. Compare two data sets and describe how one produces a biased result.
4. Finish an Hour of AI activity and share one thing you learned.
5. Explain in your own words how machine learning differs from a fixed rule.

### social_studies
1. Discuss how a biased AI tool could affect a community.
2. List fair and unfair uses of AI in a town.
3. Debate who should decide how a school uses AI.
4. Write a short letter on one rule for AI use at school.
5. Interview an adult about how AI shows up in their work.

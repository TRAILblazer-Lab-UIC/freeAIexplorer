## Overview
Planetary Planners is an unplugged project from a 2018 Grok Learning blog post. Students start with four planets and fill a table of planets against yes or no features. They turn the table into a decision tree with as few steps as possible. An extension adds the remaining four planets and asks students to research extra questions. The evidence is thin and rests on one blog post.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice sorting planets by yes or no features.
- Students turn a table into a decision tree.
- Students aim for the shortest tree.

## Benefits for teachers
- The project is short and uses a simple table.
- The extension gives early finishers more to do.
- The idea fits a science unit on the solar system.

## Benefits for schools
- The blog post is free.
- The project needs only paper.
- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Where is the AI?
The project has no running AI program. It teaches how a decision tree classifies items from a table of features. Students build a tree that sorts planets with as few questions as possible. They build skills in classification and in organizing data.

## Privacy and safety
The project runs on paper and the materials describe no student data collection. The research team could not open the blog post and found no other copy. The vendor does not say whether an account is needed. A teacher should read the post directly before class and check the planet facts.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Fill in a table of four planets against yes or no features.
2. Turn the table into a decision tree with as few steps as possible.
3. Compare your tree with a partner's and tell which is shorter.
4. Add the other four planets and revise the tree.
5. Explain how a decision tree helps a machine sort items.

### science
1. Research the features that tell the planets apart.
2. Choose yes or no features that you can check with facts.
3. Find a new question about a planet and add it to the table.
4. Check each planet's answers against a trusted source.
5. Present your tree and say which planet needs the most questions.

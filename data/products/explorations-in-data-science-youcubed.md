## Overview
Explorations in Data Science is a project-based high school data science course from Youcubed at Stanford. It covers data analysis, sampling, correlation and causation, bias, uncertainty, probability, modeling, and the role of data in society. Students finish with a portfolio of their work. Teachers can use the provided datasets or bring their own.

## Benefits for students
- Students work on projects that tell stories with data.
- Students use free tools such as Google Sheets and Python.
- Students finish with a portfolio.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teacher workshops, office hours, and a self-paced online course are offered.
- Standards and software requirement documents are provided.
- Teachers can use their own datasets.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum is free with sign-up. Professional development pricing is not listed.
- The site reports it is approved as a California A-G math course.
- Tools such as Google Sheets, Python, Data Commons, and Tableau must be available to students.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the course on the Youcubed site and sign up for a free account.

- [ ] Look at the teacher workshops.

## Where is the AI?
The course teaches data analysis and modeling with no AI tool built in. It runs partly in the vendor cloud and partly in the browser. Students analyze data and build models themselves with tools such as Sheets and Python.

## Privacy and safety
Teachers sign up for a free account to unlock all resources, and the internet is always needed. Students use third party cloud tools such as Google Sheets and Tableau, which have their own policies. The research did not read the course site privacy policy. The materials do not say what student data is collected. A teacher should check the privacy terms of the course site and of each tool students use.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Work through Unit 1 and tell a story from a data set.
2. Take a sample from a data set and compare it with the whole.
3. Find a correlation and discuss whether it shows causation.
4. Describe how bias can enter a data set.
5. Build a portfolio entry that explains a finding.

### computer_science
1. Load a data set into Google Sheets and sort it.
2. Write a short Python script that finds an average.
3. Make a chart in Tableau and describe what it shows.
4. Use Data Commons to find a public data set.
5. Compare two tools for the same task and pick one.

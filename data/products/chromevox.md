## Overview
ChromeVox is the screen reader built into ChromeOS. It reads whole pages, text from the cursor, or text under the mouse. It offers keyboard shortcuts and touch gestures for jumping between headings, links, tables, and form fields. It also reads MathML and PDFs and works with braille displays. It is an accessibility tool and the vendor does not describe AI in it.

## Benefits for students
- Students with visual impairments can hear what is on the screen.
- Students can jump by headings, links, tables, and landmarks.
- Students can use a braille display over USB or Bluetooth.

## Benefits for teachers
- A tutorial and Learn Mode help new users practice.
- Teachers can turn it on with Ctrl+Alt+Z.
- Voice and rate can be adjusted in the options.

## Benefits for schools
- ChromeVox is included with ChromeOS at no cost.
- No install is needed.
- Chromebook administrators can help with voice settings.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open a Chromebook that your student uses.
- [ ] Press Ctrl+Alt+Z to turn ChromeVox on.
- [ ] On a tablet, hold both volume buttons for five seconds.
- [ ] Run the built-in tutorial.
- [ ] Set the voice and language in the ChromeVox options.
- [ ] Ask the Chromebook administrator about voice settings.
- [ ] Connect a braille display if the student uses one.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in ChromeVox. It uses text to speech to read the screen aloud. Students practice listening, navigating by structure, and using keyboard commands. These skills help them reach online content on their own.

## Privacy and safety
The Google support page does not say whether ChromeVox needs the internet or what data it sends. It also does not say whether the voices work offline. Chromebooks in schools usually use managed accounts, which the research did not confirm. The research read no privacy policy specific to ChromeVox. A teacher should check with the Chromebook administrator on voice settings and on any online voices. A teacher should also test the tool with the websites that students use.

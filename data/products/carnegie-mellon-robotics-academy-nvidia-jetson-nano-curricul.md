## Overview
This project-based course teaches AI, machine learning, perception, and autonomy with a Jetson Nano on the JetBot platform. It has 5 units, from getting started to localization with AprilTags and ROS. Students cover classification, regression, transfer learning, and reinforcement learning. Each unit has Try It activities, challenges, and quizzes.

## Benefits for students
- Students train real AI models on a robot.
- Students try collision avoidance, path following, and autonomous racing.
- Students learn transfer learning and reinforcement learning.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each unit has activities, challenges, and quizzes.
- A separate four-day onsite educator training is offered.
- Training participants must be 18 or older.

## Benefits for schools
- Onsite training is 1299 USD per registration, with kits included.
- Course and kit prices are not listed.
- Hardware includes a Jetson Nano, a JetBot kit, a 64 GB microSD card, batteries, a monitor, and a keyboard.

## Where is the AI?
The AI runs on the JetBot robot itself. Students train and run models for classification, regression, transfer learning, and reinforcement learning. They then test those models on tasks such as collision avoidance and racing.

## Privacy and safety
A CS2N account is required for students. The page says the laptop and the Jetson Nano need the internet, and the research did not confirm how much it is used after setup. The AI runs on the robot. The materials do not state what student data is collected or where it goes. The research did not review the CS2N privacy terms. A teacher should read the CS2N privacy policy before students sign in. A teacher should also check what images the robot camera stores.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train a collision avoidance model by collecting blocked and free images.
2. Compare classification and regression in a Try It activity.
3. Use transfer learning and record how it changes accuracy.
4. Study reinforcement learning in the racing unit and describe the reward.
5. Use AprilTags to help the robot find its location.

### engineering
1. Build and power the JetBot and test its motors.
2. Mount the camera and test how the angle affects results.
3. Design a track for path following and test it.
4. Measure the battery life during training runs.
5. Document the setup steps for another team.

## Overview
Students shake a micro:bit to make a random character, setting, and problem. They use these story elements to plan and write a narrative. The lesson is for ages 7 to 11 and has Mild, Medium, and Spicy levels. Learners use the generator, change it, or code their own in MakeCode.

## Benefits for students
- Students get a fresh writing prompt each time they shake the board.
- Students choose a challenge level, which lets each learner work at a comfortable pace.
- Students who code their own generator learn how random choices work in a program.

## Benefits for teachers
- The page suggests three formats, which are whole class, station rotation, and choice board.
- The lesson zip includes a project overview, slides, and a recording sheet.
- The lesson lists links to California ELA writing and computer science standards.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons are free, and the micro:bit boards are a paid purchase.
- The lesson works in a browser on Windows, Mac, Linux, and Chromebook.
- A teacher can install it, and IT work is limited.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. The random results come from the shake gesture and a simple program, and the page does not describe machine learning. Students build skills in logic and randomness. They see how a program can pick one item from a list.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The pages read do not discuss data handling, and they did not require a sign in. The internet is used at setup, and the work stays on the device. The research read no privacy policy for this item. A teacher should check the MakeCode privacy terms and preview the Spicy level prompts before sharing them with students.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Shake the micro:bit and write a narrative that uses the character, setting, and problem it shows.
2. Plan a story on the recording sheet before writing the first sentence.
3. Swap generated prompts with a partner and write two different stories from the same elements.
4. Revise a draft so the problem is clear in the first paragraph.
5. Read a story aloud and ask listeners to name the character, setting, and problem.

### computer_science
1. Use the generator and describe how the shake starts the program.
2. Change the list of characters in MakeCode and test the new result.
3. Add a new setting block and run the program again.
4. Code a new generator for a different topic, such as a science report.
5. Explain to a partner how the program picks a random item.

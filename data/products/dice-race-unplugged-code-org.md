## Overview
Dice Race Unplugged is Lesson 10 of Code.org CS Fundamentals 2017 Course E. Students play a dice game and write an algorithm. They then follow classmates' algorithms. The plan lists 60 minutes, with 15 for the warm-up, 30 for the main activity, and 15 for the wrap-up.

## Benefits for students
- Students break a game into smaller events and put them in order.
- Students test an algorithm by following a classmate's version.
- Students practice math skills with dice.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson includes a printed worksheet per group and an assessment per student.
- Answer keys are provided.
- The standards listed are CSTA 1B-AP-11, CCSS Math MP.2, MP.4, and MP.6, and NGSS 3-5-ETS1-1.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- Commercial use needs permission from Code.org.
- The 2017-18 version is archived but also appears in other Code.org courses.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds decomposition and sequencing, which are core programming skills. Students write an algorithm and then test it on classmates.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson runs on paper with dice, and the research found no student data collected. The page does not state account needs. It asks teachers to make copies of shared documents, which may need a Google account. The internet is used to get the plan. A teacher should check the permission terms from Code.org before any commercial use. A teacher should also check the answer keys before grading.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play the Dice Race game and write down the steps you took.
2. Break the game into smaller events and put them in order.
3. Follow a classmate's algorithm and note any step that fails.
4. Fix a step in your own algorithm after a test.
5. Complete the assessment and explain your answers.

### math
1. Roll dice and add the numbers to track progress.
2. Use the math practices MP.2, MP.4, and MP.6 to explain your game plan.
3. Solve a place value problem linked to 4.NBT.4.
4. Predict the likely totals when two dice are rolled.
5. Compare results across rounds and find the average.

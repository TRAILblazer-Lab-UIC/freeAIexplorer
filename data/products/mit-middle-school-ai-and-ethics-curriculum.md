## Overview
This curriculum was created by Blakeley Payne for middle school students. It joins technical AI ideas, such as training a simple classifier, with ethics such as algorithmic bias. Students treat algorithms as opinions, name stakeholders, and redesign YouTube's recommendation algorithm. They finish with a mural of their hopes and concerns about AI.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn that an algorithm reflects choices made by people.
- Students practice naming who is helped and who is harmed by a system.
- Students express their views about AI through a class mural.

## Benefits for teachers
- A news report describes a pilot with 225 students in grades 5 to 8.
- The activities mix discussion, design, and art, which suit many teaching styles.
- The materials also exist in German, Portuguese, and Korean.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free to access.
- The vendor does not say which devices are needed.
- No installation is described. IT load looks low.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a simple classifier that students train to see how data shapes results. The materials also look at recommendation systems and algorithmic bias. The vendor does not say where any AI tool runs. Students use the activities to ask who made a system and whose opinions it carries.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which tools or accounts students need. They do not say where student work goes. The materials are hosted as a Google Doc. The classifier activity may use a tool that the research did not identify. A teacher should check that activity for an online tool and review its privacy terms. The 225 student figure comes from a news report and not a peer-reviewed study.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train a simple classifier with a small set of examples and test it on new ones.
2. Change the training examples on purpose and watch how the results shift.
3. Explain in writing why a classifier can be biased.
4. Draw a diagram of how a recommendation algorithm picks what to show.
5. Redesign a recommendation algorithm and list what data it would use.

### social_studies
1. Treat an algorithm as an opinion and name who holds that opinion.
2. List the stakeholders of a video site and describe what each one wants.
3. Debate whether an algorithm can ever be fair to everyone.
4. Interview a family member about how they use recommendations.
5. Write a short letter to a company about a concern with its algorithm.

### library_media
1. Compare what two students see in a recommendation list and discuss why.
2. Build a display of questions to ask about any AI tool.
3. Paint a class mural of hopes and concerns about AI.
4. Write a short guide to help younger readers check where a recommendation comes from.
5. Present the redesigned YouTube algorithm to another class.

## Overview
Tinkercad is a free browser-based tool from Autodesk for 3D design, electronics circuits, and code blocks. Teachers can create Classrooms and student accounts. Designs can be exported for 3D printing. Classrooms let teachers assign activities, collect work, and track progress, and they work with Google Classroom.

## Benefits for students
- Students design 3D objects in the browser.
- Students build and test circuits on screen.
- Students use code blocks to control a design.

## Benefits for teachers
- Classrooms let teachers assign activities and collect work.
- A dashboard tracks student progress.
- Free lesson plans and starter projects for 3D CAD are available.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free.
- It runs in a browser with WebGL on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, Linux, and iPad.
- A district may sign the Autodesk data privacy agreement first.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open Tinkercad in a browser and create a teacher Autodesk account.
- [ ] Ask your district whether it has signed the Autodesk data privacy agreement.
- [ ] Create a Classroom and choose a grade level and subject.
- [ ] Add students one by one or by CSV.
- [ ] Share the class code or link. Students get generated nicknames.
- [ ] Students may sign in with a school Google account.
- [ ] Try a free starter project.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in the materials read. Students design and test their own models in the browser. The work runs in Autodesk's cloud.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are required and the internet is needed at all times. Account data and student work are stored on Autodesk servers. A school district page says Classrooms default to Safe Mode, which keeps designs private. The same page says children under 13 need parental or guardian consent, which is a consent rule. Search snippets say Autodesk signs district data privacy agreements, but the research could not read Autodesk's own privacy statement or the agreement. Check the signed district agreement and keep Safe Mode on.

## Overview
Shark AI is a free, optional online curriculum with five modules for grades 6 to 8. Students classify fossil shark teeth in Google Teachable Machine. They collect data, train and evaluate image models, and study dataset bias and limits. An NSF-funded University of Florida team built it with teachers.

## Benefits for students
- Students train a real image classifier with their own photos.
- Students learn how bias and limits show up in data.
- Students connect AI to a fossil science topic.

## Benefits for teachers
- The five modules guide the whole project.
- It lists Florida middle school science standards.
- Teachers attend a summer workshop and share the curriculum with peers.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum is free.
- It runs in a browser and works on Chromebooks.
- A webcam or uploaded photos are needed. No IT work is needed.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is an image classification model trained in Teachable Machine in the browser. It runs on the local device. Students collect data, train the model, test it, and study where it fails.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is needed to load the tools. Students train image models in Teachable Machine in the browser. The research did not verify where training images and models are stored. The research found no privacy policy for the curriculum. A teacher should avoid photos of faces and should check the Teachable Machine terms.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Sort photos of fossil shark teeth by shape and describe each group.
2. Collect at least ten photos of each tooth type for the model.
3. Test the model on a new tooth and compare with an expert key.
4. Explain how a tooth shape shows what a shark ate.
5. Write a short report on what the model could and could not tell apart.

### computer_science
1. Train an image model in Teachable Machine with two tooth classes.
2. Add more photos and record how the accuracy changes.
3. Test the model on a tooth from a class it has not seen.
4. Find a bias in your data and suggest a fix.
5. Explain the steps from collecting photos to making a prediction.

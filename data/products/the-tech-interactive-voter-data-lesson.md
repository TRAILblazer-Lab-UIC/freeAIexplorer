## Overview
Students analyze, map, and graph voter turnout data in CODAP. They look for patterns and then act as data journalists. The data comes from international datasets and the US Census Bureau. The Tech Interactive page lists the lesson at 90 minutes for grades 9 to 12.

## Benefits for students
- Students explore real voter data and see patterns across countries.
- Students graph and map the data in CODAP.
- Students write a data story as a journalist.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson plan PDF is ready to download.
- Country voting case cards support the lesson, per a search snippet.
- The lesson needs little preparation.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- It runs in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
- The internet is needed at all times.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no AI. Students do the data analysis and pattern finding themselves. They build skills in graphing and spotting patterns.

## Privacy and safety
CODAP is a web tool and the internet is needed at all times. The lesson pages mention no account. The research did not review the CODAP privacy terms. The materials do not state what data CODAP collects. Check the CODAP privacy terms and the school's rules for web tools. Watch for sensitive political topics in class.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Analyze voter turnout by country and describe the patterns.
2. Write a data story as a journalist on one finding.
3. Compare two countries using the case cards.
4. Name a reason that turnout may differ between places.
5. Present your story to the class.

### math
1. Graph turnout data in CODAP and describe the shape.
2. Map the data and explain what the colors show.
3. Compare the median and the mean for turnout.
4. Spot an outlier and explain what it may mean.
5. Make a chart that tells your story clearly.

## Overview
Orbit Odyssey is an open source camp and outreach game for the XRP robot made by SparkFun, FIRST, and educators. A search summary says it has 17 modular lessons on robotics, engineering design, and professionalism, plus field CAD files and printable worksheets. A SparkFun camp guide describes team sessions of 5, 8, or 14 hours with teams of 2.

## Benefits for students
- Students work in teams of two on a robot game.
- Students plan game strategy and program the robot.
- Students play robot matches.

## Benefits for teachers
- Lessons are modular and can be mixed.
- Worksheets and field CAD files are provided.
- The camp guide gives 5, 8, or 14 hour plans.

## Benefits for schools
- Lessons are described as free.
- The XRP robot costs about 119.95 USD, per a search summary, with a FIRST team discount.
- Setup takes days.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find Orbit Odyssey on the SparkFun site.
- [ ] Choose the 5, 8, or 14 hour plan.
- [ ] Get XRP robots, one for each team of two.
- [ ] Print the field and worksheets.
- [ ] Teams program the robot for the game.
- [ ] Test Bluetooth control before the matches.
- [ ] Plan a short round of robot matches.

## Where is the AI?
The materials have no AI. Teams write code for autonomous and Bluetooth control. They build skills in sequencing and logic.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether an account or internet is needed. The SparkFun pages returned an error. Details come from search results. A teacher should check the code editor and any sign-in, and should check school network rules for Bluetooth.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Study the field and plan a strategy for scoring.
2. Test the robot on the field and note problems.
3. Use CAD files to build a field piece.
4. Work in a team of two and share roles.
5. Review a match and plan changes.

### computer_science
1. Write an autonomous program for the game.
2. Add Bluetooth control to the robot.
3. Change a number and test the effect.
4. Compare an autonomous run to a driven run.
5. Debug a program that misses its target.

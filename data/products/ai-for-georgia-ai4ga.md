## Overview
AI for Georgia, or AI4GA, is a middle school curriculum funded by the NSF. It centers on a 9-week elective called Living and Working with Artificial Intelligence. It comes with in-person and online teacher training. Students sort robots from non-robots, study self-driving cars, try face filters, and plan a Dream Bot.

## Benefits for students
- Students try AI ideas through hands-on browser demos and Scratch.
- Students learn how perception, search, and route finding work.
- Students study the impact and ethics of AI and meet AI career cards.

## Benefits for teachers
- Unit pages on Google Sites give slides and forms that teachers can copy.
- Teacher training is publicly available and comes in person or online.
- The setup takes minutes and needs no IT help.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum and teacher training are described as publicly available. The vendor states no price.
- Units use Google, Nearpod, Kahoot, Blooket, and Scratch. IT may need to approve them.
- The activities need printed test patterns for the perception lesson.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the AI4GA unit pages on Google Sites.
- [ ] Ask IT whether Google, Nearpod, Kahoot, Blooket, and Scratch are approved.
- [ ] Copy the slides and forms for Unit 1.
- [ ] Print the test patterns for the perception activity.
- [ ] Try the Robot or Not lesson yourself.
- [ ] Try the Face Mesh browser demo and the Scratch face filter.
- [ ] Plan the 9-week sequence or choose single units.

## Where is the AI?
The AI sits in browser demos and in Scratch projects. Students see perception, face detection, search, and route finding at work. Students then adjust the demos and projects to see how the results change. They also discuss ethics and fairness.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether accounts are needed or where student data goes. Unit 1 is hosted on Google Sites, Slides, Docs, Drive, and Forms. It also links to Nearpod, Kahoot, Blooket, and Scratch. The activities need the internet during use. The Face Mesh demo runs in the browser. A teacher should check the terms of each service with the school and test whether the demo asks for a camera.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Sort pictures into robot and not robot, then explain the rule you used.
2. Label the parts of a self-driving car and tell which parts sense the world.
3. Build a face filter in Scratch after exploring the Face Mesh demo.
4. Color a map with graph coloring and use tree search to find a route.
5. Plan a My Dream Bot and list the sensors it would need.

### social_studies
1. Discuss how self-driving cars could change a town and who benefits.
2. Read the AI career cards and interview a family member about how AI touches their work.
3. Debate whether a face filter app should store pictures of its users.
4. Make a poster about one fair and one unfair use of AI.
5. Write a short class rule list for living and working with AI.

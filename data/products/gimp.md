## Overview
GIMP is a free, open-source raster image editor. Users retouch photos, create original artwork, and design graphics with layers, brushes, and filters. The current stable release is 3.2.8. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and suits middle and high school art classes.

## Benefits for students
- Students retouch photos and make original artwork.
- Students learn layers, brushes, and filters used in real image work.
- Students keep their files on the school computer.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers describe students retouching images in art classes.
- The tool has no account and no sign-in.
- Free software means students can use the same tool at home.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and open source under the GPL.
- It runs offline after installation.
- It is not a Chromebook app, and IT may need to approve the install on managed devices.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download GIMP from its official site.
- [ ] Check that computers run Windows 10 1903 or newer, macOS 11 or newer, or a current Linux system.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve and install it.
- [ ] Open a sample photo and try cropping and brightness.
- [ ] Practice with layers on a simple image.
- [ ] Decide where students save their files.
- [ ] Teach one short task before the full project.

## Where is the AI?
GIMP has no AI. It builds visual design skills. Students work with layers, color, and composition. The program runs on the device.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the internet is used only for setup. The app runs locally and files stay on the device. The research did not read a privacy policy for the desktop app. A teacher should set rules for which photos students may edit. Photos of classmates need permission. Sources disagree on a suitable age. Check your school policy.

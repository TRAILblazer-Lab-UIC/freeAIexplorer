## Overview
This lesson by Jessie McKinley on the ASU STEM Teachers site is for 3rd grade and upper elementary. Students use Scratch 3.0 and story elements to build a digital story to share with an audience. The official page returned an error. Details come from search listings. Independent lesson plans and two NSF-hosted studies describe similar Scratch storytelling.

## Benefits for students
- Students turn story elements into a program.
- Students practice setting and sequence.
- Students share their story with an audience.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson includes a story outline activity.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no AI. Students build skills in sequencing and events. They put scenes in order and make characters act at the right time.

## Privacy and safety
An account is optional in Scratch. The online version needs an account only to save to the cloud. Scratch Desktop saves files locally. The research did not confirm which version the lesson uses or whether the internet is needed. A teacher should decide whether students use the online version or Scratch Desktop. A teacher should also check where student projects are saved.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Plan a story with a setting, characters and a problem.
2. Write a short outline for the story in order.
3. Use dialogue in a Scratch story to show what a character says.
4. Share the story with an audience and ask for feedback.
5. Revise the story after feedback.

### computer_science
1. Make a sprite move across the stage with blocks.
2. Change the backdrop to show a new scene.
3. Use a broadcast block to start the next scene.
4. Test the story from start to finish and fix any problems.
5. Explain how your blocks match the order of your story.

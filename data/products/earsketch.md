## Overview
EarSketch is a free web programming environment from Georgia Tech. Students write Python or JavaScript to create and remix music. It includes a code editor, a digital audio workstation, a sound browser, and a loop library. Research papers describe high school and undergraduate use, and a batch note says grades 6-12.

## Benefits for students
- Students hear their code turn into music.
- Students practice Python or JavaScript in a creative setting.
- Students remix loops and add effects with short commands.

## Benefits for teachers
- A curriculum aligned with AP Computer Science Principles is available.
- A teacher site offers materials.
- The tool needs no installation.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free.
- It runs in a browser, including on Chromebooks.
- Accounts are optional. The site is always online, and saved work likely goes to the vendor cloud.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the EarSketch site in a browser.
- [ ] Review the privacy terms with your school before students sign in.
- [ ] Open the teacher site and look at the curriculum materials.
- [ ] Try a sample script in the code editor and press play.
- [ ] Choose Python or JavaScript for your class.
- [ ] Decide whether students will create accounts to save work.
- [ ] Check that the audio plays on your student devices.

## Where is the AI?
EarSketch has no AI. It builds sequencing and pattern skills. Students arrange loops in order and repeat them with code.

## Privacy and safety
The site is always online, and the research did not find the privacy policy. Wikipedia says no account is required to create or view projects, but the research did not verify this. Scripts are reportedly saved to Georgia Tech servers when a user signs in, which was not verified. The materials do not say what student data the site collects. A teacher should read the privacy policy and decide whether students need accounts.

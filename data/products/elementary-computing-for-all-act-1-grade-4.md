## Overview
This grade 4 Scratch curriculum comes from UC Irvine. It adds linguistic scaffolds for multilingual learners and students with disabilities. It has five units: Scratch basics, Sequence (Name Poem), Events (About Me), Loop (Dance Party), and a Culture Cartoon project. Students modify existing projects before building their own.

## Benefits for students
- Students start by changing a project that already works.
- Students use Language Frames to explain their ideas.
- Students finish with a Culture Cartoon of their own.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers are typically new to Scratch, and the units guide them.
- A student workbook and assessments are included.
- Standards from ELA, CSTA, and ISTE are listed.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum is free.
- Students use Scratch in Chrome.
- Google Forms assessments are provided.

## Where is the AI?
This curriculum has no AI. It builds sequencing, event, and loop skills. Students order steps, respond to events, and repeat actions in Scratch.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need or where work is stored. Students use Scratch in a browser. The internet is always needed. The materials also use Google Docs, Slides, Forms, and Sites, and the research did not review Scratch data practices. Scratch Desktop could keep work local, but the page does not discuss it. A teacher should review the Scratch privacy terms and check how student projects are saved before class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Explore the Scratch screen with the scavenger hunt.
2. Modify a project to change what the sprite does.
3. Use a loop to build a Dance Party.
4. Use events to build an About Me project.
5. Plan and build a cumulative project with sequences, events, and loops.

### ela
1. Write a Name Poem and use Scratch to show each line in order.
2. Use Language Frames to explain what a project does.
3. Tell a story in a Culture Cartoon with at least two characters.
4. Write dialogue for the cartoon and read it aloud.
5. Present a project and answer a classmate's question using a sentence frame.

## Overview
Algodoo is a 2D physics sandbox. Users build and simulate scenes with simple drawing tools. The official site says it is now free, with optional donations. It runs on Windows, Mac and iPad.

## Benefits for students
- Students can draw machines, gears, motors, lenses and lasers.
- Students see motion simulated right away.
- Students can share creations in an online space called Algobox.

## Benefits for teachers
- Algoryx offers videos and webinars on building educational scenes.
- A set of lesson plans is available as a PDF.
- Teachers use free exploration to prompt class discussions.

## Benefits for schools
- Algodoo is free with optional donations.
- IT needs to install it on Windows or Mac, or install the iPad app.
- The vendor materials do not say what student data is collected.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the installer for Windows or Mac from the Algodoo site.
- [ ] Or install the iPad app from the App Store.
- [ ] Ask IT to install it on lab machines.
- [ ] Open the lesson plans PDF.
- [ ] Watch a vendor video on building scenes.
- [ ] Build a simple scene yourself.
- [ ] Decide whether students will use Algobox.

## Where is the AI?
Algodoo has no AI. It is a physics simulator. Students build skills in testing ideas and noticing patterns as they change a scene and watch the result.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether an account is needed or what data the software collects. The Algoryx privacy policy covers the algoryx.se website and does not name Algodoo or Algobox. The website uses Google Analytics, HubSpot, LinkedIn, and YouTube cookies. Algobox is an online sharing space. A teacher should check with the vendor what Algodoo and Algobox collect and decide whether students may share creations online.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Explore freely in Algodoo and write one question that the scene raises.
2. Build a ramp and compare how different objects roll down.
3. Change the gravity setting and describe the effect on motion.
4. Use a lens and laser to show how light bends.
5. Predict what a scene will do and then run it to check.

### engineering
1. Build a gear train and describe how it transfers motion.
2. Design a simple machine that moves a ball from one side to the other.
3. Add a motor to a scene and test the speed.
4. Improve a classmate's design after testing it.
5. Record your design steps and what you changed after each test.

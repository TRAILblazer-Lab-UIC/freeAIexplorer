## Overview
This is a five-unit drone course for the Robolink CoDrone EDU. The units are Rebuild Race, Drone Joust, Launch Autonomy Retrieval, Double Dare and Tandem Lift. Students move from assembly and flight to autonomous navigation and obstacle avoidance. They use block-based and text-based coding in high school classes.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and fly a real drone.
- Students move from flying by hand to autonomous flight.
- Students use block and text coding.

## Benefits for teachers
- Daily tasks last about 30 minutes.
- The course is hosted on CS2N.
- The catalog lists it as a Robotics Technician course.

## Benefits for schools
- The pages list no price. The CoDrone EDU is paid hardware.
- It needs an up-to-date Windows PC or Mac.
- Teachers also need tape, meter sticks and open space.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in this course. Students write block or text programs that the drone follows. Students build skills in sequencing, sensors and logic.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether accounts are required or whether the internet is needed. Course pages say access and tracking go through CS2N. The CS2N policy lists name, email, username, group membership, and course activity. It says it does not sell or rent personal information. It allows use by children under 13 with consent from a parent, school, district, or teacher. It does not name COPPA or say where data is stored. A teacher should read that policy and set clear flight rules for the space.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Assemble the drone in the Rebuild Race unit and record each step.
2. Test the drone with different weights and note the effect.
3. Compete in the Drone Joust and describe what helped.
4. Design a plan for a drone to lift a load with a partner.
5. Document a failed flight and name one design change.

### computer_science
1. Write a block program that makes the drone take off and land.
2. Program a path around an obstacle with text code.
3. Use a sensor reading to make the drone stop.
4. Compare a block program with a text program for the same flight.
5. Debug a program that sends the drone off course.

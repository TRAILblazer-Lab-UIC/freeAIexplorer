## Overview
MapMaker is a free browser mapping app for K-12 built by the National Geographic Society and Esri. It offers 2D maps and 3D globe views with curated maps, layers, and data. Students search places, sketch on maps, add layers, and build custom maps. Guided lessons cover geography, earth science, history, and life science.

## Benefits for students
- Students build custom maps and save them as PDFs or images.
- Students switch between 2D maps and a 3D globe.
- Students add layers of data to see patterns.

## Benefits for teachers
- Guided lessons and student activities cover world cultures and geography.
- A seventh grade global studies teacher reported classroom use at a 2026 Esri event.
- Educator resources are available.

## Benefits for schools
- The app is free.
- It works in any modern browser, including on Chromebooks.
- No install is needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the MapMaker site in a browser.
- [ ] Start without signing in.
- [ ] Try a guided lesson from the educator resources.
- [ ] Build a custom map and save it as a PDF.
- [ ] Use an ArcGIS sign in only to save and share interactive maps.
- [ ] Review the ArcGIS account terms with IT before students sign in.

## Where is the AI?
This app has no AI. It builds skills in reading maps and data layers. Students practice spotting patterns across places.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed to start, and the internet is needed at all times. Esri says MapMaker needs no student login and no tracking. An optional ArcGIS sign in saves and shares maps, and the data goes to the vendor. The research could not confirm child-specific privacy terms for that sign in. A teacher should decide whether students sign in and review the ArcGIS terms first.

1. Map the places in a global studies unit and add a layer that shows population.
2. Compare two regions and write about how geography shaped daily life.
3. Sketch a trade route on the map and explain the choices.
4. Build a custom map of a historic event.
5. Present a map to the class and explain each layer.

2. Map where a species lives and write about its habitat.
3. Compare a 2D map and a 3D globe for one topic.
4. Find a place with a high earthquake count and mark it.
5. Save a map as an image and use it in a lab report.

## Overview
This 2015 article by elementary art teacher Alecia Eggers Kaczmarek walks through a stop motion unit for the art room. It includes a PowerPoint introduction, a packet of worksheets, storyboard tools and rubrics, and a list of example videos. Students plan a story, build sets and characters, and film frames with a stop motion app. The author recommends the iMotion app.

## Benefits for students
- Students plan a story with a storyboard before they film.
- Students build sets and characters from markers, paper, poster board, and modeling clay.
- Students work in groups to film and finish a short animation.

## Benefits for teachers
- The PowerPoint, packet, and rubrics are described as free downloads.
- A list of example videos helps teachers preview what students will make.
- The author suggests making a short demo video first.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free. The cost of the app and art supplies is not stated.
- Students need a device with a stop motion app.
- The article does not say what the app does with student data.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Art of Education article and download the PowerPoint and packet.
- [ ] Preview the example stop motion videos.
- [ ] Choose a stop motion app. The author recommends iMotion.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the app on student devices.
- [ ] Make a short demo video with the app.
- [ ] Gather markers, paper, poster board, and modeling clay.
- [ ] Plan group sizes and teacher check-ins. The article does not give them.

## Where is the AI?
This unit has no AI. Students plan, build, and film an animation frame by frame. They practice sequencing by arranging many small changes into a story.

## Privacy and safety
The article does not say which accounts are needed or how the app handles video. Student films may show faces and voices. A teacher should check the privacy terms of the chosen app. A teacher should also decide where student videos are stored.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Draw a storyboard for a short animated story, using the packet's tools.
2. Build a character from modeling clay and make it move a little in each frame.
3. Paint a background set from poster board and paper.
4. Review two example videos and describe how the makers show movement.
5. Use the rubric to critique a classmate's finished animation.

### theater_media
1. Write a story with a beginning, a middle, and an end for a one-minute film.
2. Film the frames with the app and play them back to check the pacing.
3. Add sound or voices to match the action.
4. Take on roles in a group, such as director, filmmaker, and set builder.
5. Hold a screening and discuss what each group would change.

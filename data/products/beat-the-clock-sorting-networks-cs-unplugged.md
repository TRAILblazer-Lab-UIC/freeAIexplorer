## Overview
Beat the Clock is a CS Unplugged topic about sorting networks. Students walk through a network drawn on the ground to sort numbers at the same time. The page lists two challenges, each at three levels for ages 5-7, 8-10, and 11-14. Search results say the classic version uses chalk and number cards and runs about an hour.

## Benefits for students
- Students use their whole bodies to learn how a computer sorts numbers.
- Students practice comparing numbers and ordering them.
- Students try a junior version, then variations and counterexamples.

## Benefits for teachers
- The topic has three levels. One idea can stretch across several ages.
- The topic lists literacy and science integrations such as Retelling a story and Growing into a butterfly.
- The activity is easy to demonstrate outside.

## Benefits for schools
- The activity is free, and the content is open source.
- It needs only chalk or tape and number cards.
- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Sorting networks topic on the CS Unplugged site.
- [ ] Choose the level that fits your class.

- [ ] Prepare a set of number cards.

- [ ] Time the class and try to beat the clock.

## Where is the AI?
This topic has no AI. It builds an understanding of data and sorting. Students see how comparing pairs of numbers can put a whole list in order.

## Privacy and safety
The activity is physical and no accounts were seen on the topic page. The research found no student data collection. The internet is used to download the page. A teacher should check that the chalked network is drawn on a safe, dry surface.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Walk a sorting network with number cards and check that the numbers end in order.
2. Run the network with a different set of numbers and compare the results.
3. Look for a set of numbers that breaks a faulty network.
4. Time the class and discuss why sorting in parallel is faster.
5. Explain to a partner what each meeting point in the network does.

### math
1. Compare two numbers at each meeting point and decide which goes left.
2. Order a set of cards from smallest to largest by walking the network.
3. Add a new number to the set and predict where it ends.
4. Try negative numbers or decimals on the cards.
5. Write the order of a list before and after the walk.

### science
1. Order the stages of a butterfly life cycle using the Growing into a butterfly integration.
2. Sort animals by size using a sorting network.
3. Compare sorting by hand with sorting by walking.
4. Record how long each run takes and compare the times.
5. Sort objects by weight and check the order with a scale.

## Overview
CodeMonkey is an online game-based coding platform. Students write block and text code, including CoffeeScript and Python. Courses include CodeMonkey Jr., Coding Adventure, Game Builder, Banana Tales, Coding Chatbots, data science and AI, and high school courses such as AP CSP. The homepage cites ages 5 to 14 and also mentions K-12 for schools.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn through games with blocks first and then text code.
- Students can try AI and data science courses.
- Students move from early coding to AP CSP on one platform.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teacher plans add a dashboard, lesson plans, and automatic grading.
- Teachers praise the lesson plans per Common Sense Education and EdTech Impact.
- Students can join with a class code or single sign-on.

## Benefits for schools
- Prices are not listed on the homepage. The free trial includes part of Coding Adventure, Banana Tales, and Hour of Code content.
- Sign-in works with Google Classroom, Clever, ClassLink, and Microsoft.
- IT will likely need to approve the platform.

## Where is the AI?
CodeMonkey offers AI and data science courses and an image-to-vector AI widget. The vendor does not say where the AI runs. Students explore AI ideas in the courses and then use them in their own projects.

## Privacy and safety
Every student needs an account, and the internet is needed at all times. The vendor collects usernames, display names, passwords, optional age, SSO email and ID, saved games, progress, time on site, and IP addresses. Data is stored with AWS. The policy states membership in the PRIVO COPPA Safe Harbor program and requires parental consent for users 12 and under. It says it does not sell personal information. Its cookie tables also list advertising and tracking cookies from Facebook, LinkedIn, Google DoubleClick, and others. It does not mention FERPA. Images in the AI widget are converted to vectors and not stored, per the policy. A teacher should read the privacy policy with the school and ask IT which cookies apply to student pages.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Complete a CodeMonkey Jr. level and explain the blocks you used.
2. Solve a Coding Adventure level and rewrite the solution with fewer commands.
3. Build a simple game in Game Builder.
4. Try a chatbot course and write three replies for it.
5. Compare a block solution with its text code.

### math
1. Use coordinates in a game level to move a character to a target.
2. Predict how many steps a loop takes and then run it.
3. Change a number in a program and see how the result changes.
4. Use a data science course to chart a small data set.
5. Write a program that adds a list of numbers.

## Overview
SolveSpace is a free parametric 3D CAD tool under the GPLv3 license. It also handles 2D parts and plane and solid geometry. Users build extrusions, revolves, helixes, and Boolean operations. They can export STL for 3D printing and DXF, PDF, or SVG for cutters.

## Benefits for students
- Students build 3D parts from sketches and constraints.
- Students export STL files for 3D printing.
- Students can simulate linkages with a constraint solver.

## Benefits for teachers
- SolveSpace tutorials build a bracket, an assembly, and linkages.
- The tool covers 2D and 3D work.
- No IT involvement is listed.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and open source.
- It runs on the computer or as a web version.
- IT approval may be needed for installs.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the program from solvespace.com or open the web version.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install if needed.
- [ ] Open the SolveSpace tutorials.
- [ ] Build the bracket from the first tutorial.
- [ ] Export an STL file.
- [ ] Try the linkage tutorial.

## Where is the AI?
SolveSpace has no AI. A constraint solver keeps a sketch consistent with the rules students set. Students build logic skills as they add constraints.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The program is a local desktop application. A web version also exists, and the research did not review it. The research found no privacy policy. A teacher should check that students save files to a folder that the school backs up.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Build the bracket from the SolveSpace tutorial and export it as STL.
2. Design a small part and dimension it with constraints.
3. Simulate a linkage and watch how the parts move.
4. Export a shape as DXF for a cutter.
5. Present a design and explain each constraint.

### math
1. Sketch a triangle and constrain its sides to given lengths.
2. Build a solid and describe its faces and edges.
3. Use a revolve to make a cylinder and calculate its volume.
4. Check an angle with a constraint.
5. Compare two plane shapes of the same area.

## Overview
Sandboxels is a free falling-sand simulator that runs in a web browser. It has over 500 elements and simulates heat, electricity, and chemical reactions. Students place and mix materials to see how they react in real time. The developer lists education uses in biology, chemistry, ecology, geology, and cooking.

## Benefits for students
- Students try ideas and see results at once.
- Students explore heat, electricity, and reactions with no goals or levels.
- Students can test many materials without a lab.

## Benefits for teachers
- The developer suggests density tests and baking soda and vinegar reactions.
- It takes little time to set up.
- Teachers should preview the site before class.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free in the browser.
- It runs on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- School filters may block the site or its ads.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Sandboxels website in a browser.
- [ ] Ask IT to check whether the school filter allows the site.
- [ ] Ask IT to review the ads and analytics that the site uses.
- [ ] Visit once while online and click Install Offline in Chrome.
- [ ] Try a density test with alcohol and other liquids.
- [ ] Try baking soda and vinegar.
- [ ] Write a short lab sheet for students.

## Where is the AI?
Sandboxels has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is pattern spotting. Students watch how materials behave and form rules from what they see. That practice is a base for scientific modeling.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the site can be installed for offline use. The R74n privacy policy says its sites use Google Analytics and show personalized Google AdSense ads. The policy does not address children. The optional Discord and Steam Workshop tabs follow those companies' policies. The research did not find how saving works. A teacher should check the site on a school device and use the offline install where possible.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Run the developer's density test and compare how alcohol and other liquids settle.
2. Mix baking soda and vinegar and describe the reaction.
3. Burn methane and compare flame colors.
4. Heat two materials and record which changes first.
5. Write a claim about a material and test it in the sandbox.

## Overview
The ArcGIS for Schools Bundle is a no-cost Esri software package for K-12 instruction. It gives teachers and students Creator accounts to ArcGIS Online. It also includes ArcGIS Pro Basic on Windows and apps such as StoryMaps, Survey123, Dashboards and National Geographic MapMaker. Districts apply through a request form and manage the software and accounts.

## Benefits for students
- Students make maps and analyze data in a browser.
- Students tell place-based stories with StoryMaps.
- Students collect field data with Survey123.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers get group sharing tools.
- Teachers can use ready apps such as MapMaker.
- Use is limited to K-12 instruction.

## Benefits for schools
- The bundle costs nothing for eligible US districts.
- A pre-signed Data Processing Addendum is available.
- Districts must manage accounts and software, and IT staff should lead the request.

## Getting started
- [ ] Ask district IT staff or a curriculum lead to submit the request form.
- [ ] Confirm the district is in the US or a US territory and has no existing accounts.
- [ ] Wait for contact, which the vendor says happens within a week.
- [ ] Review the Data Processing Addendum.
- [ ] Set up teacher and student accounts.
- [ ] Install ArcGIS Pro Basic on Windows machines if needed.
- [ ] Try a first map with the class.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor materials do not describe any AI in this bundle. Students build skills in data and mapping as they layer information and read patterns. They use maps to ask and answer questions.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are required and the work lives in the vendor cloud. Student accounts and uploaded or collected field data are stored, but the specific fields are not stated. Esri offers a pre-signed Data Processing Addendum. The page describes no other privacy protections and the research did not read the privacy policy. A public ArcGIS account is separate and for adults only. A teacher should read the Data Processing Addendum and check what students may upload.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Make a map of your town and mark places that matter to your community.
2. Build a StoryMap about a historical event and place.
3. Compare two maps of the same region from different years.
4. Map the places in a book or case study.
5. Use MapMaker to explain how geography shapes where people live.

### science
1. Collect field data with Survey123 about plants near the school.
2. Map the data and look for patterns.
3. Build a dashboard to show results for the class.
4. Compare your data with a public data layer.
5. Present a claim from the map and the evidence behind it.

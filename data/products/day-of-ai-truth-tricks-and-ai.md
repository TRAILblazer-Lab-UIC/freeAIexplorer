## Overview
Truth, Tricks, and AI is a one lesson Day of AI unit of about one hour for ages 11 and up. Students examine AI hallucinations, deepfakes, and biased outputs. They practice lateral reading and the Rule of Three to check claims. The page notes that it may need two or more class periods.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn how to spot an AI hallucination.
- Students practice lateral reading to check a claim.
- Students use the Rule of Three to confirm what they find.

## Benefits for teachers
- A Google Slides deck, an educator guide, and student resources are included.
- The skills fit media literacy and research units.
- Teachers need no technical background.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- Students need a browser.
- The materials do not say which accounts students need.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson teaches about AI hallucinations, deepfakes, and bias. No AI tool is required in the lesson. Students study examples of AI errors and then use lateral reading to check the facts. The skill helps them judge any AI answer they see later.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Read a short claim and open new tabs to check who is behind it, using lateral reading.
2. Apply the Rule of Three and find three sources that agree or disagree.
3. Write a short paragraph that explains why a claim is true or false.
4. Compare how two articles frame the same event.
5. Rewrite a biased sentence in a more neutral way.

### social_studies
1. Study a deepfake example and explain how it could mislead voters.
2. Trace a viral claim back to its first source.
3. List the steps a citizen could use to check a news story.
4. Debate who should be responsible for stopping fake media.
5. Create a short class guide on spotting misinformation.

### library_media
1. Practice lateral reading on three websites and note which ones are reliable.
2. Test an AI answer and mark any hallucination you can find.
3. Build a checklist for verifying a claim.
4. Compare the results of two search tools on the same question.
5. Teach the Rule of Three to a younger class.

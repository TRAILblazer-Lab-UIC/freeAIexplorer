## Overview
EduLogicSim is a digital logic simulator for middle and high school, built in Python. Students build and test circuits with basic gates, flip-flops, and input sources. They see live signal monitoring and waveform views. It runs on Windows 10 or 11 from a downloaded zip file.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and test logic circuits on screen.
- Students watch signals change in live waveforms.
- Students work through six step-by-step lessons.

## Benefits for teachers
- Six built-in lessons run from number systems to simple memory.
- Lessons cover gates, truth tables, flip-flops, counters, and registers.
- The tool runs from a downloaded folder.

## Benefits for schools
- Neither the page nor the GitHub release states a price.
- The app is not code-signed, and Windows SmartScreen may warn. IT approval may be needed.
- It is a single-developer project with no independent coverage and no license listed.

## Where is the AI?
EduLogicSim has no AI. The tool is a logic simulator that runs on the local computer. It builds logic and sequencing skills. Students link gates into circuits and predict their outputs.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used for the download. The site has no privacy policy and does not describe data collection. The data the tool collects is unknown. The app is not code signed, and the repository declares no license. A teacher should ask IT to review the unsigned app, and check the license, before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow the number systems lesson and convert binary numbers to decimal.
2. Build a circuit for an AND gate and fill in its truth table.
3. Use gates to add two single-bit numbers.
4. Build a flip-flop and watch it hold a value.
5. Build a small counter and read its waveform.

### engineering
1. Design a circuit that turns on an output for a chosen input pattern.
2. Test a design with several inputs and record the results.
3. Compare two circuits that give the same output and pick the simpler one.
4. Build a register and explain how it stores bits.
5. Build a simple memory cell and describe how it works.

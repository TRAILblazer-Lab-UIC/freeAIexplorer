## Overview
Kerbal Space Program is a commercial spaceflight simulation game. Players build rockets, reach orbit, plan transfers, and land on moons. An instructor at Swarthmore College used it to teach Kepler's laws and orbital dynamics. It is sold on Steam, GOG, Epic, and consoles.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn orbits by trying to reach one.
- Students build rockets and see why a design succeeds or fails.
- Students can plan a trip to a moon and test the plan.

## Benefits for teachers
- A college course used it to demonstrate Kepler's laws and as an optional Mun landing project.
- The source for this use is a college level paper. It is anecdotal.
- The game suits teachers who enjoy gaming.

## Benefits for schools
- Steam listed the game at $9.99 on sale and $39.99 at full price. Expansions cost extra and prices change.
- It needs a desktop computer that can run a 3D game.
- IT must install it on each machine.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI. The game runs a physics simulation on the computer. Students build skills in modeling and testing as they change a rocket design and watch the result.

## Privacy and safety
The official site states no privacy policy or account requirements. The game is bought through stores such as Steam, which require their own accounts. Store and publisher data practices were not reviewed. The game runs on the device, and the internet is used for setup. A teacher should check the store account and the platform terms before students play. A teacher should also set time limits for play.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Fly a rocket to orbit and describe how speed and height link up, as the Swarthmore course did for Kepler's laws.
2. Record the time of one orbit at two heights and compare.
3. Plan a transfer to a moon and explain the steps.
4. Predict where a rocket will land and test the prediction.
5. Write a short mission report with the data.

### engineering
1. Build a rocket that reaches a set height.
2. Change one part and compare the flight.
3. Redesign a rocket that tips over.
4. Plan a mission with a fuel budget.
5. Present the final design and the lessons learned.

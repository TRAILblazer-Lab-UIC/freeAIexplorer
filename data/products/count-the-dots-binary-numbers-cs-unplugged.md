## Overview
The CS Unplugged binary numbers topic teaches how computers store information with 0s and 1s. Students hold cards with 1, 2, 4, and 8 dots. They show or hide the cards to make numbers and count in binary. The topic has lessons for ages 5 to 7 and 8 to 10, and the junior lesson runs 45 minutes.

## Benefits for students
- Students act as bits and see binary work with their own hands.
- Students count from 0 to 15 in pairs.
- Students link binary to art, music, and writing.

## Benefits for teachers
- The topic lists integrations such as Binary Name Necklaces, Binary Patterns, and Binary Tunes.
- Independent plans use the dot cards with about five students at the front.
- The topic covers two age ranges.

## Benefits for schools
- The content is free and uses a CC BY-SA 4.0 license.
- It needs printed cards and a number line from 0 to 20.
- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds an understanding of data, which is how computers store numbers as 0s and 1s. Students learn that every file on a device is stored this way.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson runs on paper. The site mentions no account or login, and the research found no student data collected. The internet is used only to print the cards. A teacher should check that the card sets are complete before class. The vendor materials do not describe other safety steps.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Hold up the dot cards to show how a computer stores a number.
2. Act as bits and let the class read the number you make.
3. Count from 0 to 15 in binary with a partner.
4. Use binary to write a code for a letter.
5. Explain in your own words why computers use 0s and 1s.

### math
1. Add up the dots on the visible cards to find each number.
2. Predict the next number in a binary count.
3. Show the same number in two ways and compare.
4. Find the pattern in the columns of a binary table.
5. Make a number from 0 to 15 and challenge a partner to read it.

### visual_arts
1. Make a Binary Name Necklace with two bead colors for 0 and 1.
2. Create a Binary Pattern with dark and light squares.
3. Design a bracelet that spells a short word in binary.
4. Compare patterns and describe how they look.
5. Display your art with a key that shows the code.

### music
1. Perform a Binary Tune where a clap is a 1 and a rest is a 0.
2. Write a rhythm of 1s and 0s and swap it with a partner.
3. Compose a short tune from a binary number.
4. Compare two rhythms and describe the difference.
5. Teach your tune to the class using the code.

### ela
1. Write a message in binary code for a partner to decode.
2. Use binary codes for letters to spell your name.
3. Write a short story about a bit that lives in a computer.
4. Describe in sentences how you counted in binary.
5. Read a classmate's binary message aloud.

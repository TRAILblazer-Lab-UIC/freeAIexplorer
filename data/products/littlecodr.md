## Overview
Littlecodr is a physical card game for ages 4-8, created by Nathan Slee and Dr. Alexandra Greenhill and funded on Kickstarter in 2015. Children use Action, Mission, and Lab cards to lay out a sequence of movement instructions that gets a person from point A to point B. They then debug the sequence. A search result lists the box as 80 Action Cards and 20 Mission Cards for 2 or more players.

## Benefits for students
- Children practice sequencing with a game.
- Children program a real person to move across a room.
- Children learn to debug a card sequence.

## Benefits for teachers
- The game needs almost no setup.
- A retailer reviewer reports using the cards with K-2 children for unplugged coding lessons.
- Two or more players can play at once.

## Where is the AI?
This game has no AI. Children lay out cards that tell a person where to move. They build sequencing and debugging skills.

## Privacy and safety
The game is on paper and collects no student data. No account or internet is needed. A teacher should clear the play space of hazards. A teacher should also check availability since the retailer page was not read.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Lay out Action cards to guide a classmate from point A to point B.
2. Draw a Mission card and plan a path around the obstacles.
3. Follow another group's cards and note where the path fails.
4. Fix the sequence with a Lab card or a new order.
5. Plan a sequence that uses the fewest cards.

## Overview
Project GUTS is an integrated science and computer science program for middle school students. Modules are week-long and aligned to NGSS and CSTA standards. Students build agent-based models in StarLogo Nova, a block-based modeling tool from MIT. Code.org describes four modules in its Computer Science in Science partnership.

## Benefits for students
- Students model real science ideas with blocks.
- Students watch many agents interact and see patterns.
- Students practice science and computing together.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teacher workshops and online courses support the program.
- A Teachers with GUTS network is available.
- The Code.org welcome kit mentioned Flash. Some materials may be dated.

## Benefits for schools
- Colorado lists it as free, according to a search snippet. The official page states no price.
- Access terms conflict across sources. Confirm them.
- StarLogo Nova runs in a browser.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in this program. Students build agent-based models with blocks. They build data and pattern spotting skills as they run a model many times.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need or where student data goes. The official page does not address privacy. A search snippet says StarLogo Nova projects are hosted on slnova.org, and the research did not verify account needs there. StarLogo Nova needs the internet at all times. A teacher should check the account setup and privacy terms before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Build a model of how a disease spreads and run it several times, as a week-long module does.
2. Change one rule in a model of a food web and record the result.
3. Compare model results with a real data set.
4. Predict how a population will change and test your prediction.
5. Present what a model shows and where it falls short.

### computer_science
1. Use blocks to program an agent that moves and turns.
2. Add a rule that makes agents interact.
3. Debug a model that gives odd results.
4. Explain how a block maps to an action in the model.
5. Compare two versions of your model and describe the difference.

## Overview
This is a DFRobot classroom kit built around the HuskyLens AI vision sensor and micro:bit programming. The bundle includes a HuskyLens, a micro:bit V2, an IO extender, 7 external modules, and 28 teaching cards. The tutorial has 12 lessons and 25 projects. Search results list ages 8 to 19, and the wiki gives no age range.

## Benefits for students
- Students program an AI vision sensor in blocks.
- Students build projects such as face-recognition access control, a colorful piano, and an object tracker.
- Teaching cards cover faces, colors, objects, barcodes, QR codes, and tracking.

## Benefits for teachers
- The tutorial gives 12 lessons in order.
- The 28 teaching cards support hands-on activities.
- The kit suits new AI learners.

## Benefits for schools
- Prices vary. Little Bird lists the bundle at 242.90 AUD, and the pack without hardware at about 93 AUD.
- No networking is part of the kit, which suggests it works with no internet.
- The sensor processes images itself, though the pages read make no data flow statement.

## Where is the AI?
The HuskyLens sensor recognizes faces, objects, colors, and QR codes and tracks objects. It runs on the device. Students train the sensor on what to recognize and then program the micro:bit to react. They learn how a machine learns from examples.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and no networking is part of the kit. The HuskyLens processes images on the sensor. The pages read make no data flow statement. On device operation is inferred from the product design. Face recognition projects point a camera at people. A teacher should set rules about who is recorded and what the sensor learns.

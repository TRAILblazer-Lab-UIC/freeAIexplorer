## Overview
Kolibri is an offline first learning platform installed on a local server. Student devices connect over a local network with no internet. Content is downloaded once and shared by peer to peer transfer or an external drive. The library holds materials in over 173 languages for K-12 subjects such as STEM, public health, coding, and life skills.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn without a steady internet connection.
- Students find lessons and quizzes in many languages.
- Students keep their own progress on a learner account.

## Benefits for teachers
- Coaches see reports on quiz scores, lesson progress, and time spent.
- The library covers K-12 subjects.
- Teachers choose which content to import.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free. Hardware for a server is a separate cost.
- It runs on low cost and legacy devices.
- IT must run the server and the school network.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find Kolibri on the Kolibri site.
- [ ] Ask IT to install it on a school server or computer.
- [ ] Import content channels while online or from a USB drive.
- [ ] Create learner, coach, and admin accounts.
- [ ] Decide if the optional Data Portal sync will be on. Administrators set it.
- [ ] Test a student device on the school network.
- [ ] Plan a first lesson from the library.

## Where is the AI?
Kolibri has no AI. It delivers lessons and quizzes from a library. Students build skills in the subject they study, and teachers read the progress reports to spot patterns.

## Privacy and safety
Learner accounts are required. The platform stores accounts, progress, and optionally demographics on the school server. According to a search snippet of the privacy policy, app data is not shared unless the user syncs it to the Kolibri Data Portal. The same snippet says telemetry pings send aggregated anonymized statistics and interaction records may be used for research. Kolibri Studio has a separate policy, and the policy text itself was not read. A teacher should ask IT who can see learner records. A teacher should also check whether the Data Portal sync is turned on.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Work through a K-12 math lesson from the library and take the quiz.
2. Review the quiz score and redo the weak topics.
3. Find a math video in a second language and compare terms.
4. Pick a lesson for a partner and explain the answer.
5. Track your own progress for a week and write a short note.

### science
1. Complete a science lesson from the library and take the quiz.
2. Explain one idea to a partner in your own words.
3. Compare two lessons on the same topic.
4. Write three questions for a classmate about a lesson.
5. Review your coach report and set a goal.

### health_pe
1. Read a public health lesson and list three key points.
2. Take a quiz on a health topic.
3. Make a poster that explains one health habit.
4. Compare health tips from two lessons.
5. Write a plan for a daily healthy routine.

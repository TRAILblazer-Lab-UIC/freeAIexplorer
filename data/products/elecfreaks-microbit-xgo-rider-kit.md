## Overview
The XGO Rider is a palm-sized two-wheel-legged robot that balances itself. It has an ESP32 controller with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, brushless wheel motors, servos with encoders and an IMU sensor for balance. Students program it with MakeCode, MicroBlocks or Python. They can also drive it from the XGO mobile app over Bluetooth. A micro:bit is not included.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a robot that keeps its own balance.
- Students choose between blocks and Python.
- Students can drive the robot from a phone app and then write their own control.

## Benefits for teachers
- The robot supports several programming tools. Teachers can match the tool to the class.
- The battery lasts over 3 hours and charges by Type-C.
- The ELECFREAKS wiki has a page for the kit.

## Benefits for schools
- RobotShop lists the kit at 336.80 USD without a micro:bit, as a special order.
- The vendor page recommends adult supervision.
- The kit needs a computer with a browser and a separate micro:bit.

## Getting started
- [ ] Order the XGO Rider kit from a retailer such as RobotShop.
- [ ] Order a micro:bit separately.
- [ ] Charge the robot by Type-C.
- [ ] Add the micro:bit to the robot.
- [ ] Open the MakeCode editor in a browser and add the XGO extension.
- [ ] Flash a first program over USB.
- [ ] Try the XGO mobile app over Bluetooth.

## Where is the AI?
The XGO Rider has no AI in the materials. The robot balances with an IMU sensor and its control code. Students build skills in sensors and feedback by watching how the robot reacts to a push.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed for the robot, and the internet is used at setup. The XGO mobile app drives the robot over Bluetooth, and the research did not read its privacy terms. The vendor privacy policy covers website orders only and does not mention children or product data. A teacher should check the app permissions on any phone students use. A teacher should also supervise young builders, as the vendor page advises.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Write a MakeCode program that drives the robot forward and back.
2. Compare the same task in MakeCode blocks and in Python.
3. Use the XGO app first and then recreate the same moves in code.
4. Program a turn and tune the numbers until the robot makes a clean corner.
5. Document a program with comments that a classmate can follow.

### engineering
1. Explain how the IMU sensor helps the robot stay upright.
2. Push the robot gently and describe how it recovers.
3. Test the robot on a smooth floor and on carpet and record the difference.
4. Measure how long the robot runs on one charge.
5. Design a small payload and test how it changes balance.

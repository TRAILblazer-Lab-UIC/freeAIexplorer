## Overview
The Learning.com AI Literacy Quick Start Kit is a free sample of K-8 digital and AI literacy lessons. A teacher receives it after completing a sign-up form. Lessons run 5 to 10 minutes and can be assigned online or taught by the teacher. The kit previews the paid EasyTech curriculum.

## Benefits for students
- Short lessons fit into a busy school day.
- Six themes cover critical thinking, prompt engineering, responsible AI use, spotting misinformation, and academic integrity.
- Students practice judging what they see online.

## Benefits for teachers
- A teacher can assign lessons digitally or teach them aloud.
- Family and teacher resources come with the kit.
- The vendor lists Google, Schoology, PowerSchool, Canvas, and OneRoster integrations.

## Benefits for schools
- The sample kit is free with no credit card.
- The vendor does not give pricing for the full curriculum.
- Lessons run in a browser. No software install is needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the AI Literacy Quick Start Kit on the Learning.com website.
- [ ] Complete the sign-up form with a school email address.
- [ ] Read the privacy terms before sharing any student information.
- [ ] Browse the six themes and pick one short lesson to try.
- [ ] Decide whether to assign the lesson digitally or teach it live.
- [ ] Review the family resources and decide what to send home.
- [ ] Ask IT about the integrations if the school uses Google, Canvas, or Schoology.

## Where is the AI?
The kit teaches about AI. The lessons run on the vendor's cloud and the vendor does not describe an AI tool that students operate. Students learn to write prompts, spot AI-made content, and use AI responsibly. These lessons extend their own judgment about what they read and make.

## Privacy and safety
The kit runs in the vendor's cloud and needs an internet connection. A teacher account is required through the sign up form, which collects teacher contact details. The vendor page says the kit uses no student logins and states it is FERPA, COPPA, and CIPA compliant. These are vendor claims, and the privacy policy was not read. A teacher should read the vendor's privacy terms before assigning lessons to students.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Practice writing a clear prompt and compare it with a vague one.
2. Sort examples into things a computer can do well and things it cannot.
3. Explain in a few sentences how a prompt shapes an AI answer.
4. Create a class poster of rules for using AI responsibly.
5. Test two versions of a prompt and record which one gives a better result.

### library_media
1. Examine two online posts and decide which one could be misinformation.
2. List clues that show a picture or text may have been made by AI.
3. Write a checklist for judging whether a source is reliable.
4. Compare an AI answer with a library book on the same topic.
5. Discuss what academic integrity means when using AI for research.

### ela
1. Write a prompt for a short story idea and revise it to get a better response.
2. Compare a student paragraph with an AI paragraph and describe the differences.
3. Practice giving a prompt clear details about audience and purpose.
4. Draft a class agreement about how to credit AI help in writing.
5. Read an AI answer aloud and mark any sentence that needs checking.

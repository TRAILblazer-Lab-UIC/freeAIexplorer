## Overview
Transforming Faces is a five lesson Day of AI unit for ages 11 to 18. It takes about five class periods of 45 to 60 minutes. Students learn translation, dilation, reflection, and rotation by moving facial images on coordinate grids. They make facial puzzles for classmates and debate whether facial recognition systems should be used.

## Benefits for students
- Students use geometry to see how a machine might compare faces.
- Students make puzzles that their classmates must solve.
- Students debate accuracy, bias, fairness, and privacy.

## Where is the AI?
The unit teaches about facial recognition and does not run an AI tool. Students move faces on grids to see how a system could match them. They then use that experience to discuss accuracy, bias, and privacy in AI.

## Privacy and safety
A Day of AI login or registration is needed to view all resources, and the login is for the teacher. The materials do not state what student data is collected. Students work with facial images, and the page does not say where the images come from. The research did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should use drawings or approved images and avoid photos of real students.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Translate a face drawing on a coordinate grid and write the rule for the move.
2. Dilate a face by a scale factor and measure the new distances.
3. Reflect and rotate a face and list the new coordinates.
4. Make a facial puzzle for a classmate that uses two transformations.
5. Explain how a matching system might use distances between points.

### social_studies
1. Prepare evidence for the debate on whether to use facial recognition systems.
2. List who benefits and who might be harmed by facial recognition in public places.
3. Write a short statement about fairness and privacy.
4. Compare a rule from a city with a school rule on cameras.
5. Hold a class vote and explain how the evidence changed your view.

## Overview
MeArm is a small desktop robot arm kit with a laser-cut acrylic frame and servo motors. The version reviewed by Raspberry Pi Magazine uses an Arduino-based controller and an example sketch. The review calls it designed very much as an educational tool for electronics, robotics and some programming. Assembly takes a while and involves many parts. The arm has no sensors, and autonomous motion uses timed movements.

## Benefits for students
- Students assemble a real robot arm.
- Students learn about servos, wiring and control.
- Students can drive the arm with a joystick.

## Benefits for teachers
- The kit comes with an example Arduino sketch.
- Variants exist for Raspberry Pi and micro:bit.
- The review is more than ten years old. Look for current guides.

## Benefits for schools
- The price was not confirmed.
- Some retailer listings are marked discontinued. Check stock first.
- IT help is likely needed to upload the sketch.

## Where is the AI?
This kit has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is controlling motors with commands. Students see how timed movements make an arm repeat a task.

## Privacy and safety
The kit is local hardware with no accounts or cloud service in the Arduino version reviewed. The internet is not needed after the download. The research reviewed no privacy policy. The review is more than 10 years old, and later variants exist that were not examined. A teacher should check the wiring before powering on. Keep fingers clear of the joints while the arm moves.

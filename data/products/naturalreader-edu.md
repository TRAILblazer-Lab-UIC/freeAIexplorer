## Overview
NaturalReader is an AI text-to-speech service with Personal, Commercial, and EDU products. The EDU plan offers group licenses starting at 5 users and site licenses for whole schools. Teachers can manage students and classrooms and share a document library. Students listen to text read aloud in AI voices.

## Benefits for students
- Students hear text read aloud in AI voices in over 90 languages.
- Students can use a dyslexia font and closed captions.
- Students can listen to an MP3 of a handout offline.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers manage students and classrooms in one place.
- Teachers share a document library with the class.
- A teacher can turn a handout into an MP3 for an LMS or a QR code.

## Benefits for schools
- The site lists a free plan and discounted EDU group and site licenses.
- No prices were visible on the pages read, A school should ask for a quote.
- Student text goes to the cloud. IT should review the data terms.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the NaturalReader EDU page and read the plan options.
- [ ] Ask IT and the district to review the data agreement first.
- [ ] Request a quote for EDU group or site licenses.
- [ ] Create a teacher account and add a classroom.
- [ ] Add students to the classroom.
- [ ] Install the Chrome extension if it fits school policy.
- [ ] Test a handout with a voice and a dyslexia font.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is text-to-speech that turns written text into spoken voices. It runs in the vendor's cloud. The site also lists voice cloning and voice design. Students listen while they read, and teachers use the voices to give students another way to take in a text.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are required on EDU plans, and the internet is needed at all times. Uploaded text goes to the vendor cloud. The privacy policy pages the research tried returned 404. The research could not verify COPPA or FERPA statements. The mobile app has an offline mode. The research notes say the service fails a strict no-third-party-data test without a district data agreement. A teacher should check for that agreement before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### special_ed
1. Listen to a passage while following the text in the dyslexia font.
2. Try three voices and pick the one that is easiest to follow.
3. Turn on closed captions and read along with the audio.
4. Export a handout as an MP3 and listen to it at home.
5. Adjust the reading speed until the text is easy to follow.

### ela
1. Listen to a short story and mark the places where the voice sounds odd.
2. Read your own draft aloud with the tool and fix awkward sentences.
3. Compare reading a poem silently with hearing it read by two voices.
4. Listen to a chapter and write a summary from memory.
5. Export a class text as an MP3 and discuss how listening changes understanding.

### world_languages
1. Paste a sentence in the target language and listen to the voice.
2. Compare two voices in the same language and describe the accents.
3. Read a short passage aloud and compare it with the AI voice.
4. Listen to a text twice and write down the new words you heard.
5. Choose a voice for a language and explain why it fits the text.

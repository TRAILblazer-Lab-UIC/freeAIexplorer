## Overview
CT-STEM is a free platform of computational thinking lessons for science and math classrooms. It is aligned with NGSS. Units such as Evolution of Populations and Ideal Gas Laws use agent-based NetLogo models that run in the browser. Teachers register, build class groups, assign lessons, and view or export student responses.

## Benefits for students
- Students explore models and change settings to see what happens.
- Students learn science with computational thinking practices.
- Lessons last 40 to 50 minutes.

## Benefits for teachers
- A dashboard shows groups, assignments, progress, and feedback.
- Teachers can view and export student responses.
- Units were co-designed by high school teachers and Northwestern researchers.

## Benefits for schools
- The platform and teacher account are free.
- No installation is needed.
- Data goes to the vendor, and accounts are needed.

## Where is the AI?
This platform has no AI. It builds skills in modeling, data, and pattern spotting. Students run agent-based models and study what changes the results.

## Privacy and safety
Teachers register for an account and add students to groups by email. The research did not confirm whether students need their own accounts. The platform stores student responses and progress, and the data goes to the vendor. The privacy policy section on the page had no usable text. Data handling is not confirmed. A teacher should read the privacy information and decide whether to use school email addresses.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Study the evolution of populations with a computational model.
2. Explore ideal gas laws with Connected Chemistry models.
3. Change a model setting and record the effect.
4. Write a claim from a model run and cite evidence.
5. Compare a model with a real example from class.

### math
1. Run a model several times and compute an average.
2. Graph the results of a model for different settings.
3. Predict the result of a model change and check it.
4. Describe a rule that links two variables.
5. Explain what a model leaves out.

### computer_science
1. Describe how agents follow rules in a model.
2. Change a rule and predict the new behavior.
3. Compare two runs of the same model.
4. Identify the inputs and outputs of a model.
5. Discuss how computers help scientists test ideas.

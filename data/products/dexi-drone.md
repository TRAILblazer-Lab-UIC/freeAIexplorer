## Overview
DEXI is a family of programmable education drones from DroneBlocks. They run PX4 flight firmware and ROS 2. DEXI-3 is a classroom drone for indoor Python flight, DEXI-5 is a developer kit for outdoor autonomy, and DEXI-10 is a larger research airframe. Students progress from block coding in a browser simulator to Python, ROS 2, C++, and computer vision.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice in a simulator before flying a real drone.
- Students move from blocks to Python and ROS 2.
- Students try computer vision and autonomy lessons.

## Benefits for teachers
- The same PX4 firmware runs in the simulator and on the drone.
- Four curriculum levels are aligned to CSTA and NGSS per the vendor.
- DEXI-3 flies indoors without GPS.

## Benefits for schools
- DEXI-3 single kits cost 799 USD. DEXI-5 kits run 2,149 to 2,645 USD.
- Classroom 5-packs cost 9,999 to 13,225 USD depending on seller.
- Teachers need an account and a license for the full curriculum.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the DEXI page on the DroneBlocks site and compare sellers.
- [ ] Buy a DEXI kit and build or unpack it.
- [ ] Log in at my.droneblocks.io for lessons.
- [ ] Get a teacher account and a license.
- [ ] Try the browser simulator before any flight.
- [ ] Connect to the drone from a computer over Wi-Fi.
- [ ] Plan a safe indoor space for flight.

## Where is the AI?
AI is optional and appears as computer vision in the advanced lessons. It runs on the drone. Students write code that uses the camera and then watch the drone respond. Earlier lessons teach flight and autonomy with no AI.

## Privacy and safety
The policy says DroneBlocks does not require student personal information. Students may optionally give an email to save flight plans through Google Firebase. Educators give name, email, school and billing details, and Google Analytics and cookies track site usage. The curriculum platform needs a login, and the online lessons and simulator are hosted by the vendor. The research did not find where video and flight data go when the drone uses the cloud platform. A teacher should check the DroneBlocks account terms and set clear flight safety rules.

2. Move the same flight to Python and compare the code.
3. Program a square flight path and fix the corners.
4. Use the camera to detect a marker in a computer vision lesson.
5. Explain why the simulator uses the same firmware as the drone.

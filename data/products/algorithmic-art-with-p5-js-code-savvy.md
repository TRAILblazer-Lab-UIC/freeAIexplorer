## Overview
Students explore links between art and computer science. They experiment in the p5.js editor and create an interactive piece of algorithmic art. Code Savvy lists the lesson for grades 6 to 12. The lesson plan is reached through a Google Form.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how code can make art.
- Students practice text coding with instant visual results.
- Students make an interactive piece that responds to the viewer.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson links two subjects in one project.
- The p5.js editor opens in a browser.
- The page does not give a duration or license.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- The editor runs in a browser on Chromebooks, Windows and Mac.
- The materials do not say what student data the editor collects.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no AI. Students build skills in logic, loops and patterns as they write rules that draw an image. They then change the rules to see new results.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state whether an account is needed. The lesson plan sits behind a Google Form and the research did not review how that form handles data. The p5.js web editor may offer optional accounts. The research did not review its privacy terms. The internet is used at setup. A teacher should check whether the editor asks students to sign in and where saved sketches are stored.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Draw a pattern of shapes with code and change its colors.
2. Make a piece that changes when the mouse moves.
3. Create a design with repeated shapes in a grid.
4. Describe the artistic choices in a piece you made.
5. Compare an algorithmic piece to a hand drawn one.

### computer_science
1. Write a sketch that draws a circle at the mouse position.
2. Use a loop to repeat a shape across the canvas.
3. Add a random value to change the shape size.
4. Fix an error in a classmate's sketch.
5. Explain how your code makes the picture.

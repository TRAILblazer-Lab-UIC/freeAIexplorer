## Overview
This LEGO Education kit serves four students and combines building, block coding in the Coding Canvas app, and AI computer vision. It has 321 bricks, a double motor, a color sensor, a connection card, a USB cable, and a storage box. The page lists 30 lessons across six units, with AI and data as a unit. The Teacher Portal provides presentations and facilitation notes.

## Benefits for students
- Students build, code, and test with their own hands.
- Students try computer vision and pose detection.
- Students learn how a model sorts things into groups.

## Benefits for teachers
- The free Teacher Portal gives presentations and notes.
- Students join lessons with a PIN.
- Some lessons are screen-free.

## Benefits for schools
- The product page lists 429.95 USD for one kit and 2799 USD for a 24 student bundle.
- Professional learning is priced separately.
- One device serves each group of four for some lessons.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the kit on the LEGO Education site.
- [ ] Sign up for the free Teacher Portal.
- [ ] Install the Coding Canvas web app or iOS app while online.
- [ ] Charge the motor and controller with the USB cable.
- [ ] Build the first model with a group of four.
- [ ] Share the PIN so students can join the lesson.
- [ ] Review the privacy terms before students sign in.

## Where is the AI?
The kit uses computer vision, pose detection, and classification. The AI runs on the device in the Coding Canvas app. Students use it to make their models react to what a camera sees. They then change their code to improve the results.

## Privacy and safety
Students join lessons with a PIN and need no account. Teachers sign up for the free Teacher Portal. LEGO states that all AI features run locally on the student device, that no data goes to the LEGO Group or third parties, and that no models are trained on student data. It says only pose data is kept locally. The statement names Google MoveNet as the engine and does not say whether any data goes to Google. The internet is needed for first install, updates, and language changes. A teacher should read the LEGO privacy terms before teachers or students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Open the AI and data unit and train a model to sort a few objects.
2. Use pose detection to control a model with a body movement.
3. Predict where the model will make mistakes and then test it.
4. Code a motor to act when the color sensor sees a color.
5. Explain to a partner how the model learned from examples.

### engineering
1. Build the first model from the kit and test its motor.
2. Redesign a model to carry a small load.
3. Test how the color sensor works at different distances.
4. Record which build changes helped most.
5. Present a finished model and explain how it works.

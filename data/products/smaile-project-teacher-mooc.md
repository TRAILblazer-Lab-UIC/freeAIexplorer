## Overview
SmAIle is an Erasmus+ co-funded project with partners from five European countries. It builds an AI e-HUB with a toolkit of learning scenarios, courses for teachers and students, and tutorials. The site presents the hub as an open educational resource under CC BY 4.0. The page does not describe individual course modules.

## Benefits for students
- Students can take part in an AI Challenge.
- Students can use courses made for learners.
- Students learn about responsible AI use.

## Benefits for teachers
- The toolkit offers learning scenarios to adapt.
- Courses for teachers cover AI literacy.
- Materials are open under CC BY 4.0.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free.
- A browser and internet are enough.
- The courses may live on a separate platform that was not located.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the SmAIle project site.
- [ ] Look for the AI Toolkit and the learning scenarios.
- [ ] Find the links to the MOOCs.

- [ ] Check whether the MOOCs ask for an account.
- [ ] Review the CC BY 4.0 terms before you share the materials.

## Where is the AI?
The materials teach AI literacy and responsible AI use. The vendor does not say that any AI tool runs inside the hub. Students and teachers read, discuss, and try scenarios, and use them to build their own understanding of AI.

## Privacy and safety
The site is a static GitHub Pages site that does not mention accounts or data collection. The courses may live on a separate platform that the research did not locate. The materials do not state which accounts students need or what data is collected. A teacher should check whether the course platform needs an account and read its privacy terms before students join.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Pick a learning scenario and explain what AI idea it teaches.
2. Complete a section of the student MOOC and share one new fact.
3. Take part in the AI Challenge as a small team.
4. Describe how an AI tool you use makes its choices.
5. Write a short guide on responsible AI use for classmates.

### library_media
1. Use a tutorial to learn how to check AI answers against sources.
2. Write a list of questions to ask about any AI tool.
3. Compare two AI literacy resources and say which is clearer.
4. Make a poster on responsible AI use.
5. Present one lesson from the toolkit to another class.

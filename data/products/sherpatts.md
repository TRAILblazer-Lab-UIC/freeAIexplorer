## Overview
SherpaTTS is an Android text-to-speech engine built on Next-gen Kaldi. It uses Piper and Coqui voice models that users download in the app from Hugging Face. After a voice is downloaded, speech runs offline. It works as a system engine that other Android apps can use, such as screen readers.

## Benefits for students
- Students who rely on speech output get natural offline voices.
- Students can hear text in several languages.
- Voices work in other apps through the Android settings.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can pick a voice that suits a student.
- Manual sideloading of Piper .onnx models is possible.
- Teachers need only an Android phone or tablet to try it.

## Benefits for schools
- The app is free and the code is open source.
- It is distributed through F-Droid or an APK source, which may conflict with managed school devices.
- IT will likely need to help with the sideload steps.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find SherpaTTS on F-Droid or another APK source.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install on managed devices.
- [ ] Install the app.
- [ ] Open Manage Languages and download a voice.
- [ ] Stay online while the voice downloads.
- [ ] Select SherpaTTS as the system text-to-speech engine in Android settings.
- [ ] Go offline and test it with a screen reader.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a neural text-to-speech model that turns written words into speech. It runs on the Android device once the voice is downloaded. Students pick a voice, listen to text, and use the sound to support their own reading and language work.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The internet is used at setup to download voices from Hugging Face. The README says speech then runs offline. The F-Droid page lists network, foreground service, run at startup and wake lock permissions. The research found no separate privacy policy and no other data collection described. A teacher should check that sideloading is allowed on school devices and ask IT to review the permissions.

## Ideas by subject
### special_ed
1. Try three voices and choose the one that is easiest to understand.
2. Use a screen reader with SherpaTTS to hear a class reading.
3. Listen to directions and repeat them back.
4. Adjust the speed of the voice and find a comfortable pace.
5. Listen to a story and draw the main scene.

### world_languages
1. Download a voice in the target language and listen to a short text.
2. Compare two voices reading the same sentence and describe the differences.
3. Read a sentence aloud and compare your accent with the voice.
4. Listen to a dialogue and write down the words you hear.
5. Choose a voice for a language project and explain your choice.

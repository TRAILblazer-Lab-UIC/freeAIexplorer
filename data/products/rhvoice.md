## Overview
RHVoice is a free, open source multilingual speech synthesizer. It was made to give visually impaired users a good free voice in their own language. It installs as a speech engine for screen readers and other programs on Windows, Linux, and Android. It began with Russian and now covers many more languages.

## Benefits for students
- Students who have visual impairments or reading needs get a free voice.
- Students can listen to text in many languages.
- Students can pair it with a screen reader such as NVDA.

## Benefits for teachers
- It is easy to set up as a speech engine.
- It works with NVDA, Orca, TalkBack, and SAPI 5 programs.
- Documentation exists in English, Russian, and Ukrainian.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free. The GitHub repository shows GPL-2.0.
- It runs on the device and needs the internet only at setup.
- IT approval may be needed to install it on school computers.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the RHVoice project and download the Windows package or the Android app.
- [ ] Ask IT for approval to install on school computers.
- [ ] Install the package or the app from F-Droid or Google Play.
- [ ] Linux users build it from source.
- [ ] Choose RHVoice as the speech engine in the screen reader or system settings.
- [ ] Pick a voice in the language you need.
- [ ] Test with a short passage.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a speech synthesis engine that turns text into spoken audio. It runs on the device. Students or teachers pick a voice, then listen to text read aloud. The voice helps students follow along and check their own reading.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. The project site describes a locally installed speech engine and mentions no upload. The research found no privacy policy, and the privacy policy page returned 404. The pages do not state offline operation. Chromebooks may not run it, and the Android app was not checked. A teacher should test it.

## Ideas by subject
### special_ed
1. Listen to a class reading while following the printed text.
2. Try three voices and choose the one that is easiest to understand.
3. Slow the speech rate and note which speed helps most.
4. Use a screen reader with RHVoice to read a school web page.
5. Record which words the voice reads clearly and which it does not.

### world_languages
1. Listen to a short text in the language you are studying.
2. Compare two voices in the same language and describe the sound.
3. Read a sentence aloud, then listen to the voice and compare the stress.
4. Choose a language the class does not study and listen to a greeting.
5. Write a list of words the voice pronounces in an odd way.

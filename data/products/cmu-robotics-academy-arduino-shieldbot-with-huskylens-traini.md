## Overview
This is a teacher training course for the Arduino with Parallax ShieldBot, with the HuskyLens AI vision sensor added. The announced session ran July 21 to 25, 2025, with recordings, and totals 36 hours with CMRA Teacher Certification. It includes two student curricula, Robotics Integration with ShieldBot and Software Foundations with ShieldBot.

## Benefits for students
- Students program a robot that sees colors and tags.
- Students learn Arduino programming.
- Students use machine vision on a real robot.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers receive 36 hours of professional development and certification.
- Sessions were recorded.
- Two student curricula come with the training.

## Benefits for schools
- Registration included a free ShieldBot kit.
- Tuition is not listed on the page.
- Batteries and the HuskyLens are bought separately.

## Where is the AI?
The HuskyLens is an AI vision sensor that recognizes colors and AprilTags. It is set up on the device itself. No software install is needed. Students set it to recognize colors or tags and then program the robot to react. The CS2N site runs in the vendor cloud.

## Privacy and safety
Course pages say access and tracking go through CS2N. The CS2N policy lists name, email, username, group membership, and course activity. It says it does not sell or rent personal information. It allows use by children under 13 with consent from a parent, school, district, or teacher. It does not name COPPA or say where data is stored. The page says the HuskyLens is configured on the device. The materials do not state whether an account is needed or how much the internet is used. A teacher should read the CS2N policy. A teacher should also decide which camera images students may capture.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Work through Software Foundations with ShieldBot and write what each program does.
2. Program the robot to stop when it sees a chosen color.
3. Read an AprilTag and make the robot choose a path.
4. Debug a program that makes the robot miss a tag.
5. Explain how the HuskyLens recognizes a color.

### engineering
1. Assemble the ShieldBot and list its parts.
2. Mount the HuskyLens and test the best view angle.
3. Measure how the robot behaves in bright and dim light.
4. Design a track with colored markers.
5. Compare the ShieldBot with another robot and note the trade offs.

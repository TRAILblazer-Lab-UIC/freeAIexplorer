## Overview
This PDF is the Digital Toolbox Unplugged Activities guide from Canada Learning Code. It holds five lesson plans. They are Do the Robot, Speed Stacker, Pixel Programming, Deconstructing Code, and Functions Unplugged Songwriting. Activities run from 10 minutes to an hour, and the grade range is not stated.

## Benefits for students
- Students act out algorithms with their own bodies in Do the Robot.
- Students practice debugging with cup stacking in Speed Stacker.
- Students write songs to learn how functions work.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each lesson has prep notes, an extension, and an assessment.
- Some lessons include printables and decode sheets.
- The guide explains why unplugged activities help and how to move to devices afterward.

## Benefits for schools
- The PDF is free.
- The activities need only simple supplies such as cups and printed sheets.
- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Digital Toolbox Unplugged Activities guide on the Canada Learning Code site.

## Where is the AI?
This guide has no AI. It builds skills such as sequencing, debugging, and understanding functions. Students practice giving exact instructions and fixing instructions that fail.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play Do the Robot with a partner and give step-by-step spoken instructions for a task.
2. Stack cups with a given sequence and fix the sequence when it fails.
3. Color a pixel grid by following a printed program.
4. Decode a message with a decode sheet and explain the steps.
5. Write a song with a repeated chorus and name the chorus as a function.

### health_pe
1. Act as the robot in Do the Robot and follow only the exact instructions given.
2. Give a partner movement instructions to cross a gym space.
3. Create a short movement routine and write it as a sequence.
4. Repeat a movement with a loop and count the repeats.
5. Reflect on which role, programmer or robot, was harder.

### social_studies
1. Write exact steps for a daily task and test them on a partner.
2. Discuss why clear instructions matter in a community.
3. Give directions to a classroom location and see if a partner arrives.
4. Compare two sets of directions and pick the clearer one.
5. Talk about how people and machines follow instructions in different ways.

### music
1. Write a short song and mark the part that repeats.
2. Name the repeated chorus as a function and use the name in the song plan.
3. Swap songs with a partner and find the functions in their song.
4. Change one line of a function and listen to how the song changes.
5. Complete the worksheet and the assessment for the songwriting lesson.

## Overview
This is a 50 minute lesson for grades 6 to 12 from the AI Unlocked video series. Students watch a video on spotting AI generated images, video, and audio. They answer guiding questions on paper. They end by writing a personal rule for responsible sharing online.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn signs that point to AI generated media.
- Students discuss reverse image search and the limits of detection tools.
- Students write their own rule for sharing online.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson is a video plus discussion and needs little preparation.
- It maps to Common Core ELA standards and to ISTE 1.2 and 1.3.
- A vocabulary list is ready to print.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- It needs a screen and internet to show the video.
- No IT work is needed. Students need only paper.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is not run in this lesson. The lesson teaches students about AI generated images, video, audio, and deepfakes. Students learn to spot such media and to use verification tools. They build the skill of checking sources before they share.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson page names no required account or AI tool, and the research found no student data collection. The video plays from YouTube, and the internet is needed. A Google account is needed only to copy the Google Doc version. A teacher should preview the video and any examples of deepfakes. A teacher should also check that the school allows YouTube.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Answer the guiding questions on paper after the video and share one answer.
2. Write a short paragraph on how to judge whether a source is trustworthy.
3. Discuss with a partner why detection tools can be wrong.
4. Write a personal rule for responsible sharing online and read it aloud.
5. Compare two images and explain the clues that suggest one is AI generated.

### library_media
1. Try a reverse image search on a class photo and describe the results.
2. Build a checklist for judging images, video, and audio before sharing.
3. Review the vocabulary list and make a poster of three key terms.
4. Trace an image back to its first source and report the steps.
5. Teach a younger class one tip for spotting AI generated media.

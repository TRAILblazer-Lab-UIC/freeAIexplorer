## Overview
Face Sensing began as a Scratch Lab experiment and is now in the main Scratch editor. The blocks use a pre-trained model to detect a face and the position of the nose, eyes, and mouth. It tracks one face at a time. Students make a sprite follow their nose, build face filters, or steer a game with their head.

## Benefits for students
- Students use their own face to control a project.
- Students make filters and sound boards with blocks.
- Students try the 30 minute activity Can You Fool the AI.

## Benefits for teachers
- The Scratch Foundation provides coding cards and a lesson guide.
- Starter projects include Hat and Glasses, Flapping Bird, and Sound Board.
- Sessions run 10 to 30 minutes.

## Benefits for schools
- It is free and needs no install or account.
- It needs a webcam and camera permission in the browser.
- It may run slowly on some Chromebooks or older computers.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Scratch editor in a browser.
- [ ] Add the Face Sensing extension.
- [ ] Allow the browser to use the camera.
- [ ] Open a starter project such as Hat and Glasses.
- [ ] Download the coding cards and lesson guide.
- [ ] Test on a Chromebook or an older computer first.
- [ ] Read the fairness note from the Scratch team before class.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a pre-trained face detection model with facial landmark tracking. It runs in the browser on the local device. Students use the blocks to react to where their face is. They then test the limits in the Can You Fool the AI activity.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed to try the blocks. The Scratch Foundation states that only the computer senses the face and that none of the data is stored or sent to Scratch or any other site. The Scratch editor must load from the internet, and saving to a Scratch account follows the normal Scratch account terms. The Scratch team has noted fairness concerns about face sensing. A teacher should talk with students about who the model works well for.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Make a sprite follow your nose on the stage.
2. Run the Can You Fool the AI activity and list what tricked the model.
3. Build a game that you steer with a head tilt.
4. Test the model with glasses, hats, and dim light and record the results.
5. Discuss why a pre-trained model may work better for some faces than others.

### visual_arts
1. Make a Hat and Glasses filter that sits on your face.
2. Design a sprite that changes when you open your mouth.
3. Build a sound board that plays notes when you move your mouth.
4. Draw custom costumes and attach them to the nose and eyes.
5. Share the filter and ask a partner to give design feedback.

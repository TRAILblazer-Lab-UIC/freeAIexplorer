## Overview
Cubetto is a wooden robot that children program by placing physical blocks on a control board. Blocks include Forward, Left, Right, Function, Backward, Negation and Random. The program runs left to right when the child presses the blue button. The 2016 teacher's guide describes it as Montessori-inspired for ages 3 to 6.

## Benefits for students
- Students code with blocks that they can touch and need no reading.
- Students learn sequencing, debugging and functions.
- Students explore story maps and adventure maps.

## Benefits for teachers
- The free teacher's guide lists activities on action causality and block sequences.
- Setup takes about an hour and the robot has no screen.
- Primo sells story maps and storybooks that link coding to stories.

## Benefits for schools
- The playset costs about 200 to 250 USD per discovery notes. The official page showed no price.
- The robot charges by USB-C and needs no internet or account.
- No student data is collected.

## Getting started
- [ ] Buy a Cubetto playset from Primo Toys or a seller.
- [ ] Download the free teacher's guide.
- [ ] Charge the robot with a USB-C charger.
- [ ] Lay out a map on a flat floor or table.
- [ ] Place blocks on the board and press the blue button.
- [ ] Follow the guide's session set-up steps.
- [ ] Start with a short sequence of three blocks.

## Where is the AI?
Cubetto has no AI. The robot runs the blocks that children place on the board. Students build skills in sequencing, debugging and functions.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the robot works with no internet. The research found no student data collected by the robot. The Primo privacy page covers website customers and cookies and does not mention children or the Cubetto product. Primo sells a separate Cubetto app whose privacy practices were not reviewed. A teacher should check the app privacy terms before using it. A teacher should also note one critique that says Cubetto does not support if-conditional ideas.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Place three blocks to move Cubetto to a goal on the map.
2. Predict where Cubetto ends before pressing the blue button.
3. Fix a sequence that misses the goal and explain the change.
4. Use the Function block to repeat a short set of moves.
5. Build a route for a partner and let them test it.

### ela
1. Use a story map and move Cubetto through the scenes in order.
2. Retell a favorite story by placing blocks for each scene.
3. Create a new adventure map and name each stop.
4. Tell a partner what Cubetto does at each step.
5. Write a short story that matches the route.

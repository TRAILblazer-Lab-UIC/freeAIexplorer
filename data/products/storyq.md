## Overview
StoryQ is a web based machine learning and text mining tool from the Concord Consortium. Students work with text data without coding. Linked tables, graphs, and texts make the models easy to explore. Activities cover English language arts and history, with some math, for grades 6 to 12.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and test a text classifier without writing code.
- Students see which words push a model toward one label.
- Students explore demos such as a Markov chain next word predictor.

## Benefits for teachers
- A teacher dashboard creates classes and assigns materials.
- Pre and post tests are part of the dashboard.
- Teachers can email storyq@concord.org to request a demo or professional development.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free per the sweep. No price was found on the page.
- It runs in a browser on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- Concord collects usage data. Schools should review the data agreement.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a text classification model that students train in the browser. It extracts features from text and predicts a label, and a demo predicts the next word. Students pick features, watch the weights change, and test the model on new text.

## Privacy and safety
A teacher account is needed, and the page does not state what accounts students need. The tool needs the internet. The Concord policy says it may collect student data when a school adopts its tools, plus usage data, IP addresses and pre and post test responses, with Google Analytics for performance data. It says the data is not sold or used for targeted advertising and cites FERPA, COPPA and signed data privacy agreements. The research did not confirm where model training runs. A teacher should review the data agreement before students use it.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a classifier that sorts movie reviews into positive and negative.
2. Look at the weights and name the five words that matter most.
3. Test the model on a sentence you wrote and explain the result.
4. Try the Markov chain demo and describe how it guesses the next word.
5. Find a case where the model fails and suggest more training data.

### ela
1. Classify sentences from a short story by mood and review the results.
2. Compare how a model and a reader judge the tone of a poem.
3. Write test sentences that fool the classifier.
4. Use the next word demo to predict the end of a sentence and compare with your own.
5. Explain in a paragraph how word choice affects the model.

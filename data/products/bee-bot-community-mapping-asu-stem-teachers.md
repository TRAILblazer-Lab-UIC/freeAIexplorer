## Overview
This is a first grade lesson by Tina Kennedy on the ASU STEM Teachers site. Students draw map symbols and program a Bee-Bot to travel to a key symbol on a grid map. Search results say it can be split over two days. It starts with a map read-aloud and a robot introduction. Groups of two to three students each get a map, key cards and a Bee-Bot. The official page returned an error. These details come from search listings.

## Benefits for students
- Students draw their own map symbols and a key.
- Students program a robot to reach a place on a map.
- Students share one robot and practice taking turns.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson links social studies and coding.
- Teachers can split it over two days.
- The research found a note about a browser Bee-Bot emulator for classes with no robots.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- Schools must buy Bee-Bot robots. The research did not check a price.
- The robots need no internet.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is sequencing. Students enter an ordered list of moves and watch the robot follow the route.

## Privacy and safety
The Bee-Bot is a standalone robot with no accounts and the research found no student data collection. The teacher opens the lesson plan slides online. The official lesson page was unreadable. A teacher should set clear robot rules for sharing and handling. The robots should stay on a flat floor or mat.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Draw symbols for places in a community and add them to a map key.
2. Read a map and find the symbol the robot should reach.
3. Describe the route with words such as forward and left.
4. Make a map of the school neighborhood.
5. Share the map with another group and have them find a place.

### computer_science
1. Program the Bee-Bot to travel from the start square to the key symbol.
2. Write down the moves before you press the buttons.
3. Debug a route when the robot ends at the wrong square.
4. Plan a longer route with two stops.
5. Work with a partner and take turns programming.

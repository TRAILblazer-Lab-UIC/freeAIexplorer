## Overview
AI Snapshots are quick discussion activities from aiEDU that build AI readiness inside existing subjects. The curriculum site lists them for grades 6 to 12. Each Snapshot gives a scenario and a challenge question. Pages disagree on length and list 5, 10, or 20 minutes.

## Benefits for students
- Students talk about real AI scenarios in short, focused sessions.
- Students practice judging whether an AI output is right.
- Students connect AI to the subjects they already study.

## Benefits for teachers
- Snapshots work as warm-ups and need little prep.
- The site filters by grade, subject, time, and language.
- Spanish versions exist according to search results.

## Benefits for schools
- The activities are free.
- A teacher can run them with a slide deck and no IT install.
- A free account is needed to download materials.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the AI Snapshots on the aiEDU curriculum site.
- [ ] Create a free teacher account.

- [ ] Try the sample titled Will You Be My Friend.
- [ ] Download the slide deck.
- [ ] Read the scenario and challenge question before class.
- [ ] Run the Snapshot as a warm-up and lead the talk.

## Where is the AI?
Snapshots do not run an AI tool. They use a scenario that shows how AI behaves and ask students to judge the outputs. The discussion happens in class with a slide deck. The activity builds the skill of evaluating AI answers.

## Privacy and safety
Teachers need a free aiEDU account to download the slides. Students watch slide decks led by the teacher and the materials list no student data collected. The site needs the internet at all times. The research did not read the aiEDU privacy policy. A teacher should check the aiEDU privacy terms before registering. A teacher should also read each scenario to confirm that it suits the class.

## Ideas by subject
### math
1. Discuss how an AI might guess a pattern in a number set and check its answer.
2. Compare an AI answer to a worked solution and mark where they differ.
3. Talk about how data choices change a result in a short scenario.
4. Decide whether an AI's confident answer is correct and show the proof.
5. Write a challenge question about AI and numbers for a partner.

### science
1. Use a Snapshot as a warm-up before a science lesson and share first reactions.
2. Discuss how an AI might read a data set from an experiment.
3. Judge an AI claim about a science topic and name the evidence needed.
4. Compare an AI answer to a lab result and list the differences.
5. Describe one way scientists could use AI and one risk.

### ela
1. Read a short scenario about an AI chat reply and discuss its tone.
2. Compare an AI written line with a student written line and describe the differences.
3. Debate whether an AI can understand a friend's feelings in the Will You Be My Friend sample.
4. Write a short reply to a scenario challenge question.
5. Spot words in an AI output that might be biased.

### social_studies
1. Discuss how AI choices could affect a community in a short scenario.
2. Decide who should check an AI output before people act on it.
3. Compare views on a scenario in a small group and share a class view.
4. Name who gains and who loses from a given AI tool.
5. Write a rule that a town could use for AI in public services.

### computer_science
1. Run the Will You Be My Friend sample and list what the AI does well and poorly.
2. Discuss how an AI learns from examples in a short scenario.
3. Judge an AI output and explain the reasons for the judgment.
4. Compare two Snapshots and describe which AI idea each one shows.
5. Write a new scenario and challenge question for a classmate.

## Overview
LLMs Unplugged was created by Dr. Ben Swift at the ANU School of Cybernetics. Students build hand-made n-gram language models by counting word patterns on grid paper or sorting paper cutouts. They then generate text with dice. The site offers classroom lessons, workshops from 60 minutes to a half day, and a 20-minute self-guided try-it. The materials are pen, paper, dice, and printed sheets.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a working language model by hand.
- Students see how word counts lead to new text.
- Students can go on to modules about sampling, fine-tuning, and agents.

## Benefits for teachers
- The site lists lessons, workshops, and a short self-guided option.
- The source code is on GitHub.
- A UNSW outreach day used it with about 100 high school students.

## Benefits for schools
- The materials are free.
- The activities need no computers.
- No accounts are needed and no student data is collected.

## Where is the AI?
The activity has no running AI program. Students build the same kind of model that sits at the heart of a language model, but on paper. They count word patterns, train the model, and use dice to generate new text. They build skills in probability and in reading data.

## Privacy and safety
The activities use paper, pen, and dice. The pages read mention no devices or accounts. The research read no privacy policy for this item. The research found no student data collection. The internet is used to get the sheets. The site license text differs between sources. The research did not confirm it. A teacher should confirm the license on the GitHub repository before sharing the sheets. A teacher should also review generated text before students share it.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Count word pairs in a short text and fill in a grid.
2. Roll dice to pick next words and write the sentence your model makes.
3. Compare two models trained on different texts and describe how their output differs.
4. Explain to a partner how the counts decide which word comes next.
5. Try a module on sampling or fine-tuning and describe what changed.

### ela
1. Choose a favorite short story and use it as training text for a model.
2. Read the model's sentences aloud and mark any that make sense.
3. Revise a generated sentence into a clear one.
4. Compare model text with human writing and list the differences.
5. Write a short story from a model's first sentence.

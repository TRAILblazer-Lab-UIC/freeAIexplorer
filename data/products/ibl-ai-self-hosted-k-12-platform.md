## Overview
ibl.ai markets a sovereign AI platform for building AI agents and apps. It targets universities, corporations, K-12, and government. It can be hosted by ibl.ai or deployed in the customer's own cloud, on-premise, or air-gapped environment under a code license. All privacy and pricing claims on the site are vendor assertions.

## Benefits for students
- Students can use chat tools that the district builds.
- Advanced students can see how AI agents are made.
- Students may work with tools that the school controls.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can ask the district for agents that suit a class.
- The platform is model-agnostic. A district can choose commercial or open models.
- Audit logging records queries.

## Benefits for schools
- The vendor claims a free cloud tier with monthly credits. Pilots start from $15K.
- The vendor says a district can host the platform itself and keep control of data.
- Self-hosting needs district infrastructure, including GPUs for local models.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a set of agents and chat tools that route to commercial or open models. The models run in the vendor's cloud or on the school's own servers. Students and teachers talk to agents and adapt them to class work. The vendor claims 1.6M+ users and 400+ organizations, and these figures come from the vendor.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are needed, and the materials do not say whether the internet is required. Where data goes depends on the deployment. The vendor claims it can run behind the customer firewall with zero retention by model vendors and lists SOC 2 Type II, FERPA, GDPR, and HIPAA. The home page does not mention COPPA, and none of these claims was independently verified. The hosted cloud option and cloud model routing send data to ibl.ai and model providers. A teacher should ask IT which deployment the school uses and request proof of the privacy claims.

## Overview
Students make text dialog scenes in Scratch using words from class Spanish vocabulary lists. Project 1 is a text-only dialog with speech bubbles using at least three vocabulary words. In Project 2, partners write a 10 to 30 second dialog, record a finger-puppet video on an iPad, and build a narrated Scratch animation. Project 3 adds a bilingual dialog with a Coco movie theme.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice Spanish words in a creative project.
- Students record their own voices for each sentence.
- Students work with a partner on a short animation.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers can reuse their class vocabulary lists.
- Tutorial videos support each project.
- The lesson comes from a single school's teacher page.

## Benefits for schools

- It needs an iPad for the puppet video.
- Students need Scratch and Google Classroom access.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds the skill of sequencing, as students put sentences in order in a dialog. It also builds logic as they time each speech bubble.

## Privacy and safety
Students need Scratch accounts, and a search snippet says they set them up with school email. The lesson uses online Scratch, Google Docs, Slides, Classroom, and Seesaw. Projects are shared to a class studio, and the internet is needed throughout. The materials do not say what student data is collected. A batch note says Scratch Desktop avoids cloud storage, and the research did not confirm this. A teacher should check the privacy terms of Scratch, Google Classroom, and Seesaw. A teacher should also set rules for student voice recordings.

## Ideas by subject
### world_languages
1. Write a text dialog with speech bubbles using at least three Spanish vocabulary words, as in Project 1.
2. Record each sentence in Scratch in your own voice.
3. Write a short partner dialog of 10 to 30 seconds.
4. Make a finger-puppet video to match the dialog.
5. Add English and Spanish captions to a movie scene.

### computer_science
1. Make a sprite speak with a speech bubble block.
2. Use a wait block to time a conversation.
3. Switch costumes to show a character talking.
4. Add a background to set the scene.
5. Test a partner's project and give one suggestion.

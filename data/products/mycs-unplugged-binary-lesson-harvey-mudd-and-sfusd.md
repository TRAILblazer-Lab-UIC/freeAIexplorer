## Overview
This is a binary data lesson from the MyCS middle-years computer science curriculum. It builds on the CS Unplugged Count the Dots activity. Five students hold dot cards worth 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 and flip them to count in binary. The class then encodes and decodes messages.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn binary by acting out the bits with their own bodies.
- Students write journal entries before and after the activity and see how their thinking grows.
- Students practice encoding and decoding messages with a partner.

## Benefits for teachers
- The plan includes journal prompts, a class discussion and worksheets.
- A printable day 1 handout saves planning time.
- The plan reuses the well known Count the Dots activity. A teacher can read about it ahead of time.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is a free download.
- It needs only paper, a printer and a few minutes of setup.
- It uses no devices and collects no student data.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Data Representation with Binary lesson plan in the MyCS curriculum from Harvey Mudd.
- [ ] Download the lesson plan and the day 1 handout.
- [ ] Read the CS Unplugged Count the Dots activity before class.
- [ ] Print the dot cards for 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16.
- [ ] Print the worksheets on counting higher than 31 and on sending messages.
- [ ] Plan about three class periods. The listed activities total 165 minutes.
- [ ] Run the first journal entry, then the card activity, then the worksheets.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is understanding how computers store data as ones and zeros. Students see that text and numbers can be turned into a code and turned back again.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson runs on paper, and the research found no account needs and no student data collection. The internet is used only to download the materials. The research read a 2015 PDF plan and did not read the batch document link. A teacher should check that students are comfortable standing in front of the class.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Hold dot cards in groups of five and flip them to count from 0 to 31 in binary.
2. Encode a short secret word as binary numbers and trade it with a partner to decode.
3. Write a journal entry that explains why computers use binary.
4. Count backward from 31 to 0 as a class and describe the pattern in the cards.
5. Design a message code on paper and test whether a classmate can read it.

### math
1. Count forward in binary with the dot cards and write the matching base ten numbers.
2. Count backward in binary and describe how each card changes.
3. Work out why five cards reach 31 and what a sixth card would add.
4. Add the values of the flipped cards to find the number a group shows.
5. Make a chart that pairs each number from 0 to 15 with its binary form.

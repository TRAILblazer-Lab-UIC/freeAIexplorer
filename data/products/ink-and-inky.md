## Overview
ink is a narrative scripting language for games where text comes first and logic is inserted within it. Inky is the editor that lets users write, test, and export ink stories. It plays stories as you write, highlights errors, and exports JSON or a web page. Both are MIT licensed. It suits high school writers and coders.

## Benefits for students
- Students write branching stories with choices.
- Students see the story play as they type.
- Students add logic such as variables and conditions.

## Benefits for teachers
- A 2021 study ran a weeklong interactive fiction workshop for twelve students at an all-girls school.
- A Writing with ink tutorial is available.
- Stories export to a web page for sharing.

## Benefits for schools
- Both tools are free.
- Inky runs offline on Mac, Windows, and Linux.
- The ink tutorial is comprehensive and may be daunting for beginners.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download Inky from the GitHub releases page.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install on school computers.
- [ ] Open Inky and run the sample story.
- [ ] Read the first part of the Writing with ink tutorial.
- [ ] Write a three-choice story and test it in the preview.
- [ ] Export the story as a web page.
- [ ] Note that Inky 0.15.2 was released in May 2026.

## Where is the AI?
ink and Inky have no AI. They build logic and writing skills. Students plan branches and use conditions to control what the reader sees. Inky runs on the local computer.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used for setup. Inky is a local desktop editor, and the site mentions no accounts or data collection. No privacy policy was found. Stories stay on the device until students export them. A teacher should review stories before they go online.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Write a short story with two choices at the start.
2. Add a third path that loops back to an earlier scene.
3. Revise a scene so each choice has a clear result.
4. Swap stories with a partner and give feedback on the choices.
5. Export a story as a web page and share it with the class.

### computer_science
1. Use a variable to track whether the reader found a key.
2. Add a condition that shows a line only when a variable is set.
3. Find an error that Inky highlights and fix it.
4. Draw a flowchart of the story branches.
5. Explain how the story logic works to a classmate.

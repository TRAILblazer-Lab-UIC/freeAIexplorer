## Overview
Quick, Draw! is a Google experiment. A player draws a prompt and a neural network tries to guess it. The game was trained on a few hundred concepts. Players can share their drawings to a large public doodle dataset. AI4K12 lists it as an image recognition demo.

## Benefits for students
- Students see a neural network guess in real time.
- Students learn that AI learns from examples that people draw.
- Students can draw with a mouse, a touch screen, or a trackpad.

## Benefits for teachers
- There is nothing to install and there is no account.
- The game is short and works as a class opener.
- The dataset holds about 50 million drawings in 345 categories under CC BY 4.0.

## Benefits for schools
- The game is free.
- It runs in any browser with internet.
- No IT work is needed. Drawings and survey answers go to Google.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Quick, Draw! website.
- [ ] Play a few rounds yourself.
- [ ] Notice the survey between rounds and the Skip option.
- [ ] Decide whether students may share drawings to the dataset.
- [ ] Check school rules for web games.
- [ ] Show the game on a class screen first.
- [ ] Let students play in pairs.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a neural network that recognizes doodles. The vendor does not say where it runs, and the game needs the internet. Students draw, watch the guesses, and use what they see to explain how a model learns from many drawings.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is needed at all times. Drawings and survey responses go to Google, and survey responses fall under Google's Privacy Policy. Drawings can be shared to a public research dataset. That dataset records the prompt, whether the game recognized the drawing, a timestamp, a country code, and the strokes. Its page warns that it may contain inappropriate content even though drawings were moderated. The research did not confirm whether drawings are shared by default or where the recognition model runs. A teacher should tell students that shared drawings become public and check district rules for Google tools.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play ten rounds and record how often the network guessed right.
2. Draw an object in two different ways and compare the guesses.
3. Explain in a sentence why the network knows only a few hundred concepts.
4. Try to fool the network with an odd drawing and describe what happened.
5. Explore the public dataset and describe how people drew the same object.

### visual_arts
1. Draw a simple object with as few lines as you can and see whether it is guessed.
2. Compare how your classmates draw the same word.
3. Sketch a prompt in 20 seconds and then redraw it with more detail.
4. Discuss which lines make a drawing recognizable.
5. Make a class poster of different doodles for one prompt.

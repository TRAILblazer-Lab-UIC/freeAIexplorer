## Overview
This middle school curriculum is led by Safinah Ali. It teaches generative machine learning and creative expression in art, music, poetry, and other media. Students learn about generators and discriminators, examine algorithmic bias, and explore style transfer. They co-create with pre-trained GANs and build a personal GAN project.

## Benefits for students
- Students create art with pre-trained GANs.
- Students explore ethics such as creative ownership and fake media.
- Students build their own GAN project.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Co-create a picture with a pre-trained GAN and describe your role.
2. Use style transfer to restyle a photo and compare the result.
3. Discuss who owns art made with AI help.
4. Build a personal GAN project on a theme you choose.
5. Show your art to the class and explain how you made it.

### music
1. Co-create a short melody with a generative tool.
2. Compare a human tune with an AI tune and list differences.
3. Discuss who should get credit for AI-made music.
4. Describe how a generator and a discriminator would work on music.
5. Plan a short piece that mixes human and AI parts.

### ela
1. Co-write a short poem with a generative tool and edit it by hand.
2. Compare a human poem with an AI poem and discuss the style.
3. Write about how a poet might use AI as a tool.
4. Discuss how fake media could be used to mislead readers.
5. Write a statement that credits AI help in a creative piece.

### computer_science
1. Explain the roles of the generator and the discriminator.
2. Examine how biased data could shape a GAN's output.
3. Test style transfer on two different images and compare results.
4. Describe what pre-trained means in your own words.
5. Plan a personal GAN project and list the data it needs.

## Overview
Nature Net is a paper lesson from the AI Unplugged collection on semantic networks. Students cut out concept cards and arrow cards and glue them into a network. Partners swap networks and answer questions using only the network. This simulates an AI agent with limited knowledge. Reflection questions ask about false information, new knowledge, and how semantic networks differ from neural networks.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a network of nature ideas from cards.
- Students answer questions using only what the network knows.
- Students think about false information and new knowledge in a network.

## Benefits for teachers
- The pack has a card deck with blank cards, a worksheet, and slides.
- A summary document supports the teacher.
- The lesson is closely related to the Model AI Assignments semantic network activity.

## Benefits for schools

- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Where is the AI?
The lesson has no running AI program. It teaches how a knowledge-based AI agent holds facts in a network. Students build the network and use it to answer questions. They then think about what happens when the network holds false facts or lacks new ones.

## Privacy and safety
The lesson runs on paper, no account is needed, and the materials describe no student data collection. The setup needs internet only for the download. A teacher should check the cards for accuracy before class. The summary suggests links to biology, but those are suggestions and not documented classroom uses.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Glue concept cards and arrow cards into a nature network.
2. Swap networks and answer a partner's questions using only their network.
3. Add a false fact to a network and see what answers it produces.
4. Add a new concept and a new link to give the network new knowledge.
5. Explain how a semantic network differs from a neural network.

### science
1. Build a network of plants, animals, and their habitats.
2. Add arrows that show who eats whom in a food web.
3. Use the network to answer a question about an animal's needs.
4. Find a missing link in your network and add it.
5. Compare your network with a classmate's and talk about the differences.

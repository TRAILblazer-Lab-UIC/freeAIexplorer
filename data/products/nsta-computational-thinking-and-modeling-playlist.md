## Overview
This NSTA playlist has four introductory resources on computational thinking and modeling. It is tagged elementary and grade 5. One task has students create a free StarLogo Nova account and program a collision between two agents. A second task has students build a model to explain bananas rotting in a closed jar.

## Benefits for students
- Students program agents in block based StarLogo Nova.
- Students build a model to explain a real event.
- Students link computer models with physical and diagram models.

## Benefits for teachers
- Two further items give teaching ideas, a Science and Children article and an NSTA blog post.
- The article covers a grade 5 class and the blog covers multilingual learners.
- The playlist is short and focused.

## Benefits for schools
- StarLogo Nova is free. The page does not say whether the articles need NSTA membership.
- Students need an account for StarLogo Nova.
- Lesson files are on Google Drive and NSTA servers.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the NSTA playlist and read the four items.
- [ ] Create a StarLogo Nova account.

- [ ] Try the What Happened to the Bananas in the Jar task.

## Where is the AI?
This playlist has no AI. It builds skills in modeling and in programming agents. Students learn how a model can explain a real process.

## Privacy and safety
Students need a StarLogo Nova account, and the internet is needed at all times. The lesson files are on Google Drive and NSTA servers. The research did not find StarLogo Nova's privacy policy or its handling of student data. The materials do not say whether NSTA items need a membership. A teacher should check the data practices and the account setup before students sign up.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Build a model that explains why bananas rot in a jar.
2. Compare the computer model with a diagram of decomposition.
3. Record what the model shows after each change.
4. Predict what will happen before running the model.
5. Explain the model to a partner using the evidence.

### computer_science
1. Complete the Orientation Task and program two agents that collide.
2. Change a block and describe the new behavior.
3. Add a second agent to a model.
4. Debug a model that does not run.
5. Describe what each block tells the agent to do.

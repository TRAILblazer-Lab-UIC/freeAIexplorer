## Overview
Synfig Studio is free and open source 2D animation software. It combines vector and bitmap artwork and calculates in-between frames from key positions. It offers more than 50 layer types and a bone system for cutout animation. Students place keyframes and link parameters to build animations.

## Benefits for students
- Students draw less frame by frame because the program fills in the motion.
- Students can try vector art, bitmap art, and cutout animation.
- Students can link parameters with math expressions for more control.

## Benefits for teachers
- A Synfig video course is available.
- The New York Film Academy list says it has a steeper learning curve than Pencil2D.
- The site offers stable and development builds. The latest release is 1.5.5 from March 2026.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free and donations are optional.
- It needs a dual core CPU near 2 GHz and at least 2 GB of RAM.
- IT approval for installs may be needed. No account is required.

## Getting started
- [ ] Download the installer for Windows, macOS, or Linux from the Synfig site.
- [ ] Choose the stable build for students.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve and install it on each computer.
- [ ] Check that each computer meets the hardware needs.
- [ ] Work through the Synfig video course.
- [ ] Make a short test animation with two keyframes.
- [ ] Plan a first project that is short.

## Where is the AI?
Synfig Studio has no AI. The program calculates in-between frames with simple math, and students decide every key pose. They practice sequencing as they plan motion over time.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed and the internet is used only at setup. Files are saved on the device. The privacy policy covers the synfig.org website and does not address the desktop software. The website shares data with outside services such as Paddle, ElasticEmail, Zoho Desk, Zapier, and Akismet. Whether the desktop app sends any telemetry was not documented. Ask IT to review this before install and decide where students save finished animations.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Draw a shape and animate it moving between two keyframes.
2. Make a character from vector shapes and move its arm with bones.
3. Combine bitmap art and vector art in one scene.
4. Change the timing of a motion and describe the effect.
5. Present a short animation and explain your art choices.

### theater_media
1. Storyboard a ten second scene and build it in Synfig.
2. Use cutout animation to create a puppet character.
3. Add a background and a moving foreground for depth.
4. Export the animation and play it for the class.
5. Compare vector tweening with hand drawn frames and discuss the difference.

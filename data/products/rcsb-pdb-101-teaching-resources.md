## Overview
RCSB PDB-101 is a collection of exercises, readings, and paper models on proteins and other biomolecules. Students use the Mol* 3D viewer or UCSF Chimera on topics such as GFP, antibodies, insulin, sickle cell hemoglobin, and catalase. Printable paper models cover DNA, tRNA, insulin, GFP, antibodies, and the HIV capsid. Resources are marked Intro or Advanced.

## Benefits for students
- Students turn molecules in 3D in a browser.
- Students build paper models that they can hold.
- Students see how structure links to function.

## Benefits for teachers
- Resources are marked Intro or Advanced.
- Activity PDFs and videos support the paper models.
- Molecule of the Month pages give extra background.

## Benefits for schools
- The resources are free.
- Mol* runs in a browser on Chromebooks, Windows, and Mac.
- UCSF Chimera exercises need a desktop install.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the RCSB PDB-101 teaching resources on the RCSB PDB education portal.
- [ ] Open the Biomolecular Structures and Models page.
- [ ] Try one Mol* exercise in a browser.
- [ ] Print one paper model PDF and cut and fold it.
- [ ] Choose an Intro or Advanced level for your class.
- [ ] Ask IT to install UCSF Chimera for any exercise that needs it.
- [ ] Plan the lesson with the activity PDF.

## Where is the AI?
These resources have no AI. They build an AI-related skill, which is pattern spotting. Students look for shapes and parts in molecules and link them to jobs in a cell. That practice is a base for later work with data.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is needed for the browser exercises. The Mol* viewer loads structure data from the PDB over the internet. The page lists no student data collection, but the research did not read a privacy policy. Paper models need no device. A teacher should check which exercises need the desktop install.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Explore GFP in Mol* and describe where the glowing part sits, as the Mol* exercises on GFP do.
2. Build a paper model of DNA, a documented classroom use for grades 9-12.
3. Compare insulin and an antibody in the 3D viewer.
4. Look at sickle cell hemoglobin and describe how a small change matters.
5. Read a Molecule of the Month and write a short summary.

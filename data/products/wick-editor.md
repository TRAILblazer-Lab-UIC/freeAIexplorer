## Overview
Wick Editor is a free, open source, browser based tool for making animations, games, and other media. It combines a timeline with JavaScript scripting. Its design was inspired by HyperCard and Scratch. Students animate and add interactions such as keyboard control and buttons.

## Benefits for students
- Students draw and animate on a timeline.
- Students add buttons and keyboard control with code.
- Students build projects for stories and science.

## Benefits for teachers
- The site offers tutorials and examples.
- A Common Sense Education listing suggests grades 4 to 12.
- The tool suits art, English, and computer science.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free and the code is open source.
- It runs in a browser and suits Chromebooks.
- No account is needed, but the internet is always needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Wick Editor in a web browser.
- [ ] Work through a tutorial on the site.
- [ ] Draw a simple shape and animate it on the timeline.
- [ ] Add a script that responds to a key press.
- [ ] Export a project and test it.
- [ ] Check where student projects will be saved.
- [ ] Ask IT to allow the site on the school network.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI. The tool runs animations and scripts that students make. Students build sequencing and logic skills as they time frames and write scripts.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is always needed. No privacy policy was found. The site loads a cookie consent banner and a Plausible analytics script. The research did not confirm how or where projects are saved. A teacher should test saving and exporting before class and ask IT to review the analytics.

## Overview
NLP4Science is a middle school science curriculum and interactive visualization app that teach natural language processing. Students run sentiment analysis and keyword extraction on text. They view results as word clouds, pie charts, and tables. A 2023 poster describes a 2-day study with 48 eighth graders in Indiana and a 5-day study with 50 sixth graders in Florida.

## Benefits for students
- Students analyze real tweets about plastics in the ocean.
- Students view results in word clouds, pie charts, histograms, and gauges.
- Students can upload their own CSV or TXT data or type text directly.

## Benefits for teachers
- The design process included four teachers.
- The poster reports 83 percent of interviewed students preferred the word cloud for keywords.
- About 95 percent preferred the pie chart for sentiment.

## Benefits for schools
- The research found no public release of the app or curriculum.
- The price is not verified.
- The app appears to be a research platform used in partner classrooms, and the project is funded by NSF grants DRL-2147810 and 2147811.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is natural language processing models for sentiment analysis and keyword extraction. The vendor does not say where they run. Students pick a text dataset, run the models, and use the charts to describe what people think about a topic.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need or where data goes. The poster does not say where the app is hosted or where uploaded text goes. The research team recorded interviews with 19 students for their own study. The tweets used were real. A teacher should preview the text for language that is not suitable for students. A teacher should also ask the team how student uploads are stored.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Analyze tweets about plastics in the ocean and describe the main concerns.
2. Compare the sentiment of tweets from two different weeks.
3. Use keyword extraction to list the topics people raise most.
4. Write a short claim about ocean plastics and back it with the charts.
5. Discuss whether tweets give a full picture of a science issue.

### computer_science
1. Run sentiment analysis on a short text and explain the score.
2. Compare a word cloud with a pie chart and say which is clearer.
3. Test a model on a sentence with sarcasm and describe the result.
4. Combine two models in a workflow and describe the output.
5. Use the ethical matrix to list who is affected by the analysis.

### ela
1. Read ten tweets and mark each as positive, negative, or neutral by hand.
2. Compare your marks with the model's sentiment scores.
3. Pick keywords from a text and compare them with the model's list.
4. Write a short summary of what a word cloud shows.
5. Discuss how word choice shapes the tone of a message.

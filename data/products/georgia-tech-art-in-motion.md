## Overview
Art in Motion is a project-based curriculum from Georgia Tech CEISMC that brings computing into art classes. Students pick inspiration pieces from local art museums and galleries. They build moving robotic sculptures with the Hummingbird Robotics Kit programmed in Scratch. They follow an art process of sketching, journaling, revising and peer critique.

## Benefits for students
- Students see how computing can serve art.
- Students sketch, journal and revise their sculptures.
- Students give and receive peer critique.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Pick an artwork from a museum and sketch ideas for a moving version.
2. Keep a journal of how your sculpture changes.
3. Revise your design after peer critique.
4. Explain how motion adds meaning to your piece.
5. Display the sculptures in a class gallery.

### computer_science
1. Program a servo in Scratch to move your sculpture.
2. Add light and test the timing.
3. Debug a motion that does not match the plan.
4. Repeat a movement with a loop.
5. Explain your program to a classmate.

### engineering
1. Build a frame that holds the motors firmly.
2. Test how much weight a servo can move.
3. Fix a part that jams or slips.
4. Compare two designs for the same artwork.
5. Document your build with photos and notes.

## Overview
AI Unplugged is a free set of hands-on AI literacy activities from Northwestern's Creative Interfaces studio, funded by NSF. The site lists activities under three topics. They are Introducing AI, Representation and Reasoning, and Data Privacy and Ethics. A pilot ran in a five-day middle school summer workshop.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn AI ideas through crafts and games.
- Students try activities such as Large Language MadLibs and Monster Mapping.
- Students discuss data privacy and ethics.

## Benefits for teachers
- The set has 13 activities across three topics.
- Most need only paper, scissors, glue, and string.
- A free textbook link and CC BY-NC 4.0 license make sharing simple.

## Benefits for schools
- Everything is free.
- Kernel Quest needs cardstock, polarizer film, and a tablet.
- The internet is needed only to download the materials.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the AI Unplugged site from Northwestern's Creative Interfaces studio.
- [ ] Open the textbook and the activity pages.
- [ ] Pick one activity from a topic that fits your class.
- [ ] Print the materials.
- [ ] Gather scissors, glue, string, and pencils.
- [ ] Order polarizer film only for Kernel Quest.
- [ ] Try the activity with a small group first.

## Where is the AI?
The activities have no software and no AI runs on any device. They teach classification, representation and reasoning, language models, data privacy, and AI ethics. Students build skills in sorting data, spotting patterns, and thinking about how models use language.

## Privacy and safety
The activities are hands on and the research found no student data collection. The materials do not state whether accounts are needed. Kernel Quest uses a tablet and the site does not say whether it needs an account. The textbook is hosted on Canva. A teacher should check the Kernel Quest tablet use and the Canva terms with IT.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Play Are you smarter than AI? and compare a human guess with an AI guess.
2. Sort items with DataBites and test the sorter on a new item.
3. Map made-up monsters by features in Monster Mapping.
4. Build a small net of connected ideas in Nature Net.
5. Explain in a short note how one activity shows what an AI model does.

### ela
1. Fill in a Large Language MadLibs and note how the choices change the story.
2. Predict the next word in a sentence and compare guesses with a partner.
3. Write a short story with a predicted word at each step.
4. Discuss why a language model may pick a likely word and not the best word.
5. Explain how the MadLibs game is like a language model.

### social_studies
1. Complete Datasets and Data Brokers and discuss what the profiles reveal.
2. Draw a Data Portrait that shows what an app might know about a person.
3. Use EthiQuest to talk about a hard choice involving data.
4. Debate who should own personal data.
5. Write a short rule that protects privacy in a school app.

## Overview
This is a set of family-friendly activities described in a post from the MIT Media Lab. Three activities fit different ages. YouTube recommendations suit ages 7 to 9, advertisement design suits ages 9 to 11, and online consent suits ages 12 to 14. The fourth is an at-home AI debate guide for parents.

## Benefits for students
- Younger students learn why a video site suggests certain videos.
- Students design an advertisement and see how ads aim at certain people.
- Older students learn what online consent means and why COPPA exists.

## Ideas by subject
### library_media
1. Compare the videos a recommendation system suggests to two different viewers and explain the differences.
2. Sort a list of recommendations by who might benefit from each one.
3. Design a short guide for younger students on how to spot an ad.
4. Hold a class debate on whether apps should ask for consent in plain words.
5. Write a library display that explains how recommendations work.

### social_studies
1. Read a short summary of COPPA and list who it protects.
2. Role-play a family deciding whether to accept an app's terms.
3. Write a classroom rule for sharing personal data online.
4. Discuss how ads shape what people want to buy.
5. Debate whether companies or families should decide what data apps collect.

### computer_science
1. Act out a recommendation algorithm by sorting cards based on past choices.
2. Draw a flowchart showing how a video site picks the next video.
3. List the data a recommendation system might use and rank it by privacy risk.
4. Design a better recommendation rule and explain who it helps.
5. Test a rule on a small class poll and find where it fails.

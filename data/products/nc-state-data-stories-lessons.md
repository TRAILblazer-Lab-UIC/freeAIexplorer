## Overview
These free lessons come from the NC State Data Science Academy and use the CODAP web tool. In the PFAS Water Contamination lesson, students act as data scientists for an environmental consulting firm. A second lesson uses a large North Carolina health and environment survey with more than 100 variables. The official page lists grades 8 to 12 in science, social studies, and math.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with real datasets and codebooks.
- Students collect, wrangle, and analyze data in CODAP.
- Students take on the role of a data scientist.

## Benefits for teachers
- A student guide in slides walks through each lesson.
- Teachers in a professional development workshop used the PFAS lesson with grade 8 through high school science classes.
- The lessons came out of the Real Teaching with Real Data program.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons are free.
- CODAP runs in a browser with no install.
- IT work is minimal.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson set has no AI. It builds data skills, such as sorting, graphing, and finding patterns. These skills prepare students to judge the data behind AI tools.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is needed at all times. CODAP has no user accounts, and its privacy page says Concord collects no personal information when the main app is used without a login. Documents can be saved to Google Drive, which follows Google's policy. Sharing a document by URL stores a copy on the CODAP server. The page does not address student data specifically. A teacher should keep students from saving to personal Google accounts and preview the health survey questions.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Study the PFAS water data and decide which sites need action.
2. Graph contamination levels by location and describe the pattern.
3. Write a short report for the consulting firm with a recommendation.
4. Compare two water sources and explain the difference.
5. List questions the data cannot answer.

### math
1. Make a graph of a variable from the survey and describe its shape.
2. Find the mean and median of a set of readings.
3. Compare two groups in the data and report the difference.
4. Clean a dataset by removing an entry that looks wrong.
5. Explain which graph best shows the pattern.

### social_studies
1. Use the health and environment survey to compare communities.
2. Describe how a community might be affected by water contamination.
3. Write a question about the survey that a government could study.
4. Discuss who should decide how to act on the results.
5. Present findings to the class as a short briefing.

## Overview
The Arduino UNO Q is a dual-brain board in the UNO shape. A Qualcomm quad-core processor runs Linux, and an STM32 microcontroller handles real-time input and output. Arduino App Lab pairs Python on Linux with C sketches on the microcontroller. App Lab bricks add ready-made functions, including vision and audio models.

## Benefits for students
- Students see two kinds of processors work together.
- Students use ready-made vision and audio bricks.
- Students write both Python and C code.

## Benefits for teachers
- Three modes are available. They are standalone, PC-connected, and network.
- App Lab includes guided lessons and examples.
- University workshops in Taiwan used it with Edge Impulse.

## Benefits for schools
- The store lists the 2 GB model at 59.90 euros with VAT. No USD price was found.
- It costs well above the Uno R3.
- Teacher setup is needed, and some data may go to services such as Edge Impulse.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the UNO Q page on the Arduino store.
- [ ] Buy a board and a monitor with a keyboard for standalone mode.
- [ ] Use the pre-installed App Lab in standalone mode.
- [ ] Install App Lab on a PC for the PC-connected mode.
- [ ] Stay online during setup.
- [ ] Try a guided lesson with a vision or audio brick.
- [ ] Decide whether students need accounts for any cloud tool.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is ready-made vision and audio models in App Lab bricks. They run on the board. Students pick a brick, write code around it, and test the results. They can also train a model in a tool such as Edge Impulse and deploy it.

## Privacy and safety
An account is optional. Edge Impulse model training needs an Edge Impulse account and uses a cloud workflow. The research did not read the Edge Impulse terms. The product page says bricks also cover data storage and cloud integration. The research found no privacy policy specific to App Lab. A teacher should check the terms of any tool the class uses and decide where student camera and audio data is stored.

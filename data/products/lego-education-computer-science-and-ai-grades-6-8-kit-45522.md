## Overview
This LEGO Education kit serves four students and combines building, block coding in the Coding Canvas app, and AI computer vision. It has 379 bricks, a double motor, a single motor, a color sensor, a controller, two connection cards, a USB cable, and a storage box. The page lists 30 lessons across six units, with AI and data as a unit. The Teacher Portal provides presentations and facilitation notes.

## Benefits for students
- Students build, code, and test real models.
- Students try computer vision and pose detection.
- Students learn how a model is trained to classify things.

## Benefits for teachers
- The free Teacher Portal gives presentations and notes.
- Students join lessons with a PIN.
- Some lessons are screen-free.

## Benefits for schools
- The product page lists 529.95 USD for one kit and 3499 USD for a 24 student bundle.
- Professional learning is priced separately.
- One device serves each group of four for some lessons.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the kit on the LEGO Education site.
- [ ] Sign up for the free Teacher Portal.
- [ ] Install the Coding Canvas web app or iOS app while online.
- [ ] Charge the motor and controller with the USB cable.
- [ ] Build the first model with a group of four.
- [ ] Share the PIN so students can join the lesson.
- [ ] Review the privacy terms before students sign in.

## Where is the AI?
The kit uses computer vision, pose detection, and classification. The AI runs on the device in the Coding Canvas app. Students use it to make their models respond to what a camera sees. They then test and improve how well the model works.

## Privacy and safety
Students join lessons with a PIN and need no account. Teachers sign up for the free Teacher Portal. LEGO states that all AI features run locally on the student device, that no data goes to the LEGO Group or third parties, and that no models are trained on student data. It says only pose data is kept locally. The statement names Google MoveNet as the engine and does not say whether any data goes to Google. The internet is needed for first install, updates, and language changes. A teacher should read the LEGO privacy terms before teachers or students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Open the AI and data unit and train a model to classify a set of objects.
2. Use pose detection to trigger a motor with a gesture.
3. Collect test results and find where the model fails.
4. Code the controller to react to the color sensor.
5. Debate what kinds of mistakes a vision model can make.

### engineering
1. Build a model that uses both motors and test its balance.
2. Redesign a part to improve speed or strength.
3. Place the color sensor at several heights and compare readings.
4. Keep a design log of each change and its effect.
5. Present the final model to another class.

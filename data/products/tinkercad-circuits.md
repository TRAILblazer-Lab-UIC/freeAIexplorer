## Overview
Tinkercad Circuits is the browser-based circuit and Arduino simulator inside Autodesk Tinkercad. Students build virtual circuits and can program Arduino and micro:bit boards in the simulator. Teachers can create classes and add or invite students. No physical board is needed for simulation.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and test circuits with no risk to real parts.
- Students program Arduino and micro:bit boards in the simulator.
- Students can try again as often as they like.

## Benefits for teachers
- Teachers create classes and add or invite students.
- A dashboard shows student work.
- An Arizona STEM Acceleration Project lesson offers three online simulation lessons on changing components.

## Benefits for schools
- The tool is free.
- Autodesk offers a Student Data Privacy Agreement that schools sign.
- Students need only a web browser, including on a Chromebook.

## Where is the AI?
Tinkercad Circuits has no AI. The simulator runs the circuits and code that students build. Students build skills in logic and in how circuits and code work together.

## Privacy and safety
Accounts are required and designs are stored on Autodesk and service provider servers worldwide. Student accounts made by a school may collect names, email, birthdate, and school address, or less with single sign on. Autodesk states kidSAFE COPPA certification and FERPA school official status, and says child accounts need parent or guardian consent. Cookies and persistent identifiers are collected, some by third parties, and public content may be visible to others. The research did not read the Student Data Privacy Agreement or the Common Sense privacy rating. Read the privacy statement, sign the agreement before adding students, and use aliases.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Build a simple LED circuit and test that it lights.
2. Follow the ASU lesson on changing components and record what changes.
3. Add a resistor and compare the brightness.
4. Build a circuit with a switch and test it.
5. Draw a circuit plan before building it and compare the two.

### computer_science
1. Program an Arduino in the simulator to blink an LED.
2. Change the delay value and observe the result.
3. Add a button and write code that reacts to it.
4. Program a micro:bit in the simulator.
5. Debug a sketch that does not light the LED.

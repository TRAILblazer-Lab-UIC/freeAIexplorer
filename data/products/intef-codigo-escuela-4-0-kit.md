## Overview
This is a 2023 welcome kit for Spain's Codigo Escuela 4.0 program from INTEF. It sets out the curriculum framework for infant, primary, and secondary education. It lists teacher training on computational thinking, programming, educational robotics, 3D design, XR, programmable boards, and artificial intelligence. The kit includes 12 sample learning situations from age 3 through secondary. It is written in Spanish.

## Benefits for students
- Students meet computing through learning situations built for their age.
- Primary students can explore how senses work in people and machines.
- Primary students can explore physics with Scratch.

## Benefits for teachers
- The kit includes a teacher training catalog.
- The AI section lists webinars and self-paced courses such as AI with Scratch.
- The material uses a CC BY-SA 4.0 license.

## Benefits for schools
- The kit is free.
- Hardware for robotics activities is bought separately.
- It is a PDF with links. IT has little to install.

## Where is the AI?
The kit is a curriculum guide and has optional AI content. The AI section lists webinars for teachers and an AI with Scratch course. The vendor does not say where any AI tools run. Students learn from the activities how machines sense and use data.

## Privacy and safety
The kit is a PDF with links to other resources. The PDF needs no account. The linked courses and tools were not read. Their accounts, data flow, and AI location are unknown. A teacher should check each linked tool before students use it. A teacher should also check what data each tool collects.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Try the primary learning situation about how senses work in people and machines.
2. Compare how a robot senses its world with how students sense theirs.
3. Write a short Scratch program that follows a plan from the kit.
4. Sort a list of everyday tools into those that use computing and those that do not.
5. Present what you learned from one of the sample learning situations.

### science
1. Explore physics with Scratch by following the primary learning situation.
2. Predict how an object will move and then test it in a Scratch model.
3. Compare the Scratch results with a real object rolling down a ramp.
4. Record the results in a simple table.
5. Explain which parts of the model match the real world and which do not.

## Overview
This magazine tutorial by Dale Lane has students build a virtual classroom assistant in Scratch 3. First they write a rules-based version that turns a virtual lamp and fan on and off. Then they train a text model in Machine Learning for Kids using example phrases. They compare how the two versions handle new wording. No hardware is needed.

## Benefits for students
- Students see the limits of rules written by hand.
- Students train a text model with their own example phrases.
- Students test it with phrases it never saw.

## Benefits for teachers
- The tutorial has 12 steps.
- It suits grades 5-9 according to the discovery notes.
- Teachers may need a class account on the training site. This was not confirmed.

## Benefits for schools
- The tutorial is free.
- It works in a browser and on Chromebooks.
- Student phrases go to the vendor site. A privacy review is needed.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a text classifier trained in Machine Learning for Kids. It runs in the vendor's cloud and the browser. Students type example phrases for each label, train the model, and load it into Scratch. They then compare it with their hand-written rules.

## Privacy and safety
Typed example phrases and project data go to the Machine Learning for Kids vendor site, which hosts the training. The internet is needed throughout. The tutorial does not say whether student accounts are needed, and the research did not read the site's privacy terms. A teacher should review the site privacy terms and tell students to avoid personal details in phrases.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Write a rules-based script that reacts to the phrase turn on the lamp.
2. Try new wording such as switch the light on and see where the rules fail.
3. Create four labels and add at least ten example phrases for each.
4. Train the model and test it with phrases it did not see.
5. Write a short comparison of rules and machine learning for this task.

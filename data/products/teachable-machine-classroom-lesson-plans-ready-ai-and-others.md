## Overview
These ReadyAI lesson plans have students train a Teachable Machine model on a task such as sorting poisonous and non poisonous fish. Students first sort fish images by hand in an unplugged game and build a decision tree. They then give the same problem to Teachable Machine and compare how humans and machines learn. The author says the full plan is free after setting up an account.

## Benefits for students
- Students compare how people and machines learn.
- Students test a model on new images and student drawn images.
- Students record accuracy as percentages.

## Benefits for teachers
- The lesson mixes an unplugged game with a hands on tool.
- One teacher adapted it to colors and artistic styles.
- A related Code.org lesson exists for another approach.

## Benefits for schools
- The plan is free after setting up an account.
- Teachable Machine runs in a browser.
- Students need a device with a browser and a camera or image files.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is an image classification model that students train in Teachable Machine. A search snippet says it trains locally in the browser. Students add examples, test the model, and compare the results with their own sorting. The lesson shows how the training data shapes the answers.

## Privacy and safety
A teacher account is needed to open the full lesson plan. The research could not read the ReadyAI post or the plan. Lesson data handling is unconfirmed. Search snippets say Teachable Machine trains in the browser and keeps samples on the device unless the user saves or shares them. The research did not confirm this on a vendor page. Saving a project to Google Drive or uploading a model is separate data handling. Check whether images stay in the browser and prefer sample images over photos of students.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Sort fish images by hand and draw the decision tree.
2. Train a Teachable Machine model on the same fish images.
3. Test the model with new images and record the percentages.
4. Draw a fish of your own and test whether the model gets it right.
5. Compare how you learned the task with how the model learned it.

### science
1. Study the traits that mark a poisonous fish and list them.
2. Sort fish images by trait before using the tool.
3. Test the model on a fish the class has not seen.
4. Discuss what mistakes could mean in real life.
5. Change the topic to leaves or rocks and train a new model.

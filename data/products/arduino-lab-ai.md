## Overview
Arduino Lab - AI Edition is a classroom bundle for teaching AI, TinyML, and robotics. It holds 10 Nano 33 BLE Sense Rev2 boards, 8 Nicla Vision units, 4 Portenta H7 modules with Vision Shield accessories, 8 Alvik robots, and 10 Plug and Make Kits. The store targets STEM and CTE programs, secondary and post-secondary education, clubs, and makerspaces.

## Benefits for students
- Students work with real vision and sound hardware.
- Students run AI models on tiny boards.
- Students program Alvik robots and see the results move.

## Benefits for teachers
- One purchase covers a full class set.
- The bundle works with the Arduino AI Coding Assistant.
- It connects to Arduino Cloud for Schools, which is sold separately.

## Benefits for schools
- The store listed the bundle at 2,949 USD, which is a high cost.
- Arduino Cloud for Schools costs extra and its price was not found.
- School IT approval is likely needed for accounts.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is computer vision and TinyML that runs on the boards. The Cloud tools run in the browser and the Arduino cloud. Students collect data, train a model, and load it on a board. They then test how well the board recognizes what it sees.

## Privacy and safety
Student accounts are needed for Arduino Cloud. The policy says Junior Accounts for users under 14 need parental consent and that COPPA may apply. Under 14 data may be visible to other students and the teacher. The policy collects username, email, and usage data and allows transfers worldwide with Qualcomm Group entities. It does not describe Arduino Cloud for Schools separately. The Google Classroom page says teacher and student data is never sold, but the policy says some U.S. user data may be shared with advertising partners. The materials do not state whether the AI features work offline. A teacher should read both pages before use.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Collect sound samples with a Nano 33 BLE Sense and train a small model.
2. Use a Nicla Vision camera to sort two kinds of objects.
3. Ask the AI Coding Assistant for help and then check its code.
4. Compare the accuracy of a model with a small and a large data set.
5. Program an Alvik robot to react to what a model sees.

### engineering
1. Identify the sensors on each board and say what each measures.
2. Build a circuit with a Plug and Make Kit and test it.
3. Compare the Portenta and the Nano for power and size.
4. Design a task for an Alvik robot and test its path.
5. Measure how fast a board answers and suggest how to improve it.

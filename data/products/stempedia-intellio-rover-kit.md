## Overview
The Quarky Intellio Rover Kit is a rover with a camera, microphone, and speaker. Students program it in PictoBlox with blocks or Python. It can run TinyML models on the device. The vendor lists classes 3 to 8 for blocks and classes 7 to 12 for Python.

## Benefits for students
- Students move from blocks to Python on the same rover.
- Students test object detection that works offline.
- Students can import models trained in Edge Impulse.

## Benefits for teachers
- The classroom pack includes 100 student course licenses.
- The pack also includes 2 online teacher training sessions.
- PictoBlox gives teachers one tool for blocks and Python.

## Benefits for schools
- A single kit is listed at 169 USD. The classroom pack of 12 has no listed price and offers a quote.
- Offline object detection works after code is uploaded.
- GPT-based features need an internet connection. IT should plan network access.

## Getting started
- [ ] Order a single kit or request a quote for the classroom pack from STEMpedia.
- [ ] Install PictoBlox on a Windows, Mac, Linux, or Chromebook computer. Platform support was not confirmed on the product page.
- [ ] Upload the firmware to the rover.
- [ ] Connect over Bluetooth, USB-C, or Wi-Fi.
- [ ] Use a 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network for camera streaming.
- [ ] Plan for about 3.5 hours of battery life while streaming.
- [ ] Choose whether students will use accounts, and review the privacy details first.

## Where is the AI?
The rover runs object detection and TinyML on the device. PictoBlox also offers GPT-based features, and those use a company's cloud. Students program the rover to react to what it sees and test models of their own.

## Privacy and safety
PictoBlox accounts are optional. They collect a username, profile picture and age. Users 16 and older also give an email, country, date of birth and gender, and users under 16 give a guardian email. The STEMpedia policy says it does not knowingly collect information from children under 13, which is a data rule and not a minimum age. Edge tasks run on the device. GPT features need the internet, and the research did not find where that data goes. A teacher should review the privacy details before choosing whether students use accounts.

## Overview
Google AI Quests is a game-based learning experience for students aged 11 to 14. Students play researchers in a fantasy world with a mentor named Professor Skye. The first quest covers flood forecasting and how data quality affects AI results. Two more quests, on diabetic retinopathy and mapping the human brain, were announced in September 2025.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn through a game and need no coding skills.
- Students see how data quality changes what an AI can do.
- Students watch a video message from researchers at the end of each quest.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each quest has a lesson plan and a teacher guide with pre and post game activities.
- The Teacher Resources tab holds lesson plans and slides.
- The game is based on real Google Research projects.

## Benefits for schools
- The blog says the quests are open and free to educators.
- The site opens in Chrome with no install.
- The recommended minimum is an Intel i5-5250U processor, 4 GB RAM, and Chrome v139 or later.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open the Google AI Quests site in Chrome.
- [ ] Check that the browser is Chrome v139 or later.
- [ ] Open the Teacher Resources tab for the lesson plan and slides.
- [ ] Play the flood forecasting quest yourself.
- [ ] Run the pre game activity with students.
- [ ] Have students play the quest.
- [ ] Run the post game activity and discuss data quality.

## Where is the AI?
The quests teach ideas about AI such as flood forecasting, data quality, and medical image analysis. The activity runs on the vendor's web service, and students play inside a game. Students make choices about data and see how the results change. They use these ideas to judge real AI tools.

## Privacy and safety
The internet is always needed and the game runs on the vendor's web service. The materials do not state whether accounts are needed or what student data is collected. The About page links a privacy notice that the research did not read. The general Google Privacy Policy describes broad collection of device and activity data and refers to separate notices for school and child accounts. The research did not confirm whether AI Quests uses analytics. A teacher should read the AI Quests privacy notice and test the site on student devices.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Play the flood forecasting quest and explain how data helps predict floods.
2. Compare the data in the game with real river and rainfall data.
3. Write a short note on how a forecast can help a community.
4. Discuss what a researcher in the game learns from good data.
5. Draw a flow chart that shows how data becomes a forecast.

### computer_science
1. Change the data in the game and describe how the AI result changes.
2. List three signs of poor quality data.
3. Explain why more data is not always better.
4. Write a rule for a team that collects data for an AI project.
5. Teach a classmate one idea from the quest.

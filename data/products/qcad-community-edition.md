## Overview
QCAD is a 2D CAD system for technical drawing with layers, blocks, dimensioning, and precision tools. The Community Edition is the free open source core. A paid Professional edition adds proprietary plugins, mainly for DWG support and better DXF handling. It suits older students in drafting or engineering classes.

## Benefits for students
- Students draw precise 2D plans with layers and dimensions.
- Students practice drafting skills that carry over to other CAD tools.
- Students can script drawing tasks through ECMAScript.

## Benefits for teachers
- The core handles DXF files for sharing drawings.
- The layout is familiar to anyone who has used a drafting program.
- Teachers can show layers, blocks, and dimensions in one tool.

## Benefits for schools
- The Community Edition is free.
- It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
- IT may need to approve the install.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find QCAD on its official site and read the download page.
- [ ] Check how to get the free Community Edition. A search snippet says the official download is the Professional trial and the free edition comes from removing its add-on or compiling the source.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install on managed computers.
- [ ] Install the build for your platform on a test computer.
- [ ] Draw a simple floor plan with layers and dimensions.
- [ ] Save the plan as a DXF file and reopen it.
- [ ] Plan for DWG files, which need the paid Professional edition.

## Where is the AI?
QCAD has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is working with precise data. Students enter exact measurements and organize them in layers. That discipline helps them treat any design as structured information.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the internet is used at setup. Drawings stay on the device. No privacy policy was reviewed, and the vendor materials list no student data collection. A teacher should confirm which edition IT installs and check that the free version works for the class.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Draw a 2D technical plan of a classroom with layers and dimensions.
2. Use blocks to reuse one symbol, such as a door, across a plan.
3. Measure an object and draw it to scale.
4. Add dimensions to a drawing and check them with a partner.
5. Redesign a small room and compare the old and new plans.

### cte
1. Practice drafting by copying a simple technical drawing line by line.
2. Create a layer system for walls, doors, and notes.
3. Draw a plan for a shelf with exact measurements.
4. Export a drawing as DXF and open it in a second program.
5. Write a short guide that shows a new student how to draw a rectangle with dimensions.

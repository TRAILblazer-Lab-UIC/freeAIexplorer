## Overview
Insight Maker is a browser-based tool for building simulation models with no download. Users create rich pictures and causal loop diagrams and turn them into System Dynamics or Agent Based simulations. Models can be shared by link. A free account is offered to build and save models. It suits high school science and math.

## Benefits for students
- Students build models of real systems such as ecosystems.
- Students change a setting and watch the model respond.
- Students can share a model by link.

## Benefits for teachers
- A Coursera guided project builds an ecosystem model.
- Rich pictures and causal loop diagrams help students plan.
- No download is needed.

## Benefits for schools
- Model building and sharing are free per the site. Paid tiers were not seen.
- It runs in a browser and works on Chromebooks.
- The interface may have changed since the Coursera course was recorded.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open insightmaker.com in a web browser.
- [ ] Create a free teacher account to save models.
- [ ] Note that users under 13 are outside the stated audience.
- [ ] Build a small sample model with one stock and one flow.
- [ ] Read the privacy policy with your school.
- [ ] Try the ecosystem model project.
- [ ] Decide whether students share models publicly.

## Where is the AI?
Insight Maker has no AI. It builds modeling skills. Students decide which parts of a system matter and test how they connect. It runs in the browser and the vendor's cloud.

## Privacy and safety
An account collects an email and personal details, saved models, IP address, browser type, pages visited, and error data. Data goes to the vendor, and display names appear publicly on shared Insights. The policy says the services are not aimed at children under 13 and that the company does not knowingly collect their data. That is a data rule, and COPPA is not named. The policy does not describe sharing personal data with outside parties. A teacher should read the privacy policy, keep model titles free of student names, and decide whether models stay private.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Build a model of a pond with fish and plants.
2. Change the number of predators and watch the population.
3. Draw a causal loop diagram of a food chain.
4. Compare a model with real data from a class survey.
5. Explain what the model leaves out.

### math
1. Set up a stock and a flow with a rate.
2. Graph how a stock changes over time.
3. Change a rate and describe the new graph.
4. Build a model of savings with interest.
5. Compare two models and say which fits the data better.

## Overview
This is Unit 4 of the CodeHS Coding with BBC micro:bit course for grades 3 to 5. Students program social studies ideas with the micro:bit and Scratch. Projects include an interactive map of the 13 colonies and a Revolutionary War timeline. The whole course has 5 units and 13 lessons, about 14 hours.

## Benefits for students
- Students tie coding to history they study in class.
- Students use a real micro:bit board with blocks.
- Students make a map and a timeline that others can explore.

## Benefits for teachers
- The unit connects social studies content to coding.
- Chromebooks connect through the Microsoft MakeCode site.
- Teachers can pair students, with one board for each pair.

## Benefits for schools
- The course page lists no price and points teachers to a contact link.
- Micro:bit boards and USB cables cost extra.
- Data goes to CodeHS, and MakeCode is a Microsoft service with its own terms.

## Where is the AI?
This unit has no AI. It builds skills in sequencing and events. Students program a board and a Scratch project to respond to what a user does.

## Privacy and safety
The course page does not state whether accounts are required, and the internet is needed at all times. CodeHS holds account data and coursework in its cloud. The policy says it acts as a school official under FERPA, relies on school consent for users under 13, and does not sell or rent personal information. MakeCode is a Microsoft service with its own terms, and the research did not review them. A teacher should confirm which accounts students need before the first lesson.

## Ideas by subject
### social_studies
1. Build an interactive map of the 13 colonies and add a fact to each one.
2. Program a Revolutionary War timeline with events in order.
3. Make a map button that shows the name of a colony.
4. Quiz a partner on colony locations with the project.
5. Compare two timelines and explain which event came first.

### computer_science
1. Program the micro:bit to show a message when a button is pressed.
2. Use Scratch blocks to react to a click on the map.
3. Debug a project that shows events in the wrong order.
4. Use a variable to track a quiz score.
5. Explain the steps of a program to a partner.

## Overview
In this project of about two hours, students build a machine learning model that classifies music with Google Teachable Machine. A search snippet lists it at medium difficulty for high school. The same project appears in a CodeHS Introduction to AI for Middle School course. Students also reflect on how training data affects accuracy.

## Benefits for students
- Students train an audio model on music clips.
- Students test the model and see its mistakes.
- Students reflect on how data changes accuracy.

## Benefits for teachers
- The project fits in one or two class periods.
- Students can use a microphone or audio files.
- It links music and computer science.

## Benefits for schools
- A batch note says teacher signup is free for grades 6-12, but a page did not confirm it.
- It runs in a browser on Chromebooks, Windows, and Mac.
- The CodeHS page returned only a login screen.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is an audio classifier made in Teachable Machine. It trains in the browser, and CodeHS runs in the vendor's cloud. Students collect music samples, train the model, and test it. They then change the samples to see how accuracy shifts.

## Privacy and safety
A CodeHS account is needed and holds account data and coursework. Teachable Machine audio samples stay in the browser unless students choose to save to Drive. Teachable Machine is a Google web tool with its own terms, and the research did not review them. The CodeHS policy says it acts as a school official under FERPA and does not sell personal information. The research could not read the project page. A teacher should check the district terms for both services. A teacher should also use clips that are free to use.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Train a model on clips of two genres and test it with a new song.
2. Add a third genre and see how the accuracy changes.
3. Find a song that the model gets wrong and explain why.
4. Compare how the model hears a quiet song and a loud song.
5. Build a playlist sorted by the model and review its choices.

### computer_science
1. Collect training samples and label them with care.
2. Change the number of samples and compare the accuracy.
3. Test the model with new sounds and record the results.
4. Write a reflection on how training data shapes the model.
5. Explain how a model could be unfair to a music style.

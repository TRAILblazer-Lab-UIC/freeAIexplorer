## Overview
Robot Dance Party is a computational thinking activity listed on Education.com for third grade. Students write a sequence of commands that moves a robot to the rhythm of a song. They dance like robots first. Then they program a floor robot such as a Bee-Bot or a Code-and-Go Robot Mouse and run the program beside it.

## Benefits for students
- Students move their bodies first, which makes the idea of a command sequence easy to feel.
- Students plan a series of steps and then test whether the robot matches the music.
- Students answer reflection questions at the end of the activity.

## Benefits for teachers
- The activity is a download that a teacher can prepare in about an hour.
- The page lists a Computer Science subtopic. That fits a coding lesson.
- Teachers can pair it with a floor robot the class already owns.

## Benefits for schools
- The page labels the download free.
- The robot is a separate cost.
- The activity runs on paper and the robot. It needs no student accounts or screens.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Robot Dance Party activity on Education.com and download it.
- [ ] Gather a programmable floor robot such as a Bee-Bot or a Code-and-Go Robot Mouse.
- [ ] Choose a song with a steady beat.

## Where is the AI?
This activity has no AI. The robot follows the exact commands that students enter. Students build the skill of sequencing, which means putting steps in the right order.

## Privacy and safety
The activity page shows Log In, Sign Up and Upgrade links, and the materials do not say whether an account is needed to download. The robot runs on its own, and students enter no personal information. The research did not read a privacy policy. A teacher should check whether Education.com asks for a login before the download. A teacher should also test the robot on the classroom floor.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Write a sequence of forward and turn commands that moves a robot across the floor in time with a song.
2. Dance like a robot and then record each move as a command on paper.
3. Trade command lists with a partner and predict where the robot will end up.
4. Find a mistake in a sequence and fix it until the robot matches the plan.
5. Answer the reflection questions and explain how the program changed after testing.

### music
1. Clap the beat of a song and count how many beats the robot needs for each move.
2. Match one robot command to each beat of a short song.
3. Compare a fast song and a slow song and describe how the program must change.
4. Add a pause command at a rest in the music.
5. Perform the finished dance with the robot for the class.

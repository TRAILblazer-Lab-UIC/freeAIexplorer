## Overview
This multi-week unit of CS Discoveries is a hands-on introduction to machine learning. Students use AI Lab to train and save models and work with numeric and survey data. They study bias and then build apps in App Lab. The unit includes the AI Code of Ethics lesson. A page from Code.org notes that newer AI Discoveries and AI Foundations courses also exist.

## Benefits for students
- Students train real models on tabular data.
- Students build an app that uses their own model.
- Students study bias and ethics.

## Benefits for teachers
- A free teacher account supports sections and assignments.
- Lessons guide the teacher step by step.
- The unit runs over several weeks.

## Benefits for schools
- The unit is free.
- It works on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- App Lab works best on a laptop or desktop with a keyboard.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a machine learning classifier trained in AI Lab. The tools run in the browser with the vendor's cloud. Students pick data, train and test a model, and then use it inside an app they build. They also explore bias in training data.

## Privacy and safety
Student accounts are optional and store progress, code, and projects. Without accounts only an IP address is collected. The data goes to the vendor, and the internet is needed throughout. The policy says it does not sell personal data or show ads. It says the nonprofit is not subject to COPPA and treats school directed accounts as FERPA student records. The research did not confirm where AI Lab inference runs. A teacher should decide whether students need accounts. A teacher should also read the Code.org privacy terms with the district.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Train a model in AI Lab on a table of numbers and report the result.
2. Save the model and import it into an App Lab project.
3. Test the model with new data and describe errors.
4. Find a bias in a data set and write a fix.
5. Present the app and explain how the model works.

### math
1. Collect survey data from the class and enter it in a table.
2. Find which numeric feature predicts the label best.
3. Calculate the accuracy of the model as a percent.
4. Graph the training results and describe the trend.
5. Explain how adding more data changes the results.

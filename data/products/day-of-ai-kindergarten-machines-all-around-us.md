## Overview
Day of AI Kindergarten: Machines All Around Us is a unit for ages 5 to 6. It teaches that machines are human-made tools that are not alive. Machines only do what they are designed or programmed to do. Two lessons, What Is a Machine and How Do Machines Sense the World, run over two 30-minute periods.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn that machines are tools that people make.
- Students compare how machine sensors and human senses work.
- Students find machines in their everyday surroundings.

## Benefits for teachers
- The unit comes with a slide deck, an Educator Guide, and student resources.
- A Google Slides copy of the deck is available.
- The two short lessons fit easily into a kindergarten day.

## Benefits for schools

## Where is the AI?
This unit has no running AI program. It teaches the base ideas that come before AI. Students learn that machines follow programmed behavior and that sensors collect information from the world. These ideas prepare young learners for later lessons about how AI systems work.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Name three machines in the classroom and say what each one was made to do.
2. Sort pictures into machines and living things and explain each choice.
3. Act out a machine that follows one simple instruction and stops when told.
4. Match each machine picture to the sensor it might use.
5. Draw a machine and label the part that senses the world.

### science
1. Compare how your eyes see with how a camera sensor sees.
2. Compare how your ears hear with how a microphone listens.
3. Play a sense game with a blindfold and talk about what information is missing.
4. List the five senses and match a machine sensor to each one.
5. Go on a classroom walk and point to every sensor you can find.

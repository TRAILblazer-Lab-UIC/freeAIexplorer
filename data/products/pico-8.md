## Overview
PICO-8 is a fantasy console for making, sharing, and playing tiny games. It includes code, sprite, map, and sound editors. Programs are written in Lua and shared as cartridge images. A free web version called the Education Edition runs at pico-8-edu.com.

## Benefits for students
- Students make a complete small game in one tool.
- Students draw sprites and design maps in the built-in editors.
- Students learn Lua in a small and focused space.

## Benefits for teachers
- A Nerdy Teachers PICO-8 course exists for classroom use.
- The Education Edition starts in a browser with no install.
- Duke and Tartu universities use it in courses.

## Benefits for schools
- The web Education Edition is free.
- The paid desktop version costs 14.99 USD.
- Search results report a site-wide license for a school on the homepage.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open pico-8-edu.com in a browser to try it free.
- [ ] Ask the vendor about the school license on the homepage.
- [ ] Buy the desktop build only if you need storage and export.
- [ ] Note that the Education Edition has no SPLORE and adds URL cartridges.
- [ ] Look at the Nerdy Teachers PICO-8 course.
- [ ] Make a sprite and draw it on screen.
- [ ] Save a cartridge image of a finished game.

## Where is the AI?
The vendor does not describe any AI in PICO-8. Students write Lua code that runs the game. They build logic and sequencing skills as they code how a game responds to buttons.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state what data the site collects, and no privacy policy was found. No account is needed to use the app. The site has an optional login for the cartridge BBS. The FAQ says the web version has no permanent storage. Games are shared as cartridges. A teacher should check the vendor terms and decide how students share their games.

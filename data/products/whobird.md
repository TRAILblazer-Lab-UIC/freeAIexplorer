## Overview
whoBIRD is an Android app that identifies birds by sound in real time. It is based on the BirdNET project and covers more than 6,000 species worldwide. It runs on the device and needs the internet only to download model files at first launch. Students use it outdoors to listen and learn which birds are nearby.

## Benefits for students
- Students can name a bird from its song while they listen.
- Students see a list of detections stored on the device.
- Students can save sound clips as .wav files if they choose.

## Benefits for teachers
- A location and date filter narrows results to likely species.
- The app works offline in the field.
- It is easy for students to use.

## Benefits for schools
- The app is free and open source.
- Recognition happens on the device.
- It is Android only. A developer verification warning may block install on school Android devices.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find whoBIRD on F-Droid.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install on school Android devices.
- [ ] Install the app. It needs Android 9 or newer.
- [ ] Open it once online to download the model.
- [ ] Go offline and test it near a window.
- [ ] Turn on location and date filtering.
- [ ] Practice with a short outdoor walk.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is an audio classification model that identifies bird species from sound. It runs on the phone. A second model uses location and time of year to rank likely species. Students use the guess to start their own observations and then check it in a field guide.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed. The README says detection happens entirely on the device, and detections are stored in a local database. The internet is needed to download model files, and the optional Show Images setting downloads bird images. F-Droid lists permissions for location, audio recording, Bluetooth, and full network access. Saved audio files are not deleted automatically, and the project has no privacy policy. A teacher should check that students allow location only when needed and clear saved recordings on shared devices.

## Overview
This curriculum teaches computer science with music in EarSketch. Students write Python or JavaScript to create their own music, sounds, and effects. It links a curriculum guide and a Teacher Site with free materials and a community. Teachers get lesson plans, slides, projects, and assessments per Georgia Tech.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn real code by making music they care about.
- Students choose Python or JavaScript.
- Students remix songs and build their own tracks.

## Benefits for teachers
- Lesson plans, slides, projects, and assessments are provided.
- Wikipedia says it is aligned with AP Computer Science Principles and has three units.
- Annual summer teacher workshops are offered.

## Benefits for schools
- The curriculum is free.
- It runs in a browser and works on Chromebooks.
- Accounts are optional. The site is always online, and saved work likely goes to the vendor cloud.

## Getting started
- [ ] Open EarSketch in a browser and find the curriculum guide.
- [ ] Visit the Teacher Site for lesson plans and slides.
- [ ] Review the privacy terms with your school before students sign in.
- [ ] Pick a unit and try its first lesson yourself.
- [ ] Decide whether students will create accounts to save work.
- [ ] Look into the summer teacher workshops.

## Where is the AI?
This curriculum has no AI. It builds sequencing and pattern skills. Students arrange sounds in order and repeat them with code.

## Privacy and safety
The site is always online. Per Wikipedia an account is optional for creating or viewing projects, and saved scripts with an account would be stored by EarSketch. The research did not read a privacy policy, and the materials do not say what student data is collected. A teacher should read the EarSketch privacy policy and decide whether students need accounts.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Follow the first lesson and write a short script that places a loop on a track.
2. Use a loop in code to repeat a beat.
3. Add a decision to a script that changes the song in a chosen section.
4. Write a function that builds one verse and call it twice.
5. Share a script with a partner and explain each line aloud.

### music
1. Create an original track with sounds and effects built from code.
2. Remix a song and describe which choices changed its feel.
3. Compare the structure of two songs and recreate one section in code.
4. Add effects to a track and listen for how each one changes the sound.
5. Perform a short listening session for the class and explain the musical choices.

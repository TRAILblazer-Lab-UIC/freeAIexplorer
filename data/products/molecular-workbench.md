## Overview
Molecular Workbench is free modeling software for secondary science from the Concord Consortium. Teachers and students run computational experiments and can build their own simulations. A 2016 TCEA post describes hundreds of prebuilt simulations with embedded assessments. They cover gas laws, chemical bonding and reactions, protein synthesis, and quantum phenomena.

## Benefits for students
- Students watch atoms and molecules move.
- Students change settings and see the result.
- Students can build their own simulations.

## Benefits for teachers
- Embedded assessments check student understanding.
- The simulations cover many topics in secondary science.
- An authoring system lets teachers build new ones.

## Benefits for schools
- The software is free, with noncommercial licensing terms.
- The classic version runs on Java on Windows, Mac, and Linux.
- Concord also offers a separate HTML5 Next-Generation version.

## Getting started
- [ ] Ask IT to install Java.
- [ ] Download Molecular Workbench and open an activity.
- [ ] Ask IT to approve the install, which is likely needed.
- [ ] Avoid Chromebooks for the classic version.
- [ ] Look at the Next-Generation HTML5 version for browser use.
- [ ] Try one gas law activity before class.

## Where is the AI?
This tool has no AI. It runs physics based simulations. Students build skills in modeling and in reading how a change in one variable affects a system.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the classic version is a downloaded local application. The internet is used at setup. The research read no privacy policy for the classic version and did not read the Next-Generation page. The materials do not say whether the old Java version is still supported. A teacher should check the license terms and ask IT whether Java and the version choice suit the school.

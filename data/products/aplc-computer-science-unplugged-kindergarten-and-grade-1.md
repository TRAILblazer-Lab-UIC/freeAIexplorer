## Overview
This resource has two units, one for Kindergarten and one for Grade 1. Each unit holds five unplugged lessons with a scripted teacher guide and a student printable. It suits early elementary teachers in Alberta and anywhere else. Students sequence steps on paper grids with turn arrows and playing cards.

## Benefits for students
- Students practice putting two or three steps in the right order to follow a path.
- Students work with grids, arrows, and cards that they can touch and move.
- Students meet early computing ideas without any screen time.

## Benefits for teachers
- Each lesson comes with a scripted teacher guide.
- Student printables and assessments are included.
- Instructional videos show how to use paper grids, turn arrows, and playing cards.

## Benefits for schools
- The lessons need only paper, playing cards, and grid mats.
- No devices are needed in the room.
- The vendor lists no price and links the PDFs directly.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Move a paper character along a grid by laying out two or three turn arrow cards in order.
2. Swap arrow cards with a partner and follow each other's path step by step.
3. Fix a path that fails by finding the one wrong card and replacing it.
4. Use playing cards as steps and act out the sequence on the floor.
5. Draw a short path on a grid and write the arrows needed to follow it.

### science
1. Sequence two or three steps to complete a simple task, which matches the Alberta computer science outcomes.
2. Order the steps of a plant growing and compare the order with a classmate.
3. Lay out arrow cards that guide a paper bee to a flower on a grid.
4. Describe the steps of washing hands and check that the order works.
5. Trace a path on a grid map of the schoolyard and say each step aloud.

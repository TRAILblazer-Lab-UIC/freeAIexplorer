## Overview
This is a lesson on the ASU STEM Teachers site where students program an Ozobot to sing a familiar song. Search results describe a set of Google Slides credited to Hannah Evans from 2023. Students learn notes and octaves and drag and drop note codes in the OzoBlockly block language. They load the program onto the Ozobot and listen and revise. The official page returned an error. These details come from search results.

## Benefits for students
- Students learn music ideas such as notes and octaves.
- Students build the song with drag and drop blocks.
- Students listen, revise and record a video of their song.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds an AI-related skill, which is sequencing. Students arrange note blocks in order and listen to the result.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need or whether the internet is required. OzoBlockly is a browser editor. The slides name Flipgrid for sharing videos. Flipgrid is a separate account based service and its use is optional. The research did not review the platform privacy terms. A teacher should check the OzoBlockly and Flipgrid rules and confirm that Flipgrid is still available. Student videos should stay within school rules.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Learn the names of notes and octaves and match them to blocks.
2. Build the first line of a familiar song with note blocks.
3. Listen to the Ozobot play the song and fix a wrong note.
4. Change the octave and describe how the sound changes.
5. Record a video of your song and play it for the class.

### computer_science
1. Drag and drop note blocks in OzoBlockly to build a program.
2. Load the program on an Ozobot and test it.
3. Debug a note that plays in the wrong order.
4. Use the three level rubric to check your work.
5. Explain to a partner how your blocks match the song.

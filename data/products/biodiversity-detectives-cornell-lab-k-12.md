## Overview
Biodiversity Detectives is a grades 6 to 8 curriculum in the eBird Explorers suite from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. Students collect and analyze bird observations with the eBird citizen science project. Related lab resources use the Merlin Bird ID app. The official page returned an error. The details come from search results.

## Benefits for students
- Students investigate real bird data from their area.
- Students practice science and math with live data.
- Students can try the Merlin app to identify birds.

## Benefits for teachers
- A free 6-8 lesson is available.
- A teacher guide called eBird Essentials for Educators supports the work.
- Binoculars and the Merlin app are optional.

## Benefits for schools
- The educator resources are free per the Lab.
- Full access terms were not confirmed.
- A teacher can download resources and optionally make an eBird account for the class.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is optional. The Merlin Bird ID app identifies birds from photos and sound, and it runs partly on the phone and partly in the vendor's cloud. The eBird lessons themselves work with data and observations. Students can use Merlin to check a guess while they record sightings.

## Privacy and safety
An eBird account is optional and the Lab has a single sign in. The educator guide says a teacher can make one account for a class. Observations that go to eBird become public data held by the Cornell Lab. Merlin runs as a phone app. The research did not review the privacy terms and the official page was unreadable. A teacher should check the privacy terms and decide what location detail students share.

## Ideas by subject
### science
1. Investigate local biodiversity with eBird observations for your region.
2. Record the birds seen on school grounds for a week and list the species.
3. Compare bird counts in two habitats and explain the difference.
4. Graph the number of species by season.
5. Use the Merlin app to check an identification and note where it was unsure.

## Overview
Cubroid is a kit of wireless coding blocks listed for ages 5 and up. The RobotShop page names a Master Block, DC Motor, LED, Sound, Proximity Sensor, and Light and Touch Sensor blocks. They work with Lego pieces. Children program them from the Coding Cubroid 2 app by drag and drop.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and program with their hands.
- Students start with drag and drop and can move to Scratch 2.0.
- Students can try camera AI in the Coding Cubroid AI app.

## Benefits for teachers
- The blocks suit early elementary learners.
- An Advanced Cubroid tablet app serves older students.
- The AI Stage uses the camera for face, hand, pose, and color recognition.

## Benefits for schools
- RobotShop listed the Premium Kit at about 551 USD.
- The Coding Cubroid AI app is free on the App Store.
- Support beyond iOS was not verified.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Cubroid kit on the RobotShop page.
- [ ] Gather an iPhone or iPad on iOS or iPadOS 14.0 or later.
- [ ] Install the Cubroid app and turn on Bluetooth.
- [ ] Pair the wireless blocks.
- [ ] Connect the Master Block to a computer for Scratch mode.
- [ ] Review the App Store privacy label.
- [ ] Test the AI Stage with the device camera.

## Where is the AI?
The Coding Cubroid AI app has an AI Stage that uses the device camera. It recognizes faces, hands, poses, and colors. The vendor does not say where the AI runs. Children use what the camera sees to trigger actions with the blocks.

## Privacy and safety
The App Store label says no data is collected, but the developer reports this and Apple does not verify it. No privacy policy for the Cubroid apps was found. The materials do not state whether an account or the internet is needed. Bluetooth connects the blocks, and the AI Stage uses the camera. The research did not confirm where the camera AI runs. A teacher should check the privacy label and tell children when the camera is on.

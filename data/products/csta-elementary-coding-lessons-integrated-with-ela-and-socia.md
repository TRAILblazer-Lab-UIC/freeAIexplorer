## Overview
This CSTA resource library entry offers three units that teach coding together with ELA and social studies standards. The lessons use the free story coder feature in codeSpark Academy. They were developed under a Department of Education grant. The listing offers slides and video, and the full content is locked behind a CSTA membership.

## Benefits for students
- Students tell stories with code.
- Students link coding to ELA and social studies topics.
- Students work with block-based tools.

## Benefits for teachers
- The units come with slides and video.
- Teachers can tie coding to standards they already teach.
- The listing suits elementary teachers.

## Benefits for schools
- The cost depends on CSTA membership, and the price was not checked.
- Students use a browser.
- Data goes to the vendor.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson set has no AI. It builds skills in sequencing and storytelling. Students arrange blocks to make a story play in order.

## Privacy and safety
Access to the lessons needs a CSTA login, and the lesson details sit behind CSTA membership. The research did not see them. The lessons use codeSpark Academy in a browser, and its privacy policy was not reviewed. The listing does not say what student data is collected, and student data goes to the vendor. A teacher should read the codeSpark and CSTA privacy information and check what the story coder collects before students sign in.

## Ideas by subject
### ela
1. Write a short story and build it in the story coder.
2. Retell a story from class with new characters.
3. Add dialogue that shows what a character feels.
4. Trade stories with a partner and describe the plot.
5. Revise a story after feedback.

### social_studies
1. Tell the story of a community event in the story coder.
2. Make a short animation about a historical person.
3. Show a map of a neighborhood in a story scene.
4. Compare how two people describe the same event.
5. Present a story and explain its main idea.

### computer_science
1. Order blocks to make a story play in sequence.
2. Add a loop to repeat a scene.
3. Fix a story in which the scenes run in the wrong order.
4. Explain how the blocks tell the story.
5. Plan a story on paper before coding it.

## Overview
This unplugged activity has 36 printable animal cards for teaching classification. Students pick features such as legs, feathers, and fins and build a decision tree. The instructor hides 7 animals in an envelope as a test set. Students check how accurate their tree is.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a decision tree by hand.
- Students test their tree on animals they have not seen.
- Students talk about training data, bias, and overfitting.

## Benefits for teachers
- The page suits kids and adults with no machine learning background.
- The PowerPoint file can be edited for other categories.
- Variants cover 0/1 feature graphs and a linear classifier.

## Benefits for schools
- The downloads are free in pptx and pdf.
- Cardstock is optional.
- No devices or accounts are needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Find the Animal Card Set activity page and download the cards.
- [ ] Print the cards and cut them into 36 pieces.
- [ ] Put 7 cards in an opaque envelope as the test set.
- [ ] Prepare decision tree arrows and feature names.
- [ ] Give each group the remaining cards.
- [ ] Let groups build a tree for 10 to 15 minutes.
- [ ] Reveal the test set and check each tree's accuracy.

## Where is the AI?
The activity has no software and no AI runs on any device. Students act as the learner by building a decision tree from training cards. They then test it on hidden cards. This builds skills in classification, in reading accuracy, and in spotting bias and overfitting.

## Privacy and safety
The activity uses printed cards and the research found no accounts or student data collection. The internet is used to download the files. The animal pictures were generated with Hotpot.ai and the data comes from a Kaggle dataset. A teacher should check the animal cards before class and edit any that do not fit the class. A teacher should also decide how to run the hidden test set fairly.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Build a decision tree from the training cards using three features.
2. Test the tree on the hidden animals and count the right answers.
3. Compare the tree from one group with a tree from another group.
4. Remove half the training cards and see how the accuracy changes.
5. Discuss how a tree can fit the training cards too closely and fail on new ones.

### science
1. Choose features such as legs, feathers, and fins to sort animals.
2. Name an animal that does not fit the groups and explain why.
3. Draw a tree to sort animals into mammals, birds, and fish.
4. Compare your tree with how scientists group animals.
5. Edit the cards to sort plants or rocks and test the new tree.

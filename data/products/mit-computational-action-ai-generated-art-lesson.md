## Overview
This MIT Computational Action lesson runs about 90 minutes in three parts. Students explore tools that use GANs to make art. They discuss who creates and owns art made by humans, by humans with AI, and by AI alone. Two activities follow, called Art or Not and GANs Art. The activities need Google Chrome.

## Benefits for students
- Students try tools that generate art.
- Students debate who owns art made with AI.
- Students learn how GANs make images.

## Benefits for teachers
- A script and slides are provided.
- The lesson has three timed parts.
- An Everyday AI adaptation exists for reference.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free.
- The activities use web tools in Chrome.
- The lesson was created in 2022, and GAN technology has advanced since.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a generative adversarial network, or GAN, that makes images. The materials do not say where it runs. Students try web tools, judge which art came from AI, and then make their own GAN art.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need. The activities use GAN web tools in Google Chrome and Google Slides copies, and the internet is always needed. The page does not name the tools or discuss data. The research read no privacy policy. The discussion part needs no student data. A teacher should check each web tool for sign ins and for how images are handled. A teacher should also review any art that students make.

## Ideas by subject
### visual_arts
1. Play Art or Not and explain the clues you used to decide.
2. Debate who owns a picture made by a human with AI.
3. Make GAN art with a web tool and describe your choices.
4. Compare a hand-drawn piece with a GAN piece and list differences.
5. Write an artist statement that credits the tools you used.

### computer_science
1. Explain how a GAN uses a generator and a discriminator.
2. Test how changing an input changes the generated image.
3. Describe what training data a GAN might need.
4. List three ways a GAN image can look wrong.
5. Draw a diagram of how a GAN learns over time.

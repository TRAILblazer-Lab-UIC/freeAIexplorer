## Overview
The Crazy Circuits Bit Board adapts a BBC micro:bit to parts such as buttons, a potentiometer, LEDs, a Neopixel strip, a 7-segment display, a distance sensor and a servo. Parts snap onto a LEGO-compatible base and connect with conductive Maker Tape. No soldering is needed. Students in grades 4 and up build circuits and program the micro:bit.

## Benefits for students
- Students build real circuits with no soldering.
- Students program sensors and outputs on the micro:bit.
- Students use LEGO-compatible parts to build around a circuit.

## Benefits for teachers
- Sets come in packs of 5, 10 and 25.
- A pack of 10 reportedly holds 9 student kits and 1 teacher kit.
- Retailers say free lesson plans, activities, assessments and videos are included.

## Benefits for schools
- Search snippets show prices from about 185 USD to 337 USD for a single set.
- Sets come with or without micro:bits.
- No account is needed.

## Getting started
- [ ] Order a pack size from a reseller. Some reseller listings mark the sets as discontinued.
- [ ] Ask the reseller what is in the set. Vendor pages were not fetched in the research.
- [ ] Decide whether to buy micro:bits with the set.
- [ ] Build a first circuit on the base with Maker Tape.
- [ ] Open the micro:bit editor on a computer or tablet.
- [ ] Program a button and an LED.
- [ ] Check the lesson materials before class.

## Where is the AI?
The Bit Board has no AI. The micro:bit runs the program that students write. Students build skills in circuits, sensors and logic.

## Privacy and safety
The kit is hardware, and no account is needed for it. The micro:bit editor has its own data practices, and the research did not review them. The materials do not state whether the editor needs the internet. No privacy policy was reviewed. A teacher should check the micro:bit editor privacy information and test the editor on school devices.

## Ideas by subject
### engineering
1. Build a circuit that lights an LED with a button.
2. Use the potentiometer to dim an LED and explain the circuit.
3. Add a servo and design a small moving part.
4. Test the distance sensor and record its readings.
5. Design a small device that uses three parts and present it.

1. Program a button press to turn on an LED.
2. Show a number on the 7-segment display.
3. Make the Neopixel strip change colors in a pattern.
4. Use the distance sensor to trigger an action.
5. Debug a program that reads the wrong input.

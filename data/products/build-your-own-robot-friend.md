## Overview
This open source module has students build a simplified, low-cost version of the Blossom companion robot. The robot is 3D printed with a knitted or crocheted exterior. Students customize it and program it to mirror their head posture using pre-trained pose tracking and gesture recognition models. It was piloted with 15 undergraduates and is aimed at high school and college learners.

## Benefits for students
- Students build and decorate their own robot.
- Students program it to copy their head nods.
- Students discuss AI ethics and fairness.

## Benefits for teachers
- The module covers human-centered AI, robotics, machine learning, and ethics.
- Assembly guides, video tutorials, and crochet patterns are provided.
- The pages do not give a full parts list.

## Benefits for schools
- The USC article reports about 250 USD per robot.
- The module needs a 3D printer or printed parts and craft materials.
- Platforms and network needs are not confirmed. IT should check the software.

## Where is the AI?
The AI is a set of pre-trained models for head pose tracking and gesture recognition. The materials do not say where they run. Students program the robot to mirror their posture. They also study fairness and ethics in human-robot interaction.

## Privacy and safety
The materials do not state which accounts students need, whether the internet is required, or where data goes. The pages do not say whether the pose models run on a local computer or in the cloud. The pose tracking uses a camera that sees the student, and the research could not confirm whether camera data leaves the device. A teacher should confirm whether video is stored or sent anywhere. A teacher should also use clear consent steps for any camera use.

## Ideas by subject
### computer_science
1. Run the pose tracking models and see how the robot mirrors a nod.
2. Change one setting in the code and observe the difference.
3. Test the gesture recognition with different classmates and record the results.
4. Discuss whether the models work equally well for everyone.
5. Write a short program that makes the robot respond to a gesture.

### engineering
1. 3D print the robot parts and check their fit.
2. Assemble the robot and test its movements.
3. Crochet or knit a cover and test how it affects movement.
4. Customize the robot's look for a purpose and explain the design.
5. Record problems during the build and describe the fixes.

## Overview
This is a 5E project unit for grades 6 to 8 on interactive microcontroller systems. Students build a paper piano from cardstock and copper tape or foil. They wire it to a micro:bit with alligator clips and write block code in MakeCode to play the notes of a scale. The unit takes about 6 hours and fits music, music technology, or intro computer science classes.

## Benefits for students
- Students build a working instrument with their own hands.
- Students learn conditionals and digital input in block code.
- Students compose and share short melodies.

## Benefits for teachers
- The unit is a ready PDF that covers a full project.
- Teachers should know micro:bit programming, binary conversion, and the C major scale.
- It fits music and computer science classes.

## Benefits for schools
- The lesson is free. Each student needs a micro:bit board, a USB cable, a battery pack, and 5 alligator clips.
- The unit lists alignment to Georgia CS and Georgia Music Technology standards.
- MakeCode runs in a browser and the setup load for IT is small.

## Where is the AI?
This lesson has no AI. It builds skills in logic, sensors, and sequencing. Students use conditionals to decide which note plays when a key is touched.

## Privacy and safety
No account is needed, and the PDF does not mention accounts. The internet is used for setup, and MakeCode runs in a browser. MakeCode projects save in the browser unless a student signs in. The micro:bit then runs the program on the board. A teacher should check whether students sign in to MakeCode and how they save and share projects.

## Ideas by subject
### music
1. Build and play a paper piano with the notes of a scale, as the unit describes.
2. Compose a short melody and play it on the piano.
3. Play a song for a partner and ask them to name it.
4. Compare the sound of your piano with a real keyboard.
5. Share a melody with the class and explain the notes you chose.

### computer_science
1. Write MakeCode blocks that use conditionals to play a note for each key.
2. Test each key and fix any wire that does not respond.
3. Convert a number to binary and relate it to digital input.
4. Add a new block to change what happens when two keys are pressed.
5. Explain your program step by step to a classmate.

### engineering
1. Cut and tape seven cardstock keys with copper tape.
2. Wire the keys to the micro:bit with alligator clips.
3. Test the circuit and find a loose connection.
4. Redesign the key layout to make it easier to play.
5. Document your build with a sketch and a short list of steps.
